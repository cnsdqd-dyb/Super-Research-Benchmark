# In Monitoring Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD), What Guides the Balance Between Immune Reconstruction and Infection Risk When Adjusting Immunosuppressants Based on Histopathology?

## Abstract

Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD) is a significant complication following allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). Balancing immune reconstruction and infection risk is crucial for optimal patient outcomes. This report explores the current guidelines and strategies for adjusting immunosuppressants based on histopathological findings. We discuss the role of biomarkers, clinical scoring systems, and novel therapeutic agents in managing GVHD while minimizing infection risk.

## Introduction

GVHD occurs when donor immune cells recognize the recipient's tissues as foreign and mount an immune response, leading to tissue damage. This condition can be acute (aGVHD) or chronic (cGVHD) and is associated with significant morbidity and mortality. The management of GVHD involves a delicate balance between suppressing the immune response to prevent tissue damage and maintaining sufficient immune function to prevent opportunistic infections. This report aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the current practices and emerging strategies for monitoring and managing GVHD, focusing on the integration of histopathological findings and the use of biomarkers to guide immunosuppressive therapy.

## Pathophysiology and Clinical Manifestations

### Acute GVHD (aGVHD)

aGVHD typically occurs within the first 100 days post-transplant and primarily affects the skin, gastrointestinal tract, and liver. The pathophysiology involves the activation of donor T cells, which recognize recipient antigens and initiate an inflammatory response. Clinical manifestations include:

- **Skin:** Rash, erythema, and blistering
- **Gastrointestinal Tract:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain
- **Liver:** Elevated bilirubin levels and jaundice

### Chronic GVHD (cGVHD)

cGVHD can develop months to years after transplantation and can involve multiple organ systems. It often presents with symptoms similar to autoimmune diseases, such as Sjögren's syndrome and scleroderma. Common manifestations include:

- **Skin:** Lichenoid rash, hyperpigmentation, and sclerosis
- **Mucous Membranes:** Dry eyes and mouth, and oral ulcers
- **Lungs:** Bronchiolitis obliterans and restrictive lung disease
- **Liver:** Cholestasis and liver fibrosis

## Diagnosis and Staging

### Histopathological Findings

Histopathology plays a crucial role in the diagnosis and staging of GVHD. Key findings include:

- **Skin:** Interface dermatitis, apoptotic keratinocytes, and lymphocytic infiltration
- **Gastrointestinal Tract:** Crypt abscesses, apoptotic bodies, and lymphocytic infiltration
- **Liver:** Lymphocytic infiltration around portal tracts and bile duct damage

### Clinical Scoring Systems

Two primary systems are used to clinically determine the severity of aGVHD:

- **Glucksberg System:** Grades severity from 1 (mild) to 4 (life-threatening)
- **CIBMTR Severity Index:** Grades severity from A to D

Staging at specific sites is stratified into four stages, with higher stages indicating more severe disease.

## Immunosuppressive Therapy

### Corticosteroids

Corticosteroids, such as methylprednisolone and prednisone, are the first-line treatment for both aGVHD and cGVHD. However, long-term use is associated with significant side effects, including:

- **Infections:** Increased susceptibility to opportunistic infections
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Hyperglycemia, osteoporosis, and hypertension
- **Immunodeficiency:** Delayed immune recovery and increased risk of relapse

### Second-Line Therapies

Several second-line therapies have been approved for the treatment of GVHD, particularly in steroid-refractory cases:

- **Ruxolitinib (Jakafi):** A JAK inhibitor that reduces inflammation and has shown promise in both aGVHD and cGVHD.
- **Ibrutinib (Imbruvica):** A kinase inhibitor that targets BTK and ITK, reducing inflammation and improving skin and mouth manifestations of cGVHD.
- **Belumosudil (Rezurock):** Targets scar pathways and rebalances immune subpopulations, with minimal side effects.

### Biomarkers and Prognostic Indicators

Biomarkers can provide valuable information for early diagnosis and treatment response. Key biomarkers include:

- **REG3α:** Specific for gastrointestinal GVHD
- **Elafin:** Useful for systemic GVHD
- **ST2 and Reg3α:** Predictive of treatment response and mortality

## Balancing Immune Reconstruction and Infection Risk

### Immune Reconstitution

Achieving and maintaining optimal immune reconstitution is crucial for post-transplant success. Rapid recovery of T and NK cells can be beneficial for tumor control and infection prevention but may increase the risk of aGVHD. Strategies to promote immune reconstitution while minimizing GVHD include:

- **Non-T-cell Depletion Protocols:** Such as the Beijing and Baltimore protocols, which improve immune reconstitution and reduce the risk of aGVHD.
- **Vitamin D3:** Enhances antimicrobial defense mechanisms and supports the intestinal barrier, potentially reducing the risk of infections and GVHD.

### Monitoring and Adjusting Immunotherapy

- **Early Response:** A very early response to initial therapy, as evidenced by the ability to begin a steroid taper on Day 5, is associated with a favorable prognosis.
- **Biomarker-Guided Therapy:** Serial evaluation of biomarkers can guide the adjustment of immunosuppressive therapy, ensuring that patients remain low risk while minimizing infection risk.
- **Personalized Protocols:** Tailoring immunosuppressive therapy based on individual patient characteristics and biomarker profiles can optimize outcomes.

## Conclusion

Balancing immune reconstruction and infection risk in the management of GVHD is a complex challenge. Histopathological findings, clinical scoring systems, and biomarkers are essential tools for guiding immunosuppressive therapy. Novel therapeutic agents, such as ruxolitinib, ibrutinib, and belumosudil, offer promising options for treating GVHD while minimizing the risk of infection. Personalized approaches that consider individual patient characteristics and biomarker profiles are crucial for optimizing outcomes and improving the quality of life for patients undergoing HSCT.
