# Role of Thermal Runaway Propagation Mechanism in Designing Safety Redundancy for Lithium-Ion Battery Packs

## Abstract
Thermal runaway (TR) in lithium-ion battery packs is a critical safety concern, particularly in applications such as electric vehicles and large-scale energy storage systems. This report reviews the role of thermal runaway propagation (TRP) mechanisms in designing safety redundancy for lithium-ion battery packs, with a focus on electrolyte and separator stability. The report discusses the development of mathematical and numerical models to predict TR, the importance of battery thermal management systems, and mitigation strategies to contain and minimize damages when TR occurs. The impact of cell chemistry, particularly the differences between NMC-811 and LFP cathodes, is also analyzed.

## 1. Introduction
Thermal runaway in lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) is a self-accelerating exothermic reaction that can lead to catastrophic failure, including fire and explosion. The propagation of TR within a battery pack can result in widespread damage and poses significant safety risks. Therefore, understanding and mitigating TRP is crucial for the design and development of safe and reliable battery systems. This report explores the role of TRP mechanisms in designing safety redundancy, focusing on the stability of electrolytes and separators.

## 2. Thermal Runaway Mechanisms
### 2.1. Initial Triggering
Thermal runaway can be initiated by various factors, including internal short circuits, overcharging, and external heating. The initial triggering stage is characterized by the onset of exothermic reactions within the battery cell. These reactions can be triggered by the decomposition of the electrolyte, the breakdown of the separator, or the reaction between the electrodes and the electrolyte [1].

### 2.2. Propagation
Once TR is initiated, it can propagate to adjacent cells within the battery pack. The propagation of TR is influenced by several factors, including cell spacing, intermediate materials, and the entire cell stack setup. The choice of cell chemistry also plays a decisive role in the safety design of a battery system. For instance, NMC-811 cells have a faster TR reaction speed compared to LFP cells, making them more prone to TRP [2].

## 3. Role of Electrolyte and Separator Stability
### 3.1. Electrolyte Stability
The electrolyte is a critical component in LIBs, and its stability significantly affects the safety of the battery. The flammability of the electrolyte can be reduced by adding functional additives, using new lithium salts, or employing new solvents. For example, flame retardant additives can reduce the flammability of the electrolyte, thereby enhancing the safety of the battery [3].

### 3.2. Separator Stability
The separator is another crucial component that plays a vital role in preventing internal short circuits. Polyolefin-based separators, such as polyethylene (PE) and polypropylene (PP), are commonly used but exhibit insufficient thermal stability under high-temperature conditions. Advanced separator materials, such as those incorporating ceramic coatings (Al\(_2\)O\(_3\), SiO\(_2\)), can create effective thermal barriers that maintain structural integrity at elevated temperatures, preventing internal short circuits during thermal events [4].

## 4. Mitigation Strategies
### 4.1. Thermal Management Systems
Effective thermal management is crucial for preventing the initiation of TR. Battery thermal management systems (BTMS) can help regulate the temperature of the battery pack, reducing the risk of overheating. Techniques such as air cooling, liquid cooling, and phase change materials (PCMs) can be employed to manage heat dissipation and prevent the onset of TR [5].

### 4.2. Cell-to-Pack Design
The design of the battery pack, including cell spacing and the use of intermediate materials, can significantly impact TRP. Proper cell spacing and the use of thermal insulating materials can reduce the likelihood of TRP. For example, the use of PCMs can effectively suppress the propagation of TR by absorbing and dissipating heat [6].

### 4.3. Advanced Cell Chemistry
The choice of cell chemistry is a critical factor in the safety design of battery systems. NMC-811 cells, while offering higher energy density, are more prone to TRP compared to LFP cells. Therefore, the design of battery packs should consider the trade-off between energy density and safety. Advanced system integration strategies can help mitigate the disadvantages of lower energy density in LFP cells [7].

## 5. Case Studies and Experimental Results
### 5.1. TRP in NMC-811 and LFP Cells
A comparative analysis of TRP in NMC-811 and LFP cells from a Mini Cooper SE and Tesla Model 3 SR+ revealed that NMC-811 cells have a significantly faster TR reaction speed. The thermal propagation in NMC-811 modules is five times faster than in LFP modules. However, LFP cells maintain better mechanical integrity and are less prone to TRP, making them a safer choice for battery packs [8].

### 5.2. Impact of Cell Spacing and Intermediate Materials
Experimental studies have shown that cell spacing and the use of intermediate materials can significantly affect TRP. For example, a 2 mm spacing between cells in a 21700 NMC-811 cell configuration can reduce the likelihood of TRP. The use of compressed graphite and phase change materials (PCMs) can further enhance the thermal management of the battery pack, preventing the propagation of TR [9].

## 6. Conclusion
Thermal runaway propagation is a critical safety concern in lithium-ion battery packs. The stability of electrolytes and separators plays a significant role in preventing the initiation and propagation of TR. Effective thermal management systems, proper cell-to-pack design, and the choice of cell chemistry are essential for designing safe and reliable battery systems. Advanced materials and innovative design strategies can help mitigate the risks associated with TRP, ensuring the safety and longevity of lithium-ion battery packs.
