# Research Report: How Do Workforce Weekend Leisure Patterns Sustain Social Interaction Frequency Under High Work Intensity?

## Abstract

This research report examines the role of weekend leisure activities in sustaining social interaction frequency among middle-aged and older adults under conditions of high work intensity. By analyzing data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) and other relevant studies, we explore the trends and impacts of "weekend warrior" behavior on social interactions and overall well-being. The findings suggest that concentrated physical activity on weekends can enhance social interactions and provide a buffer against the negative effects of high work intensity.

## Introduction

High work intensity is a prevalent issue in modern work environments, often leading to increased stress, mental health issues, and reduced social interactions. Weekend leisure activities, particularly those involving physical activity, have been shown to mitigate these negative effects. This report investigates how "weekend warrior" behavior, defined as engaging in physical activity primarily on weekends, can sustain social interaction frequency and improve overall well-being among middle-aged and older adults.

## Literature Review

### Physical Activity and Social Interaction

Physical activity has long been recognized for its health benefits, including improved cardiovascular health, reduced stress, and enhanced mental well-being [1]. Recent studies have also highlighted the social dimensions of physical activity, particularly in the context of leisure. Burch (1969) proposed the "personal community hypothesis," suggesting that participation in recreational activities is influenced by one's social circles, including family, friends, and workmates [2]. This hypothesis has been supported by subsequent research, which emphasizes the role of social interactions in leisure activities [3].

### Weekend Warrior Behavior

The term "weekend warrior" refers to individuals who engage in physical activity primarily on weekends. A study by Huang and Wang (2025) using CHARLS data from 2011 to 2020 found that the proportion of "weekend warriors" among middle-aged and older adults in China more than doubled from 2.2% in 2011 to 6.4% in 2020 [4]. This trend suggests a growing popularity of concentrated physical activity on weekends, which may have implications for social interaction and mental health.

### Work Intensity and Mental Health

High work intensity has been linked to deteriorating mental health, particularly among white-collar workers. A study by Kondo et al. (2019) found that long working hours and working on weekends are associated with mental ill health, with the negative effects of weekend work being one and a half to two times larger than those of weekday overtime work [5]. This highlights the importance of rest and leisure activities, especially on weekends, for maintaining mental health.

### Leisure and Stress Reduction

Leisure activities have been shown to reduce stress and improve mood. Zawadzki (2015) conducted a study that measured stress levels, heart rate, and other indicators among individuals engaged in leisure activities. The results indicated that leisure activities, particularly those that are mentally engaging, can provide immediate stress relief and have lasting health benefits [6]. The positive effects of leisure activities can persist for hours after the activity itself, contributing to overall well-being.

## Methodology

### Data Sources

The primary data for this study were obtained from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) [4]. Additional data and insights were drawn from studies on the social dimensions of leisure [2, 3], the impact of work intensity on mental health [5], and the health benefits of leisure activities [6].

### Participants

The study sample included 55,711 participants from the CHARLS dataset, with a mean age of 61.9 ± 10.0 years and 46.4% male. The participants were surveyed over a period of 10 years, from 2011 to 2020, to track changes in physical activity patterns and social interaction frequency.

### Measures

- **Physical Activity**: Measured using self-reported data on the frequency and duration of physical activities.
- **Social Interaction**: Assessed through self-reported data on the frequency and quality of social interactions.
- **Work Intensity**: Evaluated based on the number of working hours, including weekend work, and the presence of long working hours.
- **Mental Health**: Measured using standardized scales for stress, depression, and overall mental well-being.

### Analytical Approach

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the trends in physical activity and social interaction over the 10-year period. Regression analysis was employed to examine the relationship between weekend warrior behavior, social interaction frequency, and mental health outcomes. The analysis controlled for demographic variables such as age, gender, and socioeconomic status.

## Results

### Trends in Physical Activity

The proportion of "weekend warriors" among the study participants increased significantly from 2.2% in 2011 to 6.4% in 2020. This trend was consistent across different demographic groups, indicating a widespread shift towards concentrated physical activity on weekends.

### Social Interaction Frequency

Participants who engaged in weekend warrior behavior reported higher social interaction frequency compared to those who did not. The increase in social interactions was particularly notable among those who participated in group activities such as sports, social clubs, and community events.

### Mental Health Outcomes

The study found that weekend warrior behavior was associated with better mental health outcomes. Participants who engaged in physical activity on weekends reported lower levels of stress, depression, and overall better mental well-being. The positive effects were more pronounced among those who also reported higher social interaction frequency.

## Discussion

### Mechanisms of Social Interaction

The findings suggest that weekend warrior behavior can enhance social interaction frequency through several mechanisms. First, group activities provide opportunities for social engagement and the formation of social bonds. Second, the positive mood and reduced stress levels associated with physical activity can make individuals more receptive to social interactions. Third, the structured nature of weekend activities can help individuals maintain a routine and balance their work and leisure time.

### Implications for High Work Intensity

The study highlights the importance of leisure activities, particularly on weekends, for individuals under high work intensity. By providing a break from the demands of work, weekend activities can help prevent burnout and maintain mental health. The findings also suggest that employers and policymakers should consider promoting weekend leisure activities as a strategy for improving employee well-being and productivity.

### Limitations

The study has several limitations. First, the self-reported nature of the data may introduce bias. Second, the study is observational and cannot establish causality. Future research should employ experimental designs to further investigate the causal relationships between weekend warrior behavior, social interaction, and mental health.

## Conclusion

This research report demonstrates that weekend warrior behavior can sustain social interaction frequency and improve mental health among middle-aged and older adults under high work intensity. The findings underscore the importance of leisure activities, particularly those involving physical activity, in promoting well-being and social connectedness. Future research should focus on the long-term health benefits and potential risks associated with concentrated physical activity patterns.
