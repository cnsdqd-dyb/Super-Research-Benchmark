## 2. Theoretical Framework

### 2.1 Fractal Geometry and Cultural Attractors

#### 2.1.1 Fractal Dimension

The fractal dimension is a measure of the complexity and self-similarity of a pattern. It is defined as the slope of the line when plotting the logarithm of the number of boxes \( N \) covering the pattern against the logarithm of the box size \( r \):

\[
D = \lim_{r \to 0} \frac{\log N(r)}{\log \left(\frac{1}{r}\right)}
\]

This method, known as the box-counting method, is widely used to determine the fractal dimension of complex patterns, including cultural attractors.

#### 2.1.2 Cultural Attractors

Cultural attractors are points in a cultural landscape towards which cultural variants tend to converge over time. These attractors are influenced by various factors, including cognitive biases, social norms, and environmental conditions. The stability and distribution of cultural attractors are crucial for understanding the dynamics of cultural evolution.

### 2.2 Global-Local Interactions

The interaction between global and local factors is a key aspect of cultural evolution. Global factors, such as technological advancements and media influence, can shape cultural trends on a large scale. Local factors, such as community norms and individual experiences, play a crucial role in the stabilization and adaptation of cultural elements. The interplay between these factors can lead to the formation of branch points in cultural evolutionary trajectories.
