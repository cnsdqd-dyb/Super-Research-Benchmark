# Research Report: Do Partition Coefficients in Solvent Extraction Affect the Economic Feasibility of Urban Mining Recovery?

## Abstract

Urban mining, the process of recovering valuable metals from electronic waste (e-waste), has emerged as a critical component of the circular economy. This report investigates the role of partition coefficients in solvent extraction and their impact on the economic feasibility of urban mining. By analyzing the efficiency, scalability, and environmental benefits of solvent extraction, this study aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of how partition coefficients influence the economic viability of urban mining operations.

## 1. Introduction

Urban mining is a promising approach to address the growing challenge of electronic waste (e-waste) and the depletion of natural resources. E-waste is a rich source of valuable metals such as copper, gold, silver, and rare earth elements (REEs). Solvent extraction, a key technology in urban mining, involves the selective transfer of metals from an aqueous solution to an organic solvent. The efficiency of this process is significantly influenced by partition coefficients, which determine the distribution of metals between the aqueous and organic phases.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Urban Mining and E-Waste

Urban mining involves the recovery of valuable materials from urban waste streams, particularly e-waste. E-waste contains high concentrations of metals, often exceeding those found in natural ores. For instance, one ton of printed circuit boards can contain approximately 200 kg of copper, 0.4 kg of silver, and 0.09 kg of gold [1]. The United Nations estimates that e-waste generated in 2022 contained valuables worth approximately $91 billion, most of which remains uncaptured [2].

### 2.2 Solvent Extraction in Urban Mining

Solvent extraction is a hydrometallurgical process that uses organic solvents to selectively extract metals from aqueous solutions. The process involves several steps: acid leaching, solvent extraction, and electrowinning. The partition coefficient (\(K_d\)) is a critical parameter that describes the distribution of a metal between the aqueous and organic phases. It is defined as:

\[
K_d = \frac{C_{\text{organic}}}{C_{\text{aqueous}}}
\]

where \(C_{\text{organic}}\) and \(C_{\text{aqueous}}\) are the concentrations of the metal in the organic and aqueous phases, respectively.

## 3. Economic Feasibility of Urban Mining

### 3.1 Cost Analysis

The economic feasibility of urban mining is influenced by various factors, including the cost of chemical inputs, environmental compliance, and infrastructure investment. Chemical inputs for hydrometallurgical processing, such as acids and extractants, can cost $150-300 per ton [3]. Environmental compliance, including wastewater treatment and emissions control, can add $200-400 per ton [3].

### 3.2 Infrastructure Investment

The capital expenditure for urban mining facilities scales with processing capacity. Small-scale facilities with an annual capacity of 5,000 metric tons require an investment of $5-12 million, while large-scale facilities with a capacity of 100,000+ metric tons can cost $150-300 million [3].

### 3.3 Return on Investment

The return on investment (ROI) for urban mining is influenced by the efficiency of metal recovery and the market value of the recovered metals. For example, recovering one ton of copper via urban mining costs around $3,000, significantly less than conventional mining operations [4]. The economic advantages of urban mining are further enhanced by the high concentrations of valuable metals in e-waste.

## 4. Role of Partition Coefficients in Solvent Extraction

### 4.1 Efficiency of Metal Recovery

Partition coefficients play a crucial role in the efficiency of metal recovery. High partition coefficients indicate a strong preference for the metal to transfer to the organic phase, enhancing the selectivity and purity of the recovered metal. For instance, Murali et al. [5] successfully recovered copper from e-waste using a bioleaching process followed by solvent extraction and electrowinning. Under optimized conditions, more than 94% of copper and less than 10% of iron were transferred from the e-waste leach solution during the loading phase, with a 92% stripping efficiency from the loaded organic phase.

### 4.2 Environmental Impact

Solvent extraction offers significant environmental benefits compared to traditional mining and smelting practices. By extracting valuable metals from discarded electronics, solvent extraction minimizes the volume of waste destined for landfills and incineration. This directly reduces the risk of leaching heavy metals such as lead, mercury, and cadmium into soil and groundwater [6].

### 4.3 Technological Advancements

Recent advancements in solvent extraction include the development of greener, cost-effective extractants with enhanced selectivity and recyclability. For example, ionic liquids (ILs) and deep eutectic solvents (DESs) have shown promise in minimizing the co-extraction of impurities and improving the efficiency of metal recovery [7].

## 5. Case Studies and Comparative Analysis

### 5.1 Copper Recovery

Copper is one of the most valuable targets for urban mining due to its high recovery potential. The average recovery potential of copper from e-waste ranges from 822.6 ± 420.6 t to 2,467.8 ± 1,261.7 t [8]. Solvent extraction has been shown to achieve high recovery rates of copper, with studies demonstrating recoveries of over 90% [5].

### 5.2 Gold and Silver Recovery

The recovery of precious metals such as gold and silver is also a significant focus in urban mining. The average recovery potential of gold is estimated to have a lower limit of 0.165 ± 0.084 t and an upper limit of 4.94 ± 2.52 t [8]. For silver, the average recovery potential ranges from 1.65 ± 0.84 t to 16.5 ± 8.41 t [8]. Solvent extraction, particularly when combined with thiosulfate processes, has shown high recovery rates for gold and silver [9].

### 5.3 Rare Earth Elements (REEs)

REEs are critical for many advanced technologies, and their recovery from e-waste is essential for resource security. Tunsu et al. [10] tested different extractants for REEs in simulated acidic solutions and identified that the blended DEHPA–Cyanex 923 is effective in minimizing the co-extraction of iron. This approach has the potential to significantly improve the recovery of REEs from e-waste.

## 6. Challenges and Future Directions

### 6.1 Technological Barriers

Despite the potential of solvent extraction, there are several challenges that need to be addressed. These include the complexity of reverse logistics, the variety of waste compositions, and the need for specific technologies and technical knowledge. The development of continuous flow systems and the integration of solvent extraction with complementary technologies such as bioleaching and electrochemical recovery are essential for scaling up urban mining operations [11].

### 6.2 Economic and Regulatory Frameworks

The economic viability of urban mining is dependent on the quality and volume of the material, as well as the regulatory and economic frameworks in place. Ongoing research should focus on developing greener, cost-effective extractants and addressing the regulatory and economic barriers to widespread adoption [11].

## 7. Conclusion

Partition coefficients in solvent extraction play a critical role in the economic feasibility of urban mining. High partition coefficients enhance the efficiency and selectivity of metal recovery, reducing costs and environmental impacts. The integration of solvent extraction with other advanced technologies, such as bioleaching and electrochemical recovery, offers a promising approach to maximizing the economic and environmental benefits of urban mining. Continued technological advancements and supportive regulatory frameworks are essential for the widespread adoption of urban mining as a sustainable resource management strategy.
