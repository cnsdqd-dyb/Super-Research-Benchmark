# Research Report: Influence of Connected Components of Local Node Clustering on the Isomorphism Group Structure of Identity Construction in Cultural Semantic Networks

## Abstract
This report explores the influence of connected components and local node clustering on the isomorphism group structure of identity construction in cultural semantic networks. By analyzing the structural properties of these networks, we aim to understand how the clustering of concepts and the formation of connected components affect the way cultural identities are constructed and maintained. The report synthesizes findings from various studies on semantic networks, small-world properties, and clustering coefficients to provide a comprehensive analysis.

## 1. Introduction
Cultural semantic networks represent the interconnectedness of concepts and ideas within a cultural context. These networks are characterized by high sparsity, short average path lengths, and high local clustering, which are hallmarks of small-world networks [1, 2]. The structure of these networks plays a crucial role in how cultural identities are constructed and maintained. Specifically, the clustering of concepts and the formation of connected components can influence the isomorphism group structure, which is a measure of the network's symmetry and the ways in which different parts of the network can be mapped onto each other.

## 2. Background
### 2.1. Semantic Networks and Small-World Properties
Semantic networks are graphs where nodes represent concepts and edges represent relationships between these concepts. These networks often exhibit small-world properties, characterized by:
- **High Sparsity**: Most nodes are not directly connected to each other.
- **Short Average Path Length**: It is possible to reach any node from any other node in a small number of steps.
- **High Local Clustering**: Neighbors of a node are likely to be connected to each other.

### 2.2. Clustering Coefficient
The local clustering coefficient \( C_j \) for a node \( j \) quantifies the degree to which the node's neighbors are interconnected. For an unweighted graph, it is given by:
\[
C_j = \frac{2v_j}{k_j(k_j - 1)}
\]
where \( v_j \) is the number of edges between the \( k_j \) neighbors of node \( j \) [3]. The average clustering coefficient \( C \) is the mean of the local clustering coefficients across all nodes in the network.

### 2.3. Connected Components
A connected component in a graph is a subgraph where every node is reachable from every other node within the subgraph. In cultural semantic networks, connected components can represent clusters of related concepts or communities of shared cultural practices.

### 3.3. Isomorphism Group Structure
We used graph isomorphism algorithms to determine the isomorphism group structure of the networks. This involves identifying the symmetries and mappings between different parts of the network.

- **Single Large Component**: The presence of a single large connected component ensures that the network has a high degree of interconnectedness, which facilitates the construction of cultural identities by allowing for the spread of ideas and practices.
