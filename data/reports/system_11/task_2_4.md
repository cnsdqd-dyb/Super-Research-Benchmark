# Research Report: Mechanism of Interface Reinforcement in Carbon Nanotube Composites Under Tensile Loading

## Abstract

This report investigates the mechanism by which the interface reinforcement of carbon nanotube (CNT) composites reconciles stress transfer efficiency with the energy dissipation of defect dislocations under tensile loading. The study integrates experimental findings and theoretical models to elucidate the role of interfacial slip, mechanical interlocking, and chemical bonding in enhancing the mechanical properties of CNT-reinforced composites. The report highlights the importance of optimal dispersion and alignment of CNTs, as well as the synergistic interactions between CNTs and the polymer matrix, in achieving high energy dissipation and stress transfer efficiency.

## 1. Introduction

Carbon nanotube (CNT) composites have gained significant attention due to their exceptional mechanical properties, including high strength, stiffness, and energy dissipation capabilities. The performance of these composites is heavily influenced by the interface between the CNTs and the polymer matrix. Under tensile loading, the interface plays a crucial role in stress transfer and energy dissipation. This report aims to explore the mechanisms by which interface reinforcement in CNT composites reconciles stress transfer efficiency with the energy dissipation of defect dislocations.

## 2. Mechanisms of Stress Transfer and Energy Dissipation

### 2.1 Interfacial Slip

Interfacial slip is a critical mechanism for energy dissipation in CNT-reinforced composites. When subjected to tensile loading, the relative displacement at the CNT-polymer interface can lead to interfacial debonding and friction. This process is particularly significant in systems with aligned CNTs, where the alignment enhances the interfacial area and the potential for slip [1]. The initiation of interfacial slip occurs at axial strains as low as 0.028%, primarily due to shear stress concentration along the CNT-polymer interface [2].

### 2.2 Mechanical Interlocking

Mechanical interlocking between CNTs and the polymer matrix is another key mechanism for stress transfer. This interlocking can occur through physical anchoring, where the CNTs are embedded in the matrix, and through chemical bonding, where covalent or van der Waals forces enhance the interfacial interaction [3]. The degree of interlocking is influenced by the dispersion and alignment of CNTs, with well-dispersed and aligned CNTs providing more effective stress transfer [4].

### 2.3 Chemical Bonding

Chemical bonding at the CNT-polymer interface is essential for efficient stress transfer. Surface functionalization of CNTs, such as the introduction of polar functional groups, can significantly improve the interfacial interaction. For example, carboxyl-functionalized CNTs have been shown to increase the interlayer shear strength (ILSS) of CNT/CF/epoxy composites by 58.6% [5]. The enhanced chemical bonding not only improves stress transfer but also increases the energy dissipation capacity of the composite.

### 2.4 Synergistic Interactions

Synergistic interactions among neighboring CNTs can further enhance the mechanical properties of the composite. These interactions can lead to the formation of local stress field distortions, which reinforce both interlayer and interfacial frictional effects [6]. In systems with higher CNT mass fractions, agglomeration can occur, leading to intensified stress fields at regions of CNT-CNT overlap. This enhances viscous dissipation within the surrounding matrix, contributing to overall energy dissipation [7].

## 3. Experimental and Theoretical Studies

### 3.1 Experimental Studies

Experimental studies have provided valuable insights into the mechanisms of stress transfer and energy dissipation in CNT-reinforced composites. For instance, the alignment of CNTs in polystyrene (PS) composites has been shown to significantly affect the interfacial slip mechanics. At low strains, energy dissipation is primarily due to CNT-CNT friction within agglomerates, while at higher strains, interfacial slip between the CNTs and the matrix becomes more significant [8].

### 3.2 Theoretical Models

Theoretical models have been developed to predict the behavior of CNT-reinforced composites under tensile loading. These models often incorporate the effects of interfacial slip, mechanical interlocking, and chemical bonding. For example, a micromechanics model has been used to study the interfacial slip mechanics in aligned CNT-PS composites, revealing that the variation in storage and loss modulus can indicate the initiation of interfacial slip [9].

## 4. Impact of Interface Reinforcement

### 4.1 Enhanced Stress Transfer

Interface reinforcement through optimal dispersion and alignment of CNTs significantly enhances stress transfer efficiency. The large effective interfacial area provided by well-dispersed and aligned CNTs ensures that the load is effectively transferred from the matrix to the CNTs, leading to improved mechanical properties [10].

### 4.2 Increased Energy Dissipation

The same interface reinforcement that enhances stress transfer also increases energy dissipation. The synergistic interactions among CNTs and the polymer matrix, along with the interfacial slip and mechanical interlocking, contribute to the overall energy dissipation capacity of the composite. This is particularly important for applications where dynamic loading may compromise structural stability and functionality, such as in rotary wing structures and antennas [11].

## 5. Conclusion

The interface reinforcement of CNT composites under tensile loading reconciles stress transfer efficiency with energy dissipation through a combination of interfacial slip, mechanical interlocking, and chemical bonding. Optimal dispersion and alignment of CNTs, along with synergistic interactions among CNTs and the polymer matrix, are crucial for achieving high mechanical performance. The findings from this report can be used to design CNT-reinforced composites with enhanced energy dissipation and stress transfer capabilities, making them suitable for a wide range of high-performance applications.
