# Research Report: Determining Qualification Requirement Standards in Merchant Onboarding Audits

## Abstract

This report delves into the methodologies and standards used to determine qualification requirements in merchant onboarding audits. The focus is on the regulatory, risk-based, and technological aspects that influence the onboarding process. Key areas explored include Customer Due Diligence (CDD), Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD), risk assessment, and the role of automation and compliance with regulatory bodies. The report aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the qualification standards and the mechanisms that ensure the integrity and security of the payment ecosystem.

## 1. Introduction

Merchant onboarding is a critical process for payment service providers (PSPs) and acquiring banks. It involves verifying the identity, legitimacy, and risk profile of potential merchants to ensure compliance with regulatory standards and to mitigate financial and reputational risks. The qualification requirements for merchant onboarding are determined through a combination of regulatory mandates, risk assessment methodologies, and technological solutions. This report examines the key factors and processes involved in setting these standards.

## 2. Regulatory Framework

### 2.1 Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC) Regulations

#### 2.1.1 Global Standards

- **AML Requirements**: Payment processors must comply with AML regulations to prevent money laundering and terrorist financing. This includes conducting thorough due diligence on merchants, especially those in high-risk industries or geographies [1].
- **KYC Verification**: KYC processes are essential for verifying the identity of merchants and their beneficial owners. This involves collecting and validating government-issued documents, business registration certificates, and other relevant information [2].

#### 2.1.2 Regional Regulations

- **European Union (EU)**: The Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) and the 6th Anti-Money Laundering Directive (AMLD6) impose stringent requirements on payment service providers, including enhanced due diligence and continuous monitoring [3].
- **United States (US)**: The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) sets AML standards, requiring PSPs to implement robust compliance programs and conduct regular audits [4].

### 2.2 Risk-Based Approach (RBA)

- **Risk Assessment**: A risk-based approach enables PSPs to allocate compliance resources efficiently by identifying and categorizing customer, product, and geographic risk factors. This involves segmenting applicants based on their risk profiles and applying controls accordingly [5].
- **Dynamic Risk Scoring**: Implementing dynamic risk scoring systems allows PSPs to adjust the level of friction in the onboarding process based on perceived risk. High-risk applicants undergo more thorough verification, while low-risk applicants experience a faster onboarding process [6].

## 3. Risk Assessment and Due Diligence

### 3.1 Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

- **Initial Eligibility Review**: The onboarding process begins with an initial review of merchant data, including business type, category, geography, and required regulatory documents. Early filters help identify incomplete submissions or those that violate platform policies [7].
- **KYC Verification**: This involves verifying the merchant’s legal existence, the identities of owners and directors, and the authenticity of government-issued documents. Automated document extraction and image analysis improve accuracy and reduce friction [8].

### 3.2 Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

- **High-Risk Merchants**: EDD is required for high-risk entities, such as those in high-risk geographies or industries susceptible to financial crime. This includes more rigorous vetting, continuous monitoring, and additional documentation [9].
- **Compliance Documentation**: Merchants must demonstrate adherence to relevant regulatory standards and security frameworks, such as the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) [10].

### 3.3 Ongoing Due Diligence

- **Continuous Monitoring**: Robust due diligence is only the first step. PSPs must implement mechanisms for ongoing transaction monitoring, especially for high-risk segments. This includes identifying red flags and reporting suspicious activity [11].
- **Adaptive Verification Processes**: Using adaptive verification techniques that adjust based on the applicant’s risk profile ensures that high-risk applicants undergo more thorough checks, while low-risk applicants experience a smoother onboarding process [12].

## 4. Technological Solutions

### 4.1 Automation and AI

- **Automated Onboarding Systems**: Automation reduces the inefficiencies of manual onboarding processes by offering a faster, smoother, and more reliable experience. It helps businesses stay compliant with local and international requirements, such as PSD2 and AMLD6 [13].
- **AI-Driven Document Authentication**: Advanced onboarding systems rely on Optical Character Recognition (OCR), machine learning, and document forensics to detect tampering, extract data, and validate authenticity. These technologies raise verification accuracy and significantly reduce fraud attempts [14].

### 4.2 Data Governance and Audit Trails

- **DPDP-Compliant Audit Trails**: Data Protection and Data Privacy (DPDP) regulations require platforms to demonstrate lawful and responsible processing of personal data. This includes clear purpose limitation, minimal data retention, verifiable consent mechanisms, and traceable audit trails [15].
- **Real-Time Monitoring**: Implementing real-time monitoring and intelligence sharing helps PSPs identify and respond to emerging threats and evolving fraud patterns. AI and machine learning models can rank the risk level of each merchant, similar to how transaction fraud is assessed [16].

## 5. Case Studies and Best Practices

### 5.1 Mastercard’s New Merchant Monitoring Requirements

- **Pre-Transaction Scans**: Mastercard requires pre-transaction scans for every new merchant, deeper checks for BRAM violations, and oversight of password-protected areas. This ensures that merchants are thoroughly vetted before the first transaction goes live [17].
- **Persistent Merchant Monitoring (PMM)**: Continuous monitoring of merchant behavior helps identify subtle anomalies early, reducing the risk of fraud and ensuring compliance with changing regulations [18].

### 5.2 CAMSPay’s Merchant Onboarding Policy

- **Customer Acceptance Policy (CAP)**: CAMSPay’s CAP lays down explicit criteria for the acceptance of merchants, including no anonymous or fictitious business relationships and thorough identity verification [19].
- **Risk Profiling**: CAMSPay evaluates merchants based on their business category, transaction models, and beneficiary structures. This helps in determining the appropriate level of due diligence and onboarding requirements [20].

## 6. Challenges and Future Trends

### 6.1 Challenges

- **Regulatory Complexity**: Merchant onboarding must comply with AML and KYC regulations, which vary by jurisdiction. This requires adaptable compliance systems to handle geographic variations and evolving regulatory landscapes [21].
- **Merchant Abandonment**: Lengthy and complex onboarding processes can lead to merchant abandonment. PSPs must strike a balance between thorough verification and a seamless onboarding experience [22].

### 6.2 Future Trends

- **Real-Time Intelligence Sharing**: The future of merchant onboarding risk assessment is real-time, intelligent, and proactive. AI and machine learning will play a crucial role in identifying risky merchant profiles and stopping fraud before transactions occur [23].
- **Cross-Border Compliance**: As merchants expand internationally, PSPs must ensure compliance with multiple jurisdictions simultaneously. This adds complexity and increases the risk of missing local requirements [24].

## 7. Conclusion

Determining qualification requirement standards in merchant onboarding audits is a multifaceted process that involves regulatory compliance, risk assessment, and technological innovation. By adopting a risk-based approach, leveraging automation and AI, and maintaining continuous monitoring, PSPs can ensure the integrity and security of the payment ecosystem. Future trends in real-time intelligence sharing and cross-border compliance will further enhance the effectiveness of merchant onboarding processes.
