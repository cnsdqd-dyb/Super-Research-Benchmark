## 1. Introduction

The black hole information loss paradox is a profound challenge in theoretical physics, rooted in the apparent contradiction between the thermal nature of Hawking radiation and the unitarity principle of quantum mechanics. Hawking radiation, as initially derived by Stephen Hawking, is thermal, meaning it is indistinguishable from any other thermal radiation. This thermal nature implies that the information about the initial state of the black hole is lost, leading to a mixed state and a violation of unitarity [1, 2].

To address this paradox, various approaches have been proposed, including the firewall hypothesis and the concept of quantum extremal surfaces (QES). The AdS/CFT correspondence, a cornerstone of string theory, has provided a framework to understand how information might be preserved in black hole evaporation. This report delves into how the non-thermal spectrum of Hawking radiation, as suggested by recent research, aligns with the constraints imposed by holographic duality.

## 2. Black Hole Thermodynamics and Hawking Radiation

### 2.1 Black Hole Thermodynamics

Black holes are not just gravitational objects but also thermodynamic systems. The four laws of black hole thermodynamics, analogous to the laws of classical thermodynamics, were formulated by Bekenstein and Hawking. The key quantities in black hole thermodynamics are:

- **Entropy**: The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy \( S_{\text{BH}} \) is proportional to the area \( A \) of the event horizon:
  \[
  S_{\text{BH}} = \frac{A}{4G_N}
  \]
  where \( G_N \) is the gravitational constant.

- **Temperature**: The Hawking temperature \( T_H \) is given by:
  \[
  T_H = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi}
  \]
  where \( \kappa \) is the surface gravity of the black hole.

### 2.2 Hawking Radiation

Hawking radiation is the process by which black holes emit particles due to quantum effects near the event horizon. The spectrum of Hawking radiation is thermal, with a temperature \( T_H \). The number of particles created by the black hole is given by:
\[
\langle N_\omega \rangle = \frac{\Gamma_\omega}{\exp\left(\frac{\omega}{T_H}\right) - 1}
\]
where \( \Gamma_\omega \) is the greybody factor, accounting for the fact that not all particles escape the black hole.

## 3. The Information Loss Paradox

### 3.1 Thermal Spectrum and Information Loss

The thermal nature of Hawking radiation implies that the state of the black hole evolves from a pure state to a mixed state, leading to a loss of information. This is in direct contradiction with the unitarity principle of quantum mechanics, which requires that the evolution of a quantum system be described by a unitary operator, preserving the purity of the state.

### 3.2 The Page Curve

The Page curve describes the evolution of the entanglement entropy of Hawking radiation over time. It is characterized by an initial increase in entropy, followed by a decrease after the Page time \( t_{\text{Page}} \), when the black hole has radiated away half of its initial entropy. The Page curve is given by:
\[
S_{\text{rad}}(t) = \begin{cases} 
\frac{A(t)}{4G_N} & \text{if } t < t_{\text{Page}} \\
\frac{A(0)}{4G_N} - \frac{A(t)}{4G_N} & \text{if } t \geq t_{\text{Page}}
\end{cases}
\]
where \( A(t) \) is the area of the black hole at time \( t \).

## 4. Holographic Duality and Quantum Extremal Surfaces

### 4.1 The Holographic Principle

The holographic principle, proposed by Gerard 't Hooft and Leonard Susskind, posits that all information about a volume of space can be encoded on the boundary of that volume. This principle is a cornerstone of the AdS/CFT correspondence, which provides a duality between a gravitational theory in Anti-de Sitter (AdS) space and a conformal field theory (CFT) on the boundary of that space.

### 4.2 Quantum Extremal Surfaces

Quantum extremal surfaces (QES) are surfaces that extremize the generalized entropy, which is the sum of the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy and the entanglement entropy of quantum fields. The inclusion of QES in the calculation of the entanglement entropy of Hawking radiation is crucial for resolving the information loss paradox. The generalized entropy \( S_{\text{gen}} \) is given by:
\[
S_{\text{gen}} = S_{\text{BH}} + S_{\text{ent}}
\]
where \( S_{\text{ent}} \) is the entanglement entropy of the quantum fields.

### 4.3 Non-Thermal Spectrum and Unitarity

Recent research has shown that the thermal spectrum of Hawking radiation is a result of the geometric optics approximation in the derivation of the Bogolubov coefficients. When treated more accurately, the spectrum of Hawking radiation is non-thermal, which is necessary to preserve unitarity. The non-thermal spectrum can be expressed as:
\[
\langle N_\omega \rangle = \frac{\Gamma_\omega}{\exp\left(\frac{\omega}{T_H}\right) - 1} + \text{corrections}
\]
These corrections encode the quantum correlations that carry information about the black hole's interior, ensuring that the final state of the black hole is pure.

## 5. Conclusion

The non-thermal spectrum of Hawking radiation, as suggested by recent research, is a crucial aspect of resolving the black hole information loss paradox. The inclusion of quantum extremal surfaces and the application of the holographic principle, particularly through the AdS/CFT correspondence, provide a framework for understanding how information is preserved in black hole evaporation. The non-thermal spectrum ensures that the entanglement entropy of Hawking radiation follows the Page curve, thereby maintaining the unitarity of the process.
