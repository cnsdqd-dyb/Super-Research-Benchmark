# Research Report: Balancing Mathematical Rigor with Emotional Expressiveness in Algorithmic Composition of Digital Music

## Abstract

Algorithmic composition, the technique of using algorithms to create music, has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly with the integration of deep learning models. This report explores how these algorithms balance mathematical rigor with emotional expressiveness, a critical aspect of music that resonates with human listeners. We analyze the effectiveness of different AI models, such as Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), and Transformer models, in generating emotionally resonant music. We also discuss the psychological and physiological responses of listeners to AI-generated music and the implications for the design of more anthropomorphic and emotionally intelligent systems.

## 1. Introduction

Music is a powerful medium for emotional expression, capable of evoking a wide range of feelings and moods. Algorithmic composition, the use of algorithms to create music, has evolved from early rule-based systems to sophisticated deep learning models. These models aim to balance mathematical rigor, which ensures the technical accuracy and structure of the music, with emotional expressiveness, which captures the nuanced and subjective aspects of human emotion.

### 1.1. Background

The history of algorithmic composition dates back to the mid-1950s, with the creation of the Illiac Suite by Hiller and Isaacson [1]. Since then, the field has seen significant advancements, particularly with the advent of deep learning techniques. Modern AI models, such as RNNs, GANs, and Transformers, have demonstrated the ability to generate music that is not only technically proficient but also emotionally resonant.

### 1.2. Research Questions

This report addresses the following research questions:
1. How do different AI models balance mathematical rigor with emotional expressiveness in algorithmic composition?
2. What are the psychological and physiological responses of listeners to AI-generated music?
3. How can these findings inform the design of more anthropomorphic and emotionally intelligent systems?

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1. Data and Models

We analyzed the performance of three leading deep learning architectures: RNNs, GANs, and Transformers, using the MAESTRO dataset [2]. The MAESTRO dataset contains structured information combining symbolic and audio formats of classical piano performances, enabling researchers to conduct sophisticated deep-learning research on music generation, transcription techniques, and performance modeling approaches.

### 2.2. Evaluation Metrics

The effectiveness of these models was evaluated using both quantitative and qualitative metrics:
- **Quantitative Metrics**: Note density, harmonic content, and Mean Opinion Score (MOS) ratings.
- **Qualitative Metrics**: Human-centered evaluations from listeners, including self-reported emotional responses and physiological measurements.

## 3. Results

### 3.1. Model Performance

#### 3.1.1. RNNs

RNNs, particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, are effective in capturing temporal dependencies in music. However, they often struggle with generating music that is both structurally coherent and emotionally expressive. The results show that RNNs achieved moderate MOS ratings, indicating their ability to generate technically accurate but less emotionally resonant music.

#### 3.1.2. GANs

GANs are particularly effective in generating music with unique rhythmic patterns and emotional depth. The study found that GANs excelled in generating happy music, with high MOS ratings and positive emotional responses from listeners. However, GANs were less effective in generating calm and coherent pieces, suggesting a trade-off between emotional expressiveness and structural coherence.

#### 3.1.3. Transformers

Transformer models, known for their ability to handle long-range dependencies and capture complex patterns, performed the best in generating emotionally resonant and structurally coherent music. The results show that Transformer models achieved the highest overall MOS ratings, with listeners reporting strong emotional responses to the generated music.

### 3.2. Listener Responses

#### 3.2.1. Emotional Responses

Participants in the study were exposed to AI-generated music under various labeling conditions (correctly labeled as AI or human, incorrectly labeled, and unlabeled) and emotion cases (calm and upbeat). The results indicate that participants generally perceived human-composed music as more effective in eliciting target emotional states, particularly in more stimulating, upbeat contexts. However, AI-generated music was also effective in evoking targeted emotional states, especially in calm and peaceful contexts.

#### 3.2.2. Physiological Responses

Physiological measurements, such as parasympathetic nervous system activity, were also collected to assess the emotional impact of AI-generated music. The study found that individuals in the AI condition experienced decreased parasympathetic nervous system activity compared to those in the human condition, suggesting a physiological response to the perceived origin of the music.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1. Balancing Mathematical Rigor and Emotional Expressiveness

The balance between mathematical rigor and emotional expressiveness is a critical challenge in algorithmic composition. RNNs, GANs, and Transformers each have their strengths and weaknesses in this regard. RNNs excel in capturing temporal dependencies but may lack emotional depth. GANs are effective in generating emotionally expressive music but may struggle with structural coherence. Transformers, on the other hand, strike a balance by generating music that is both structurally coherent and emotionally resonant.

### 4.2. Psychological and Physiological Implications

The psychological and physiological responses of listeners to AI-generated music highlight the importance of perceived origin. While AI-generated music can effectively evoke emotional responses, the belief that the music is human-composed can enhance the emotional and physiological impact. This suggests that the perceived authenticity of the music plays a significant role in listener engagement.

### 4.3. Design Implications

The findings of this study have important implications for the design of more anthropomorphic and emotionally intelligent systems. AI-generated music can serve as a crucial non-verbal cue to foster deeper and more effective human-machine emotional connections. To achieve this, future models should focus on:
- **Emotional Expressiveness**: Incorporating subtle emotional cues and details to enrich the music.
- **Structural Coherence**: Ensuring that the generated music is structurally coherent and technically accurate.
- **Perceived Authenticity**: Addressing the psychological and physiological responses to perceived origin to enhance listener engagement.

## 5. Conclusion

Algorithmic composition has made significant strides in balancing mathematical rigor with emotional expressiveness. While RNNs, GANs, and Transformers each have their strengths, Transformer models currently offer the best balance, generating music that is both structurally coherent and emotionally resonant. The psychological and physiological responses of listeners to AI-generated music highlight the importance of perceived authenticity and emotional expressiveness. Future research should focus on developing models that can better replicate the subtle expressive cues found in human-composed music, thereby enhancing the emotional and cultural significance of AI-generated music.
