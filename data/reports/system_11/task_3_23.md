## 2. The Geodesic Principle in General Relativity
### 2.1 Geodesic Principle and Free Particles
The geodesic principle in GR states that free massive point particles move along timelike geodesics in spacetime. Mathematically, this is expressed through the geodesic equation:
\[
\frac{d^2 x^\mu}{d\tau^2} + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho} \frac{dx^\nu}{d\tau} \frac{dx^\rho}{d\tau} = 0,
\]
where \( x^\mu \) are the coordinates of the particle, \( \tau \) is the proper time, and \( \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho} \) are the Christoffel symbols derived from the metric tensor \( g_{\mu\nu} \) [1].

### 2.2 Derivation of the Geodesic Equation
The geodesic equation can be derived from the principle of least action. The action for a free particle in spacetime is given by:
\[
S = \int \sqrt{g_{\mu\nu} \frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda} \frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}} d\lambda,
\]
where \( \lambda \) is an affine parameter. Applying the Euler-Lagrange equations to this action yields the geodesic equation [2].

### 2.3 Geodesic Principle as a Theorem
While the geodesic principle is often claimed to be a theorem in GR, it is not a direct consequence of Einstein's field equations alone. Additional assumptions are required to derive the geodesic principle as a theorem. For instance, the principle of equivalence and the conservation of energy-momentum are crucial in establishing the geodesic motion of particles [3].
