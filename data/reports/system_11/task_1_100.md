# Research Report: Establishing Decision Priorities in Autonomous Weapon Systems to Protect Civilian Life

## Abstract

This report delves into the methodologies and ethical frameworks governing the establishment of decision priorities in autonomous weapon systems (AWS) to protect civilian life. The analysis is grounded in international humanitarian law (IHL), ethical principles, and technical considerations. The report examines the challenges and solutions in ensuring that AWS can distinguish between combatants and civilians, assess proportionality, and take necessary precautions to minimize civilian harm. It also discusses the role of human oversight and the implications of delegating life-and-death decisions to machines.

## 1. Introduction

Autonomous weapon systems (AWS) represent a significant advancement in military technology, capable of selecting and engaging targets without direct human intervention. However, their deployment raises profound ethical, legal, and technical challenges, particularly in the context of protecting civilian life. This report explores how decision priorities are established in AWS to ensure compliance with international humanitarian law (IHL) and ethical standards.

## 2. Legal Framework

### 2.1 International Humanitarian Law (IHL)

IHL, as codified in the Geneva Conventions and Additional Protocols, provides the primary legal framework for the use of force in armed conflicts. Key principles include:

- **Distinction**: The obligation to distinguish between combatants and civilians, and between military objectives and civilian objects (Article 48, Additional Protocol I).
- **Proportionality**: The requirement to ensure that the expected civilian harm is not excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated (Article 51, Additional Protocol I).
- **Precautions in Attack**: The duty to take all feasible precautions to avoid or minimize incidental loss of civilian life, injury to civilians, and damage to civilian objects (Article 57, Additional Protocol I).

### 2.2 Collateral Damage Estimation (CDE) and Collateral Damage Estimation Methodology (CDEM)

CDE and CDEM are standardized approaches used to predict and minimize civilian harm. CDEM combines methods and techniques for collateral damage analysis and estimation, classifying attacks by modeling expected civilian harm to determine approval authority and guide harm-minimizing methods [1].

## 3. Ethical Framework

### 3.1 Ethical Principles

The ethical use of AWS is guided by principles that ensure compliance with international law and respect for human dignity. Key ethical principles include:

- **Human Responsibility**: The responsibility and accountability for decisions to use force cannot be transferred to a machine. Human agency is essential in the decision-making process [2].
- **Proportionality and Discrimination**: AWS must be designed and deployed to support the principles of proportionality and discrimination, ensuring that military actions are just and necessary [3].
- **Human Dignity**: The use of AWS should not dehumanize the use of force or violate the right of nondiscrimination [4].

### 3.2 Ethical Charter

The development of an ethical charter for programmers and manufacturers of AWS is crucial. Such a charter would outline the ethical responsibilities and standards for the design, deployment, and use of AWS, ensuring that they align with international humanitarian and ethical norms [5].

## 4. Technical Considerations

### 4.1 Target Identification

AWS must be capable of positive identification of prohibited civilian targets such as schools, hospitals, and places of religious worship. This requires advanced sensor and algorithmic technologies that can accurately distinguish between combatants and civilians in complex, dynamic battlefield scenarios [6].

### 4.2 Proportionality Assessment

The rule of proportionality requires a balance between the expected civilian harm and the concrete and direct military advantage. AWS must be designed to assess this balance, taking into account the immediate and long-term effects of an attack [7].

### 4.3 Precautions in Attack

AWS must be programmed to take all feasible precautions to avoid or minimize civilian harm. This includes the ability to suspend or cancel an attack if the target is non-military or if the attack is likely to cause excessive civilian harm [8].

## 5. Human Oversight

### 5.1 Meaningful Human Control

Meaningful human control is essential to ensure that AWS operate within the bounds of IHL and ethical standards. This involves:

- **Human-in-the-Loop**: Human operators must retain the ability to intervene and override AWS decisions in real-time [9].
- **Human-on-the-Loop**: Human operators must be able to monitor and adjust the operation of AWS, providing continuous oversight and control [10].

### 5.2 Decision Support Systems

Decision support systems (DSS) can assist human operators in making informed decisions by providing real-time data and analysis. However, DSS should not replace human judgment, especially in complex and unanticipated situations [11].

## 6. Challenges and Solutions

### 6.1 Challenges

- **Technical Limitations**: Current sensor and algorithmic technologies face significant challenges in accurately distinguishing between combatants and civilians in complex environments [12].
- **Ethical and Legal Uncertainty**: The delegation of life-and-death decisions to machines raises ethical and legal questions about responsibility and accountability [13].
- **Bias and Inequality**: AI systems can perpetuate and exacerbate societal biases, leading to disproportionate harm to marginalized communities [14].

### 6.2 Solutions

- **Advanced Sensor and Algorithmic Technologies**: Continued research and development in sensor and algorithmic technologies can improve the accuracy and reliability of target identification [15].
- **Ethically Embedded Design**: Embedding ethical principles directly into the design of AWS can ensure that they operate in accordance with IHL and ethical standards [16].
- **Hybrid Governance**: A hybrid governance model combining categorical bans on full autonomy, strict liability regimes, and continuous IHL compliance can address accountability deficits and humanitarian risks [17].

## 7. Conclusion

The establishment of decision priorities in AWS to protect civilian life is a multifaceted challenge that requires a comprehensive approach. This includes adherence to IHL, ethical principles, and technical advancements. Meaningful human control and oversight are essential to ensure that AWS operate within the bounds of international law and ethical standards. By addressing the technical, ethical, and legal challenges, it is possible to develop and deploy AWS in a manner that minimizes civilian harm and upholds the principles of just warfare.
