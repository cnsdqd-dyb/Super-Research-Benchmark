## 1. Introduction

Integrated Information Theory (IIT) is a theoretical framework developed by neuroscientist Giulio Tononi to explain the nature of consciousness. According to IIT, consciousness is a fundamental property of physical systems that can be quantified by a measure called integrated information (Φ). The theory posits that the quantity and quality of consciousness are directly related to the causal structure of a system, which is represented by a high-dimensional geometric structure in a conceptual space.

The central claim of IIT is that an experience is identical to the cause-effect structure (Φ-structure) of a system in its current state. This identity is not a mere correlation but a proposed explanation for how subjective experience arises from physical mechanisms. The theory aims to provide a principled way to determine whether a system is conscious, to what degree, and what particular experience it has.

### 4.1 Empirical Validation

IIT proposes several empirical predictions that can be tested to validate the theory. These include:

1. **Measuring Φ in Various Systems**: IIT predicts that systems with high Φ are more likely to be conscious. This can be tested by measuring Φ in various systems, such as brains, simple animals, and artificial systems, and comparing the results with behavioral and neural data.

### 4.2 Challenges

Despite its theoretical elegance, IIT faces several challenges:

1. **Computational Intractability**: Calculating Φ for even moderately sized systems is computationally intractable. This makes it difficult to apply the theory to real-world systems and to test its predictions.

2. **Ambiguity in the Definition of Information**: The definition of information in IIT is context-dependent and can vary at different scales of description. This ambiguity makes it challenging to define a universal measure of integrated information.
