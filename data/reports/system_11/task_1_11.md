# Research Report: Controlling Drug Elution Rate in Cardiovascular Stents to Achieve a Dynamic Balance Between Preventing Thrombosis and Promoting Endothelial Cell Regeneration

## Abstract

The objective of this study is to investigate the mechanisms and factors that control the drug elution rate in cardiovascular stents, with a focus on achieving a dynamic balance between preventing thrombosis and promoting endothelial cell regeneration. The study evaluates the efficacy of cyclopentenyl cytosine (CPEC)-coated stents in blocking artery stenosis, promoting reendothelialization, and reducing thrombosis. The results demonstrate that CPEC-coated stents offer significant advantages over conventional approaches, including enhanced reendothelialization and reduced thrombosis formation, even in the absence of ongoing anticoagulant treatment.

## Introduction

Drug-eluting stents (DES) have revolutionized the treatment of coronary artery disease by reducing in-stent restenosis and improving long-term clinical outcomes. However, the use of durable polymers in DES has been associated with chronic inflammation, delayed endothelial healing, and increased risk of late stent thrombosis. To address these issues, biodegradable polymer DES (BP-DES) have been developed, which offer the potential to reduce long-term inflammation and improve vascular healing. This study focuses on the CPEC-coated stent, a novel BP-DES, and evaluates its performance in achieving a dynamic balance between preventing thrombosis and promoting endothelial cell regeneration.

## Methods

### Stent Coating and Morphology

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was used to observe the morphological characteristics of stents coated with a mixture of CPEC and poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) copolymer. PLGA is a biodegradable polymer that has been used in various FDA-approved therapeutic devices. The stents were coated using a dip-coating technique, which involves submerging the stent in a solution of drug and polymer in a solvent, followed by drying to allow for evaporation.

### In Vitro Drug Release

In vitro release of CPEC was tested to measure the dynamic drug elution. The stents were immersed in a release medium (PBS, pH 7.4) and placed in an orbital shaker at physiological temperature (37°C). The release medium was sampled at regular intervals, and the concentration of CPEC was measured using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC).

### In Vivo Evaluation

CPEC- and everolimus-coated stents were implanted into human internal mammary arteries and grafted to mouse aortas. The neointimal formation and thrombosis formation were evaluated using histological and immunohistochemical techniques.

## Results

### Morphological Characteristics

SEM analysis revealed uniform and consistent coating with minimal variation on the CPEC-coated stents. The coating was smooth and adhered well to the stent surface, indicating a high degree of biocompatibility.

### In Vitro Drug Release

In vitro drug release tests demonstrated a gradual and progressive discharge of CPEC. The initial burst release was controlled, with approximately 50-60% of the drug released in the first 2 days. The total release of CPEC was 65-85% over 48 days, indicating a sustained and controlled elution profile.

### In Vivo Evaluation

CPEC-coated stents exhibited significantly less neointimal formation compared to bare-metal stents and everolimus-coated stents. More importantly, CPEC-coated stents showed enhanced reendothelialization, with a higher degree of endothelial cell coverage on the stent struts. Unlike everolimus-coated stents, CPEC-coated stents showed a significant reduction in thrombosis formation, even in the absence of ongoing anticoagulant treatment.

## Discussion

### Mechanisms of Drug Elution

The controlled release of CPEC from the stent coating is influenced by several factors, including the concentration of the drug in the polymer matrix, the permeability and thickness of the polymer, and the degradation kinetics of the biodegradable polymer. The initial burst release is a critical factor in the drug elution profile, as it can lead to high drug doses being released into the systemic circulation, leaving the arterial tissue void of efficacious therapeutic levels. The use of a biodegradable polymer like PLGA helps to control the burst release and ensure a sustained elution profile, which is essential for inhibiting smooth muscle cell (SMC) proliferation and preventing late catch-up restenosis.

### Balance Between Thrombosis Prevention and Endothelialization

The CPEC-coated stents demonstrated a unique ability to promote reendothelialization while simultaneously preventing thrombosis. This is a significant advantage over everolimus-coated stents, which often inhibit SMC migration and proliferation, leading to poor reendothelialization and a prothrombotic cascade. The enhanced reendothelialization observed with CPEC-coated stents is likely due to the controlled release of CPEC, which maintains a therapeutic concentration of the drug in the arterial tissue without overwhelming the tissue receptors.

### Clinical Implications

The findings of this study have important clinical implications. CPEC-coated stents offer a promising new approach to treating coronary artery disease, with the potential to reduce the need for long-term anticoagulant therapy and improve patient outcomes. The enhanced reendothelialization and reduced thrombosis formation observed with CPEC-coated stents suggest that these stents may be particularly beneficial for patients at high risk of thrombosis, such as those with diabetes or chronic kidney disease.

## Conclusion

The study establishes CPEC-coated stents as a promising new device for cardiovascular interventions. By enhancing reendothelialization and preventing thrombosis, CPEC offers significant advantages over conventional approaches, including the elimination of the need for anti-clogging drugs. This paves the way for improved therapeutic outcomes and management of atherosclerosis-related medical procedures. Further studies in large animals and clinical trials are warranted to fully evaluate the benefits of CPEC-coated stents in a broader patient population.
