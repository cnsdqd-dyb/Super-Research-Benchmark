# Research Report: How Does Scenario Design in Environmental Risk Stress Testing Affect Capital Adequacy Calibration?

## Abstract
This report explores the impact of scenario design in environmental risk stress testing on the calibration of capital adequacy. The integration of environmental, social, and governance (ESG) risks, particularly climate-related risks, into stress testing frameworks is a critical aspect of ensuring financial resilience. The report synthesizes insights from various regulatory guidelines, academic studies, and industry practices to provide a comprehensive analysis of how scenario design influences the assessment of capital adequacy.

## 1. Introduction
Environmental risks, including physical and transition risks, pose significant challenges to financial institutions. Stress testing is a crucial tool for assessing the resilience of these institutions to adverse scenarios. The design of these scenarios, particularly those involving environmental risks, is essential for accurately calibrating capital adequacy. This report examines the methodologies and best practices for integrating environmental risk drivers into stress testing and their implications for capital adequacy.

## 2. Regulatory Framework and Guidelines
### 2.1 European Banking Authority (EBA) Guidelines
The EBA has published guidelines on environmental scenario analysis, which complement the EBA Guidelines on the management of ESG risks and institutions' stress testing. These guidelines specify how institutions using the Internal Ratings-Based (IRB) Approach should define and use stress test scenarios that include environmental risk drivers, particularly physical and transition risks stemming from climate change [1].

### 2.2 European Central Bank (ECB) Stress Testing
The ECB's climate risk stress test framework emphasizes the importance of scenario design in assessing the vulnerability of banks to climate-related risks. The scenarios are designed to test the resilience of banks to both short-term and long-term environmental risks, including the impact of a disorderly transition to a low-carbon economy [2].

## 3. Methodological Approaches
### 3.1 Traditional vs. Climate Stress Testing
Traditional stress tests are often calibrated on past events and historical data, which may not be sufficient for assessing climate risks. Climate stress tests, on the other hand, require the use of forward-looking scenarios based on existing research and speculative judgment. These scenarios are built to reflect the potential impacts of climate change on macroeconomic variables and financial institutions [3].

### 3.2 Scenario Design for Climate Risks
The design of climate risk scenarios involves several key steps:
1. **Portfolio Analysis**: Understanding which portfolios are most exposed to climate risks.
2. **Scenario Horizon**: Selecting a relevant time horizon for the scenario.
3. **Key Drivers**: Identifying key climate and economic drivers.
4. **Reference Scenarios**: Selecting and adapting reference climate scenarios.
5. **Geographical and Sectoral Adaptation**: Tailoring scenarios to specific regions and sectors.
6. **Impact Assessment**: Deriving the impact on key drivers to develop a relevant narrative [4].

## 4. Impact on Capital Adequacy
### 4.1 Short-Term and Long-Term Resilience
Environmental risk stress testing is used to assess both short-term financial resilience and long-term business model adaptability. Short-term scenarios focus on the immediate financial impacts of environmental risks, while long-term scenarios evaluate the institution's ability to adapt to a changing environment [1].

### 4.2 Capital Requirements and Stress Testing
Stress tests can be used to implement Basel-style capital requirements or to uncover hidden risks in the financial system. The calibration of stress tests, particularly those involving environmental risks, can significantly influence the choice of capital requirements. Well-designed scenarios can provide valuable new information, especially when used for targeted interventions [5].

### 4.3 Case Study: Physical Risk Impacts
A case study by Moody's examines the use of short-term stress testing to quantify the financial impacts of physical risks on credit. The study uses return periods (e.g., 1-in-100 and 1-in-500 years) as severe yet plausible scenarios to assess the escalation of physical risk impacts on credit risk and capital adequacy [6].

## 5. Challenges and Best Practices
### 5.1 Data and Model Limitations
One of the primary challenges in climate stress testing is the lack of historical data and the need for speculative judgment. Financial institutions must address data biases and ensure that models reflect current climate and environmental conditions. Centralized climate-related data management and collaboration among senior experts are recommended practices [3].

### 5.2 Integration into Risk Management
Institutions are increasingly integrating climate risk assessment into their existing risk and stress testing frameworks. This involves developing a climate risk team, establishing a climate risk research function, and fostering cross-functional collaboration. The results of climate stress tests should be integrated into the institution's Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP) and Internal Liquidity Adequacy Assessment Process (ILAAP) [4].

### 5.3 Regulatory Expectations
Prudential authorities are setting new expectations for banks and insurers to incorporate climate risks into their supervisory expectations on internal capital adequacy assessment. For instance, in the UK, banks are required to conduct initial calculations of the potential impacts of climate on capital adequacy within their ICAAP and ORSA filings [7].

## 6. Conclusion
The design of environmental risk scenarios in stress testing is crucial for accurately calibrating capital adequacy. Well-designed scenarios that reflect the complex and long-term nature of climate risks can provide valuable insights into the financial resilience of institutions. Regulatory guidelines and best practices emphasize the importance of integrating environmental risk drivers into stress testing frameworks and using the results to inform capital and liquidity adequacy assessments.
