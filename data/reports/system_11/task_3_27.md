Social stratification can be represented mathematically using matrices, and the Jordan form of these matrices provides valuable insights into the structure and behavior of social systems. ## 2. Theoretical Framework
### 2.1 Jordan Form and Social Stratification Matrices
The Jordan form of a matrix \( A \) is a block diagonal matrix where each block corresponds to a Jordan block associated with an eigenvalue of \( A \). For a given eigenvalue \( \lambda \), the Jordan block \( J \) is of the form:
\[
J = \begin{pmatrix}
\lambda & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
0 & \lambda & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & \lambda
\end{pmatrix}
\]
The dimension of the eigenspace corresponding to \( \lambda \) is the number of Jordan blocks with \( \lambda \) on the main diagonal. The sum of the sizes of all Jordan blocks with eigenvalue \( \lambda \) is the multiplicity of \( \lambda \) as a root of the characteristic equation [3].

### 2.2 Ergodicity and Social Mobility
Ergodicity is a property of dynamical systems that ensures the long-term behavior of the system is predictable and stable. In the context of social mobility, ergodicity implies that the long-term distribution of individuals across different social strata is independent of the initial conditions. A system is ergodic if any invariant measure set \( A \) satisfies \( m(A) = 0 \) or \( m(X - A) = 0 \) [4].

Counter-globalization movements often create flow fields that are characterized by localized and fragmented social interactions. These flow fields can be modeled using dynamical systems, where the mobility functions describe the transitions between different social strata. The stability and ergodicity of these systems are influenced by the structure of the social stratification matrices.
