Chaos theory, with its focus on complex, nonlinear systems, provides a useful framework for understanding these dynamics.

## 2. Theoretical Framework
### 2.1 Chaos Theory and Global Governance
Chaos theory studies the behavior of dynamical systems that are highly sensitive to initial conditions, leading to unpredictable outcomes. In the context of global governance, these systems can be modeled as chaotic attractors, where the state of the system is influenced by a multitude of interacting parameters. Key concepts from chaos theory, such as sensitive dependence on initial conditions, topological transitivity, and dense periodic orbits, are relevant to understanding the behavior of global governance systems.

### 2.2 National Sovereignty and Time-Lag Effects
National sovereignty parameters, such as state autonomy, policy-making capacity, and international cooperation, are subject to time-lag effects. These lags can arise from various sources, including bureaucratic delays, political resistance, and the inherent complexity of global issues. Time-lag effects can significantly influence the dynamics of global governance, leading to delays in policy implementation and response to crises.
