**Abstract**

### 2. Geometric Principles in Ballet Choreography  
#### 2.1 Spatial Organization as Geometric Abstraction  
Choreography translates movement into geometric relationships:  
- **Body vectors**: Limbs define directional lines (e.g., arabesques as radial spokes). Balanchine’s *Apollo Musagete* arranges dancers in circular formations, where "stretched legs fit mathematically into a circle" (Fig. 1), symbolizing Apollonian solar geometry [2].  
- **Stage topology**: Dancers navigate Cartesian grids, with pathways (e.g., diagonals, arcs) creating "spatial and bodily geometry" [2]. Site-specific works (e.g., *Scaffolding*) merge architectural structures with choreography, where dancers "negotiate relationships between bodies, spaces, and light" [1].  

#### 2.2 Choreographic Structures  
- **Mise-en-scène**: Configures dancers as dynamic modules within stage parameters. Geometric constraints (e.g., symmetry, tessellation) generate "non-verbal communication units" [2].  
- **Folding/unfolding**: William Forsythe’s "states of fold" decompose ballet positions into transformable curves. Movements extend "keyframe" poses (e.g., overhead port de bras) through spatial continuums, revealing "imagined relationships between body points" [5].

### 3. Mechanical Balance in Ballet Dynamics  
#### 3.1 Physics of Equilibrium  
- **Static balance**: Requires CoM alignment over base-of-support (BoS). En pointe stability demands:  
  ```latex  
  \text{Stability} \propto \text{BoS area} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Core tension}  
  ```  
  Rolling feet inward/outward reduces BoS, increasing fall risk [3].  
- **Dynamic balance**: Governed by Newtonian mechanics:  
  - *Turns*: Angular momentum conservation (Newton’s First Law). Clean axial alignment minimizes disruptive torque ($\tau = r \times F$). Arm whipping increases angular momentum [3].  
  - *Jumps*: Force production follows $F = ma$. Maximizing vertical acceleration requires optimal plié depth [3].  

#### 3.2 On-Balance vs. Off-Balance Techniques  
- **Classical**: Emphasizes controlled CoM positioning (e.g., sustained balances in *Sleeping Beauty*) [6].  
- **Neoclassical**: Balanchine’s style exploits off-balance poses (e.g., suspended arabesques) to express vitality. Falling "from one position into the next" creates kinetic tension [6].

### 4. Interdisciplinary Synthesis  
#### 4.1 Architecture as Choreographic Partner  
Cultural heritage sites introduce "silent memory" layers. Dancers become "encounter points" between environmental multiplicity and embodied history, creating "space-time collisions" [1]. Geometric choreography (e.g., *Scaffolding*) responds to architectural affordances, transforming stages into "laboratories for spatial behavior" [7].  

#### 4.2 Biomechanical Integration  
- **Gait analysis**: Ballet dancers exhibit altered locomotion due to turnout training. Motion capture (e.g., Vicon systems) models leg-surface geometries:  
  ```latex  
  S_{\text{rond de jambe}} = \int_{t_0}^{t_1} \! \| \vec{\text{knee}}(t) - \vec{\text{ankle}}(t) \| \, dt  
  ```  
  where $S$ = surface area traced by limbs [4].  
- **Injury mitigation**: Core strength redistributes CoM, preventing joint overload [4].
