### Executive Summary  
Algorithmic composition leverages computational systems to generate music through rule-based, probabilistic, or machine learning methods. This report examines how these systems reconcile mathematical precision with the subjective goal of emotional expressiveness. Key findings indicate that:  
1. **Rule-based systems** (e.g., pDM/Director Musices) use mathematical models of harmony, rhythm, and tension to structure compositions [1].  
2. **Emotional mapping** is achieved by linking musical parameters (tempo, harmony) to psychological models (e.g., valence-arousal space) [1][3][5].  
3. **Listener perception** studies reveal that AI-generated music can evoke human emotional responses comparable to human-composed music *when origin is undisclosed* [3][6][8].  
4. **Human-AI collaboration** is critical for refining outputs to enhance authenticity [4][7].

### 1. Mathematical Rigor: Foundations of Algorithmic Composition  
Algorithmic systems formalize music theory into computable rules. Key frameworks include:  

#### 1.1 Rule-Based Performance Systems (e.g., pDM)  
- **Harmonic tension models**: Quantify consonance/dissonance using interval ratios. For a chord $C$, tension $T_C$ is calculated as:  
$$  
T_C = \sum_{i<j} \frac{1}{|f_i - f_j| \cdot d_{ij}}  
$$  
  where $f_i$, $f_j$ are frequencies and $d_{ij}$ is the dissonance coefficient [1].  
- **Rhythmic manipulation**: Algorithms vary note density and syncopation based on user-defined arousal levels [1][5].  

#### 1.2 Parameter Optimization  
Systems like Stable Audio use prompts to optimize:  
- **Valence-arousal coordinates**: User inputs map emotions to musical features (e.g., high arousal → fast tempo) [5][9].  
- **Mode shifts**: Transition between major/minor scales via probability matrices [1].
