\begin{abstract}
This report investigates how multisensory interaction in digital installations constructs an ontology of audience participation. Drawing on interdisciplinary research spanning cognitive science, embodied interaction, and participatory design, it analyzes how sensory modalities—from traditional exteroceptive cues to emerging interoceptive and synesthetic phenomena—reshape audience agency in curated environments. The study identifies critical linkages between sensory fidelity, embodied cognition, and participatory ontology in museum and gallery contexts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Digital installations increasingly employ multisensory interfaces that transcend conventional visual/auditory paradigms, leveraging haptic, proprioceptive, and even visceral feedback to reconfigure audience participation \cite{1}. This sensory expansion challenges traditional ontologies of spectatorship by creating:

\begin{equation}
P(t) = \int_{S} \phi(s) \cdot \tau(s,t) \, ds
\end{equation}

Where participation \( P(t) \) emerges through temporal integration (\( \tau \)) of sensory stimuli \( \phi(s) \) across modality space \( S \). Recent installations demonstrate how biodata-driven environments convert physiological signals into participatory gestures \cite{3}, while UNESCO's framework emphasizes sensory literacy as cultural right \cite{6}.

\section{Literature Review}

\subsection{Sensory Hierarchy in Curated Spaces}
Parker et al. \cite{1} establish sensory prevalence gradients in museums: visual (64\%), auditory (28\%), olfactory (5.7\%), tactile (2.3\%), with interoceptive modalities statistically negligible. However, Prototype \#03 \cite{3} demonstrates heartbeat-synchronized light patterns increasing participant retention by 37\% compared to visual-only exhibits.

\subsection{Embodied Interaction Theory}
Antle's \cite{10} sensorimotor framework distinguishes three interactive layers:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Proprioceptive:} Skeletal tracking and spatial orientation (e.g., rumble platforms \cite{2})
\item \textbf{Visceral:} Biofeedback loops using respiration/heart rate \cite{3}
\item \textbf{Synaesthetic:} Cross-modal associations (color-temperature mappings \cite{12})
\end{enumerate}

The Magnum Infinity Pleasure Pod \cite{10} exemplifies this hierarchy, with galvanic skin response modulating aroma diffusion rates.

\section{Case Analysis}

\subsection{Prototype \#04 (Chanter 2023)}
This bioresponsive installation uses:

\begin{equation}
\Lambda = \frac{\beta_1 HR + \beta_2 RR}{1 + e^{-k(t - t_0)}}

Where light intensity \( \Lambda \) responds to heart rate (\( HR \)) and respiratory rate (\( RR \)) through logistic activation \cite{3}. Participants exhibited 22\% greater movement complexity (Levy flight \( \alpha = 1.78 \)) compared to static installations.
