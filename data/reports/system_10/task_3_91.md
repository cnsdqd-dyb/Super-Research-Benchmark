# Destination Cognition Formation Through Virtual Previews and On-Site Visits: An Integrated Model of Pre-Travel and In-Situ Experience Interaction

The formation of destination cognition represents a complex psychological process whereby travelers develop comprehensive mental representations of destinations through multiple touchpoints across their journey. This process fundamentally transforms when virtual reality previews interact with subsequent on-site physical experiences, creating a dynamic cognitive system where expectations established through digital media encounter, integrate with, and become modified by direct sensory engagement. Research demonstrates that the interplay between virtual previews and on-site visits creates distinctive patterns of destination perception, memory encoding, and behavioral intention that differ substantially from either experience occurring in isolation. Understanding how these two experiential modalities combine to shape destination cognition requires examining the underlying cognitive mechanisms, the role of expectations and confirmations, the multisensory dimensions of learning, and the temporal dynamics of how travelers process and integrate information across their pre-travel and in-travel phases.

## The Theoretical Architecture of Destination Cognition Formation

### Foundational Models of Destination Image Development

Destination cognition, formally conceptualized as destination image, represents far more than simple factual knowledge about a location.[10][20][31] Rather, it comprises an integrated system of cognitive, affective, and conative components that interact in hierarchical fashion to create the overall mental representation travelers maintain of a destination.[8][11][19] The cognitive component refers specifically to beliefs and knowledge about a destination's tangible and intangible attributes—its natural features, infrastructure, cultural characteristics, and service offerings.[8][11][31] This cognitive layer forms the foundational information layer upon which other dimensions of destination perception build, though research reveals it does not exist in isolation but rather becomes continuously intertwined with emotional responses and behavioral tendencies.

The process of destination image formation historically has been understood through organic and induced image typologies.[10][31][33] Organic images emerge through non-commercial information sources, including word-of-mouth communication, media coverage, educational materials, and personal travel experiences of others.[31][33][34] These organically-formed images develop independently of formal destination marketing efforts and typically carry substantial credibility with potential travelers because they appear to originate from authentic, unbiased sources.[33][34] By contrast, induced images result from deliberate marketing communications by destination marketing organizations, travel agencies, tourism boards, and commercial tourism providers.[31][33][34] These induced images employ persuasive messaging, carefully curated visual content, and promotional narratives designed to influence destination choice decisions. Importantly, the distinction between these image types has become increasingly blurred in contemporary tourism contexts where destination marketing organizations leverage social media and user-generated content platforms, effectively blending induced and organic information sources.[34] Virtual reality previews occupy an interesting position within this framework, functioning as both formally produced marketing materials (induced images) while simultaneously attempting to simulate authentic, direct experiences (approaching organic image characteristics through their immersive, experiential nature).[1][23]

### Three-Component Model of Destination Image and Information Processing

The tripartite destination image model conceptualizes destination perception as comprising cognitive, affective, and conative dimensions operating hierarchically.[8][11][19][22][23] The cognitive component forms the informational foundation, encompassing beliefs about destination attributes, understanding of what facilities and attractions exist, and factual knowledge about accessibility, climate, and cultural characteristics.[8][11] The affective component represents emotional responses to the destination, including feelings of attraction, excitement, relaxation, or concern that arise when contemplating travel to that location.[8][11][19][22] Research consistently demonstrates that cognitive knowledge about a destination's attributes precedes and influences affective responses, though this relationship is not strictly unidirectional—emotionally resonant experiences can subsequently reshape cognitive beliefs about what destinations offer.[8][19] The conative component represents behavioral intentions and predispositions toward actual visitation or recommendation of the destination to others.[8][11][19] This hierarchical model proves crucial for understanding how virtual previews and on-site visits interact cognitively because each experience type engages these components differently and to different degrees of intensity.

## Virtual Preview Experiences and Cognitive Formation

### Technology-Mediated Destination Knowledge Construction

Virtual reality previews and 360-degree video tours represent fundamentally different information-processing contexts compared to traditional destination marketing materials, yet research reveals surprising similarities and important distinctions in how they shape destination cognition compared to actual physical visitation.[2][5][46] When potential travelers engage with VR previews of destinations, they encounter carefully designed visual environments providing spatially rich, interactive representations of destination features.[1][5][21] These experiences employ multiple technological mechanisms to construct vivid mental imagery including high-resolution graphics, 360-degree panoramic views enabling spatial navigation, interactive elements allowing user control over exploration pathways, and in some cases, spatial audio creating multisensory context.[5][21][32] The interactivity component proves particularly significant for destination cognition formation because it transforms travelers from passive observers of destination information into active agents exploring destination spaces, manipulating viewpoints, and exercising agency over the exploration sequence.[1][5][21][23]

Research on telepresence—defined as the psychological sense of "being there" in a mediated environment despite physical absence—reveals that VR previews generate stronger telepresence sensations than traditional video or website-based destination information.[21][23][46] The mechanism through which this occurs involves the interaction of visual immersion, sensory bandwidth, and user control.[21][23] When individuals wear VR headsets, the panoramic visual field, stereoscopic depth cues, and responsive motion tracking create powerful vestibular and proprioceptive signals that the brain interprets as genuine spatial presence, even while conscious awareness remains that the experience is mediated.[17][21][23] This sense of presence significantly enhances cognitive engagement with destination information, increasing the depth of mental imagery formation and the elaboration of destination attributes in memory.[21][23]

The cognitive processing occurring during virtual preview experiences follows heuristic-systematic patterns wherein potential travelers engage in both automatic, intuitive evaluation and deliberative, analytical processing of destination information.[1][51] Interactive features in VR experiences facilitate systematic processing by allowing travelers to examine specific destination attributes in detail, rotate objects, zoom into fine details, and compare multiple visual perspectives of the same location.[1][21] Simultaneously, the immersive, emotionally engaging qualities of high-quality VR previews activate heuristic processing through vivid imagery, compelling visual narratives, and sensory richness that create intuitive impressions and emotional reactions.[1][13] This dual-processing engagement during VR preview experiences creates particularly robust destination cognition structures because information is encoded through multiple cognitive pathways simultaneously.

### Information Density and Attribute Integration in Virtual Environments

Virtual tourism experiences present destination information in spatially organized, visually coherent formats that leverage human spatial cognition systems for enhanced cognitive encoding.[5][14][32][39] Rather than presenting destination attributes as lists or abstract descriptions, VR previews organize information according to spatial relationships, proximity, and contextual associations that match how human brains naturally encode environmental information.[39][42][44] Research on embodied cognition reveals that when humans comprehend information about spaces, they mentally simulate the spatial relationships, movement patterns, and bodily orientations that would apply to navigating those spaces.[17][44] VR previews harness this embodied simulation by presenting information in spatially contextualized arrangements, thereby facilitating more natural cognitive encoding patterns compared to more abstract information presentation modes.[44] Travelers exploring a virtual destination form spatial cognitive maps—mental representations of how different destination elements relate to one another spatially—that subsequently guide expectations about navigation, accessibility, and the logical flow of visiting different attractions.[5][39]

The comprehensiveness of destination attributes accessible through VR previews shapes the breadth and depth of destination cognition formed during pre-travel phases. High-quality VR experiences can showcase multiple destination attributes including natural features, architectural landmarks, cultural practices, hospitality settings, dining venues, and transportation infrastructure.[5][23][32] This multifaceted presentation of destination attributes creates more differentiated, detailed cognitive representations compared to promotional materials focusing exclusively on signature attractions.[23][32] However, the specific selection of which attributes to represent, which perspectives to emphasize, and which details to render in high fidelity inevitably reflects design choices by experience creators, potentially biasing destination cognition toward particular attributes while excluding others entirely.[1][13][23]

### Expectation Formation and Predicted Experience Simulation

A critical cognitive outcome of engaging with virtual destination previews involves the formation of explicit expectations about what on-site experiences will entail.[38][41][45][51][55] During VR preview experiences, travelers engage in what psychologists term "affective forecasting"—predicting what emotions they will experience when physically present at the destination.[3][38] This forecasting process relies on mental simulation, whereby the brain uses the vivid visual and spatial information from the VR preview to construct imaginative scenarios about how the destination will feel, how satisfying specific activities will prove, and what emotional tone the destination visit will carry.[38][44][51] Research on expectation confirmation theory demonstrates that these pre-formed expectations subsequently function as comparison standards against which actual on-site experiences are evaluated.[38][41] When actual experiences match or exceed expectations, travelers experience confirmation and satisfaction; when actual experiences fall short of expectations, travelers experience disconfirmation and dissatisfaction.[38][41]

The specific emotional valence and realism of VR previews significantly influence the magnitude and emotional intensity of expectations formed.[13][23][51][55] High-quality, emotionally engaging VR previews depicting destinations in idealized conditions tend to generate elevated expectations that actual on-site experiences may struggle to match, particularly when on-site conditions involve crowds, weather variations, or service inconsistencies not present in the curated VR preview.[13][30][43] Conversely, more modest or realistic VR previews depicting authentic conditions including less-than-perfect weather or typical crowd levels may generate expectations that actual on-site experiences more readily confirm.[13][30][43] This dynamic creates a crucial interaction mechanism: the specific characteristics of virtual previews shape the expectation baselines that subsequent on-site experiences will encounter and either confirm or disconfirm.

## On-Site Experience and Direct Destination Cognition Development

### Multisensory Engagement and Embodied Experience Integration

Physical visitation to destinations engages cognitive systems through direct sensory contact fundamentally different in character from virtual mediated experiences.[14][43][44] On-site visitors access complete multisensory information including visual input from natural sunlight and atmospheric conditions, olfactory information from environments and local food, tactile sensations from temperature, textures, and physical contact with environments, and auditory input from ambient soundscapes including natural sounds, human activity, and cultural expressions.[5][40][43][44] These multisensory inputs engage embodied cognition systems whereby the body's direct interaction with physical environments contributes essential information to understanding and remembering those environments.[17][44][47] When travelers physically navigate destination spaces, their proprioceptive system (awareness of body position and movement) integrates with visual input to create spatial memories encoding not merely what destinations look like but what they feel like to traverse and inhabit.[17][39][44]

The cognitive representations formed through on-site experience possess qualitatively different characteristics than those formed through virtual previews precisely because they engage the full embodied cognitive system including vestibular (balance and spatial orientation), proprioceptive (body position awareness), and somatosensory (touch and temperature) systems.[17][44][47] Research on spatial navigation demonstrates that individuals who physically walk through environments and manipulate their body position develop spatial memories superior to those who observe identical visual information from fixed positions.[14][17][39] This finding has profound implications for destination cognition: on-site visits generate spatially detailed, embodied cognitive representations enabling travelers to develop nuanced understanding of destination spatial layouts, distances between attractions, accessibility considerations, and the physical demands of activities—information that visual information alone, however high-fidelity, cannot fully convey.

### Experience Categories and Multidimensional Destination Cognition

Research on tourist experiences identifies distinct experience dimensions that collectively shape destination cognition during on-site visits. These dimensions include "feel" experiences (emotional sensations), "sense" experiences (sensory perceptions), "think" experiences (intellectual engagement and learning), "act" experiences (behavioral participation and physical activity), and "relate" experiences (social connection and human interaction).[9][27] Each experience dimension contributes distinctly to destination cognition formation, with research revealing that action-oriented experiences particularly enhance cognitive elaboration of destination attributes.[9][27] When travelers physically participate in destination activities—preparing local food, engaging in adventure activities, participating in cultural practices—they develop deeper, more detailed cognitive representations of those activities compared to merely observing them.[9][27][40] The act of physical participation engages motor planning and execution systems in the brain, creating additional cognitive encoding pathways beyond purely visual or intellectual understanding.[44][47]

On-site destination experiences generate both cognitive and affective dimensions of destination image simultaneously through integrated multisensory engagement.[9][27][42] The cognitive dimension reflects beliefs about destination attributes and factual understanding developed through direct experience, while the affective dimension reflects emotional responses to the destination's atmosphere, residents, and ambient characteristics.[9][27][42] These dimensions remain closely intertwined during on-site experience: the sight of a beautiful landscape may simultaneously encode factual knowledge about terrain characteristics and generate positive emotional responses; interaction with friendly residents simultaneously conveys information about local hospitality culture and creates emotional warmth toward the destination.[9][27]

### Memory Formation and Consolidation From Direct Experience

Direct on-site experiences establish particularly robust and durable destination cognition through the operation of memory consolidation mechanisms occurring during and after physical destination visits. Research on memory formation reveals that meaningful, emotionally engaging experiences undergo deep processing whereby information becomes elaborately encoded in long-term memory through connections to existing knowledge structures.[12][25][28] On-site destination visits frequently involve meaningful activities, novel stimuli, and social interactions that trigger emotional engagement, thereby promoting deep memory processing compared to more passive observation-based experiences.[12][25][28] Moreover, research on memorability in tourism reveals that unforgettable travel experiences subsequently exert powerful influences on revisit intentions and destination loyalty, mediated through the emotional and nostalgic responses those memories evoke.[25][28]

The phenomenon of the "rosy retrospection" effect—whereby travelers tend to remember travel experiences more positively than the experiences felt in the moment—introduces an important cognitive dynamic into how on-site visits contribute to destination cognition formation.[12] Post-visit, travelers tend to reconstruct memory narratives of their destination experiences, emphasizing positive elements and downplaying negative aspects, thereby generating idealized retrospective destination images more positive than the mixed experiences actually involved.[12][28][30] This memory reconstruction process means that destination cognition formed from on-site visits becomes increasingly refined and emotionally valenced in the weeks and months following physical visitation, as travelers process, narrativize, and integrate their travel experiences into broader life narratives and identity constructions.

## The Interaction Mechanism: Virtual Previews Meeting On-Site Reality

### Expectation Confirmation and Cognitive Updating

The critical interaction between virtual previews and on-site visits centers on the confirmation or disconfirmation of expectations formed during the virtual preview phase, which subsequently drives cognitive updating and modification of destination cognition.[38][41][42][51][55] Expectation confirmation theory provides the theoretical foundation for understanding this dynamic: travelers arriving on-site with expectations derived from VR previews compare actual on-site conditions against these mental standards, and the degree of discrepancy between expectations and reality shapes satisfaction and subsequent cognitive evaluation of the destination.[38][41] When on-site conditions match or exceed expectations from VR previews, travelers experience positive disconfirmation or confirmation, resulting in satisfaction and positive destination cognition.[38][41][42][51] When on-site conditions fall short of VR preview expectations, travelers experience negative disconfirmation, resulting in dissatisfaction and potential downward revision of destination cognition.[38][41][42][51]

Research examining the relationship between virtual tourism and actual destination visits reveals that individuals who experienced high-quality VR previews subsequently develop destination images closely aligned with those previews when physically visiting destinations, provided that on-site conditions match the previewed conditions.[2][4][23][46][55] Importantly, however, when on-site conditions diverge from virtual previews—due to weather differences, seasonal variations, crowds, or infrastructure changes—travelers experience significant expectation disconfirmation that prompts substantial cognitive adjustment and reframing of destination understanding.[30][43][55] This dynamic creates a fascinating interaction pattern wherein virtual previews simultaneously facilitate destination learning (by establishing baseline knowledge and generating informative expectations) and constrain cognition (by anchoring expectations that reality must subsequently confirm or disconfirm).

### The Role of Specificity in Virtual Representation

The degree to which virtual previews represent destination conditions with photographic fidelity and specificity significantly influences the magnitude of expectation confirmation or disconfirmation experienced during on-site visits.[1][2][4][13][23][46] VR experiences depicting specific times of day, particular weather conditions, or characteristic crowd levels generate more specific expectations that actual on-site conditions may or may not match.[1][13][23] Conversely, generic VR representations emphasizing architectural features or attractions without specific environmental context generate more abstract expectations capable of accommodating reasonable environmental variation.[1][13] Research findings indicate that 360-degree video tourism experiences, which capture actual destination footage rather than constructed digital environments, generate stronger sense of presence and more realistic expectations compared to fully computer-generated VR environments, though both formats successfully influence destination cognition formation.[2][46]

The authenticity and realism of virtual representations creates a paradox for destination cognition formation: highly realistic, detailed VR previews successfully establish vivid mental imagery and robust expectations, yet these very expectations become more vulnerable to disconfirmation when on-site conditions inevitably deviate from the specific conditions depicted in the virtual preview.[13][30][43][46] More stylized or impressionistic VR representations may create less detailed pre-visit cognition but simultaneously generate broader, more flexible expectations capable of accommodating diverse on-site conditions, potentially resulting in fewer experiences of expectation disconfirmation.[13][43]

### Cognitive Schemas and Knowledge Integration

Virtual previews and on-site visits interact through the operation of cognitive schemas—organized knowledge structures representing categories of experiences, objects, or places.[42][44] When travelers encounter VR previews of destinations, these experiences contribute to the formation or activation of destination schemas, which represent consolidated knowledge about what that destination typically involves.[42] These schemas organize information hierarchically, incorporating both specific factual details about destination features and more abstract knowledge about destination categories, associated activities, and typical experiences.[42][44] When travelers subsequently visit destinations on-site, their existing destination schema (formed through VR preview) functions as a cognitive framework organizing attention, guiding interpretation of on-site experiences, and directing memory encoding.[42][44]

Importantly, destination schemas formed through VR previews may either facilitate or constrain on-site destination cognition development, depending on the accuracy and comprehensiveness of the schema.[42][44] Well-formed, accurate schemas based on high-quality VR previews facilitate on-site cognition by providing organizing frameworks that help travelers understand how destination elements relate to one another, predict how experiences will unfold, and anticipate what activities will involve.[42][44] Conversely, incomplete or inaccurate schemas derived from limited or misleading VR previews may constrain on-site cognition by leading travelers to overlook destination elements not predicted by the flawed schema or to misinterpret on-site experiences through the lens of incorrect preconceptions.[42][44]

## Cognitive Complexity and Dimensional Integration Across Experiences

### From Simple Attribute Knowledge to Holistic Destination Understanding

The progression from virtual preview to on-site experience produces a systematic evolution in destination cognition from relatively simple, attribute-focused understanding toward increasingly holistic, multidimensional destination understanding integrating cognitive, affective, social, and embodied dimensions.[7][8][9][27][42] Virtual previews typically emphasize specific destination attributes—architectural landmarks, natural features, dining venues—presenting these elements in carefully designed visual contexts optimizing their appeal and accessibility to understanding.[1][5][23] Travelers viewing virtual previews develop cognition organized around these featured attributes, creating knowledge structures focused on "what the destination contains" and "what attractions are available."[1][23][42]

On-site visits fundamentally transform this attribute-focused cognition into increasingly holistic understanding encompassing relationships between attributes, ambient qualities of destinations, social dimensions of destination experience, and embodied sensations of inhabiting destination spaces.[9][27][40][42][49] Research on experiential destination image reveals that tourists' on-site experiences generate complex, multifaceted understanding of destinations organized around dimensions like atmosphere, authenticity, accessibility, and social encounters—dimensions not readily accessible through virtual preview experiences.[3][9][27] As travelers physically navigate destination spaces, they encounter unexpected juxtapositions of attractions, discover informal gathering places and authentic local spaces not featured in formal destination marketing, experience the temporal flow of activities across days, and develop nuanced understanding of destination rhythms and patterns of activity.

### Affective Destination Image Formation Through On-Site Emotional Engagement

While virtual previews can effectively communicate cognitive destination image—factual knowledge about destination attributes—affective destination image forms more robustly through direct on-site emotional engagement.[2][4][8][9][23][27] The affective component of destination image encompasses emotional responses to the destination's atmosphere, residents, and ambient qualities, developed through cumulative emotional experiences during destination visits.[8][9][27] Research demonstrates that while VR previews can evoke emotional responses through carefully designed visual aesthetics and immersive presentation, the emotional resonance of on-site destination experiences typically exceeds virtual emotions substantially because on-site experiences involve authentic human interactions, real environmental stimuli, and embodied emotional responses.[2][4][9][23][27]

The formation of affective destination image during on-site visits involves emotional responses to multiple destination dimensions including interactions with residents and tourism workers, experiences of landscape and natural features, participation in cultural activities, and encounters with the ambient atmosphere of places.[9][27][40] Research on "feel," "sense," and "relate" experience dimensions reveals that emotional engagement during on-site experiences substantially exceeds that occurring during virtual previews, even highly immersive VR previews.[9][27] These emotional experiences undergird memorable tourism experiences and subsequently influence revisit intentions and destination loyalty through the operation of emotional connections to destinations.[25][28][49] Interestingly, virtual previews appear to influence affective destination image formation primarily through their impact on expectations and the subsequent confirmation or disconfirmation of those expectations during on-site experiences, rather than through direct affective response to the virtual media itself.

## Temporal Dynamics and Cognitive Development Trajectories

### Pre-Visit Phase: Virtual Preview as Anticipatory Cognitive Structuring

The temporal progression of destination cognition formation proceeds through distinct phases with virtual previews characterizing the pre-visit anticipatory phase and on-site experiences dominating the during-visit cognitive engagement phase.[3][33][42][51][55] During the pre-visit phase, when potential travelers engage with virtual destination previews, cognitive activity centers on imaginative simulation of what destination experiences will entail, formation of expectations, and preliminary assessments of destination appeal and fit with travel motivations.[3][38][42][51] Virtual previews serve cognitive functions during this anticipatory phase including reducing perceived risk of unfamiliar destinations by providing detailed visual information, generating affective forecasts about anticipated satisfaction, and facilitating informed destination choice decisions by enabling comparison across destination options.[1][13][23][38][51]

Research on anticipatory consumption phases reveals that mental simulation of future experiences substantially influences pre-consumption affect and expectations, functioning as a form of "mental travel" preceding physical travel.[3][38][45] High-quality VR previews enable particularly vivid mental simulation of destination experiences by providing detailed visual stimuli, spatial organization information, and interactive exploration opportunities that support elaborate imaginative elaboration.[1][5][21][23][38] This pre-visit mental simulation of destination experiences, grounded in detailed virtual preview information, establishes expectation baselines and generates preliminary destination affect that substantially influences destination choice decisions.[1][23][38][45]

### During-Visit Phase: On-Site Experiences and Real-Time Cognitive Integration

The during-visit phase involves real-time cognitive engagement with actual destination environments, characterized by dynamic updating of expectations, active sensory integration, and elaboration of destination understanding through participatory engagement.[9][27][42] Throughout on-site visits, travelers continuously compare actual experiences against expectations formed during the virtual preview phase, updating destination cognition in light of the confirmations and disconfirmations encountered.[42][51][55] This real-time cognitive updating occurs relatively automatically as travelers process multisensory destination information, navigate physical spaces, interact with residents, and participate in activities.[9][27][42][44]

Importantly, on-site experience frequently reveals aspects of destinations not accessible through or not emphasized in virtual previews, introducing novel information into destination cognition and potentially reshaping cognitive understanding formed during pre-visit phases.[30][40][43] Travelers may discover informal local gathering places, experience unexpected sensory qualities of environments, or encounter cultural practices not featured in formal destination marketing materials.[30][40][43] These discoveries during on-site experiences expand and refine destination cognition beyond what virtual previews could establish alone, creating more comprehensive, experientially grounded understanding of destinations.[30][40][43]

### Post-Visit Phase: Memory Consolidation and Retrospective Cognition Formation

Following on-site destination visits, destination cognition undergoes further transformation through memory consolidation processes occurring over hours, days, and weeks post-visit.[12][25][28][42] This post-visit phase involves retrospective cognitive processing wherein travelers construct narrative accounts of their destination experiences, integrate experiences into broader life narratives and identity constructions, and consolidate destination memories into long-term semantic knowledge.[12][25][28][42] Research on memory reconsolidation reveals that memories become malleable during this post-visit period, vulnerable to influence from subsequent information, personal reflection, and social sharing of travel narratives.[12][28]

The post-visit cognitive processing of destination experiences frequently involves comparison between pre-visit expectations (formed through virtual previews) and actual on-site experiences, with this comparison process substantially influencing post-visit destination cognition.[42][51][55] Travelers whose on-site experiences matched or exceeded virtual preview expectations frequently exhibit positive affect during post-visit cognitive processing, generating idealized retrospective destination images incorporating the best aspects of experienced reality combined with imaginative elaborations.[12][28] Conversely, travelers whose on-site experiences fell short of virtual preview expectations often engage in cognitive work of reconciling disappointing reality with elevated expectations, sometimes engaging in "cognitive dissonance reduction" through rationalization and reframing of destination qualities.[30][42][50][53]

## Factors Moderating the Virtual-to-On-Site Cognition Transition

### Individual Differences in Information Processing and Expectation Formation

The interaction between virtual previews and on-site experience cognition formation does not proceed uniformly across all travelers but rather exhibits systematic variation based on individual characteristics including prior travel experience, demographic factors, personality dispositions, and cultural backgrounds.[7][8][11][53] Experienced travelers who have previously visited multiple destinations demonstrate more sophisticated, differentiated destination schemas enabling them to more readily accommodate expectation disconfirmation and integrate novel on-site information into existing destination knowledge frameworks.[7][42][53] Conversely, travelers with limited prior travel experience tend to develop simpler, less differentiated destination schemas from virtual previews and may experience greater difficulty integrating contradictory on-site information, potentially resulting in more problematic expectation disconfirmation experiences.[7][42][53]

Cultural background and individualism-collectivism dimensions influence how travelers form expectations from virtual previews and subsequently respond to on-site experiences.[11][19][22][53] Research reveals that travelers from individualistic cultural backgrounds emphasize personal preferences and emotional responses in forming expectations and evaluating on-site experiences, whereas travelers from collectivist backgrounds place greater emphasis on social appropriateness and family considerations in destination cognition formation.[11][19][22] These cultural differences mean that the same virtual preview and on-site experience combination may generate substantially different destination cognition outcomes depending on the cultural orientation of the traveler engaging with the experiences.

### Traveler Motivation and Mindfulness in Destination Cognition Development

Travel motivations—the personal reasons driving interest in particular destinations—substantially influence how virtual previews shape destination cognition and subsequently how on-site experiences are interpreted and integrated.[7][49][52] Travelers motivated by escapism and relaxation may form destination cognition from virtual previews emphasizing tranquility and aesthetic beauty, subsequently becoming highly sensitive to expectation disconfirmation if on-site conditions involve crowds, noise, or infrastructure disruptions.[7][49][52] Conversely, travelers motivated by cultural learning and personal growth may form destination cognition emphasizing authenticity and cultural distinctiveness, potentially demonstrating greater tolerance for on-site experiences diverging from idealized virtual previews if they perceive cultural authenticity in those divergences.[7][49][52]

Traveler mindfulness—characterized by open, non-judgmental awareness and present-moment engagement—influences the quality and flexibility of destination cognition formed through both virtual previews and on-site experiences.[7] Research on mindfulness in tourism reveals that highly mindful travelers develop more nuanced, complex destination cognition characterized by appreciation for unexpected experiences, openness to novel perspectives, and flexibility in adapting expectations when on-site conditions diverge from virtual previews.[7] Conversely, less mindful travelers may demonstrate rigid adherence to expectations formed during virtual preview phases, resulting in higher susceptibility to expectation disconfirmation and difficulty adapting destination cognition when on-site reality diverges from virtual representations.[7][53]

### Media Characteristics and Cognitive Processing Demands

The specific technical characteristics of virtual preview media—resolution, frame rate, interactivity, sensory modalities represented—influence both the robustness of destination cognition formed through virtual engagement and the specificity of expectations that on-site experiences must subsequently confirm.[1][5][21][23][46] High-resolution 360-degree video with interactive navigation generates more vivid, spatially detailed destination cognition and more specific expectations than lower-resolution video or static images.[1][2][5][21][23][46] This means that on-site experiences must more closely match virtual preview specifics to result in expectation confirmation, whereas lower-resolution virtual previews generate more general, abstract expectations capable of accommodating greater on-site variation.[1][23][46]

The presence or absence of multisensory information in virtual previews influences the comprehensiveness of destination cognition formed and the nature of expectation-reality mismatches experienced during on-site visits.[5][43] VR previews incorporating spatial audio, descriptive narration, or other non-visual sensory information generate more holistic destination cognition than purely visual VR representations.[5][43] However, this very comprehensiveness means that travelers' expectations become more multidimensionally anchored, potentially creating greater expectation disconfirmation when on-site sensory experiences diverge from the multisensory information presented in virtual previews.[5][43]

## Practical Implications and Applied Destination Cognition

### Destination Marketing Strategies Leveraging Virtual-On-Site Interaction

Understanding how destination cognition forms through virtual preview and on-site visit interaction provides strategic implications for destination marketing organizations seeking to attract visitors and foster long-term destination loyalty.[1][13][23][48] Effective destination marketing increasingly requires aligning virtual preview representations with authentic on-site conditions, preventing the gap between virtual expectations and on-site reality that generates expectation disconfirmation and dissatisfaction.[1][13][23][30][43][48] Rather than presenting idealized, heavily edited virtual previews emphasizing optimal lighting, crowd-free conditions, and photogenic angles, sophisticated destination marketing increasingly incorporates authentic representations including realistic crowd levels, typical seasonal variations, and genuine atmospheric conditions.[1][13][23][43][48]

Additionally, destination marketing organizations can leverage understanding of destination cognition formation to sequence virtual preview engagement strategically across traveler journey stages.[1][13][23][32][45][48] Early-stage virtual previews during inspiration and consideration phases might emphasize aspirational imagery and emotional appeals fostering destination interest and initial destination cognition formation.[13][23][32][45][48] Later-stage virtual previews during decision and booking phases might emphasize practical information, accurate representations of conditions, and authentic experiences helping travelers establish realistic expectations aligned with actual on-site conditions.[1][13][23][48]

### Enhancing Destination Cognition Through Complementary On-Site Experiences

Destinations seeking to optimize visitor satisfaction and foster positive destination cognition can strategically design on-site experiences to build upon and extend the destination cognition developed through virtual previews.[40][42][48][49] Welcome programs, orientation experiences, and guided tours at destination arrival can explicitly bridge the virtual-to-on-site transition by helping travelers integrate their virtual preview knowledge with on-site reality, clarifying any expectation-reality divergences, and directing attention toward destination features and experiences not featured in virtual previews.[40][42][48][49] This bridging process facilitates smoother cognitive adjustment, reduces perceived expectation disconfirmation, and enables travelers to more readily expand destination cognition beyond virtual preview-anchored understanding toward more comprehensive on-site-grounded understanding.

Moreover, destination management can strategically highlight authentic, unexpected destination qualities discovered during on-site experiences—informal cultural practices, unique local gathering places, resident interactions, and ambient qualities of destination spaces—that transcend formalized virtual representations.[30][40][43][49] Travelers who arrive expecting idealized, curated destination representations often experience heightened satisfaction when discovering authentic, unexpected aspects of destinations that suggest their experience encompasses more than the marketed destination presentation.[30][40][49] This phenomenon of discovering authentic destination qualities not featured in virtual marketing materials can transform expectation disconfirmation from a negative experience into a positive unexpected discovery, generating "positive surprise" emotional responses that substantially enhance destination cognition and revisit intentions.

## Synthesis and Integrated Theoretical Framework

The formation of destination cognition through the interaction of virtual previews and on-site visits proceeds through a complex, dynamic process wherein each experience mode contributes distinct cognitive elements, each subsequent experience modifies cognition formed through prior experiences, and individual factors substantially influence how travelers integrate these experiences into comprehensive destination understanding. Virtual previews establish preliminary destination cognition through immersive, interactive visualization of destination features, generating vivid mental imagery, spatially organized knowledge structures, and explicit expectations about anticipated experiences.[1][5][21][23][42] These virtual experiences function as cognitive scaffolding, organizing attention toward particular destination attributes, facilitating initial destination knowledge formation, and establishing expectation baselines against which on-site experiences will subsequently be evaluated.

On-site destination visits transform preliminary virtual preview-based cognition through direct multisensory engagement, embodied spatial navigation, authentic social interaction, and participation in destination activities.[9][27][40][42][44] These on-site experiences fill sensory and embodied dimensions not accessible through virtual media, discover destination elements not featured in formal marketing materials, and provide emotional engagement typically exceeding virtual experience intensity.[9][27][40][42] The on-site phase of destination cognition development is characterized by continuous real-time updating of expectations, integration of novel multisensory information, and elaboration of destination understanding beyond virtual preview parameters.[42][51][55]

Post-visit cognitive processing consolidates destination experiences into long-term memory, integrates diverse on-site observations into coherent destination narratives, and establishes enduring destination cognition patterns influencing future travel decisions and destination recommendations.[12][25][28][42] The quality of post-visit destination cognition reflects both the accuracy of expectations formed through virtual previews (determining the degree of expectation confirmation or disconfirmation experienced) and the richness of on-site experiences (determining the multidimensional elaboration of destination understanding achieved). Travelers whose virtual preview expectations proved broadly accurate experience positive confirmation and satisfaction, subsequently exhibiting high destination loyalty and positive word-of-mouth recommendations.[12][25][28] Conversely, travelers whose virtual preview expectations were substantially disconfirmed by on-site reality may experience dissatisfaction and negative destination cognition despite potentially having positive on-site experiences, if they frame those experiences primarily through the lens of expectation disappointment rather than autonomous evaluation of experienced reality.

## Conclusion: Toward Optimized Destination Cognition Development

The interaction between virtual previews and on-site visits represents a fundamentally important mechanism through which contemporary travelers develop destination cognition, combining the cognitive scaffolding and expectation-setting functions of virtual media with the multisensory engagement, embodied learning, and authentic experience of physical visitation. The increasingly sophisticated quality of virtual reality previews means that virtual destination cognition formation increasingly approximates in richness and detail the cognition formed through actual visitation, yet important qualitative differences remain in the embodied, multisensory, and authentic dimensions of knowledge accessed through on-site experience. Destination marketing organizations, tourism operators, and destination management authorities can optimize visitor satisfaction and foster long-term destination loyalty by understanding and strategically leveraging this virtual-to-on-site cognition transition process, ensuring that virtual previews generate realistic expectations aligned with authentic on-site conditions, that on-site experiences explicitly bridge and extend virtual preview-anchored cognition toward more comprehensive destination understanding, and that post-visit cognitive processing consolidates diverse pre-visit and during-visit experiences into enduring destination knowledge supporting future travel decisions and destination recommendations.
