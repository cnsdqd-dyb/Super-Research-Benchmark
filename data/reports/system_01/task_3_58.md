The relationship between effective population size (**N<sub>e</sub>**) and **genetic load** during **range contraction** driven by climate change involves complex evolutionary dynamics. Here's a step-by-step explanation:

### 1. **Range Contraction & Reduced N<sub>e</sub>**
   - Climate change forces species to track shifting habitats (e.g., poleward/toward higher elevations), fragmenting populations and reducing geographic range.
   - **N<sub>e</sub> drops dramatically** due to:
     - Smaller population size.
     - Increased fragmentation (subpopulations become isolated).
     - Higher variance in reproductive success (e.g., only some individuals colonize new areas).

### 2. **Genetic Load: The Burden of Deleterious Alleles**
   Genetic load (L) = Reduction in mean fitness of a population due to:
   - **Mildly deleterious mutations** (harder to purge).
   - **Inbreeding depression** (exposing recessive deleterious alleles).
   - **Loss of adaptive alleles** due to drift.

### 3. **How Reduced N<sub>e</sub> Affects Genetic Load**
   - **Weakened Purifying Selection**:  
     Selection becomes less efficient against **mildly deleterious mutations** when **N<sub>e</sub>s < 1** (where *s* = selection coefficient). Mutations drift to fixation instead of being removed.
     > *Result*: Genetic load **increases**.

   - **Enhanced Genetic Drift**:  
     Random allele frequency changes dominate over selection, especially for mutations with small *s*. This:
     - Fixes deleterious alleles (increasing load).
     - Losens beneficial/adaptive alleles critical for climate adaptation.

   - **Accelerated Inbreeding**:  
     Smaller N<sub>e</sub> → increased mating among relatives →  
     - Higher homozygosity of recessive deleterious alleles → **inbreeding depression** → reduced fitness.

   - **Reduced Adaptive Potential**:  
     Range contraction leaves less genetic variation for climate-driven adaptation, trapping populations under new stressors.

### 4. **Potential For "Purging" (But Often Short-Term)**
   - **Strong selection** (e.g., severe bottlenecks) can **purge** highly deleterious alleles by killing carriers.
   - **Caveats**:  
     - Mostly effective for *strongly* deleterious mutations (common in small populations).
     - Gentle selection (for mild deleterious alleles) is *less efficient*.
     - Purging is temporary: New deleterious mutations accumulate as N<sub>e</sub> remains low.

### 5. **Additional Climate-Linked Risks**
   - **Mutation Accumulation**: Bottlenecks reduce N<sub>e</sub>, making selection less effective against new mildly harmful mutations. Load rises over generations ("Muller's ratchet" analog).
   - **Extinction Debt**: Populations may persist while load is high, but environmental stochasticity (e.g., disease, extreme weather) triggers collapse.
   - **Maladaptation**: Loss of locally adapted alleles during range shift compounds fitness losses.

### 6. **Conservation Implications**
   - **Critical Thresholds**: N<sub>e</sub> < 50–100 often leads to unsustainable load + drift.
   - **Management Strategies**:  
     - **Restore gene flow** between fragments to increase N<sub>e</sub>.
     - **Protect climate refugia** (areas resisting climate change).
     - **Assisted migration** (controversial but may introduce adaptive alleles).

### Key Takeaway:
**Reduced N<sub>e</sub> during range contraction** weakens selection, allowing deleterious alleles to accumulate and reducing adaptive capacity. This amplifies extinction risk beyond direct climate stressors. While purging may occur during strong bottlenecks, it is often incomplete and outweighed by drift-driven load increases.
