The construction of an **ontology of audience participation** in sensory-rich digital installations arises from how **multimodal sensory interfaces** redefine the relationship between human perception, agency, and reality. Here's a breakdown of the mechanisms:

### 1. **Sensory Ecology & Embodiment**
   - **Beyond Visual Primacy:** Interactive installations engage **multiple senses** (haptics, sound, scent, proprioception), rejecting the "eyeball" paradigm of traditional media. This creates an **embodied ontology**, where participation is felt physically and affectively.
   - *Example:* A sound-reactive light field that pulses with the audience's breath or movement dissolves the boundary between observer and object, making participation a **kinesthetic being-in-the-world**.

### 2. **Prosthetic Agency & Extended Self**
   - **Sensory Translation:** Sensors act as *prosthetic extensions* of human senses (e.g., infrared mapping, gyroscope tracking). Participation becomes a **dialogue between biological senses and machine interpretation**, co-creating a hybrid self.
   - *Example:* Wearables converting heartbeats into projected visuals make the audience's internal state an *exteriorized agency*, blurring body/machine boundaries.

### 3. **Non-Hierarchical Engagement**
   - **Sensory Redundancy:** When multiple sensory channels (e.g., touch and sound simultaneously) convey information, participation becomes **resilient and inclusive**. This challenges top-down control models, fostering a **collective ontology** where agency is distributed.
   - *Example:* A room where footsteps trigger audio echoes, and hand gestures alter lighting—no single input "dominates," creating a **polyphonic participation grammar**.

### 4. **Affective Universes & Immersive Ontology**
   - **Sensory Immersion:** Enveloping environments (e.g., surround sound, spatial projection) dissolve spatial/temporal boundaries, inducing a **liminal state**. The user's ontology shifts from "participant" to "inhabitatant" of an **affective artificial world**.
   - *Example:* Calming blue light and binaural solo violin inducing a meditative state transforms passive viewers into **participants in a shared emotional ecosystem**.

### 5. **Feedback Loops & Co-Creation**
   - **Reciprocity as Ontology:** Real-time input-output systems create **eternal feedback loops**. The audience's body, the machine, and the space become **interdependent actors in an ongoing co-creation**—not subjects of, but contributors to, the work's being.
   - *Example:* An algorithm that evolves through audience touch, sound, and proximity: the installation has **no fixed identity**, existing only as a **collective emergence**.

### 6. **Transgressive Perception**
   - **Sensory Augmentation:** Installation design *permittedly* disrupts sensory norms (e.g., disorienting soundscapes, impossible textures). This **erodes ontological certainty**—the audience confronts a reality *designed to feel provisional*, questioning permanence of self/spatial rules.

### Conclusion:
Sensory interaction constructs an ontology where **participation is non-transferable, irreplaceable, and constitutively entangled with technology**. The audience doesn't *use* the work—they participate in its *becoming*, redefining agency, selfhood, and reality as **situated, multisensory, and perpetually co-constructed**. This reshapes aesthetics from representation to *generation*—where art exists *through* interaction, not *despite* it.
