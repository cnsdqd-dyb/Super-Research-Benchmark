The mechanism that forms satisfaction evaluations from the gap between planning expectations and actual experience is often referred to in various theories and models across different fields, such as marketing, psychology, and organizational behavior. This concept is most prominently associated with the Expectancy Theory of Consumer Satisfaction or Cognitive Dissonance Theory in consumer behavior.

Here's a simplified version of how this mechanism works:

1. **Expectation Formation**: Before an experience or purchase, individuals form expectations based on prior experiences, recommendations, advertisements, or other forms of information. These expectations set a standard against which future experiences are compared.

2. **Experience**: The actual experience then occurs. This could be the use of a product, service, or any experience that the individual has planned or anticipated.

3. **Evaluation of Gap**: After experiencing the product or service, individuals compare their actual experience with their expectations. If the experience meets or exceeds expectations, they are likely to be satisfied. If it falls short, dissatisfaction occurs.

4. **Satisfaction Evaluation**: Based on this comparison, consumers form a satisfaction evaluation. This is influenced by the magnitude and direction of the gap—whether the experience was better than expected (leading to satisfaction) or worse than expected (leading to dissatisfaction).

This mechanism is crucial in fields like customer service and product design because understanding how expectations are formed and managed can help organizations improve customer satisfaction and loyalty. For example, if a company can accurately predict what customers expect and then ensure that their products or services meet or exceed those expectations, they are more likely to receive positive feedback and repeat business. On the other hand, if the company consistently fails to meet expectations, customers are likely to switch to competitors offering alternatives that fulfill their needs better.
