The local chemical ordering in high-entropy alloys (HEAs) critically influences dislocation-twin boundary (TB) interactions during plastic deformation through **three key mechanisms**, fundamentally altering the plasticity pathways:

1.  **Modulation of Twin Boundary Energy & Stability:**
    *   **Ordered Domains Lower TB Energy:** Local chemical ordering (e.g., superlattice formation like B2 in FCC alloys or specific atomic arrangements near TBs) reduces the stacking fault energy (SFE) required to form twins. This creates **ultra-low-energy TBs**.
    *   **Enhanced TB Stability:** Lower energy stabilizes TBs against coalescence or absorption by other defects. This makes TBs more effective **reflection sites** for dislocations rather than transient obstacles.
    *   **Impact on Dislocation Absorption:** The lowered SFE facilitates the nucleation of twins via shockley partial dislocations. This promotes easier **dislocation absorption** into TBs when a dislocation's Burgers vector aligns with the twin habit plane and its local stress field matches the ordering-induced SFE reduction.

2.  **Altered Dislocation Core Structure & Peierls Barrier:**
    *   **Localized Strain Field Modification:** Order creates spatial variations in atomic size and bonding (lattice distortion/composition fluctuation). This modifies the **dislocation core structure**.
    *   **Reduced Peierls Stress:** In some ordered FCC HEAs, core expansion or core spread can lower the Peierls stress, making dislocation **cross-slip more facile** within or near ordered regions adjacent to TBs.
    *   **Enhanced Interaction with Partials:** Ordered regions may stabilize specific partial dislocation configurations (e.g., extended stacking faults corresponding to the ordering). This alters how dissociated dislocations interact with the TB structure itself.

3.  **Interactive Steering Mechanism (Dislocation-Driven Ordering/Disordering):**
    *   **Cascading Chemical Flux:** As dislocations glide, their stress fields can locally **induce or disrupt chemical ordering** near TBs.
    *   **Dynamic Tensor:** This creates a **feedback loop**: Local dislocation stress zones can order or disorder, which in turn strengthens or weakens the stress field experienced by *other* nearby dislocations. Dislocations may "steer" their interaction with the TB by transiently altering local chemistry, influencing whether they absorb, reflect, transmit via partials, or are pinned.

**Synthesis of Interaction Mechanisms:**

*   **Dislocation Absorption Dominance:** In low-to-intermediate SFE ordered HEAs, the **ordered ultra-low-energy TB acts as a potent sink**. Dislocations (often partials) are readily absorbed to form new twin lamellae, accelerating twin thickening.
*   **Dislocation Reflection/Pinning:** Highly stable ordered TBs generated by significant ordering act as **strong barriers**. Dislocations encounter high image forces and are **reflected or pile up**, contributing to strain hardening but potentially limiting ductility.
*   **Enhanced Cross-Slip & Terrain Flattening:** The combination of reduced Peierls stress in ordered regions and ultra-low SFE can promote **cross-slip of dissociated dislocations**, allowing them to navigate near the TB complex and potentially diffuse across it via climb or suppress pile-ups.
*   **Facilitated Transfer via Partials:** Ordered structures can stabilize the intermediate stacking fault phases. This can enable **dislocation transfer across the TB** through the emission and absorption mechanism involving shockley partials, especially when the partials match the local SFE/stability landscape created by ordering.

**Consequences for Plastic Behavior:**

*   **Twinning-Induced Plasticity (TWIP):** Enhanced absorption into ordered TBs promotes TWIP, yielding high uniform elongation.
*   **Strain Hardening:** Reflection and pinning by ordered TBs significantly increase strain hardening rates.
*   **Potential Embrittlement:** If ordered regions near TBs create very strong, brittle pinning points, or if cascading disordering occurs under stress, it could contribute to early decohesion or void formation.
*   **Layer Thickness Control:** The stability of ordered TBs influences the achievable twin lamella thickness, directly impacting strength (Hall-Petch) and ductility.

In essence, local chemical ordering acts as a **multifunctional tuning parameter** that dictates whether dislocations interact with TBs by being readily absorbed to form twins, strongly reflected to harden the material, or guided across via complex partial mechanisms. This occurs through coupled effects on TB thermodynamics (energy), dislocation kinetics (core structure, mobility), and dynamic chemical fluxes at the nanoscale interface. The outcome is a profound influence on the overall softening/hardening balance and ductility of the HEA during deformation.
