MEMS sensors, which stands for Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems sensors, are known for their miniaturization and integration capabilities, but like any precision instrument, their long-term stability is influenced by various factors. 

When it comes to resonant frequency stability, this is indeed a key parameter for certain types of MEMS sensors - particularly those utilizing the principle of mechanical resonance for sensing - such as piezoelectric or resonant-based mass, force, or pressure sensors. The resonant frequency can be sensitive to temperature changes, mechanical stress, and other environmental factors.

Process deviations refer to the manufacturing variations that can occur during the fabrication of MEMS devices. This can include minor differences in material properties, dimensions, or structural integrity due to the limitations of semiconductor manufacturing processes. Designers employ sophisticated design rules and compensation techniques to mitigate the effects of these deviations, including trimming during the manufacturing or calibration post-fabrication.

Environmental vibration can cause significant disturbances to the operation of sensitive MEMS sensors. To counteract this, sensor packages are often designed with shock and vibration damping in mind, and the sensors themselves may incorporate active or passive isolation mechanisms. Moreover, digital signal processing techniques can be used to filter out high-frequency noise caused by vibrations.

Additionally, the electronics associated with MEMS sensors, particularly the signal conditioning and readout circuits, play a critical role in sustaining long-term accuracy. Techniques such as on-chip calibration, temperature compensation, and self-diagnostics can help maintain the required performance standards despite the variations introduced by the manufacturing process and environmental conditions.

While it is challenging to maintain absolute long-term stability and detection accuracy under all conditions, with careful design and advanced compensation algorithms, MEMS sensors can achieve the stability and accuracy required for many applications. These devices are widely used in automotive, aerospace, industrial, and consumer electronics sectors where robustness and reliability are paramount. Their success also depends on the ongoing developments in materials science and manufacturing technology.
