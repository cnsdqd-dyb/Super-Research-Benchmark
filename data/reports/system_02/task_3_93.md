Title: Quantum Non-locality and Information Transfer in Distributed Cognitive Systems

Executive Summary:

1. Theoretical Framework

1.1 Quantum Non-locality
Quantum non-locality manifests when separated quantum systems exhibit correlations that cannot be explained by classical local theories [1]. This is demonstrated through violations of Bell's inequalities, where measurements on entangled particles show stronger correlations than possible under local realist theories.

1.2 Distributed Cognitive Systems
Distributed cognitive systems involve multiple interacting components that process information collectively. Key characteristics include:
- Non-decomposability of cognitive subsystems
- Parallel information processing
- Emergent system-level behaviors

2. Information Transfer Mechanisms

2.1 Quantum Entanglement in Cognition
The quantum cognition framework suggests that cognitive systems utilize quantum-like processes where:
- Mental states exist in superposition
- Measurement/observation affects state collapse
- Non-classical probability distributions emerge

2.2 Mathematical Framework
The quantum probability framework for cognitive systems can be expressed as:

$|\psi\rangle = \sum_i c_i|i\rangle$

where $|\psi\rangle$ represents the cognitive state and $c_i$ are complex amplitudes [2].
