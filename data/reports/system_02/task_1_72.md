How Scenario Design in Environmental Risk Stress Testing Affects Capital Adequacy Calibration

Executive Summary:
This report analyzes how the design of climate risk scenarios impacts banks' capital adequacy assessments and regulatory calibration. The analysis reveals that scenario design choices significantly influence capital requirements through their effects on risk measurement and stress test outcomes.

1. Introduction
Environmental risk stress testing is becoming a critical component of banking supervision, with European regulators planning full integration into standard stress testing frameworks by 2027 [1]. This evolution requires careful consideration of how scenario design affects capital adequacy measurements.

2. Key Components of Scenario Design

2.1 Time Horizon Considerations
The choice of time horizon significantly impacts capital calibration:
- Short-term scenarios (1-5 years): More precise for regulatory capital assessment but may underestimate long-term risks
- Long-term scenarios (>5 years): Better capture transition risks but face increased uncertainty
- Optimal approach: Multi-horizon framework combining both perspectives [2]

2.2 Risk Factor Integration
Scenario design must incorporate multiple risk dimensions:
- Physical risks: Acute events and chronic changes
- Transition risks: Policy, technology, and market changes
- Integration method: \[ RWA_{adjusted} = RWA_{base} \times (1 + \alpha_{climate}) \]
where \(\alpha_{climate}\) represents the climate risk adjustment factor

3. Impact on Capital Adequacy

3.1 Risk-Weight Recalibration
Climate scenarios affect capital requirements through:
- PD and LGD adjustments in IRB models
- Sectoral risk weight modifications
- Geographic exposure considerations [1]

3.2 Capital Buffer Assessment
The scenario design influences ICAAP through:
- Stress magnitude calibration
- Risk factor correlation assumptions
- Time horizon of capital planning

4. Methodological Challenges

4.1 Data Limitations
- Granular exposure data requirements
- Historical data limitations
- Need for forward-looking metrics

4.2 Model Integration
Complex interaction between:
\[ Capital_{required} = f(PD_{climate}, LGD_{climate}, EAD_{climate}) \]
where climate adjustments must be integrated into existing risk models

5. Recommendations

5.1 Scenario Design Framework
- Implement proportional approach based on bank size and exposure
- Ensure consistency with existing stress test infrastructure
- Incorporate both short and long-term perspectives

5.2 Capital Calibration
- Develop standardized climate risk factors
- Establish clear transmission channels
- Regular review and adjustment of scenarios

6. Conclusion
Effective scenario design is crucial for accurate capital adequacy assessment. A balanced approach combining multiple time horizons and risk factors, while acknowledging data and modeling limitations, is essential for robust capital calibration.
