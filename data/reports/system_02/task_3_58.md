Title: Impact of Effective Population Size on Genetic Load During Range Contraction Due to Climate Change

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report analyzes how effective population size (Ne) influences the accumulation and purging of genetic load in populations experiencing range contraction due to climate change. Understanding these dynamics is crucial for predicting population viability and implementing conservation strategies.

METHODOLOGY
Analysis based on synthesis of provided research literature examining population genetics, range dynamics, and climate change impacts.

KEY FINDINGS

1. Relationship Between Ne and Genetic Load
- Genetic load is inversely related to effective population size, following the equation θ = 4Neμ, where μ is mutation rate [3]
- Selection effectiveness depends on the inequality 1 ≤|4Nes|, where s is the selection coefficient [3]
- Smaller Ne leads to increased genetic drift and reduced selection efficiency against deleterious mutations

2. Range Contraction Effects
- Range contractions lead to:
  * Reduced Ne through population bottlenecks
  * Altered demographic patterns favoring older age classes
  * Increased isolation between remaining populations [3]
  * Greater genetic erosion in edge populations [12]

3. Population Edge Dynamics
- Trailing edge populations during range contractions experience:
  * Reduced Ne and increased accumulation of deleterious variants
  * Environmental marginality approaching ecological tolerance limits
  * Different evolutionary pressures compared to leading edges [12]

4. Climate Change Implications
- Climate-driven range contractions can:
  * Accelerate genetic load accumulation through reduced Ne
  * Create novel selective pressures requiring rapid adaptation
  * Disrupt gene flow between populations
  * Increase extinction risk through combined demographic and genetic effects

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Conservation Priorities
- Maintain connectivity between populations to preserve gene flow
- Focus on preserving large enough Ne to prevent genetic load accumulation
- Consider genetic rescue for small, isolated populations

2. Management Strategies
- Monitor both demographic and genetic parameters
- Establish migration corridors to facilitate range shifts
- Protect both core and edge populations to maintain adaptive potential

3. Research Needs
- Long-term studies of genetic load dynamics during range shifts
- Better integration of climate models with population genetic data
- Improved understanding of interaction between Ne and selection efficiency

LIMITATIONS
Analysis limited to available literature context. Additional empirical studies needed to validate theoretical predictions across different taxa and scenarios.
