Title: Stability Boundaries in Magnetohydrodynamics and Fusion Reactor Design Constraints

Executive Summary:
This report analyzes how magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) stability limits, particularly through the plasma beta parameter, constrain the design space of fusion reactors. The focus is on understanding the key stability boundaries and their implications for practical reactor configurations.

1. Fundamental Stability Constraints

The primary MHD stability limit in tokamak fusion reactors is characterized by the Troyon beta limit, given by:

\[\beta_N = \beta_{max}(\%) \frac{aB_T}{I_p} \leq 2.8\]

where:
- \(\beta\) is the ratio of plasma pressure to magnetic pressure
- \(B_T\) is the toroidal magnetic field
- \(I_p\) is the plasma current
- \(a\) is the minor radius

2. Key Stability Modes

Two critical MHD instabilities dominate the stability boundaries [1]:

a) External Kink Modes:
- Occur at the plasma boundary
- Can be partially stabilized through wall effects
- Set fundamental limits on achievable plasma current

b) Ballooning Modes:
- Pressure-driven instabilities
- Occur at high beta values
- Particularly limiting in regions of adverse magnetic curvature

3. Design Space Implications

The stability boundaries create several key constraints on reactor design [2]:

- Maximum achievable beta typically limited to ~5% in conventional tokamaks
- Requires careful optimization of:
  * Plasma cross-sectional shape
  * Current profile
  * Pressure profile
  * Aspect ratio

4. Design Optimization Strategies

To maximize stable operating space:
- Utilize strong plasma shaping (high elongation and triangularity)
- Optimize pressure and current profiles
- Consider lower aspect ratio configurations
- Implement active feedback control systems

5. Conclusions

MHD stability boundaries, particularly through beta limits, fundamentally constrain fusion reactor design space by:
- Setting upper limits on achievable plasma pressure
- Requiring specific geometric configurations
- Necessitating careful profile control
- Influencing overall reactor size and magnetic field requirements

References:
[1] Turnbull et al., "Stability in DIII-D"
[2] IAEA Technical Documents on Advanced Reactor Design

This analysis demonstrates that MHD stability considerations are central to determining viable fusion reactor configurations and must be carefully considered in design optimization.
