RESEARCH REPORT
Title: Heparin Dosage Management During ECMO Support: Balancing Thromboembolism and Bleeding Risks

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report analyzes current practices and challenges in managing heparin anticoagulation during extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) support, focusing on monitoring strategies and dosing protocols to optimize patient outcomes.

1. CURRENT ANTICOAGULATION PRACTICES

1.1 Initial Dosing
- Unfractionated heparin (UFH) remains the primary anticoagulant [1]
- ELSO guidelines recommend initial heparin infusion rate of 7.5-20.0 units/kg/h [2]
- Initial bolus typically 50-100 units per kilogram before cannulation

1.2 Monitoring Methods
Three main monitoring approaches are utilized:

a) Activated Clotting Time (ACT):
- Traditional target range: 180-220 seconds
- Recent evidence supports lower targets (140-160s) with comparable safety
- Limited by multiple confounding factors

b) Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT):
- Target range: 50-90 seconds
- More readily available and less expensive
- Better predictor of bleeding risk

c) Anti-Xa Levels:
- Target range: 0.3-0.7 IU/mL
- Most direct measure of heparin activity
- Better correlation with heparin dosing

2. RISK OPTIMIZATION STRATEGIES

2.1 Bleeding Risk Management
- Lower heparin targets for high bleeding risk patients
- Consider delaying anticoagulation initiation for 12 hours post-ECMO start
- Regular monitoring of hemoglobin and coagulation parameters

2.2 Thrombosis Prevention
- Maintain antithrombin levels >50-70%
- Regular circuit inspection
- Consider higher targets for VA-ECMO vs. VV-ECMO

3. MONITORING PROTOCOL RECOMMENDATIONS

The following tiered approach is suggested:

```latex
\text{Monitoring Frequency} = \begin{cases}
\text{Every 2-4 hours} & \text{Initial stabilization} \\
\text{Every 4-6 hours} & \text{Stable patients} \\
\text{Every 1-2 hours} & \text{Active bleeding/thrombosis}
\end{cases}
```

4. CONCLUSIONS
Evidence supports using anti-Xa levels as the primary monitoring tool, supplemented by aPTT for bleeding risk assessment [3]. Lower heparin targets may be appropriate for select patients, particularly those on VV-ECMO. Individualized protocols based on patient characteristics and ECMO configuration are essential.
