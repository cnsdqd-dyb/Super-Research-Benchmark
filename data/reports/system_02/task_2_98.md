1. Theoretical Framework

1.1 Quantum Mechanical Foundations
The Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI) of quantum mechanics suggests that with each quantum measurement, reality branches into multiple possibilities [1]. This creates a fundamental challenge to deterministic knowledge as it implies:

- Multiple concurrent realities existing in superposition
- Probabilistic rather than deterministic outcomes
- Observer-dependent state realization

1.2 Epistemological Implications
The epistemic framework is complicated by several factors:

- Information-theoretic constraints on knowledge acquisition
- The role of consciousness in quantum state realization 
- Limitations of classical logic in quantum contexts

2. Key Challenges to Deterministic Knowledge

2.1 The Measurement Problem
The quantum measurement problem creates an inherent uncertainty in knowledge claims, as expressed by:

\[ \Psi = \sum_i c_i |i\rangle \]

Where \(\Psi\) represents the quantum state and \(c_i\) are probability amplitudes [2].

2.2 Cognitive Access Limitations
Several barriers exist to cognitive access of parallel worlds:
- Inability to directly observe other branches
- Dependence on mathematical models rather than empirical observation
- Constraints of classical logic and language in describing quantum phenomena

3. Analysis of Epistemological Foundations

3.1 Information-Theoretic Perspective
The analysis suggests that quantum mechanics may be fundamentally about information rather than deterministic physical states. This implies:

- Knowledge claims are inherently probabilistic
- Classical deterministic frameworks may be emergent approximations
- Information processing rules may be more fundamental than physical laws

3.2 Implications for Deterministic Knowledge
The cognitive accessibility of parallel worlds appears to challenge deterministic knowledge in several ways:

- Questions the possibility of complete knowledge
- Suggests inherent limits to predictability
- Indicates fundamental role of probability in nature

4. Conclusions

The evidence suggests that the cognitive accessibility of parallel worlds does pose significant challenges to deterministic knowledge frameworks. Key findings include:

- Quantum mechanics implies fundamental limits to deterministic knowledge
- Information-theoretic approaches may be more appropriate than classical determinism
- Traditional epistemological frameworks may need revision to accommodate quantum phenomena
