TECHNICAL RESEARCH REPORT
Title: Optimization of Threshold Current Density in Quantum Dot Lasers Through Quantum Confinement and Surface State Effects

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report analyzes how quantum confinement mechanisms and surface state recombination processes affect the threshold current density optimization in quantum dot (QD) lasers, with implications for spectral purity and output power performance.

1. QUANTUM CONFINEMENT EFFECTS

The discrete energy states in QDs arising from three-dimensional quantum confinement lead to several key advantages:

- Delta function-like density of states (DoS) due to carrier momentum restriction
- Reduced transparency current density compared to quantum well lasers
- Decreased thermal energy distribution of electrons in quantized DoS
- Enhanced temperature insensitivity

The threshold current density ($J_{th}$) can be expressed as:

$J_{th} = J_{QD} + J_{OCL}$

where $J_{QD}$ is the QD component and $J_{OCL}$ is the optical confinement layer component [1].

2. SURFACE STATE RECOMBINATION

Surface state effects significantly impact threshold behavior through:

- Trap-assisted recombination at QD-barrier interfaces
- Competition between surface recombination and carrier injection
- Modified carrier dynamics affecting gain characteristics

The characteristic temperature ($T_0$) relationship is given by:

$\frac{1}{T_0} = \frac{J_{QD}}{J_{QD}+J_{OCL}}\frac{1}{T_0^{QD}} + \frac{J_{OCL}}{J_{QD}+J_{OCL}}\frac{1}{T_0^{OCL}}$ [2]

3. OPTIMIZATION STRATEGIES

Key approaches for threshold current density optimization include:

- P-type modulation doping to reduce gain saturation
- Engineered quantum dot size and density control
- Minimization of surface defect states
- Optimization of optical confinement layer design

4. PERFORMANCE METRICS

State-of-the-art QD lasers have achieved:
- Threshold current densities as low as 17 A/cm² under RT-CW operation
- Differential quantum efficiencies up to 88%
- Temperature-stable operation up to 220°C

CONCLUSIONS

The dual optimization of quantum confinement and surface state effects is critical for achieving low threshold current densities. P-doping and careful control of QD uniformity remain key technological approaches for realizing high-performance QD lasers.
