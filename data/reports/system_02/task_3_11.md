TECHNICAL RESEARCH REPORT
Title: Balancing Anti-inflammatory Efficacy and Adrenal Suppression Risk in Glucocorticoid Therapy

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report analyzes the critical balance between achieving therapeutic anti-inflammatory effects while minimizing hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression in glucocorticoid (GC) therapy. The analysis focuses on dosing strategies, monitoring approaches, and risk mitigation.

KEY FINDINGS

1. Dose-Response Relationship
- Therapeutic anti-inflammatory effects typically require doses exceeding physiological cortisol levels (>8 mg/m²/day hydrocortisone equivalent) [1]
- Risk of adrenal suppression increases significantly with:
  * Doses >5 mg/day prednisone equivalent
  * Treatment duration >3 weeks
  * Higher potency GC formulations

2. Risk Assessment Parameters
- Morning cortisol thresholds for monitoring:
  * >350 nmol/L: Normal HPA function
  * 275-350 nmol/L: Possible suppression
  * <100 nmol/L: High risk, requires endocrine consultation [2]

3. Mitigation Strategies
- Morning dosing schedule to align with natural cortisol rhythm
- Use lowest effective dose for shortest duration
- Regular dose re-evaluation
- Gradual tapering when discontinuing
- Route-specific considerations for systemic exposure

RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Dosing Protocol
- Initial dose based on condition severity
- Taper to <5 mg/day prednisone equivalent when possible
- Morning administration preferred

2. Monitoring Schedule
- Baseline morning cortisol before therapy
- Regular cortisol monitoring during treatment
- ACTH stimulation testing for high-risk cases

3. Risk Stratification
High Risk:
- Treatment >3 weeks
- Doses >20-30 mg prednisolone equivalent
- Multiple routes of administration
- Cushingoid features

CONCLUSION
Successful glucocorticoid therapy requires careful balance between therapeutic efficacy and HPA axis preservation. This can be achieved through evidence-based dosing protocols, systematic monitoring, and individualized risk assessment.

LIMITATIONS
- Variable individual responses to GC therapy
- Limited long-term comparative data
- Challenges in standardizing measurements across different GC formulations
