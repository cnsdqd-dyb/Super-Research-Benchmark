Title: Quantifying Subjective Experience Entropy in Information Geometric Frameworks of Integrated Information Theory

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report analyzes how subjective experience entropy is quantified within the information geometric framework of Integrated Information Theory (IIT). The analysis focuses on the mathematical formalization of consciousness measurement and its relationship to phenomenological properties.

METHODOLOGY
The analysis is based on examining the theoretical frameworks and mathematical measures proposed in IIT for quantifying conscious experience, particularly focusing on:
- Information geometric representations
- Entropy-based measures
- Integration metrics

KEY FINDINGS

1. Mathematical Framework
IIT quantifies consciousness through Φ (Phi), which measures integrated information as:

$\Phi = \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \{ I(X_0; X_1 | P) \}$

where P represents possible system partitions and I represents mutual information between past (X₀) and future (X₁) states [1].

2. Information Geometric Structure 
Subjective experiences are represented in a hyperspatial geometry called "qualia space", where:
- Each point represents a specific conscious experience
- Distances reflect phenomenological similarities
- The structure is characterized by integrated information measures [2]

3. Entropy Quantification
The entropy of subjective experience is quantified through:
- Cause-effect structures
- Irreducible information
- Integration metrics that capture system wholeness

LIMITATIONS

1. Computational Tractability
Computing full Φ values is computationally intractable for large systems, requiring approximations for practical applications.

2. Measurement Validation
Direct empirical validation remains challenging due to the subjective nature of consciousness and limitations in current measurement techniques.

CONCLUSIONS
While IIT provides a mathematical framework for quantifying subjective experience entropy through information geometric structures, significant challenges remain in computational implementation and empirical validation. The theory offers promising directions but requires further development of practical measurement approaches.
