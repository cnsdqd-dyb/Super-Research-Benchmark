TECHNICAL RESEARCH REPORT

Title: Air Circulation Filtration Technologies for Space Station Life Support: 
Analyzing the Trade-off Between Microbial Control and Energy Efficiency

Executive Summary:
This report evaluates the relative merits of High-Efficiency Particulate Air (HEPA) filtration versus Photocatalytic Oxidation (PCO) technology for space station air purification, focusing on the balance between microbial contamination control and energy consumption.

1. Current Technologies Overview

1.1 HEPA Filtration
- Mechanical filtration capturing particles down to 0.3 microns
- Proven technology currently used on ISS
- Regular maintenance requirement with filter replacement every 6-12 months
- Energy consumption increases as filters become loaded

1.2 PCO Technology 
- Originally developed by NASA for space missions
- Uses UV light with titanium dioxide catalyst
- Converts contaminants to H₂O and CO₂
- Maintenance-free operation
- Continuous operation without degradation

2. Performance Analysis

2.1 Microbial Control Effectiveness
HEPA:
- Excellent for particles ≥0.3μm
- Limited effectiveness for smaller bacteria (0.1-0.2μm)
- Cannot address molecular pollutants

PCO:
- Works at molecular level
- Effective against broad spectrum of contaminants
- Destroys rather than traps microorganisms
- Performance varies with environmental conditions

2.2 Energy Efficiency
The energy consumption relationship can be expressed as:

E_{HEPA} = P_0 + k\Delta P

Where:
- E_{HEPA} = Total energy consumption
- P_0 = Base power requirement
- k = System constant
- \Delta P = Pressure drop across filter

PCO systems maintain constant power draw:

E_{PCO} = P_{UV} + P_{fan}

Where:
- P_{UV} = UV lamp power
- P_{fan} = Air circulation power

3. Key Findings

3.1 Advantages of HEPA
- Proven reliability [1]
- No harmful byproducts
- Predictable performance
- Well-established testing standards

3.2 Advantages of PCO
- Lower maintenance requirements
- Consistent performance over time
- Comprehensive contaminant removal
- No filter replacement costs

4. Limitations

4.1 HEPA Limitations
- Increasing energy consumption over time
- Regular maintenance requirements
- Limited effectiveness against sub-micron particles
- Filter disposal considerations

4.2 PCO Limitations
- Potential formation of byproducts [2]
- Performance variation with environmental conditions
- Lack of standardized testing
- Reduced efficiency in scaled-up applications

5. Conclusions and Recommendations

Based on the analysis, a hybrid approach combining both technologies offers the optimal solution:

- Use HEPA as primary filtration for larger particles
- Implement PCO as secondary treatment for molecular contaminants
- Monitor and control PCO operation to minimize byproduct formation
- Implement smart control systems to optimize energy usage

This combination provides:
- Robust particulate removal
- Enhanced microbial control
- Optimized energy consumption
- Reduced maintenance requirements
