Title: Impact of Partition Coefficients on Economic Feasibility of Urban Mining Recovery

Executive Summary:
This report analyzes how partition coefficients in solvent extraction processes influence the economic viability of urban mining operations. The analysis considers both technical extraction efficiency and cost implications to determine overall feasibility.

1. Technical Analysis

1.1 Partition Coefficient Fundamentals
The partition coefficient (P) represents the ratio of concentrations between two immiscible phases at equilibrium [1]:

P = \frac{C_{oil}}{C_{water}}

Where:
- P > 1 indicates greater affinity for oil phase
- P < 1 indicates greater affinity for water phase
- Log P is commonly used to normalize values

1.2 Impact on Extraction Efficiency
Multiple extraction stages are typically required to achieve high recovery rates. The efficiency depends on:
- Partition coefficient value
- Volume ratio of phases
- Number of extraction stages

For a partition coefficient of 2 and equal phase volumes, theoretical recovery rates are [2]:
- First extraction: 67%
- Second extraction: 89% 
- Third extraction: 96%

2. Economic Implications

2.1 Cost Factors
Partition coefficients directly impact several cost elements:
- Solvent volume requirements
- Number of extraction stages needed
- Processing time
- Energy consumption
- Equipment sizing

2.2 Economic Viability Analysis
Urban mining becomes economically viable when:
- Recovery costs < Traditional mining costs
- Environmental externalities are considered
- High-value metals are targeted

Current data indicates [1]:
- Copper recovery: ~$3,000/ton via urban mining
- E-waste contains 100x more gold than ore
- Estimated $91 billion in recoverable value from 2022 e-waste

3. Conclusions

The research indicates that partition coefficients significantly influence economic feasibility through their impact on:
1. Required solvent volumes
2. Process efficiency
3. Operating costs
4. Capital equipment needs

While unfavorable partition coefficients can make recovery uneconomical when considering only direct costs, inclusion of environmental factors and targeting high-value metals can tip the balance toward economic viability [2].

Recommendations:
1. Optimize solvent selection based on partition coefficients
2. Focus on high-value metal recovery
3. Consider environmental costs in feasibility calculations
4. Invest in process optimization research
