Title: Mass Transfer Resistance Management in Fuel Cell Catalyst Layers: Balancing Power Density and Durability

Executive Summary:
This report analyzes the complex relationship between catalyst layer (CL) thickness and mass transfer resistance in fuel cell membrane electrode assemblies (MEAs), specifically examining how these parameters impact power density and long-term durability.

Key Findings:

1. Thickness-Performance Relationship
- Thicker catalyst layers generally increase mass transport resistance, leading to higher concentration losses [1]
- Optimal thickness exists as a trade-off between:
  * Active reaction area (improves with thickness)
  * Mass transport limitations (worsen with thickness)
  * Ohmic losses (increase with thickness)

2. Mass Transfer Considerations
- Mass transport resistance is affected by:
  * Porosity and pore size distribution
  * Water/gas transport balance
  * Ionomer content and distribution
- The pressure-independent mass transport resistance varies with:
  * Temperature
  * Relative humidity
  * Electrochemically active surface area [2]

3. Design Optimization
- Key parameters requiring optimization:
  * Catalyst loading
  * Ionomer volume fraction
  * Agglomerate porosity
  * Layer thickness

Mathematical Expression:
The relationship between mass transport resistance ($R_{mt}$) and catalyst layer thickness ($\delta$) can be expressed as:

$R_{mt} = \frac{\delta}{\epsilon D_{eff}}$

Where:
- $\epsilon$ = porosity
- $D_{eff}$ = effective diffusion coefficient

Recommendations:

1. Design Considerations:
- Implement bimodal pore size distribution
- Optimize ionomer-to-catalyst ratio
- Control compression during assembly

2. Performance Enhancement:
- Consider carbon nanofiber additives for improved water management
- Maintain balanced hydrophobic/hydrophilic properties
- Monitor and control operating temperature and humidity

3. Durability Measures:
- Implement proper mechanical support structures
- Avoid excessive compression during assembly
- Consider dynamic load effects on degradation

Limitations and Future Work:
- More research needed on dynamic loading conditions
- Further investigation of degradation mechanisms required
- Development of in-situ measurement techniques recommended
