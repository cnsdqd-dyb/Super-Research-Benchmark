Title: Optimization of Cardiovascular Drug Combinations for Blood Pressure Management

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report analyzes how cardiovascular drug combinations are optimized to achieve effective blood pressure reduction while maintaining adequate cardiac output. The analysis focuses on mechanism-based drug selection and personalized combination strategies.

KEY FINDINGS

1. Mechanism of Action & Combination Principles
- Multiple drug classes work through complementary mechanisms:
  * ACE inhibitors: Decrease angiotensin II production and increase bradykinin [2]
  * Calcium channel blockers (CCBs): Block L-type calcium channels for vasodilation [2]
  * Beta-blockers: Reduce heart rate and myocardial contractility [2]

2. Optimization Strategy
The optimal approach involves:
- Initial combination therapy for stage 2 hypertension using ≥2 agents [3]
- Single-pill combinations to improve adherence and BP control [3]
- Consideration of patient-specific factors including:
  * Comorbidities
  * Treatment response patterns
  * Side effect profiles
  * Physical activity tolerance [1]

3. Mathematical Modeling
Treatment effect estimation can be expressed as:

$TE(x) = E[Y(1)|X=x] - E[Y(0)|X=x]$

Where:
- $Y(1)$ represents outcome with treatment
- $Y(0)$ represents outcome without treatment
- $X$ represents patient characteristics [1]

4. Balancing Considerations
Key factors requiring balance include:
- Cardiac output maintenance (normal range 5-6 L/min at rest)
- Blood pressure reduction targets
- Risk of adverse effects
- Patient-specific response patterns

RECOMMENDATIONS

- Use deep learning models to predict optimal drug combinations based on patient characteristics [1]
- Consider genetic factors and biomarkers for precision medicine

2. Monitoring Strategy
- Regular assessment of:
  * Blood pressure response
  * Cardiac output

3. Combination Selection
