# How Destination Cognition is Formed Through the Interaction of Virtual Previews and On-Site Visits

## Abstract

This research report examines the psychological and cognitive mechanisms through which destination cognition is formed via the interplay between virtual previews and physical on-site visits. Drawing upon contemporary scholarship in tourism marketing, virtual reality (VR) applications, and consumer behavior, this analysis reveals that destination cognition emerges from a dynamic, multi-stage process wherein virtual experiences serve as cognitive scaffolding that shapes initial perceptions, emotional frameworks, and behavioral intentions, while subsequent on-site visits validate, refine, or transform these pre-formed cognitions. The synthesis of findings indicates that the formation of destination cognition operates through interconnected pathways involving mental imagery generation, affective response development, and the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework, with virtual previews functioning as both independent stimuli and moderating influences that ultimately determine the conversion of virtual interest into physical visitation. Understanding these mechanisms provides substantial theoretical and practical implications for destination marketers seeking to optimize the virtual-to-physical tourism conversion pipeline.

---

## 1. Introduction

The contemporary tourism landscape has witnessed a fundamental transformation in how potential visitors form cognitive representations of destinations. Traditional models of destination cognition relied primarily upon indirect information sources such as brochures, photographs, and word-of-mouth recommendations. However, the emergence of immersive virtual reality technologies has introduced unprecedented opportunities for individuals to construct detailed cognitive and affective images of destinations before physically visiting them [1]. This technological evolution raises critical questions regarding the psychological mechanisms through which virtual previews interact with subsequent on-site experiences to shape ultimate destination cognition.

Destination cognition, broadly defined as the sum of beliefs, ideas, and impressions that a person holds about a destination, serves as the foundational element upon which travel decisions are made [2]. When potential tourists have not yet traveled to a particular destination, this cognitive representation functions as a proxy for the actual experience and exerts substantial influence on decision-making processes. The significance of understanding how this cognition forms becomes particularly acute given the growing adoption of virtual tourism applications across the industry, with research indicating that virtual experiences can meaningfully influence psychological engagement and behavioral intentions toward physical destinations [3].

The theoretical framework underpinning this analysis draws upon multiple interconnected perspectives. The Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) model provides a structural understanding of how external factors—specifically virtual reality experiences—impact internal psychological states and subsequently influence behavioral outcomes [4]. Complementarily, destination image theory offers insights into the dual-dimensional nature of destination cognition, encompassing both cognitive evaluations (beliefs about destination attributes) and affective responses (emotional reactions to destinations) [2]. Mental imagery theory contributes an understanding of how virtual experiences generate cognitive simulations that prepare individuals for eventual on-site visitation [5].

This report synthesizes contemporary research findings to address the central question: Through what mechanisms does the interaction between virtual previews and on-site visits shape the formation of destination cognition? The analysis proceeds through systematic examination of the cognitive and affective pathways involved, the role of emotional responses in cognition formation, the quality dimensions of virtual experiences, and the dynamic interplay between virtual previews and physical visitation.

## 2. Theoretical Foundations of Destination Cognition Formation

### 2.1 The Dual-Dimensional Structure of Destination Image

Destination cognition does not emerge as a monolithic construct but rather develops through the interplay of two distinct but interrelated dimensions: cognitive image and affective image [2]. The cognitive dimension encompasses an individual's beliefs and knowledge about a destination's attributes, including physical characteristics, facilities, accessibility, and experiential offerings. The affective dimension, conversely, captures the emotional responses and feelings evoked by the destination, ranging from arousal and pleasure to relaxation or excitement.

Research has demonstrated that these two dimensions do not operate independently but rather engage in a hierarchical relationship wherein cognitive evaluations significantly and positively influence affective responses [2]. When potential tourists form positive beliefs about a destination's attributes—derived from virtual previews or other information sources—these cognitive assessments subsequently shape emotional reactions. This cascading effect is particularly relevant in the context of virtual previews, which can simultaneously provide factual information about destination attributes while eliciting emotional responses through immersive experiences.

The formation of destination cognition through virtual previews thus operates on both dimensions simultaneously. Virtual reality experiences allow users to acquire knowledge about destination features while simultaneously generating emotional engagement. This dual-channel processing distinguishes VR from traditional information sources, which typically emphasize either cognitive information provision or affective imagery but rarely achieve both simultaneously [1]. The result is a more holistic cognitive representation that integrates factual knowledge with emotional resonance.

### 2.2 The Stimulus-Organism-Response Framework in Virtual Tourism

The S-O-R framework provides a robust theoretical foundation for understanding how virtual previews influence destination cognition formation [4]. Within this model, external stimuli (S) activate internal psychological processes within the organism (O), which subsequently manifest in behavioral responses (R). In the context of virtual tourism, the stimuli consist of the virtual reality experiences themselves—the visual, auditory, and interactive elements that constitute the virtual preview.

The organism component encompasses the cognitive and emotional processing that occurs in response to these stimuli. Research has identified several key internal processes that mediate between virtual experience and behavioral intention, including mental imagery generation, emotional response development, and cognitive evaluation [4]. These processes transform the raw sensory input of the virtual experience into meaningful cognitive representations that inform subsequent decision-making.

The response component manifests primarily as visit intention—the individual's interest or eagerness to visit the destination to discover its offerings [4]. However, the S-O-R framework suggests that this behavioral response is not directly determined by the stimulus but rather filtered through the organism's internal processing. This theoretical perspective implies that the effectiveness of virtual previews in shaping destination cognition depends not merely on the technical quality of the presentation but on how individuals cognitively and emotionally process the experience.

### 2.3 Mental Imagery and Cognitive Construction

Mental imagery represents a critical mechanism through which virtual previews contribute to destination cognition formation [5]. When individuals engage with virtual tourism experiences, they generate mental images that serve multiple functions in cognitive construction. These images allow individuals to simulate potential experiences, construct narratives about their prospective visits, and develop anticipatory emotional responses.

Research has operationalized mental imagery in the context of virtual tourism through several dimensions [5]. The first dimension involves the construction of event sequences, wherein mental images create a series of imagined experiences in which the individual participates. The second dimension encompasses the feeling of presence, wherein mental imagery generates a sensation of actually experiencing the destination. The third dimension involves fantasy and aspiration, wherein virtual previews stimulate imagination about the opportunities the destination offers. The fourth dimension concerns narrative construction, wherein individuals develop stories about themselves in relation to the destination experience.

These mental imagery processes are not merely passive byproducts of virtual experiences but active cognitive operations that shape destination cognition. When potential tourists engage with virtual previews and generate vivid mental imagery, they are essentially rehearsing the actual visit experience in advance. This rehearsal function has important implications for cognition formation, as it allows individuals to develop preliminary cognitive frameworks that can be subsequently validated, refined, or transformed through on-site visitation.

## 3. Virtual Previews as Cognitive Scaffolding

### 3.1 Information Acquisition and Knowledge Structure Development

Virtual previews serve as powerful tools for information acquisition that contribute directly to the development of destination knowledge structures [1]. Unlike traditional marketing communications that present destination information through static images or textual descriptions, virtual reality enables potential tourists to actively explore and experience destinations from the comfort of their homes. This exploratory capability facilitates a more comprehensive understanding of destination attributes and their spatial relationships.

The knowledge acquired through virtual previews encompasses multiple categories of information relevant to destination cognition. Physical attribute knowledge includes understanding of landscape features, architectural characteristics, and natural surroundings. Experiential knowledge involves awareness of activities, attractions, and experiences available at the destination. Practical knowledge includes information about accessibility, facilities, and logistical considerations [1]. Together, these knowledge categories constitute the cognitive foundation upon which destination image is built.

Research indicates that the depth and breadth of knowledge acquired through virtual previews significantly influence the resulting cognitive representation [3]. More immersive and interactive VR experiences enable users to acquire richer knowledge structures, which in turn support more elaborate mental imagery and stronger visit intentions. This relationship suggests that the cognitive scaffolding function of virtual previews operates most effectively when the virtual experience provides comprehensive, accurate, and engaging information about the destination.

### 3.2 Emotional Bond Formation and Affective Commitment

Beyond cognitive knowledge acquisition, virtual previews play a crucial role in establishing emotional connections between potential tourists and destinations [1]. The immersive nature of VR technology enables users to experience emotional responses to virtual environments that parallel those evoked by actual physical environments. These emotional responses contribute to the affective dimension of destination cognition and can significantly influence subsequent behavioral intentions.

The formation of emotional bonds through virtual previews operates through several mechanisms. First, the sense of presence facilitated by high-quality VR experiences generates a feeling of actually being in the destination environment [6]. This presence experience creates conditions for authentic emotional engagement with the destination. Second, the interactive nature of virtual tourism allows users to engage with destination elements in personally meaningful ways, fostering a sense of connection. Third, the novelty and excitement of virtual exploration generate positive emotional associations that transfer to the destination itself.

Research has demonstrated that emotional bonds formed through virtual previews can persist beyond the immediate experience and influence subsequent decision-making [4]. When potential tourists develop positive emotional associations with a destination through virtual preview, these associations become integrated into the destination cognition structure and contribute to favorable behavioral intentions. The emotional dimension of destination cognition formed through virtual previews thus serves as a bridge between cognitive knowledge and behavioral response.

### 3.3 Risk Reduction and Confidence Building

Virtual previews contribute to destination cognition formation by reducing perceived risk and building confidence in travel decisions [1]. The tourism product is characterized by inherent uncertainty—consumers cannot physically experience the product before purchase, creating anxiety about whether expectations will be met. Virtual previews address this uncertainty by providing pre-purchase experience opportunities that allow potential tourists to develop realistic expectations.

The risk reduction function of virtual previews operates through expectation calibration. By experiencing a simulation of the destination before committing to physical visitation, potential tourists can develop accurate expectations about what the actual experience will entail. This calibration reduces the likelihood of disappointment while simultaneously building confidence in the decision to visit. The resulting cognitive representation is characterized by reduced uncertainty and increased predictive accuracy.

Furthermore, virtual previews can reduce anxiety associated with visiting unfamiliar places [1]. For potential tourists who have limited experience with a particular destination or type of travel, the unknown elements can create significant psychological barriers. Virtual previews make the unknown familiar, transforming abstract uncertainty into concrete anticipation. This transformation has important implications for destination cognition, as it allows individuals to incorporate the destination into their consideration set who might otherwise exclude it due to fear of the unknown.

## 4. The Mediating Role of Emotions in Cognition Formation

### 4.1 Emotional Responses to Virtual Tourism Experiences

Emotions emerge as a central mediating variable in the relationship between virtual previews and destination cognition formation [4]. According to the S-O-R framework, emotional responses constitute a primary component of the organism's internal processing that transforms external stimuli into behavioral outcomes. In the context of virtual tourism, the emotions generated by VR experiences serve as critical mediators between the virtual preview and the resulting cognitive and behavioral responses.

Research has identified several distinct emotional dimensions that characterize virtual tourism experiences [1]. Positive emotions, including interest, excitement, and anticipation, frequently emerge during and after engaging virtual previews. These positive emotional responses are associated with favorable evaluations of the destination and increased visit intentions. Additionally, virtual tourism experiences can generate emotional responses related to mental imagery, including vivid visualization of oneself at the destination and emotional engagement with imagined experiences.

The intensity and valence of emotional responses to virtual previews significantly influence the resulting destination cognition [4]. More emotionally engaging virtual experiences generate stronger affective associations with the destination, which in turn support more favorable cognitive evaluations. This relationship highlights the importance of emotional design in virtual tourism applications—creating VR experiences that elicit positive emotional responses not only enhances the immediate user experience but also contributes to more favorable destination cognition.

### 4.2 The Emotion-Cognition Interaction in Image Formation

The relationship between emotional responses and cognitive evaluations in destination image formation is characterized by bidirectional influence rather than simple linear causation [2]. While cognitive evaluations of destination attributes influence emotional responses, emotional states also shape how destination attributes are perceived and evaluated. This interaction is particularly relevant in the context of virtual previews, which simultaneously provide cognitive information and generate emotional responses.

When potential tourists engage with virtual previews, the emotional context established by the experience can color subsequent cognitive processing [4]. Positive emotional states generated during virtual preview engagement may lead to more favorable evaluations of destination attributes, while negative emotional states may trigger more critical cognitive assessment. This emotion-cognition interaction suggests that the affective dimension of destination cognition is not merely a consequence of cognitive evaluation but an active contributor to the overall image formation process.

Research has demonstrated that the emotional responses generated by virtual previews can persist beyond the immediate experience and influence subsequent cognitive processing during actual visitation [3]. When individuals arrive at a destination with pre-existing positive emotional associations formed through virtual preview, these associations can facilitate more positive on-site experiences and reinforce the initially formed destination cognition. Conversely, emotional associations formed through virtual preview that prove inconsistent with on-site experience can trigger cognitive revision of the destination image.

### 4.3 Interest, Anticipation, and Behavioral Engagement

The emotions of interest and anticipation generated by virtual previews play particularly important roles in destination cognition formation and subsequent behavioral engagement [1]. Interest represents a cognitive and emotional orientation toward the destination that motivates further information seeking and consideration. Anticipation represents the forward-looking emotional state characterized by eagerness to experience the destination firsthand.

Research indicates that positive virtual tourism experiences generate increased interest in the destination and heightened anticipation of potential visitation [1]. These emotional states contribute to the cognitive representation of the destination as an attractive and desirable travel option. Furthermore, interest and anticipation motivate behavioral engagement, including additional information search, travel planning, and ultimate visitation. The emotional dimension of destination cognition thus serves not only as a component of the cognitive representation but also as a driver of subsequent behavior.

The relationship between virtual preview-induced emotions and behavioral engagement is mediated by the strength and positivity of the resulting destination cognition [4]. When virtual previews generate strong positive emotions, these emotions become integrated into the destination cognition structure and contribute to the overall attractiveness of the destination as a travel option. The resulting cognitive representation thus carries both informational and motivational properties, providing knowledge about the destination while simultaneously driving interest in visitation.

## 5. Quality Dimensions of Virtual Experiences and Their Cognitive Impact

### 5.1 Immersion, Realism, and Interactive Engagement

The quality of virtual reality experiences significantly influences their effectiveness in shaping destination cognition [1]. Research has consistently demonstrated that more immersive, realistic, and interactive VR experiences generate more positive cognitive and emotional outcomes. These quality dimensions determine the extent to which virtual previews can successfully simulate actual destination experiences and contribute to accurate and favorable destination cognition formation.

Immersion refers to the degree to which the virtual experience creates a sense of being present in the simulated environment [6]. High-immersion VR experiences, characterized by comprehensive sensory stimulation and minimal awareness of the physical surroundings, generate stronger presence responses and more authentic emotional engagement. The sense of presence facilitated by immersive VR experiences allows potential tourists to develop cognitive representations that more closely approximate those that would be formed through actual visitation.

Realism refers to the accuracy with which the virtual environment represents the actual destination [1]. Realistic virtual previews enable potential tourists to develop accurate expectations about what the physical destination will offer, supporting cognitive representations that can be validated through on-site experience. Conversely, unrealistic virtual previews may generate cognitive representations that lead to disappointment when actual visitation fails to match expectations. The realism dimension thus has important implications for the accuracy of destination cognition formation.

Interactive engagement refers to the degree to which users can actively explore and manipulate the virtual environment [4]. Interactive VR experiences that allow users to select destinations, adjust visual or audio settings, and choose activities generate higher levels of user engagement and more positive cognitive outcomes. The interactive dimension supports active cognitive processing, as users must make decisions and explore options rather than passively receiving information.

### 5.2 Multisensory Stimulation and Cognitive Processing

The multisensory nature of advanced virtual reality experiences contributes to their effectiveness in shaping destination cognition [1]. While traditional VR applications have predominantly relied on audiovisual stimulation, emerging technologies enable more comprehensive sensory feedback including haptic, olfactory, and proprioceptive sensations. Multisensory VR experiences generate more complete cognitive representations of destinations by engaging multiple sensory modalities in the representation process.

Research indicates that multisensory stimulation enhances the sense of presence and emotional engagement in virtual environments [1]. When potential tourists can experience not only the visual and auditory aspects of a destination but also tactile sensations or environmental aromas, the resulting cognitive representation becomes more vivid and comprehensive. This enhanced representation supports more elaborate mental imagery and stronger emotional associations with the destination.

The cognitive processing advantages of multisensory VR extend beyond representation vividness to include better retention and recall of destination information [1]. Information presented through multiple sensory channels is encoded more deeply in memory and is more readily accessible during subsequent decision-making processes. This enhanced retention supports the persistence of destination cognition over time, maintaining the influence of virtual preview experiences on subsequent travel decisions.

### 5.3 Platform Quality and User Satisfaction

The technical quality of virtual tourism platforms significantly influences user satisfaction, which in turn affects destination cognition formation [3]. Research has demonstrated that user satisfaction with virtual tourism experiences is positively associated with favorable evaluations of the destination and increased visit intentions. The satisfaction dimension thus serves as a mediating variable between virtual preview quality and destination cognition outcomes.

Platform quality encompasses multiple dimensions including technical performance (frame rate, resolution, latency), content quality (accuracy, comprehensiveness, engagement value), and interface design (usability, accessibility, navigation) [3]. Each of these dimensions contributes to the overall user experience and influences the cognitive and emotional responses generated by the virtual preview. High-quality platforms that deliver seamless, engaging experiences generate more positive cognitive and emotional outcomes than platforms characterized by technical limitations or poor design.

The relationship between platform quality and destination cognition is mediated by both cognitive and affective evaluations [3]. On the cognitive dimension, high-quality platforms enable more effective information acquisition and knowledge structure development. On the affective dimension, satisfying virtual experiences generate positive emotional associations that contribute to favorable destination image formation. Together, these mediated effects highlight the importance of platform quality investment for destination marketers seeking to leverage virtual previews effectively.

## 6. The Dynamic Interplay Between Virtual Previews and On-Site Visits

### 6.1 Pre-Visit Cognition and Expectation Formation

The cognition formed through virtual previews prior to physical visitation establishes the baseline expectations against which actual experiences are evaluated [2]. This pre-visit cognition encompasses both cognitive beliefs about destination attributes and affective associations with the destination. When potential tourists arrive at a destination following virtual preview engagement, they bring with them cognitive frameworks that guide attention, interpretation, and evaluation of the on-site experience.

The influence of pre-visit cognition on on-site experience is substantial but not deterministic [2]. Cognitive frameworks established through virtual previews influence what aspects of the destination receive attention, how those aspects are interpreted, and what standards are applied in evaluation. However, actual on-site experiences can confirm, challenge, or contradict the pre-formed cognition, triggering cognitive adjustment processes that modify the destination image.

Research indicates that virtual previews are most effective in shaping accurate pre-visit cognition when they provide realistic representations of the destination [1]. Realistic virtual previews enable potential tourists to develop accurate expectations that can be validated through on-site experience, supporting cognitive consistency and reinforcing the initially formed destination image. Conversely, virtual previews that present idealized or inaccurate representations may generate expectations that are subsequently disconfirmed, leading to cognitive revision and potential disappointment.

### 6.2 On-Site Experience and Cognitive Validation or Revision

The on-site visit experience serves as the ultimate validation mechanism for cognition formed through virtual previews [2]. When actual experiences align with pre-formed expectations, the destination cognition is reinforced and strengthened. When actual experiences diverge from expectations—either positively or negatively—cognitive revision processes are triggered that modify the destination image.

Positive disconfirmation, wherein actual experiences exceed expectations formed through virtual preview, generates particularly favorable cognitive and emotional outcomes [2]. When potential tourists discover that the destination exceeds their pre-formed expectations, they develop enhanced satisfaction and stronger positive associations. This positive disconfirmation can transform adequate destination cognition into enthusiastic destination commitment, significantly influencing subsequent behavioral intentions including recommendation behavior and revisitation.

Negative disconfirmation, wherein actual experiences fall short of virtual preview expectations, triggers cognitive revision processes that can substantially alter destination cognition [2]. The extent of cognitive revision depends on the magnitude of the discrepancy and the importance of the unfulfilled expectations. Minor shortfalls may result in modest cognitive adjustment, while significant negative disconfirmation can fundamentally transform the destination image from positive to negative.

### 6.3 Post-Visit Integration and Memory Formation

Following on-site visitation, the virtual preview experience and the physical experience become integrated within the individual's cognitive structure [3]. This integration process combines the knowledge and emotional associations formed through virtual preview with the direct experience of actual visitation, creating a comprehensive cognitive representation that encompasses both indirect and direct knowledge of the destination.

The integration of virtual and physical experiences has important implications for destination memory and subsequent decision-making [3]. When virtual previews and on-site experiences are consistent, they mutually reinforce each other in memory, creating robust cognitive representations that persist over time. When virtual and physical experiences diverge, the integration process may involve selective retention of consistent elements and reinterpretation of inconsistent elements to reduce cognitive dissonance.

Research indicates that virtual previews can serve post-visit functions as well, acting as reminders of enjoyable experiences and supporting continued positive evaluation of the destination [4]. This post-visit function extends the influence of virtual previews beyond the pre-travel decision-making phase into the post-travel evaluation and memory phase, highlighting the ongoing role of virtual experiences in destination cognition maintenance.

## 7. Implications for Destination Cognition Formation

### 7.1 The Integrated Model of Virtual-Physical Cognition Formation

Synthesizing the research findings presented in this analysis, an integrated model of destination cognition formation through virtual preview and on-site visit interaction emerges. This model recognizes that destination cognition is not a static construct formed through a single information exposure but rather a dynamic representation that evolves through multiple stages of cognitive processing and experiential validation.

The model posits that virtual previews serve as the initial cognitive scaffolding upon which destination representations are built. Through information acquisition, emotional bond formation, and risk reduction, virtual previews establish preliminary cognitive and affective structures that constitute the initial destination cognition. This initial cognition is characterized by varying degrees of accuracy, completeness, and emotional association depending on the quality and nature of the virtual preview experience.

Subsequent on-site visitation serves as the validation and refinement mechanism that transforms preliminary cognition into mature destination understanding. The alignment or misalignment between virtual preview expectations and on-site experience determines the nature of cognitive adjustment. Consistent experiences reinforce initial cognition, while divergent experiences trigger revision processes that may either enhance or diminish the initial cognitive representation.

### 7.2 Factors Moderating the Virtual-Physical Cognition Relationship

Several factors moderate the relationship between virtual previews and destination cognition formation, influencing the extent to which virtual experiences shape ultimate cognitive representations [2]. Understanding these moderating factors is essential for optimizing virtual preview effectiveness and managing expectations regarding their cognitive impact.

Psychological distance represents a significant moderating variable, with research indicating that greater psychological distance between the potential tourist and the destination weakens the relationship between virtual preview engagement and subsequent visit intention [2]. When destinations are perceived as distant—either physically, psychologically, or temporally—the motivational impact of virtual previews may be attenuated. This finding suggests that virtual previews may be most effective for destinations that potential tourists perceive as accessible and achievable.

Prior knowledge and experience with the destination also moderates virtual preview effectiveness [5]. Individuals with greater prior knowledge may process virtual preview information differently, integrating new information with existing cognitive structures rather than forming entirely new representations. This prior knowledge integration may result in more nuanced but less dramatically affected destination cognition compared to individuals with limited prior knowledge.

### 7.3 Practical Applications for Destination Marketing

The findings of this analysis have significant implications for destination marketers seeking to leverage virtual previews as tools for cognition formation and visit motivation [3]. Effective application of these findings requires attention to multiple dimensions of virtual preview design and deployment.

First, destination marketers should prioritize realism and accuracy in virtual preview development [1]. While it may be tempting to present idealized representations that maximize immediate appeal, such presentations risk generating expectations that will be negatively disconfirmed during on-site visitation. Accurate virtual previews that set appropriate expectations support cognitive consistency and long-term destination satisfaction.

Second, emotional engagement should be a central design consideration in virtual preview development [4]. Virtual experiences that generate positive emotional responses not only enhance immediate user satisfaction but also contribute to favorable affective dimensions of destination cognition. Emotional design elements including narrative engagement, aesthetic appeal, and interactive personalization can enhance the emotional impact of virtual previews.

Third, virtual previews should be positioned as complementary to rather than substitutes for physical visitation [3]. The most effective virtual tourism applications inspire interest and anticipation for actual visitation rather than positioning virtual experience as a replacement for physical travel. This positioning supports the conversion of virtual engagement into physical visitation while avoiding the risk that virtual experience satisfies curiosity that might otherwise motivate actual travel.

## 8. Conclusion

The formation of destination cognition through the interaction of virtual previews and on-site visits represents a complex psychological process that integrates cognitive information processing, emotional response generation, and experiential validation. This analysis has revealed that virtual previews function as cognitive scaffolding, providing the initial knowledge structures and emotional associations upon which destination representations are built. The quality, realism, and emotional engagement potential of virtual previews significantly influence their effectiveness in shaping favorable destination cognition.

The interaction between virtual and physical experiences follows predictable patterns described by the S-O-R framework and destination image theory. Virtual previews serve as stimuli that activate internal cognitive and emotional processing, generating responses that manifest as visit intentions and preliminary destination evaluations. Subsequent on-site visits validate or revise these preliminary cognitions, with the degree of alignment between virtual expectations and physical experience determining the nature of cognitive adjustment.

The implications of these findings extend beyond theoretical understanding to practical application in destination marketing. Virtual previews represent powerful tools for destination cognition formation when designed and deployed effectively. However, their effectiveness depends upon attention to quality dimensions including immersion, realism, and emotional engagement, as well as positioning that emphasizes the complementary relationship between virtual preview and physical visitation.

Future research should continue to explore the dynamic interplay between virtual and physical experiences in destination cognition formation. The rapid advancement of virtual reality technologies, including multisensory stimulation and artificial intelligence personalization, creates new opportunities for enhancing virtual preview effectiveness. Understanding how these technological developments can be leveraged to optimize destination cognition formation while maintaining alignment between virtual expectations and physical reality represents a critical frontier for both scholarly inquiry and practical application.
