# How Are Online Reviews and Past Memories Integrated in Destination Decision-Making?

## Abstract

This research report examines the complex interplay between online reviews and autobiographical memories in the formation of destination decisions. Drawing upon contemporary scholarship in tourism psychology, consumer behavior, and narrative transportation theory, this analysis reveals that online reviews serve as powerful catalysts that activate and reconstruct past travel memories while simultaneously shaping future travel intentions. The integration of these two information sources operates through multiple psychological mechanisms, including narrative transportation, nostalgic emotion activation, and the functional approach to autobiographical memory. Findings indicate that travelers do not process online reviews in isolation but rather engage in a dynamic cognitive process where prior experiences and anticipated emotional outcomes are continuously evaluated against peer-generated content. This synthesis provides theoretical and practical implications for destination marketing organizations seeking to understand and leverage the intricate relationship between digital word-of-mouth and the memory-based decision-making processes of contemporary travelers.

## 1. Introduction

The contemporary tourism landscape is characterized by an unprecedented abundance of information available to prospective travelers. According to recent estimates, approximately 87 percent of international travelers utilize the Internet for planning their trips, with 43 percent actively reading reviews posted by other travelers [1]. This shift in information-seeking behavior has fundamentally transformed the destination decision-making process, introducing new complexities in how individuals evaluate potential travel experiences. However, this digital information ecosystem does not exist in isolation from the travelers' psychological makeup. Rather, online reviews must be processed through the lens of individual cognitive frameworks, among which autobiographical memory occupies a central position.

The integration of online reviews and past memories in destination decision-making represents a convergence of two critical information sources that have traditionally been studied separately. Online reviews provide vicarious experiences and peer-generated evaluations of destinations, hotels, and travel services, while autobiographical memories offer individuals access to their own experiential knowledge base. The question of how these two information streams are combined, weighted, and integrated in the formation of travel decisions remains insufficiently understood despite its theoretical and practical significance.

This research report addresses this gap by synthesizing findings from multiple scholarly sources to develop a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms through which online reviews and past memories jointly influence destination choice. The analysis draws upon the functional approach to autobiographical memory, narrative transportation theory, and emerging research on nostalgia tourism to construct an integrated framework for understanding this phenomenon. The subsequent sections examine the theoretical foundations of memory-based decision-making in tourism, the role of online reviews as information sources, the psychological mechanisms of integration, and the practical implications for destination marketing.

## 2. Theoretical Foundations of Memory in Tourism Decision-Making

### 2.1 Autobiographical Memory and Tourism Experiences

Autobiographical memory constitutes the most prominent memory type for tourism experiences, representing the most explicit form of autobiographical knowledge where individuals remember highly detailed information about specific events or times [2]. This form of memory plays a multifaceted role in tourism, serving not merely as a repository of past experiences but as an active cognitive resource that influences future behavior. The application of autobiographical memory concepts to tourism has enabled researchers to further understand the role of travel memories in well-being, explore the emotions associated with tourism, and examine how individuals learn from and utilize long-term tourism memories [3].

The initial forays into autobiographical memory research within tourism focused on Memorable Tourism Experiences (MTEs), which are defined as tourism experiences that are positively remembered and recalled after the event has occurred [2]. This concept has attracted substantial attention because of the clear role that memorable experiences play in determining future choices and influencing word-of-mouth recommendations. The recalled memories of tourism experiences emerge as important influencers when tourists make decisions about returning to particular destinations where they have registered personal or collective memories [2]. This memory-based evaluation process creates a feedback loop where past experiences shape future intentions, which in turn generate new experiences that become incorporated into the memory system.

The functional approach to autobiographical memory provides a theoretical framework for understanding how travel memories serve individuals in their daily lives and decision-making processes. This approach suggests that autobiographical memory serves multiple functions, including the directive function, which guides behavior and decision-making based on past experiences [3]. Research has begun to investigate whether travel memories can serve all three identified functions of autobiographical memory and whether each function possesses subdimensions that may differentially influence destination decisions [3]. The context of study abroad and educational tourism has proven particularly valuable for illustrating how travel-related memories are used and integrated into life trajectories in accordance with the autobiographical memory functional approach.

### 2.2 The Cognitive Architecture of Destination Decisions

Destination decision-making represents a complex cognitive process that involves the evaluation of multiple alternatives against individual preferences, constraints, and goals. Travelers do not follow a standard mode of information processing but are instead more likely to make travel decisions based on their subjective standards and contextual factors such as interests and trip purpose [4]. This subjective approach to decision-making means that the interpretation and integration of information, whether derived from past experiences or online reviews, is filtered through individual cognitive frameworks.

The Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) paradigm has been applied to understand how external stimuli, including online review content, influence travelers' cognitive and affective states, thereby affecting their behavioral intentions [5]. This framework recognizes that information processing involves not merely the cognitive evaluation of content but also emotional responses that can facilitate or inhibit decision-making. The skyrocketing importance of technology has channeled researchers' attention toward examining how technological aspects of tourism products and services function as external stimuli that trigger internal cognitive and affective responses [5].

The cognitive architecture underlying destination decisions incorporates both explicit memory traces of past experiences and the imaginative projection of future experiences based on available information. When individuals encounter online reviews, they engage in a process of mental simulation that involves reconstructing or constructing images of potential experiences. This simulation process is influenced by the richness and vividness of the review content, the individual's capacity for mental imagery, and the alignment between review content and the individual's existing knowledge structures and preferences.

## 3. Online Reviews as Information Sources in Tourism

### 3.1 The Evolution of Consumer-Generated Content in Travel Planning

Online reviews have become an important information source that allows consumers to search for detailed and reliable information by sharing past consumption experiences [1]. The proliferation of user-generated content platforms has democratized the information landscape of travel planning, enabling prospective tourists to access authentic accounts from individuals who have previously experienced the products and services under consideration. Consumer reviews are particularly important in purchasing experiential goods such as destinations, hotels, and restaurants because people find it difficult to assess the quality of intangible products before experiencing them directly [1].

The characteristics that make online reviews impactful in travel decision-making have been the subject of substantial research. Studies have identified that certain attributes of reviews are more likely to influence reader perceptions and decisions. The perceived usefulness of online reviews has been found to significantly impact consumer choice, with travelers developing sophisticated heuristics for evaluating the credibility, relevance, and applicability of review content to their own situations [4]. The time aspect of how long reviews have existed and how much has changed since they were written, along with the respectful way personnel respond to reviews, can influence how reviews affect decision-making [6].

The role of prior experience in the perception of tourism destinations in user-generated content has received particular attention. Research has demonstrated that individuals with different levels of prior experience process online reviews differently, with those possessing relevant prior knowledge being better able to integrate new information with existing cognitive structures [7]. This finding has important implications for understanding how past memories and online reviews interact in the decision-making process, as individuals with rich travel memories may process and integrate online information differently than those with limited experiential foundations.

### 3.2 Narrative Transportation and Virtual Experience Formation

The process by which people obtain virtual experiences through online reviews can be conceptualized through the lens of narrative transportation theory [4]. Narrative transportation provides a compelling explanation of how online reviews help tourists form mental images and gain knowledge of locations even if they have not previously visited them. Online travel reviews capture storytellers' accounts of destinations or travel products, creating a storytelling experience that enables the audience to travel mentally and emotionally during story reception [4].

When online reviews represent a destination, their content enables the audience to experience touristic activities vicariously through the act of reading [4]. This virtual experience formation process involves the construction of mental imagery that approximates the sensory and emotional dimensions of actual travel experiences. The effectiveness of this process depends on multiple factors, including the narrative quality of the review, the vividness of descriptive content, and the reader's capacity for imaginative involvement. Research has demonstrated that pictorial contents in online reviews affect online review platforms and users' cognitive and affective states, thereby influencing their behavioral intentions [5].

The concept of narrative transportation helps explain why some online reviews are more influential than others in shaping destination decisions. Reviews that effectively transport readers into the experiential world of the destination are more likely to generate strong cognitive and affective responses that translate into behavioral intentions. This transportation process is not merely a passive reception of information but an active construction of mental simulations that engage the reader's imagination and emotional capacities.

## 4. The Integration of Online Reviews and Past Memories

### 4.1 Memory Activation and Reconstruction Processes

The integration of online reviews and past memories in destination decision-making involves complex cognitive processes of activation, reconstruction, and synthesis. When individuals encounter online reviews, the content often triggers associations with their own past experiences, whether similar or dissimilar to the experiences described in the reviews. This activation process can bring to consciousness memories that may have been dormant, creating opportunities for comparative evaluation between one's own experiences and those described by others.

The functional approach to autobiographical memory suggests that individuals actively use their memories to serve current goals and decision-making needs [3]. When evaluating potential destinations, individuals may spontaneously retrieve relevant travel memories that provide reference points for assessing the claims and descriptions found in online reviews. This retrieval process is influenced by the accessibility of memories, which is affected by factors such as recency, emotional intensity, and the frequency with which memories have been recalled and rehearsed.

The reconstruction of memories during the decision-making process is subject to cognitive biases that can affect the accuracy and utility of memory-based evaluations. The peak-end rule, a well-documented cognitive bias, suggests that individuals disproportionately remember the most intense moments and the endings of experiences rather than the complete temporal profile of the experience [2]. This bias can influence how past travel memories are evaluated and compared against online review content, potentially creating systematic discrepancies between actual experiences and remembered experiences that affect decision-making.

### 4.2 Comparative Evaluation and Information Synthesis

The destination decision-making process involves the comparative evaluation of multiple information sources, including past memories and online reviews. Individuals develop strategies for weighing and integrating these different types of information based on their perceived credibility, relevance, and applicability to the decision at hand. Research indicates that travelers use online reviews as aids in their decision-making process, with their experiences since youth having colored their ways of thinking about traveling [6].

The synthesis of information from past memories and online reviews involves the creation of mental representations that integrate elements from both sources. When online reviews describe experiences that align with an individual's past memories, the reviews may serve to reinforce and validate existing knowledge structures. Conversely, when reviews describe experiences that diverge from past memories, individuals must reconcile the discrepancy, either by discounting the relevance of their past experiences, questioning the accuracy of the reviews, or revising their understanding of the destination in question.

The role of imagination in this integration process should not be underestimated. With help from their imagination, earlier memories, external factors, and personal choices, individuals construct comprehensive representations of potential travel experiences that draw upon multiple information sources [6]. This imaginative process allows individuals to project themselves into future experiences, using both remembered past experiences and vicarious experiences derived from online reviews as原材料 for mental simulation.

## 5. Nostalgia as a Mediating Mechanism

### 5.1 The Multifaceted Role of Nostalgia in Tourism Decisions

Nostalgia has emerged as a critical concept for understanding how past memories influence current destination decisions and how online reviews can activate nostalgic responses that shape travel intentions. Nostalgia is defined as a common emotion whereby individuals long for the past and has been identified as having multifaceted roles in tourism [8]. It functions as a push motive increasing an individual's desire to travel, as an emotion experienced during trips, as a sentimentality by recalling past travel experiences, and as an effective appeal for marketing destinations [8].

Tourists may experience nostalgia throughout the entire journey: evoked nostalgia associated with visit intention, on-site experience arousing nostalgic emotions, and nostalgia activating the core process of a memorable experience for revisitation [8]. When directed toward specific tourist destinations, nostalgia can significantly impact how visitors perceive and interact with those places [9]. This emotional dimension adds a layer of complexity to the integration of online reviews and past memories, as nostalgic responses can amplify or attenuate the influence of both information sources.

The tourism literature adopts two primary conceptualizations of nostalgia that are relevant to understanding its role in destination decision-making. Concerning stimuli that can inspire nostalgia, the literature focuses on the reflective properties of emotion and conceptualizes nostalgia as a preference toward people, places, or things from the past [8]. Alternatively, tourism literature conceptualizes nostalgia as an emotion that arises from reflection on one's past, emphasizing its implications in behavioral outcomes. Both conceptualizations contribute to understanding how nostalgia mediates the relationship between memory, online information, and destination choice.

### 5.2 Types of Nostalgia and Their Differential Effects

Research has distinguished between different types of nostalgia that may have differential effects on destination decision-making. Following established typologies, nostalgia can be divided into three types: destination nostalgia, nostalgia for past lives, and nostalgia for social activities [5]. Destination nostalgia refers to a traveler's emotional and mental state related to their previous travel experience in the destination and is closely related to nostalgia for the environment [5]. Nostalgia for past lives indicates a traveler's psychological state in which they want to relive their past lives and is highly associated with the anticipatory theme of nostalgia [5]. Nostalgia for social activities refers to an emotional state based on memories about travel experiences with others [5].

These different types of nostalgia can be activated by online reviews in different ways. Reviews that emphasize the destination environment may trigger destination-specific nostalgia, while reviews that describe social interactions and shared experiences may activate nostalgia for social activities. The anticipatory dimension of nostalgia for past lives may be particularly relevant to the decision-making process, as individuals imagine themselves recreating or reliving aspects of their previous experiences through future travel.

The positive impact of nostalgia for a destination on travelers' intention to visit a similar destination suggests that travelers seek a similar atmosphere or mood to recall their memorable travel experience due to emotional states stimulated by various stimuli, including virtual experiences derived from online reviews [5]. This finding indicates that nostalgia can serve as a bridge between past memories and future intentions, with online reviews potentially triggering nostalgic responses that enhance the attractiveness of destinations associated with positive past experiences.

### 5.3 Nostalgia Tourism and Its Determinants

Nostalgia tourism focuses on experiences that evoke past times, generating positive emotions by remembering or reliving historical, cultural, or personal moments [9]. Within this domain, several determinants of nostalgia tourism can be identified, encompassing emotional, psychological, sociocultural, and contextual factors that act as key drivers guiding the choice of destinations, activities, and consumption patterns [9]. Understanding these determinants provides insight into how past memories and online reviews interact to shape destination decisions for travelers seeking nostalgic experiences.

Primarily, nostalgia acts as a powerful catalyst, evoking emotional memories and a longing for emblematic moments or places [9]. This is evident in the attraction to destinations that evoke earlier eras, whether through cultural heritage, ancestral traditions, or popular iconography. Additionally, the quest for authenticity plays a crucial role, prompting tourists to immerse themselves in genuine and unconventional experiences [9]. Historical sites, preservation of cultural traditions, ancient architecture, and activities that evoke memories are key elements that attract travelers in search of emotional and cultural reconnection [9].

The importance of these determinants lies in their ability to preserve collective identity, transmit cultural legacies, and foster a sense of belonging, allowing people to better understand their history [9]. Online reviews can serve as vehicles for communicating these nostalgic elements, with travelers sharing accounts of how destinations have evoked memories and emotional responses. When prospective travelers encounter such reviews, the content can activate their own nostalgic associations and influence their destination evaluations and choices.

## 6. The Mediating Role of Destination Trust and Travel Intention

### 6.1 Formation of Destination Trust Through Information Integration

The integration of online reviews and past memories plays a significant role in the formation of destination trust, which in turn influences travel intention. Research has demonstrated that online reviews significantly impact destination trust and travel intention, with online review trustworthiness serving as a moderating factor in this relationship [10]. The affective experience, which encompasses emotions related to travel and conveyed through online travel reviews, is hypothesized to positively influence travel intention through the richness of affective experiences that can positively affect individuals' attitudes and behaviors [10].

The process by which trust is formed involves the evaluation of multiple information sources, including the consistency between online review content and the individual's own memories and expectations. When online reviews align with past experiences or match the expectations derived from autobiographical memories, trust in the destination is likely to be strengthened. Conversely, discrepancies between review content and memory-based expectations may trigger skepticism and reduce trust, particularly if the reviews describe experiences that contradict the individual's existing knowledge.

The authenticity of online reviews has been identified as an important factor influencing trust formation among travelers [11]. Research examining the influence of authenticity of online reviews on trust formation has demonstrated that reviews perceived as genuine and trustworthy are more likely to positively influence destination evaluations and travel intentions. This finding suggests that individuals utilize their past experiences and memory-based knowledge as benchmarks for evaluating the authenticity and credibility of online review content.

### 6.2 The Pathway from Information to Intention

The relationship between online reviews, past memories, and travel intention is mediated by multiple psychological processes, including cognitive evaluation, emotional response, and behavioral intention formation. The Stimulus-Organism-Response paradigm provides a framework for understanding how online review content serves as a stimulus that triggers internal cognitive and affective responses, which in turn influence behavioral outcomes such as destination choice and travel intention [5].

Affective experiences related to a destination can positively influence tourists' satisfaction, which in turn affects their intention to recommend the destination [10]. Given that tourists seek emotionally enriching experiences, the affective experiences documented in online reviews can provide valuable insights into the emotional rewards travelers might anticipate from their trips, thus influencing their travel intention [10]. The reading of online reviews that describe positive emotional experiences can generate anticipatory emotional responses that enhance the attractiveness of the destination and increase the likelihood of travel.

The integration of past memories into this pathway adds another layer of complexity. When online reviews trigger memories of past travel experiences, the emotional associations of those memories influence how the review content is evaluated and how the destination is perceived. Positive memories associated with similar destinations or experiences can enhance the positive evaluation of review content, while negative memories may trigger caution or avoidance. This memory-based filtering process shapes the ultimate impact of online reviews on travel intention.

## 7. Practical Implications and Applications

### 7.1 Implications for Destination Marketing Organizations

The integration of online reviews and past memories in destination decision-making has significant implications for destination marketing organizations seeking to influence traveler behavior. Understanding that travelers integrate peer-generated content with their autobiographical memories suggests that marketing strategies should address both the informational and emotional dimensions of destination communication. Content that resonates with travelers' existing memories and evokes nostalgic responses is likely to be more effective than purely informational content.

The role of narrative transportation in processing online review content suggests that destination marketing organizations should encourage the creation and dissemination of narrative-rich reviews that enable prospective travelers to mentally and emotionally experience the destination. Strategies that prompt travelers to share detailed, vivid accounts of their experiences are likely to generate more influential content than simple rating-based reviews. The emphasis on authentic, emotionally resonant storytelling aligns with the finding that review authenticity influences trust formation.

The different types of nostalgia identified in the literature suggest that marketing strategies can be tailored to specific nostalgic motivations. Destinations with heritage elements can emphasize connections to the past, while those offering social experiences can highlight the relational dimensions of travel. Understanding which types of nostalgia are most relevant to target market segments allows for more effective message targeting and content development.

### 7.2 Strategies for Leveraging Memory-Review Integration

Effective strategies for leveraging the integration of online reviews and past memories should recognize that travelers use online reviews as help in their decision-making process, with their experiences since youth having colored their ways of thinking about traveling [6]. Marketing communications that acknowledge and build upon travelers' existing knowledge and memories are likely to be more effective than those that attempt to create entirely new frameworks for understanding the destination.

The finding that online reviews can influence travelers to change their destination choices suggests that destination marketing organizations should actively monitor and respond to review content [6]. Strategies that address negative reviews promptly and professionally can mitigate their impact on destination perception, while positive engagement with reviews can enhance the perceived authenticity and trustworthiness of the destination. The time aspect of reviews and the responsiveness of destination representatives to review content are factors that influence how reviews affect decision-making [6].

Encouraging travelers to share their experiences in ways that facilitate memory activation and nostalgic response can enhance the influence of user-generated content on destination decisions. Prompts that encourage travelers to describe how destinations reminded them of past experiences, evoked memories, or created new memorable moments are likely to generate content that resonates with prospective travelers' own memory systems and influences their decision-making processes.
