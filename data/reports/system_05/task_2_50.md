**In ESG Portfolios, Can Factor Model Construction Balance Risk Quantification and Responsibility Standardization, or Does Weighting in Multi-Criteria Analysis Introduce Subjective Bias?**

## Abstract

The integration of Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) factors into portfolio management has become increasingly important for investors seeking to balance financial returns with social responsibility. However, the construction of ESG portfolios using factor models raises questions about the balance between risk quantification and responsibility standardization. This report investigates whether factor model construction can balance these competing objectives or if weighting in multi-criteria analysis introduces subjective bias.

## Introduction

ESG investing has grown significantly in recent years, with investors seeking to align their investments with their values and promote sustainable development [1]. ESG factors can be integrated into portfolio management using various approaches, including factor models [2]. However, the construction of ESG portfolios using factor models requires careful consideration of the trade-offs between risk quantification and responsibility standardization.

## Literature Review

Research has shown that ESG factors can have a positive impact on financial performance [3], but the relationship between ESG and financial returns is complex and influenced by various factors, including geography, market capitalization, and industry sector [4]. The use of factor models to construct ESG portfolios can help to quantify ESG risks and opportunities, but the choice of factors and weights can introduce subjective bias [5].

## Methodology

This report uses a multi-criteria analysis approach to investigate the balance between risk quantification and responsibility standardization in ESG portfolio construction. The analysis is based on a review of existing research and industry reports, including those from S&P Global [6], Amundi [7], and Robeco [8].

## Results

The results of the analysis suggest that factor model construction can balance risk quantification and responsibility standardization in ESG portfolios, but the choice of factors and weights is critical. The use of objective weighting methods, such as entropy-weighted methods [9], can help to minimize subjective bias. However, the results also highlight the importance of considering the investor's ESG objectives and preferences in the portfolio construction process [10].

## Discussion

The findings of this report have implications for investors and portfolio managers seeking to construct ESG portfolios that balance risk quantification and responsibility standardization. The use of factor models can provide a framework for integrating ESG factors into portfolio management, but careful consideration must be given to the choice of factors and weights. The report also highlights the importance of considering the investor's ESG objectives and preferences in the portfolio construction process.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, factor model construction can balance risk quantification and responsibility standardization in ESG portfolios, but the choice of factors and weights is critical. The use of objective weighting methods and consideration of the investor's ESG objectives and preferences can help to minimize subjective bias and ensure that ESG portfolios are constructed in a way that aligns with the investor's values and promotes sustainable development.

## Mathematical Formulation

Let $x_i$ be the weight of the $i^{th}$ ESG factor in the portfolio, and let $y_i$ be the score of the $i^{th}$ ESG factor. The portfolio's overall ESG score can be calculated as:

$$ESG_{portfolio} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \cdot y_i$$

where $n$ is the number of ESG factors.

The portfolio's risk can be quantified using a risk model, such as the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM):

$$\sigma_{portfolio} = \beta_{portfolio} \cdot \sigma_{market}$$

where $\beta_{portfolio}$ is the portfolio's beta, and $\sigma_{market}$ is the market's standard deviation.

The portfolio's return can be calculated as:

$$R_{portfolio} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \cdot R_i$$

where $R_i$ is the return of the $i^{th}$ asset.

The portfolio's Sharpe ratio can be calculated as:

$$Sharpe_{portfolio} = \frac{R_{portfolio} - R_f}{\sigma_{portfolio}}$$

where $R_f$ is the risk-free rate.

The ESG quotient can be calculated as:

$$ESG_{quotient} = \frac{ESG_{portfolio}}{ESG_{benchmark}}$$

where $ESG_{benchmark}$ is the ESG score of the benchmark portfolio.

The R3 metric can be calculated as:

$$R3 = \lambda \cdot ESG_{quotient} + (1 - \lambda) \cdot Sharpe_{portfolio}$$

where $\lambda$ is the scaling factor.
