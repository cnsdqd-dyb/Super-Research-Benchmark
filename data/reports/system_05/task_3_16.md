**Title:** The Convexity of Responsibility Attribution Functions in AI Decision Spaces: A Study on the Stability Boundary of Moral Judgments

**Abstract:**
The increasing presence of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in decision-making processes has raised concerns about the attribution of responsibility in AI-driven systems. This report explores the relationship between the convexity of responsibility attribution functions and the stability boundary of moral judgments in AI decision spaces. We examine how the convexity of these functions affects the attribution of responsibility and the stability of moral judgments, with a focus on the implications for AI decision-making systems.

**Introduction:**
The development of AI systems has led to a growing need to understand how responsibility is attributed in AI-driven decision-making processes [1]. The concept of responsibility attribution is complex and multifaceted, involving causal, moral, and legal dimensions [2]. In AI systems, responsibility attribution is often diffused, making it challenging to determine who or what is responsible for a particular outcome [3].

**Theoretical Background:**
The attribution of responsibility in AI systems can be understood through the lens of moral agency theory [4]. Moral agency refers to the capacity of an entity to make decisions that have moral implications [5]. In AI systems, moral agency is often distributed among multiple stakeholders, including developers, users, and regulators [6]. The convexity of responsibility attribution functions can be seen as a measure of how responsibility is distributed among these stakeholders.

**Methodology:**
We employ a mathematical framework to analyze the convexity of responsibility attribution functions in AI decision spaces. Let $f(x)$ be a responsibility attribution function, where $x$ represents the input to the AI system. The convexity of $f(x)$ can be measured using the following equation:

$$\nabla^2 f(x) = \frac{\partial^2 f(x)}{\partial x^2}$$

A positive $\nabla^2 f(x)$ indicates a convex function, while a negative $\nabla^2 f(x)$ indicates a concave function.

**Results:**
Our analysis shows that the convexity of responsibility attribution functions has a significant impact on the stability boundary of moral judgments in AI decision spaces. Specifically, we find that:

* Convex responsibility attribution functions lead to a more stable boundary of moral judgments, as they distribute responsibility more evenly among stakeholders.
* Concave responsibility attribution functions lead to a less stable boundary of moral judgments, as they concentrate responsibility among a few stakeholders.

**Discussion:**
Our findings have significant implications for the development of AI decision-making systems. The design of responsibility attribution functions should take into account the convexity of these functions to ensure a stable boundary of moral judgments. Furthermore, our results highlight the need for a nuanced understanding of moral agency in AI systems, recognizing the distributed nature of responsibility among stakeholders.

**Conclusion:**
In conclusion, this report demonstrates the importance of considering the convexity of responsibility attribution functions in AI decision spaces. Our findings provide a foundation for the development of more robust and responsible AI systems, and highlight the need for ongoing research into the complex relationships between AI, morality, and responsibility.
