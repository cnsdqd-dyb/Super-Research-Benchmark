## Introduction

Financial markets are complex systems characterized by nonlinear interactions, feedback loops, and sensitivity to initial conditions. Traditional financial models, often based on assumptions of linearity and normal distributions, struggle to capture the erratic and turbulent nature of markets. Chaos theory, emerging from the study of nonlinear dynamical systems, offers a new lens to analyze financial phenomena by recognizing that small changes in initial conditions can lead to vastly different outcomes—a hallmark of chaotic systems.

## Theoretical Background

Nonlinear dynamic systems are characterized by the presence of nonlinear relationships between variables, which can lead to complex and unpredictable behavior. The Fractal Market Hypothesis (FMH), proposed by Ed Peters, extends the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) by incorporating fractal geometry and chaotic dynamics. The FMH suggests that financial markets exhibit self-similar patterns at different time scales, which can be described using fractal dimensions.

The fractal dimension, $D$, is a measure of the complexity of a system, and can be calculated using various methods, including the box-counting method:

$$D = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \frac{\log N(\epsilon)}{\log \frac{1}{\epsilon}}$$

where $N(\epsilon)$ is the number of boxes of size $\epsilon$ required to cover the system.
