Time perception is a complex and multifaceted phenomenon that has been studied extensively in various fields, including psychology, neuroscience, and anthropology [1]. Recent studies have shown that time perception is not an absolute concept, but rather a relative and context-dependent phenomenon that can be influenced by cultural and linguistic factors [3].

**Neural Correlates of Time Perception:**
Research has identified the precuneus as a key brain region involved in generating the sense of "now" [4]. The precuneus is part of a larger network of brain regions that work together to process temporal information, including the cerebellum, basal ganglia, and prefrontal cortex [5]. The neural mechanisms underlying time perception are complex and involve the coordination of multiple brain regions and systems.

**Cultural and Linguistic Factors:**
Cultural and linguistic factors play a significant role in shaping our perception of time. For example, languages that have a strong future tense, such as English, tend to promote a more linear and sequential view of time, whereas languages with a weaker future tense, such as Mandarin Chinese, tend to promote a more flexible and relative view of time [6]. Additionally, cultural differences in time perception have been observed, with some cultures perceiving time as more fluid and flexible, while others perceive it as more rigid and absolute [7].

**Relativistic Time Dilation:**
Relativistic time dilation, a concept introduced by Albert Einstein, suggests that time is relative and dependent on the observer's frame of reference [8]. According to this concept, time can appear to slow down or speed up depending on the observer's velocity and proximity to a gravitational field. This concept has been experimentally confirmed and is a fundamental aspect of modern physics.
