**Abstract:**
Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS) sensors have become increasingly popular in various applications due to their high precision, cost-effectiveness, and versatility. **Theoretical Background:**
The resonant frequency of a MEMS sensor is determined by its physical properties, such as mass, stiffness, and damping. Process deviations, such as variations in material properties or fabrication errors, can affect the sensor's resonant frequency. Environmental vibrations, such as temperature fluctuations or mechanical stress, can also cause frequency drift. The frequency stability of a MEMS sensor can be described by the following equation:

$$\frac{\Delta f}{f} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta k}{k} + \frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta m}{m}$$

where $\Delta f$ is the change in resonant frequency, $f$ is the nominal resonant frequency, $\Delta k$ is the change in stiffness, $k$ is the nominal stiffness, $\Delta m$ is the change in mass, and $m$ is the nominal mass.
