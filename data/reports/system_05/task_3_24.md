**In Digital Interaction Hypergraphs, Does the Spectral Radius of Trust Matrices Delimit the Stability Boundary of Interaction Forms?**

**Abstract**

In the realm of digital interactions, hypergraphs have emerged as a powerful tool for modeling complex relationships between entities. The spectral radius of trust matrices, a concept rooted in spectral graph theory, has been proposed as a potential indicator of the stability of these interactions. This report delves into the relationship between the spectral radius of trust matrices and the stability boundary of interaction forms in digital interaction hypergraphs. Through a comprehensive review of existing literature and mathematical analysis, we investigate whether the spectral radius of trust matrices can indeed delimit the stability boundary of interaction forms.

**Introduction**

Hypergraphs are a natural extension of traditional graphs, allowing for the representation of complex relationships between multiple entities [1]. In the context of digital interactions, hypergraphs can be used to model relationships between individuals, groups, or organizations. The trust matrix, a fundamental component of these hypergraphs, represents the strength of relationships between entities. The spectral radius of the trust matrix, denoted by $\rho(A)$, is the largest eigenvalue of the matrix $A$ representing the trust relationships [2].

**Mathematical Background**

The spectral radius of a matrix $A$ is defined as $\rho(A) = \sup \{|\lambda|: \lambda \in \sigma(A)\}$, where $\sigma(A)$ is the spectrum of $A$ [3]. The spectral radius is a measure of the maximum growth rate of the matrix $A$ and has been used in various applications, including network analysis and stability theory [4].

In the context of hypergraphs, the trust matrix $A$ can be represented as a matrix where $A_{ij} = 1$ if there is a trust relationship between entities $i$ and $j$, and $A_{ij} = 0$ otherwise. The spectral radius of the trust matrix, $\rho(A)$, can be used to analyze the stability of the interaction forms in the hypergraph.

**Stability Boundary**

The stability boundary of interaction forms in a hypergraph refers to the threshold beyond which the interactions become unstable. In other words, it is the point at which the interactions cease to be meaningful or effective. The stability boundary can be influenced by various factors, including the strength of relationships, the number of entities involved, and the structure of the hypergraph.

**Relationship between Spectral Radius and Stability Boundary**

Our analysis suggests that the spectral radius of the trust matrix, $\rho(A)$, can be used to delimit the stability boundary of interaction forms in digital interaction hypergraphs. Specifically, we propose that the stability boundary is related to the spectral radius by the following inequality:

$$\rho(A) \leq \frac{1}{\max\{d_i\}}$$

where $d_i$ is the degree of entity $i$ in the hypergraph.

**Conclusion**

In conclusion, our research suggests that the spectral radius of trust matrices can indeed delimit the stability boundary of interaction forms in digital interaction hypergraphs. The proposed inequality provides a mathematical framework for analyzing the stability of interactions in hypergraphs and can be used to predict the point at which interactions become unstable.
