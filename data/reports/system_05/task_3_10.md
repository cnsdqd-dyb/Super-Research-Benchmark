**Optimizing Cardiovascular Drug Combinations to Balance Blood Pressure Reduction with Cardiac Output Maintenance**

**Introduction**

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Hypertension is a major risk factor for CVD, and its management is crucial to prevent cardiovascular events. The goal of antihypertensive therapy is to reduce blood pressure (BP) while maintaining cardiac output. This report reviews the current evidence on optimizing cardiovascular drug combinations to achieve this balance.

**Background**

The American Heart Association (AHA) recommends a BP target of <130/80 mmHg for most adults [1]. However, achieving this target can be challenging, and many patients require multiple antihypertensive agents to control their BP. The choice of antihypertensive agents and their combinations is critical to balance BP reduction with cardiac output maintenance.

**Combination Therapy**

Combination therapy with two or more antihypertensive agents is often necessary to achieve BP control [2]. The most commonly used combinations include:

1. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors + calcium channel blockers (CCBs)
2. ACE inhibitors + diuretics
3. Beta-blockers + diuretics
4. CCBs + diuretics

These combinations have been shown to be effective in reducing BP and cardiovascular risk [3]. However, the choice of combination therapy should be individualized based on patient characteristics, such as comorbidities, age, and renal function.

**Single-Pill Combinations**

Single-pill combinations (SPCs) are a convenient and effective way to deliver combination therapy [4]. SPCs have been shown to improve medication adherence and reduce BP compared to free-equivalent combinations [5]. The most commonly used SPCs include:

1. ACE inhibitors + CCBs
2. ACE inhibitors + diuretics
3. Beta-blockers + diuretics

**Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Cardiovascular Disease Management**

AI has the potential to revolutionize CVD management by providing personalized predictions and recommendations [6]. AI algorithms can analyze large datasets to identify high-risk patients and predict cardiovascular events [7]. AI-based clinical decision support systems (CDSSs) can also provide guidance on antihypertensive therapy and help clinicians make informed decisions [8].
