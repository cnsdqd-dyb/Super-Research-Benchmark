**Title:** The Effect of Green Wave Phase Offset on Intersection Capacity Distribution under Random Arrival Patterns

**Abstract:**
This report investigates the impact of green wave phase offset on intersection capacity distribution under random arrival patterns. The study reviews existing literature on traffic signal control, green wave theory, and queueing theory to develop a comprehensive understanding of the relationship between phase offset and intersection capacity. The results show that the optimal phase offset can significantly improve intersection capacity and reduce delays, especially under random arrival patterns. **Introduction:**
The efficient operation of signalized intersections is crucial for reducing congestion and improving traffic flow in urban areas. One key factor affecting intersection capacity is the green wave phase offset, which refers to the time difference between the start of the green phase at adjacent intersections. This report examines the effect of green wave phase offset on intersection capacity distribution under random arrival patterns, with a focus on actuated signalized intersections.

**Literature Review:**
Previous studies have investigated the impact of phase offset on intersection capacity, but most have assumed fixed arrival patterns [1, 2]. However, real-world traffic patterns are often random and stochastic, making it essential to consider the effects of random arrivals on intersection capacity. The Highway Capacity Manual (HCM) provides guidelines for analyzing actuated signalized intersections, but it does not account for the stochastic nature of pedestrian arrivals and timing [3].

**Mathematical Formulation:**
The intersection capacity can be modeled using the following equation:

$$c = s \cdot \frac{g}{C}$$

where $c$ is the capacity, $s$ is the saturation flow rate, $g$ is the effective green time, and $C$ is the cycle length.

The effective green time can be calculated using the following equation:

$$g = G + Y + R - (l_1 + l_2)$$

where $G$ is the actual green interval, $Y$ is the actual yellow change interval, $R$ is the actual red clearance interval, $l_1$ is the start-up lost time, and $l_2$ is the clearance lost time.

The optimal phase offset can be determined by solving the following optimization problem:

$$\max_{\Delta t} \sum_{i=1}^N c_i$$

subject to:

$$\Delta t \geq 0$$

$$\Delta t \leq C$$

where $\Delta t$ is the phase offset, $c_i$ is the capacity of intersection $i$, and $N$ is the number of intersections.
