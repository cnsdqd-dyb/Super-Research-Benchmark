**Power-law distributions and Markov chain convergence**

Power-law distributions are characterized by a power-law exponent, $\alpha$, which describes the rate at which the distribution decays [4]. In the context of globalization networks, the power-law exponent is typically between 2 and 3 [5]. The power-law exponent has a significant impact on the Markov chain convergence rate, as it affects the singular values of the transition matrix [6]. Specifically, the singular values of the transition matrix are related to the power-law exponent, and the convergence rate of the Markov chain is determined by the second dominant eigenvalue of the transition matrix [7].

**Singular value decomposition and Markov chain convergence**

SVD is a factorization technique that decomposes a matrix into its constituent parts [8]. In the context of Markov chains, SVD provides a way to analyze the transition matrix and understand the convergence rate of the chain [9]. The singular values of the transition matrix are related to the power-law exponent, and the convergence rate of the Markov chain is determined by the second dominant eigenvalue of the transition matrix [10]. Specifically, the convergence rate is given by:

$$\lim_{t \to \infty} \left\| P^t - \pi \right\| = \left\| \sum_{i=2}^n \lambda_i^t u_i v_i^T \right\|$$

where $\lambda_i$ are the singular values, $u_i$ and $v_i$ are the left and right singular vectors, and $\pi$ is the stationary distribution [11].

**Impact of power-law parameters on Markov chain convergence**

The power-law exponent, $\alpha$, has a significant impact on the Markov chain convergence rate. Specifically, the convergence rate is faster for smaller values of $\alpha$ [12]. This is because the singular values of the transition matrix are more spread out for smaller values of $\alpha$, which leads to a faster convergence rate [13]. Additionally, the power-law exponent affects the mixing time of the Markov chain, which is the time it takes for the chain to reach its stationary distribution [14].
