## Safety Stock Adjustment Boundary for Flash Sales Given Conversion Rate Fluctuations

### Introduction

In the context of inventory management, safety stock plays a crucial role in preventing stockouts and ensuring that businesses can meet customer demand. However, determining the optimal safety stock level can be challenging, especially in the face of conversion rate fluctuations and flash sales. This report aims to provide a comprehensive analysis of how to set the safety stock adjustment boundary for flash sales given conversion rate fluctuations.

### Literature Review

According to [1], the basic safety stock formula is a simple method for estimating safety stock levels, which involves estimating "safety days" and multiplying it by average sales. However, this method may not be suitable for all situations, especially those with high variability in demand or lead time. More advanced formulas, such as the standard deviation safety stock formula, can provide a more accurate estimate of the safety stock required [2].

The standard deviation safety stock formula is given by:

$$SS = Z \times \sigma_{LT} \times D_{avg}$$

where $Z$ is the desired service level, $\sigma_{LT}$ is the standard deviation of lead time, and $D_{avg}$ is the average demand.

However, in the context of flash sales, the demand variability and conversion rate fluctuations need to be taken into account. According to [3], the safety stock level should be adjusted based on the conversion rate and the desired service level.

### Methodology

To determine the safety stock adjustment boundary for flash sales given conversion rate fluctuations, we propose the following methodology:

1. **Estimate the conversion rate**: Estimate the conversion rate for the flash sale period using historical data and forecasting techniques.
2. **Determine the desired service level**: Determine the desired service level for the flash sale period based on business objectives and customer expectations.
3. **Calculate the safety stock level**: Calculate the safety stock level using the standard deviation safety stock formula, taking into account the conversion rate and desired service level.
4. **Adjust the safety stock level**: Adjust the safety stock level based on the conversion rate fluctuations and desired service level.

The safety stock adjustment boundary can be calculated using the following formula:

$$SS_{adj} = SS \times \frac{CR}{CR_{avg}}$$

where $SS_{adj}$ is the adjusted safety stock level, $SS$ is the calculated safety stock level, $CR$ is the conversion rate for the flash sale period, and $CR_{avg}$ is the average conversion rate.

### Case Study

The desired service level is 95%, and the conversion rate for the flash sale period is estimated to be 20%. The standard deviation of lead time is 1 day, and the average conversion rate is 15%.

Using the standard deviation safety stock formula, the safety stock level is calculated as:

$$SS = 1.65 \times \sqrt{(5 \times 1^2) + (100^2 \times 1^2)} = 181$$

$$SS_{adj} = 181 \times \frac{0.20}{0.15} = 241$$
