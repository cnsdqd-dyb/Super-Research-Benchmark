**Mathematical Framework**

The stability of a nonlinear system can be analyzed using the concept of Lyapunov exponents, which measure the rate of divergence or convergence of nearby trajectories [2]. The Lyapunov exponent is defined as:

$$\lambda = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{1}{t} \ln \left| \frac{d}{dt} \phi(t) \right|$$

where $\phi(t)$ is the solution to the system at time $t$. The Lyapunov exponent is related to the characteristic polynomial of the intergenerational transfer matrix, which is defined as:

$$P(\lambda) = \det(\lambda I - A)$$

where $A$ is the intergenerational transfer matrix and $I$ is the identity matrix.

**Lyapunov Stability**

A system is said to be Lyapunov stable if the Lyapunov exponent is negative, indicating that nearby trajectories converge to the equilibrium point. The characteristic polynomial of the intergenerational transfer matrix plays a crucial role in determining the Lyapunov stability of the system. Specifically, if the characteristic polynomial has all roots with negative real parts, then the system is Lyapunov stable [3].

**Intergenerational Transfer Matrices**

The intergenerational transfer matrix $A$ is a square matrix that represents the transfer of resources between different generations. The elements of the matrix represent the proportion of resources transferred from one generation to another. The characteristic polynomial of the intergenerational transfer matrix is given by:

$$P(\lambda) = \det(\lambda I - A) = \lambda^n + a_{n-1} \lambda^{n-1} + \cdots + a_1 \lambda + a_0$$

where $n$ is the number of generations and $a_i$ are the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial.
