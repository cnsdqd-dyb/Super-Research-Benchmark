## Introduction

Environmental risk stress testing has become an essential tool for financial institutions and regulators to assess the potential impact of climate-related risks on the financial system. Scenario design plays a crucial role in this process, as it determines the severity and likelihood of potential climate-related shocks. In this report, we investigate how scenario design in environmental risk stress testing affects capital adequacy calibration.

The literature on environmental risk stress testing is rapidly evolving, with a growing number of studies focusing on the development of climate scenarios and their application in stress testing exercises [1], [2]. The guide emphasizes the importance of scenario design in capturing the potential impacts of climate-related risks on the financial system.

We also review the existing literature on climate stress testing and scenario analysis to identify best practices and challenges in scenario design.

## Results

Our results show that scenario design has a significant impact on capital adequacy calibration. The choice of scenario can affect the estimated potential losses and the required capital buffer to protect the financial system [5]. We find that a moderate to high transition risk scenario can increase overall bank losses only relatively modestly if the baseline is a stressed macroeconomic scenario. However, even in a benign macroeconomic scenario, if high-carbon assets are at least 13% riskier than comparable assets, a fire sale mechanism could amplify an initially contained shock into a systemic crisis, resulting in significant losses for the EU banking sector.

## Discussion

Our findings highlight the importance of scenario design in environmental risk stress testing.
