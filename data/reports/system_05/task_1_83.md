**Title:** What Determines the Temporal Discount Function of Rating Contributions in Review Systems?

**Abstract:**
The temporal discount function of rating contributions in review systems is a crucial aspect of understanding user behavior and decision-making. This report provides an in-depth analysis of the factors that influence the temporal discount function, including psychological, contextual, and algorithmic factors. We review existing literature on temporal discounting, collaborative filtering, and recommendation systems to identify key determinants of the temporal discount function. Our findings suggest that user heterogeneity, impulsivity, risk tolerance, emotional state, social influences, and past experiences are significant psychological factors that affect the temporal discount function. Additionally, contextual factors such as time perception, delay, and reward magnitude also play a crucial role. We discuss the implications of these findings for the design of review systems and provide recommendations for future research.

**Introduction:**
The temporal discount function of rating contributions in review systems refers to the tendency of users to discount the value of future rewards or outcomes when making decisions about rating contributions. This phenomenon is closely related to the concept of temporal discounting, which has been extensively studied in psychology and economics [1]. Understanding the temporal discount function is essential for designing effective review systems that encourage users to contribute high-quality ratings.

**Psychological Factors:**
Several psychological factors influence the temporal discount function, including:

1. **Impulsivity**: Users with higher impulsivity tend to discount future rewards more heavily [2].
2. **Risk tolerance**: Risk-averse users may prefer immediate rewards over uncertain future rewards [3].
3. **Emotional state**: Emotions such as stress or pleasure can skew user preferences for immediate versus future rewards [4].
4. **User heterogeneity**: Different users exhibit varying degrees of temporal discounting based on factors such as age, income level, or cultural background [5].

**Contextual Factors:**
Contextual factors also play a significant role in shaping the temporal discount function, including:

1. **Time perception**: The way users perceive time affects their discounting behavior [6].
2. **Delay**: The length of delay between the rating contribution and the reward affects the discount function [7].
3. **Reward magnitude**: The magnitude of the reward affects the discount function, with larger rewards leading to less discounting [8].

**Algorithmic Factors:**
Algorithmic factors, such as the design of the review system and the recommendation algorithm, can also influence the temporal discount function. For example:

1. **Collaborative filtering**: The use of collaborative filtering algorithms can affect the temporal discount function by influencing user behavior and preferences [9].
2. **Recommendation algorithms**: The design of recommendation algorithms can also impact the temporal discount function by affecting the presentation of rewards and outcomes [10].

**Mathematical Modeling:**
The temporal discount function can be modeled using various mathematical functions, including:

$$V = \frac{A}{1 + kD}$$

where $V$ is the value of the reward, $A$ is the magnitude of the reward, $D$ is the delay, and $k$ is a free parameter [11].

**Conclusion:**
The temporal discount function of rating contributions in review systems is a complex phenomenon influenced by psychological, contextual, and algorithmic factors. Understanding these factors is essential for designing effective review systems that encourage high-quality rating contributions. Future research should focus on developing more sophisticated models of the temporal discount function and exploring the implications of these findings for the design of review systems.
