**Title:** How does path selection convergence speed change with network scale power-laws under capacity constraints?

**Abstract:**
The convergence speed of path selection in networks is a critical aspect of network performance, particularly under capacity constraints. **Introduction:**
Networks are complex systems that exhibit power-law degree distributions, where the number of connections between nodes follows a power-law function [1]. This property has significant implications for network performance, including the convergence speed of path selection. Path selection is the process of selecting the best path between two nodes in a network, and it is critical for network performance, particularly in large-scale networks [2]. Capacity constraints, such as limited bandwidth or processing power, can also impact network performance and convergence speed.

**Background:**
Power-law degree distributions have been observed in many real-world networks, including the Internet, social networks, and biological networks [3]. These networks exhibit a scale-free property, where the degree distribution follows a power-law function, $P(k) \sim k^{-\gamma}$, where $k$ is the degree of a node and $\gamma$ is the power-law exponent [4]. The power-law exponent $\gamma$ is a critical parameter that determines the shape of the degree distribution and has significant implications for network performance.
