**Title:** Stability Boundaries in Magnetohydrodynamics: Constraints on Fusion Reactor Design Space Regarding Beta Limits

**Abstract:**
The pursuit of achieving controlled nuclear fusion has been ongoing for decades, with magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) playing a crucial role in understanding the behavior of plasmas in fusion reactors. One of the key challenges in designing a viable fusion reactor is the limitation imposed by stability boundaries, particularly the beta limit, which constrains the plasma pressure and, consequently, the fusion power density. This report delves into the theoretical and experimental aspects of MHD stability, focusing on the beta limits and their implications for fusion reactor design.

**Introduction:**
Magnetohydrodynamics is the study of the interaction between magnetic fields and electrically conducting fluids, such as plasmas. In the context of fusion reactors, MHD stability is critical, as it determines the operational limits of the plasma, including the maximum plasma current, pressure, and density. The beta limit, which is the ratio of the plasma pressure to the magnetic pressure, is a key parameter in designing fusion reactors.

**Theoretical Background:**
The MHD stability of a plasma is governed by the interplay between various instabilities, including ideal and resistive modes. The ideal MHD model predicts that the beta limit is determined by the Troyon limit [1], given by:

$$\beta \leq \beta_{Troyon} = \frac{g_{Troyon}}{I_p} \frac{aB}{R}$$

where $g_{Troyon}$ is a constant, $I_p$ is the plasma current, $a$ is the minor radius, $B$ is the magnetic field strength, and $R$ is the major radius.

**Experimental Evidence:**
Experimental results from various tokamaks and stellarators have consistently shown that the beta limit is a critical parameter in determining the plasma performance. For example, the Joint European Torus (JET) has achieved a record beta value of 3.6% [2], while the Large Helical Device (LHD) has demonstrated stable operation at beta values up to 5.1% [3].

**Stellarator Designs:**
Stellarators, which are three-dimensional magnetic confinement devices, offer an attractive alternative to tokamaks. They have been shown to operate at higher beta values, with some designs achieving stable operation at beta values up to 23% [4]. The optimized stellarator designs have been analyzed using linear MHD stability calculations, which predict that the beta limit can be extended beyond the ideal MHD limit [5].

**Implications for Fusion Reactor Design:**
The beta limit imposes a significant constraint on the design of fusion reactors. To achieve high fusion power densities, the plasma pressure must be increased, which requires operating at higher beta values. However, this also increases the risk of MHD instabilities, which can lead to a loss of plasma confinement and, ultimately, a disruption. Therefore, the design of fusion reactors must carefully balance the competing demands of achieving high beta values while maintaining MHD stability.

**Conclusion:**
In conclusion, the stability boundaries in magnetohydrodynamics, particularly the beta limit, play a critical role in determining the design space of fusion reactors. Theoretical models, such as the Troyon limit, provide a framework for understanding the beta limit, while experimental results from tokamaks and stellarators have consistently demonstrated the importance of this parameter. The development of optimized stellarator designs offers a promising path forward for achieving high beta values while maintaining MHD stability. Ultimately, a deeper understanding of MHD stability and the beta limit will be essential for the successful design and operation of fusion reactors.
