**Computation Offloading Strategy for Real-Time Response and Battery Life in IoT Edge Nodes**

**Abstract**

The increasing demand for real-time processing and low latency in Internet of Things (IoT) applications has led to the development of edge computing and fog computing paradigms. However, the limited computational resources and energy constraints of IoT devices pose significant challenges. Computation offloading has emerged as a viable solution to address these challenges. This report investigates the computation offloading strategy that yields the best comprehensive performance for real-time response and battery life in IoT edge nodes, weighing local processing latency against transmission energy costs.

**Introduction**

The proliferation of IoT devices has generated a vast amount of data, which requires intensive processing and analysis. However, IoT devices are often resource-constrained, with limited computational power, memory, and energy. Edge computing and fog computing have been proposed to address these challenges by providing a distributed computing infrastructure that can process data closer to the source. Computation offloading is a key technique in edge computing and fog computing, where computationally intensive tasks are offloaded from IoT devices to more capable nodes, such as edge servers or fog nodes.

**Background**

Computation offloading involves transferring compute-intensive tasks from resource-constrained devices to remote computing servers with significant computing capacity. The objectives of computation offloading include maximizing battery life, minimizing latency, and reducing execution cost. The offloading location may be a remote cloud server, a fog node, an edge server, or a Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC) server. The formulation of the offloading problem considers several parameters, such as delay, user location, task deadline, and execution cost.

**Methodology**

We analyzed various computation offloading strategies, including vertical offloading (between cloud, edge, and fog layers) and horizontal offloading (between fog nodes). We also examined the impact of different factors, such as hardware capabilities, network conditions, and application characteristics, on the efficiency of task offloading.
