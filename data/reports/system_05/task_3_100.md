**How Does Genome Editing Ethics Rebuild Cross-Species Justice Foundations Beyond Anthropocentrism?**

**Abstract**

The advent of genome editing technologies, such as CRISPR-Cas9, has sparked intense debates about the ethics of modifying non-human species. This report explores the concept of cross-species justice and how genome editing ethics can be reimagined beyond anthropocentrism. We examine the implications of genome editing on non-human animals, the environment, and human societies, and discuss the need for a more inclusive and nuanced approach to ethics that considers the interests and well-being of all species.

**Introduction**

Genome editing technologies have revolutionized the field of biotechnology, enabling precise modifications to the genetic code of living organisms. While these technologies hold great promise for improving human health and agriculture, they also raise significant ethical concerns. The use of genome editing in non-human animals, for example, challenges traditional notions of species boundaries and raises questions about the moral status of non-human entities [1]. As we consider the ethics of genome editing, it is essential to move beyond anthropocentric perspectives and engage with the complexities of cross-species justice.

**The Concept of Telos**

The concept of telos, or the intrinsic and extrinsic value of animals, is central to understanding the ethics of genome editing [2]. Telos refers to the species-specific goal or inherent nature of an organism to pursue its unique form of life, complete its reproduction cycle, and fulfill its ecosystem services. Genome editing can alter an animal's telos, potentially compromising its intrinsic value and integrity [3]. For instance, the use of CRISPR-Cas9 to create "diminished" animals, such as those with reduced cognitive abilities or altered physical characteristics, raises concerns about the animal's telos and its ability to thrive in its environment [4].

**Cross-Species Justice**

Cross-species justice requires considering the interests and well-being of all species, not just humans. This involves recognizing the intrinsic value of non-human animals and the environment, and acknowledging the complex relationships between species [5]. Genome editing ethics must be reimagined to account for these relationships and to prioritize the well-being of all species. As noted by [6], "the use of genome editing in non-human animals challenges traditional notions of species boundaries and raises questions about the moral status of non-human entities."

**Environmental Considerations**

Genome editing can have significant environmental implications, including the potential for unintended consequences on ecosystems and biodiversity [8]. The use of gene drives, for example, can lead to the extinction of non-target species and disrupt ecosystem balance [9]. As noted by [10], "the use of genome editing in non-human animals can have significant environmental implications, including the potential for unintended consequences on ecosystems and biodiversity."

**Social Justice and Human Rights**

Genome editing also raises social justice and human rights concerns, particularly in the context of heritable genome editing [11]. The use of genome editing to create "designer babies" or to enhance human physical and cognitive abilities raises questions about equality, fairness, and access to these technologies [12]. As noted by [13], "the use of genome editing in humans raises significant social justice and human rights concerns, particularly in the context of heritable genome editing."

**Conclusion**

Genome editing ethics must be reimagined to account for the complexities of cross-species justice and the intrinsic value of non-human animals and the environment. A more inclusive and nuanced approach to ethics is necessary, one that prioritizes the well-being of all species and recognizes the complex relationships between them. By employing mathematical models and considering the environmental and social justice implications of genome editing, we can work towards a more just and equitable future for all species.
