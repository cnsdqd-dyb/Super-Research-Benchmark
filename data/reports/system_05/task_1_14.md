**Title:** The Role of Thermal Runaway Propagation Mechanism in Designing Safety Redundancy for Lithium-Ion Battery Packs: Considering Electrolyte and Separator Stability

**Abstract:**
Lithium-ion batteries are widely used in electric vehicles and renewable energy systems due to their high energy density and efficiency. However, they are prone to thermal runaway (TR) propagation, which can lead to catastrophic failures and fires. This report investigates the role of thermal runaway propagation mechanism in designing safety redundancy for lithium-ion battery packs, with a focus on electrolyte and separator stability. We review the current state of research on thermal management systems, phase change materials, and electrolyte design, and discuss the importance of material selection, battery management systems, and safety protocols in preventing TR propagation.

**Introduction:**
Lithium-ion batteries are a critical component of modern energy systems, but their safety is a major concern. Thermal runaway (TR) propagation is a chain reaction that can occur when a single cell fails, leading to a rapid increase in temperature and potentially causing a fire or explosion. The thermal runaway propagation mechanism is complex and involves the interaction of various factors, including electrolyte stability, separator design, and battery management systems.

**Thermal Runaway Propagation Mechanism:**
The thermal runaway propagation mechanism can be described by the following equation:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{1}{C_p} \left( Q_{gen} - Q_{loss} \right)$$

where $T$ is the temperature, $C_p$ is the heat capacity, $Q_{gen}$ is the heat generated by the battery, and $Q_{loss}$ is the heat lost to the surroundings [1]. The thermal runaway propagation mechanism is influenced by various factors, including the electrolyte stability, separator design, and battery management systems.

**Electrolyte Stability:**
The electrolyte plays a critical role in preventing TR propagation. Conventional liquid electrolytes can reduce the risk of TR via several methods, including adding flame-retardant additives, changing the interaction between the electrolyte and electrode, and using a composite electrolyte [2]. Solid and gel electrolytes are also being researched as potential solutions to mitigate TR.

**Separator Design:**
The separator is another critical component in preventing TR propagation. A good separator should have high thermal stability, high porosity, and high mechanical strength [3]. Ceramic-coated separators and high melting point polymer materials offer some improvement in thermal stability and abuse tolerance for lithium-ion cell separators.

**Battery Management Systems:**
Battery management systems (BMS) play a crucial role in preventing TR propagation. A robust BMS can continuously monitor critical parameters such as voltage, current, and temperature, and trigger alarms when anomalies occur [4]. Predictive analytics and machine learning can also be used to predict failure patterns and schedule maintenance.

**Phase Change Materials:**
Phase change materials (PCMs) can effectively control the battery temperature rise and inhibit TR propagation via three main aspects: (i) acting as a thermal barrier to stop the conduction of heat in the battery module; (ii) absorbing the heat energy generated by TR; and (iii) effectively reducing the cell temperature difference during high-magnification operation [5].

**Conclusion:**
In conclusion, the thermal runaway propagation mechanism plays a critical role in designing safety redundancy for lithium-ion battery packs. Electrolyte stability, separator design, and battery management systems are all important factors in preventing TR propagation. Phase change materials and advanced cooling systems can also be used to mitigate TR. Further research is needed to develop more effective thermal management systems and safety protocols to prevent TR propagation and ensure the safe operation of lithium-ion batteries.
