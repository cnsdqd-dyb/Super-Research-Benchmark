**Institutional Guarantees for Long-Term Value Chains in Creative Industries: Balancing Economic Benefits with Cultural Heritage**

**Abstract**

The creative industries are a significant contributor to the global economy, accounting for 6.1% of the global GDP and generating annual revenues of US$ 2,250 billion [1]. However, the long-term value chains in these industries are often threatened by the tension between economic benefits and cultural heritage. This report explores the institutional guarantees that can ensure a balance between these two competing interests. We examine the role of cultural policies, international agreements, and intellectual property laws in promoting the diversity of cultural expressions and protecting the cultural heritage of indigenous peoples and local communities.

**Introduction**

The creative industries are a vital part of the global economy, providing employment opportunities, driving innovation, and contributing to sustainable development [2]. However, the commercialization of cultural expressions and the exploitation of cultural heritage can have negative consequences, such as the loss of cultural diversity and the misappropriation of traditional knowledge [3]. To address these challenges, it is essential to establish institutional guarantees that balance economic benefits with cultural heritage.

**Cultural Policies and International Agreements**

Cultural policies and international agreements play a crucial role in promoting the diversity of cultural expressions and protecting cultural heritage. The 2005 Convention for the Protection and Promotion of the Diversity of Cultural Expressions, adopted by UNESCO, recognizes the dual nature of cultural expressions, both economic and cultural, and aims to promote the diversity of cultural expressions and protect the cultural heritage of nations [4]. The Convention encourages governments to adopt policies and measures that support the creation, production, distribution, and access to cultural goods and services.

**Intellectual Property Laws**

Intellectual property laws can also play a crucial role in protecting the cultural heritage of indigenous peoples and local communities. The use of trademarks, copyrights, and other forms of intellectual property protection can help prevent the misappropriation of traditional knowledge and cultural expressions [5]. However, the intellectual property system must be adapted to meet the specific needs of indigenous peoples and local communities, taking into account their traditional knowledge and cultural practices.

**Institutional Guarantees**

To ensure a balance between economic benefits and cultural heritage, several institutional guarantees can be established:

1. **Cultural impact assessments**: Conducting cultural impact assessments can help identify the potential risks and benefits of cultural expressions and heritage, and inform policy decisions [6].
2. **Community engagement**: Engaging with indigenous peoples and local communities in the development of cultural policies and intellectual property laws can help ensure that their rights and interests are protected [7].
3. **Benefit-sharing mechanisms**: Establishing benefit-sharing mechanisms can help ensure that the economic benefits of cultural expressions and heritage are shared fairly among all stakeholders, including indigenous peoples and local communities [8].
4. **Intellectual property protection**: Providing intellectual property protection for traditional knowledge and cultural expressions can help prevent misappropriation and ensure that the cultural heritage of indigenous peoples and local communities is protected [9].

**Conclusion**

In conclusion, the institutional guarantees outlined in this report can help balance economic benefits with cultural heritage in the creative industries. By establishing cultural policies, international agreements, and intellectual property laws that promote the diversity of cultural expressions and protect the cultural heritage of indigenous peoples and local communities, we can ensure that the long-term value chains in these industries are sustainable and equitable.

**Recommendations**

Based on the findings of this report, we recommend that:

3. Intellectual property laws be adapted to meet the specific needs of indigenous peoples and local communities, taking into account their traditional knowledge and cultural practices.
4. Benefit-sharing mechanisms be established to ensure that the economic benefits of cultural expressions and heritage are shared fairly among all stakeholders.
