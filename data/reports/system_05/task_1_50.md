**Introduction:**
Cultural attraction theory posits that cultural evolution is driven by the interaction of cognitive biases, environmental constraints, and social learning processes [1]. Cultural attractors, which are the theoretical posits that capture the way in which certain ideational variants are more likely to be the outcome of transformations than others, play a central role in this framework [2]. The fractal dimension of cultural identity attractors, which describes the complexity and self-similarity of these attractors, has been shown to influence the distribution of cultural variants [3]. In this report, we investigate the relationship between the fractal dimension of cultural identity attractors and the distribution of branch points in global-local evolutionary trajectories.

**Theoretical Background:**
Cultural attraction theory is based on a naturalistic ontology, which characterizes cultural phenomena as distributions of similar items in a community and its habitat, and across time [4]. Cultural attractors are abstract objects that exist because there are concrete factors of attraction that affect the probability that individual variants of a cultural item will depart from their models in one direction rather than in another [5]. The fractal dimension of cultural identity attractors, denoted by $D$, can be defined as:

$$D = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \frac{\log N(\epsilon)}{\log \frac{1}{\epsilon}}$$

where $N(\epsilon)$ is the number of boxes of size $\epsilon$ needed to cover the attractor [6].
