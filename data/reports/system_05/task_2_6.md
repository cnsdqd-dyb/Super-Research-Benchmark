**To what extent does the mismatch of thermal expansion coefficients in metal matrix composites drive the evolution of interface cracking and stress concentration fields under thermal cycling fatigue?**

**Abstract**

Metal matrix composites (MMCs) are prone to degradation under thermal cycling fatigue due to the mismatch in thermal expansion coefficients between the matrix and fiber. This mismatch leads to the development of internal stresses, which can cause interface cracking and stress concentration fields. In this report, we investigate the extent to which the mismatch of thermal expansion coefficients drives the evolution of interface cracking and stress concentration fields under thermal cycling fatigue.

**Introduction**

MMCs are widely used in aerospace and other high-temperature applications due to their excellent mechanical properties and resistance to corrosion. However, their performance under thermal cycling fatigue is a major concern. The mismatch in thermal expansion coefficients between the matrix and fiber leads to the development of internal stresses, which can cause interface cracking and stress concentration fields [1]. These stresses can result in premature failure of the composite, making it essential to understand the mechanisms driving their evolution.

**Thermal Expansion Mismatch**

The thermal expansion mismatch between the matrix and fiber is given by $\Delta\alpha = \alpha_f - \alpha_m$, where $\alpha_f$ and $\alpha_m$ are the coefficients of thermal expansion of the fiber and matrix, respectively [2]. This mismatch leads to the development of internal stresses, which can be calculated using the following equation:

$\sigma_{th} = \frac{E_m \Delta\alpha \Delta T}{1 - \nu_m}$

where $E_m$ is the modulus of the matrix, $\nu_m$ is the Poisson's ratio of the matrix, and $\Delta T$ is the temperature difference [3].

**Interface Cracking**

The internal stresses developed due to the thermal expansion mismatch can cause interface cracking. The crack initiation and propagation can be modeled using the following equation:

$\frac{da}{dN} = C \left( \frac{\Delta K}{K_{IC}} \right)^n$

where $a$ is the crack length, $N$ is the number of cycles, $C$ and $n$ are material constants, $\Delta K$ is the stress intensity factor range, and $K_{IC}$ is the fracture toughness [4].

**Stress Concentration Fields**

The internal stresses developed due to the thermal expansion mismatch can also cause stress concentration fields. The stress concentration factor can be calculated using the following equation:

$K_t = \frac{\sigma_{max}}{\sigma_{avg}}$

where $\sigma_{max}$ is the maximum stress and $\sigma_{avg}$ is the average stress [5].
