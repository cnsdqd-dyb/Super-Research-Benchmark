**Introduction:**
The increasing power of quantum computers poses a significant threat to our digital security, as they can potentially break certain public-key cryptography algorithms, such as RSA and elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) [1]. This has significant implications for our digital trust, as these algorithms are widely used to secure online transactions, communication, and data storage. To address this asymmetry between security needs and digital trust, a responsibility mechanism is necessary.

**Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC):**
PQC refers to cryptographic algorithms designed to resist attacks from quantum computers [2]. These algorithms are based on different mathematical problems, such as lattice-based cryptography, code-based cryptography, and hash-based signatures, which are resistant to quantum attacks [3]. PQC is essential for ensuring long-term digital security, as it provides a secure way to encrypt and decrypt data, even in the presence of quantum computers.

**Challenges Associated with PQC Migration:**
The migration to PQC poses several challenges, including:

1. **Cryptographic Inventory:** Organizations need to identify all encryption implementations across their systems, including embedded systems, database encryption, backup systems, and third-party services [4].
2. **Interoperability:** PQC algorithms need to be compatible with existing systems and protocols, such as TLS and SSH [5].
3. **Performance Overhead:** PQC algorithms can have a significant performance overhead, which can impact system performance [6].

**Hybrid Approach:**
A hybrid approach, combining classical and quantum-resistant algorithms, can help ensure a smooth transition to PQC [7]. This approach allows organizations to use classical algorithms for short-term security needs, while also deploying quantum-resistant algorithms for long-term security.

**Responsibility Mechanism:**
