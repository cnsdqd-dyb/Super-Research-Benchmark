## Background

Sustainable procurement is based on the principle that environmental, ethical, and social factors are considered in decision-making and long-term financial profitability [1]. The first step towards a sustainable supply chain is to understand an organization's ambitions and translate them into supplier selection, management practices, and performance requirements. With 75% of emissions coming from the supply chain, it is essential to start with suppliers [2].

## Supplier Selection Criteria

Traditionally, supplier selection criteria apply to the estimated total cost of ownership (TCO), product quality, supplier capabilities, consistency, and supplier risk [3]. However, the role of sustainability is increasing, and sustainability requirements should form part of the weighted evaluation criteria when undertaking a new sourcing project [4]. The criteria for supplier selection include environmental standards, social standards, and governance [5].

## Performance Metrics

Supplier performance management is one of the cornerstones of operational procurement [6]. Common metrics or key performance indicators (KPIs) for measuring supplier performance include quality, defect rate, compliance with specifications, and on-time delivery [7]. A supplier scorecard is a performance measurement tool that evaluates supplier performance using predetermined metrics [8].

## Determining Performance Boundary

The performance boundary for supplier promotion is determined by the supplier's ability to meet the organization's sustainability goals and objectives [9]. The performance boundary is influenced by various factors, including the supplier's environmental and social performance, quality, and reliability [10]. The organization's procurement team must develop a set of criteria that aligns with their sustainability goals and objectives, which may include specific environmental, social, and economic performance indicators that suppliers must meet [11].
