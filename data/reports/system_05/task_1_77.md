**Theoretical Background:**
Gibbs free energy is a measure of the energy available to do work in a system. It is defined as:

$$G = H - TS$$

where $G$ is the Gibbs free energy, $H$ is the enthalpy, $T$ is the temperature, and $S$ is the entropy. The minimization of Gibbs free energy is a fundamental principle in thermodynamics, which states that a system will always tend to minimize its free energy [3].

Entropy, on the other hand, is a measure of the disorder or randomness of a system. It is defined as:

$$S = k \ln \Omega$$

where $S$ is the entropy, $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $\Omega$ is the number of possible microstates in the system [4].
