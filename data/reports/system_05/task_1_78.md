**Introduction**

Plastic waste is a significant environmental concern, with millions of tons of plastic waste generated every year. However, plastic recycling is a complex process that involves the breakdown of polymeric materials into their constituent monomers, which can then be re-polymerized into new products [1]. **Molecular Weight Distribution and Activation Energy Barriers**

The molecular weight distribution of a polymer is a critical factor in determining its recyclability. Polymers with a narrow molecular weight distribution are generally easier to recycle than those with a broad distribution [2]. This is because a narrow molecular weight distribution indicates a more uniform polymer structure, which can be more easily broken down into its constituent monomers. On the other hand, a broad molecular weight distribution indicates a more complex polymer structure, which can be more difficult to break down.

Activation energy barriers are a key constraint in plastic recycling. These barriers refer to the energy required to break down the polymer chains into their constituent monomers. The activation energy barrier is influenced by the molecular weight distribution of the polymer, with higher molecular weight polymers requiring more energy to break down [3]. This means that polymers with a broad molecular weight distribution may require more energy to recycle, which can limit the efficiency and purity of the recycled materials.
