**Title:** Balancing Rational Equilibrium with Bias Correction: An Examination of the Behavioral Game Theory Model in Investment Decision-Making

**Abstract:**
This report delves into the realm of behavioral game theory, exploring its application in investment decision-making and the balance between rational equilibrium and bias correction. By integrating cognitive and psychological dimensions, we reexamine foundational constructs such as Nash equilibrium, strategic dominance, and Bayesian games through the lens of behavioral frameworks like prospect theory, fairness preferences, and loss aversion. Our analysis reveals that the behavioral game theory model can indeed balance rational equilibrium with bias correction, but the parameterization of prospect theory may amplify loss aversion effects, leading to suboptimal investment decisions.

**Introduction:**
Traditional game theory assumes rationality, selfishness, and utility maximization, whereas behavioral game theory acknowledges that human decision-making is influenced by social preferences, social utility, and psychological factors [1]. The prospect theory, introduced by Kahneman and Tversky, describes how people evaluate risky options, coding gains and losses relative to a reference point, and exhibiting loss aversion [2]. This theory has been widely applied in finance, explaining phenomena such as the disposition effect and the sunk cost fallacy.

**Methodology:**
Our analysis is based on a comprehensive review of existing literature on behavioral game theory, prospect theory, and investment decision-making. We examine the mathematical formulations of prospect theory, including the value function and decision weights, to understand how they capture loss aversion and probability weighting.

**Results:**
Our findings suggest that the behavioral game theory model can balance rational equilibrium with bias correction by incorporating psychological and social factors into the decision-making process. However, the parameterization of prospect theory may amplify loss aversion effects, leading to suboptimal investment decisions. The value function, which captures diminishing sensitivity for gains and losses, can result in investors being more sensitive to losses than gains, as evidenced by the equation:

\[
v(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^\alpha & \text{if } x \ge 0,\\
-\lambda (-x)^\beta & \text{if } x < 0,
\end{cases}
\]

where $0 < \alpha, \beta \le 1$ and $\lambda > 1$ [3]. This can lead to investors exhibiting loss aversion, a phenomenon where the pain of losses outweighs the pleasure of gains.

**Discussion:**
Our analysis highlights the importance of considering psychological and social factors in investment decision-making. The behavioral game theory model provides a more nuanced understanding of human behavior, acknowledging that investors are not always rational and may be influenced by biases and heuristics. However, the parameterization of prospect theory may amplify loss aversion effects, leading to suboptimal investment decisions. To mitigate this, investors can employ strategies such as diversification, hedging, and framing effects to reduce the impact of loss aversion.

**Conclusion:**
In conclusion, the behavioral game theory model can balance rational equilibrium with bias correction, but the parameterization of prospect theory may amplify loss aversion effects. Investors and financial institutions must be aware of these biases and take steps to mitigate their impact. By acknowledging the psychological and social factors that influence investment decision-making, we can develop more effective strategies for managing risk and achieving optimal investment outcomes.
