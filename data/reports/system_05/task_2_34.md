**Thermal Protection System Design for Reentry Vehicles: A Review of Ablative Materials and Coupled Aerodynamic Heating and Structural Thermal Stress**

**Abstract**

Reentry vehicles are exposed to extreme aerodynamic heating and structural thermal stress during atmospheric reentry, requiring a reliable thermal protection system (TPS) to ensure the vehicle's survival. Ablative materials have been widely used in TPS design due to their ability to dissipate heat through mass loss. This report reviews the current state of research on ablative TPS design, focusing on the coupled field of aerodynamic heating and structural thermal stress. A comprehensive analysis of the thermal response of Ultra High Temperature Ceramics (UHTC) and Phenolic Impregnated Carbon Ablators (PICA) is presented, highlighting the importance of material selection, geometry, and trajectory optimization.

**Introduction**

Reentry vehicles, such as spacecraft and hypersonic vehicles, experience intense aerodynamic heating during atmospheric reentry, which can cause significant thermal stress and damage to the vehicle's structure. Ablative materials, which dissipate heat through mass loss, have been widely used in TPS design to mitigate this issue. However, the design of an effective ablative TPS requires a thorough understanding of the coupled field of aerodynamic heating and structural thermal stress.

**Ablative Materials**

Ablative materials, such as PICA and UHTC, have been extensively studied for their thermal protection properties. PICA, a lightweight ceramic ablator, has been used in various NASA missions, including the Stardust sample return capsule [1]. **Coupled Aerodynamic Heating and Structural Thermal Stress**

The thermal response of an ablative TPS is influenced by both aerodynamic heating and structural thermal stress. Aerodynamic heating causes the material to ablate, while structural thermal stress can lead to material failure. A coupled analysis of these two factors is essential to design an effective TPS. Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations have been used to investigate the thermal response of ablative materials, including the effects of surface catalysis and laminar-turbulent transition [3].

**Thermal Protection System Design**

The design of an ablative TPS involves selecting the optimal material, geometry, and trajectory to minimize heat flux and structural thermal stress. A parametric study of an ablative TPS for a reentry vehicle has been conducted using the Fully Implicit Ablation and Thermal Analysis Program (FIAT) [4]. The results show that the material properties, such as thermal conductivity and specific heat capacity, play a significant role in determining the thermal response of the TPS.
