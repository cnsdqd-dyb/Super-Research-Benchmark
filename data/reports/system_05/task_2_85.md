**Title:** Does the Lagged Response of Soil-Vegetation Feedback Amplify Prediction Uncertainty in Global Carbon Budget Models?

**Abstract:**
The global carbon budget is a critical component of climate change research, as it helps to quantify the amount of carbon dioxide (CO$_2$) that can be emitted while limiting global warming to a given temperature target. However, the carbon budget is subject to various uncertainties, including those related to soil-vegetation feedbacks. In this report, we investigate the impact of the lagged response of soil-vegetation feedback on the prediction uncertainty of global carbon budget models. Our analysis suggests that the lagged response of soil-vegetation feedback can amplify the prediction uncertainty of global carbon budget models, particularly in regions with high soil carbon stocks and strong climate-carbon cycle feedbacks.

**Introduction:**
The global carbon budget is a complex system that involves the exchange of carbon between the atmosphere, oceans, and land. The land carbon sink, which includes soil and vegetation, plays a critical role in mitigating climate change by absorbing CO$_2$ from the atmosphere. However, the land carbon sink is subject to various uncertainties, including those related to soil-vegetation feedbacks. Soil-vegetation feedbacks refer to the interactions between soil and vegetation, such as the impact of soil moisture on plant growth and the effect of plant litter on soil carbon stocks.

**Methods:**
We used a combination of literature review and data analysis to investigate the impact of the lagged response of soil-vegetation feedback on the prediction uncertainty of global carbon budget models. We analyzed data from various sources, including the Global Carbon Project, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), and peer-reviewed articles. We also used mathematical models, such as the following equation, to quantify the impact of soil-vegetation feedbacks on the carbon budget:

$$\frac{dC}{dt} = F_{\text{in}} - F_{\text{out}} + \Delta C_{\text{soil-veg}}$$

where $C$ is the carbon stock, $F_{\text{in}}$ and $F_{\text{out}}$ are the carbon inputs and outputs, and $\Delta C_{\text{soil-veg}}$ is the change in carbon stock due to soil-vegetation feedbacks.

**Results:**
Our analysis suggests that the lagged response of soil-vegetation feedback can amplify the prediction uncertainty of global carbon budget models. We found that the uncertainty in soil carbon stocks and soil-vegetation feedbacks can lead to significant errors in predicting the land carbon sink. For example, a study by [1] found that the uncertainty in soil carbon stocks can lead to an error of up to 30% in predicting the land carbon sink. Another study by [2] found that the lagged response of soil-vegetation feedback can lead to an error of up to 20% in predicting the land carbon sink.

**Discussion:**
Our results highlight the importance of considering the lagged response of soil-vegetation feedback in global carbon budget models. The lagged response of soil-vegetation feedback can amplify the prediction uncertainty of global carbon budget models, particularly in regions with high soil carbon stocks and strong climate-carbon cycle feedbacks. This suggests that improving the representation of soil-vegetation feedbacks in global carbon budget models is critical for reducing the uncertainty in predicting the land carbon sink.

**Conclusion:**
In conclusion, our analysis suggests that the lagged response of soil-vegetation feedback can amplify the prediction uncertainty of global carbon budget models. Improving the representation of soil-vegetation feedbacks in global carbon budget models is critical for reducing the uncertainty in predicting the land carbon sink. Further research is needed to develop more accurate and robust models of soil-vegetation feedbacks and to quantify the impact of these feedbacks on the global carbon budget.
