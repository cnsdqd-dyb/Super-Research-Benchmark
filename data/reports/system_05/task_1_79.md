**Abstract**

We conclude by outlining future research directions and highlighting the potential benefits of integrating machine learning and optimization techniques in multi-echelon inventory systems.

**Introduction**

Multi-echelon inventory systems are complex networks of inventory locations, each with its own inventory management policies and objectives. Optimizing decision boundaries for cross-regional transfers in these systems is essential to ensure that inventory levels are balanced across the network, minimizing costs and maximizing service levels. The decision boundary refers to the point at which inventory is transferred from one location to another, and optimizing this boundary is critical to ensuring that inventory is allocated efficiently across the network.

**Mathematical Formulation**

Let $x_i$ denote the inventory level at location $i$, $d_i$ denote the demand at location $i$, and $c_{ij}$ denote the transfer cost from location $i$ to location $j$. The objective function can be formulated as:

$$\min \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n c_{ij} x_i + \sum_{i=1}^n h_i x_i$$

subject to:

$$\sum_{j=1}^n x_j = \sum_{i=1}^n d_i$$

$$x_i \geq 0, \forall i$$

where $h_i$ denotes the holding cost at location $i$.
