**Title:** Optimizing the Trade-off between Early Warning Sensitivity and Resource Consumption in Sentinel Surveillance Systems using ILI Reporting Thresholds and Epidemic Prediction

**Abstract:**
Sentinel surveillance systems play a crucial role in detecting and responding to infectious disease outbreaks. However, these systems must balance the need for early warning sensitivity with the consumption of resources. This report explores how ILI reporting thresholds and epidemic prediction can be used to optimize this trade-off. We review existing literature on sentinel surveillance systems, ILI reporting, and epidemic prediction, and discuss the challenges and opportunities in optimizing the trade-off between early warning sensitivity and resource consumption.

**Introduction:**
Sentinel surveillance systems are designed to provide early warning of infectious disease outbreaks, allowing for timely intervention and resource allocation. However, these systems must balance the need for early warning sensitivity with the consumption of resources, such as laboratory testing, contact tracing, and vaccination. Influenza-like illness (ILI) reporting thresholds and epidemic prediction models can be used to optimize this trade-off.

**Background:**
ILI reporting thresholds are used to determine when an outbreak is occurring, and epidemic prediction models use historical data and machine learning algorithms to forecast the spread of disease. The performance of these models can be evaluated using metrics such as mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), mean square error (MSE), and root-mean-square error (RMSE) \cite{[1]}. The choice of threshold and model parameters can significantly impact the trade-off between early warning sensitivity and resource consumption.

**Methods:**

**Results:**

**Appendix:**

The following equations are used to calculate the performance metrics:

$$MAPE = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left| \frac{y_i - \hat{y_i}}{y_i} \right|$$

$$MSE = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y_i})^2$$

$$RMSE = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y_i})^2}$$

where $y_i$ is the actual value, $\hat{y_i}$ is the predicted value, and $n$ is the number of observations.
