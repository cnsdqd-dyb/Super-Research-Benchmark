**Title:** Constructing an Ontology of Audience Participation: A Study on Sensory Interaction in Digital Installations

**Abstract:**
This report explores the concept of sensory interaction in digital installations and its impact on audience participation. By examining the intersection of technology, art, and human experience, we aim to understand how digital installations construct an ontology of audience participation. Our research draws on a comprehensive review of existing literature, including studies on immersive art, interactive media, and multisensory experiences. We argue that sensory interaction in digital installations creates a unique ontological framework, where the boundaries between the audience, artwork, and technology are blurred, giving rise to a new paradigm of audience participation.

**Introduction:**
The advent of digital technology has revolutionized the way we experience and interact with art. Digital installations, in particular, have become increasingly popular, offering immersive and interactive experiences that engage multiple senses. However, the impact of these experiences on audience participation remains poorly understood. This report seeks to address this knowledge gap by investigating the role of sensory interaction in constructing an ontology of audience participation.

**Theoretical Framework:**
Our research is grounded in the concept of "bodies-in-code" [1], which refers to the hybrid dimension of presence, where the technological mediation of the interface and the media environment leads to the dissolution of boundaries between the body of the spectator-recipient and the physical space of the installation. We also draw on the idea of "virtual body" [2], which emerges through the phenomenalizing of the algorithm and the digitization of the physical body.

**Methodology:**
Our study employed a qualitative approach, involving a comprehensive review of existing literature on digital installations, immersive art, and multisensory experiences. We analyzed a range of sources, including academic articles, conference papers, and online publications, to identify key themes and concepts related to sensory interaction and audience participation.

**Findings:**
Our research reveals that sensory interaction in digital installations creates a unique ontological framework, where the audience, artwork, and technology are intertwined. We identified several key factors that contribute to this framework, including:

1. **Multisensory experiences:** Digital installations that engage multiple senses, such as sight, sound, touch, and smell, create a more immersive and engaging experience for the audience [3].
2. **Interactivity:** Interactive elements, such as touch screens, motion sensors, and virtual reality, enable the audience to participate actively in the artwork, blurring the boundaries between the audience and the artwork [4].
3. **Embodiment:** The use of sensory feedback and haptic technology creates a sense of embodiment, where the audience feels physically present in the artwork [5].
4. **Contextualization:** The use of contextual cues, such as soundscapes and visual effects, creates a sense of presence and immersion, drawing the audience into the artwork [6].

**Discussion:**
Our findings suggest that sensory interaction in digital installations constructs an ontology of audience participation, where the audience is no longer a passive observer but an active participant in the artwork. This ontological framework is characterized by the blurring of boundaries between the audience, artwork, and technology, giving rise to a new paradigm of audience participation.

**Conclusion:**
In conclusion, our research demonstrates that sensory interaction in digital installations plays a crucial role in constructing an ontology of audience participation. By engaging multiple senses and creating a sense of embodiment and presence, digital installations enable the audience to participate actively in the artwork, blurring the boundaries between the audience, artwork, and technology. As technology continues to evolve, it is essential to consider the impact of sensory interaction on audience participation, ensuring that digital installations are designed to create inclusive and engaging experiences for diverse audiences.

**Recommendations:**
Based on our findings, we recommend that digital installation designers and artists consider the following principles when creating immersive and interactive experiences:

1. **Multisensory design:** Incorporate multiple senses, such as sight, sound, touch, and smell, to create a more immersive and engaging experience.
2. **Interactivity:** Incorporate interactive elements, such as touch screens, motion sensors, and virtual reality, to enable the audience to participate actively in the artwork.
3. **Embodiment:** Use sensory feedback and haptic technology to create a sense of embodiment, where the audience feels physically present in the artwork.
4. **Contextualization:** Use contextual cues, such as soundscapes and visual effects, to create a sense of presence and immersion, drawing the audience into the artwork.

**Future Research:**
Future research should investigate the impact of sensory interaction on audience participation in digital installations, exploring the cognitive, emotional, and social effects of these experiences. Additionally, research should examine the role of technology in shaping the ontology of audience participation, including the potential risks and benefits of immersive and interactive experiences.
