**Abstract**

The advent of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) has sparked a debate on the optimal balance between privacy and traceability. Distributed ledger architectures, such as blockchain, have been proposed as a potential solution to achieve this balance. However, the complexity of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) has raised concerns about their impact on throughput caps. This report investigates the feasibility of CBDC distributed ledger architectures in optimizing the privacy-traceability balance and explores the limitations imposed by ZKP complexity.

**Introduction**

CBDCs are digital currencies issued by central banks, which can be designed to provide varying levels of privacy and traceability. The use of distributed ledger architectures, such as blockchain, can enhance the security and transparency of CBDC transactions. However, the need to balance privacy and traceability has become a pressing concern. ZKPs have been proposed as a solution to achieve this balance, but their complexity has raised concerns about their impact on throughput caps.

**Theoretical Framework**

The theoretical framework for this report is based on the concept of privacy and traceability in CBDC transactions. We define privacy as the ability of users to conceal their identities and transaction amounts, while traceability refers to the ability of authorities to track and monitor transactions. The balance between these two concepts is critical in CBDC design.

We use the following notation:

* $P$ denotes the set of all possible transactions
* $T$ denotes the set of all possible transaction amounts
* $I$ denotes the set of all possible user identities
* $Z$ denotes the set of all possible zero-knowledge proofs

The privacy-traceability balance can be represented as a trade-off between the following two objectives:

1. **Privacy**: Minimize the amount of information revealed about user identities and transaction amounts, i.e., $\min \{ |I|, |T| \}$
2. **Traceability**: Maximize the ability of authorities to track and monitor transactions, i.e., $\max \{ |P|, |Z| \}$

**Zero-Knowledge Proofs**

ZKPs are a cryptographic technique used to prove the validity of a statement without revealing any underlying information. In the context of CBDCs, ZKPs can be used to prove the validity of a transaction without revealing the user's identity or transaction amount.

The complexity of ZKPs can be represented as:

$$C(Z) = O(C \log C)$$

where $C$ is the size of the statement being proven.

**Distributed Ledger Architectures**

Distributed ledger architectures, such as blockchain, can be used to implement CBDCs. These architectures provide a secure and transparent way to record transactions, but they also introduce additional complexity and scalability challenges.

The throughput cap of a distributed ledger architecture can be represented as:

$$T_{cap} = \frac{1}{C(Z)} \cdot \frac{1}{B}$$

where $B$ is the block size.
