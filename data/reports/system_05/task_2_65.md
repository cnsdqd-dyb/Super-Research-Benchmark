**Title:** Balancing Prevention of Lesion Progression with Risk of Visual Field Defects in Diabetic Retinopathy Laser Treatment

**Abstract:**
Diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of blindness worldwide, and laser photocoagulation is a common treatment to prevent vision loss. However, laser treatment can cause visual field defects, which can impact daily activities such as driving. This report reviews the indications for diabetic retinopathy laser treatment, balancing the prevention of lesion progression with the risk of visual field defects.

**Introduction:**
Diabetic retinopathy is a complication of diabetes that can lead to vision loss if left untreated. Laser photocoagulation is a widely used treatment to prevent vision loss, but it can cause visual field defects. The goal of this report is to identify the indications for diabetic retinopathy laser treatment that balance the prevention of lesion progression with the risk of visual field defects.

**Methods:**
A comprehensive review of the literature was conducted to identify studies that evaluated the effectiveness and safety of laser photocoagulation in diabetic retinopathy. The search included articles from PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science.

**Results:**
The review identified several studies that evaluated the effectiveness and safety of laser photocoagulation in diabetic retinopathy. The results showed that pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP) is effective in reducing the risk of severe vision loss in patients with proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) [1]. However, PRP can cause visual field defects, including peripheral visual field loss and reduced night vision [2]. Focal photocoagulation is effective in treating macular edema, but it can cause central visual field loss [3].

**Discussion:**
The results of this review suggest that the indications for diabetic retinopathy laser treatment should be carefully considered to balance the prevention of lesion progression with the risk of visual field defects. PRP is effective in reducing the risk of severe vision loss in patients with PDR, but it can cause visual field defects. Focal photocoagulation is effective in treating macular edema, but it can cause central visual field loss. The decision to use laser photocoagulation should be based on the individual patient's risk factors and the severity of their diabetic retinopathy.
