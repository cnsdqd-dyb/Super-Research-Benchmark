**Mechanisms Forming Satisfaction Evaluations from the Gap between Planning Expectations and Actual Experience**

**Abstract**

Satisfaction evaluations are a crucial aspect of human experience, influenced by the gap between planning expectations and actual experience. This report delves into the mechanisms underlying the formation of satisfaction evaluations, exploring the role of cognitive dissonance, expectancy-disconfirmation theory, self-discrepancy theory, and affective misforecasting. We examine the neural mechanisms involved in the Critic and Proposer components, which estimate the value of outcomes relative to expectations and propose plans based on situation and goal inputs. Our analysis reveals that satisfaction evaluations are influenced by the interaction between cognitive and affective processes, including the experience of dissonance, the formation of expectations, and the perception of performance.

**Introduction**

Satisfaction evaluations are a complex phenomenon, influenced by various cognitive and affective processes. The gap between planning expectations and actual experience plays a significant role in shaping satisfaction evaluations. This report aims to investigate the mechanisms underlying the formation of satisfaction evaluations, with a focus on the neural and psychological processes involved.

**Theoretical Background**

Cognitive dissonance theory [1] posits that individuals experience dissonance when their expectations are not met, leading to a motivation to reduce dissonance. Expectancy-disconfirmation theory [2] suggests that satisfaction is determined by the degree to which performance fulfills individual performance expectations. Self-discrepancy theory [3] proposes that individuals experience discomfort when their actual self-concept does not match their ideal or ought self-guides. Affective misforecasting [4] refers to the tendency to mispredict the intensity or duration of emotional experiences.

**Neural Mechanisms**

The Critic component, composed of subcortical areas, estimates the value of outcomes relative to expectations, using a reward-prediction error signal [5]. The Proposer component, involving cortical areas, proposes plans based on situation and goal inputs, integrating learned synaptic weights with current external inputs [6]. The interaction between the Critic and Proposer components is crucial in forming satisfaction evaluations.

**Mathematical Modeling**

Let $E$ represent the expected outcome, $A$ the actual outcome, and $S$ the satisfaction evaluation. The expectancy-disconfirmation theory can be mathematically represented as:

$$S = \beta_0 + \beta_1 (A - E) + \epsilon$$

where $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$ are parameters, and $\epsilon$ is an error term.

The self-discrepancy theory can be represented as:

$$S = \beta_0 + \beta_1 (I - A) + \beta_2 (O - A) + \epsilon$$

where $I$ represents the ideal self-guide, $O$ the ought self-guide, and $A$ the actual self-concept.

**Conclusion**

Satisfaction evaluations are formed through the interaction between cognitive and affective processes, including the experience of dissonance, the formation of expectations, and the perception of performance. The Critic and Proposer components play a crucial role in estimating the value of outcomes relative to expectations and proposing plans based on situation and goal inputs. The mathematical models presented provide a framework for understanding the mechanisms underlying satisfaction evaluations. Future research should aim to integrate these models with neural and psychological processes to provide a comprehensive understanding of satisfaction evaluations.
