**Designing Flexible Transmission Mechanisms for Minimally Invasive Surgical Instruments: Balancing Operation Precision with Instrument Rigidity for Optimal Force Feedback**

**Abstract**

Minimally invasive surgical (MIS) instruments have revolutionized the field of surgery, offering reduced trauma, faster recovery times, and improved therapeutic outcomes. However, the design of these instruments poses significant challenges, particularly in balancing operation precision with instrument rigidity to achieve optimal force feedback. This report reviews the current state of the art in MIS instrument design, highlighting the importance of flexible transmission mechanisms in achieving precise and intuitive control. We discuss the key design requirements, including enhanced dexterity, natural force feedback, and modularity, and present a comprehensive analysis of the trade-offs between instrument rigidity, flexibility, and force feedback.

**Introduction**

Minimally invasive surgery has become a cornerstone of modern surgical practice, with robotic-assisted systems (RAS) playing an increasingly important role [1]. The da Vinci system, developed by Intuitive Surgical, is a prominent example of a RAS that has transformed the field of MIS [2]. However, the design of MIS instruments remains a significant challenge, particularly in achieving a balance between operation precision and instrument rigidity.

**Design Requirements**

The design of MIS instruments must satisfy several key requirements, including:

1. **Enhanced dexterity**: The ability to perform complex tissue manipulations with precision and accuracy [3].
2. **Natural force feedback**: The ability to transmit forces from the instrument tip to the surgeon's hand, allowing for intuitive control and tactile sensation [4].
3. **Modularity**: The ability to interchange tool tips and adapt to different surgical procedures [5].
4. **Instrument rigidity**: The ability to maintain precise control and stability during surgical procedures [6].

**Flexible Transmission Mechanisms**

Flexible transmission mechanisms are critical in achieving the desired balance between operation precision and instrument rigidity. These mechanisms can be designed using various materials and technologies, including:

1. **Cable-driven systems**: Using cables and pulleys to transmit motion and forces [7].
2. **Pneumatic and hydraulic systems**: Using compressed air or fluid to actuate instruments [8].
3. **Shape memory alloys**: Using materials that can change shape in response to temperature changes [9].

**Mathematical Modeling**

$$\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{K} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B} \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{C} \mathbf{a}$$

**Conclusion**

The design of flexible transmission mechanisms for MIS instruments is a complex task that requires careful consideration of multiple design requirements. By balancing operation precision with instrument rigidity, designers can create instruments that provide optimal force feedback and intuitive control. Future research should focus on developing new materials and technologies that can enhance the performance of MIS instruments, such as advanced sensor systems and artificial intelligence algorithms [11].
