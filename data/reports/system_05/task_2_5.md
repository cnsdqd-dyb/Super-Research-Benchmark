**Title:** Relationship between Phase Transformation Hysteresis Effect in Shape Memory Alloys and Coupling of Fatigue Life with Energy Conversion Efficiency during Thermomechanical Cycling

**Abstract:**
Shape memory alloys (SMAs) exhibit a unique ability to recover their original shape after deformation upon heating, making them attractive for various applications. However, their fatigue life and energy conversion efficiency are crucial factors that influence their performance. This report investigates the relationship between the phase transformation hysteresis effect in SMAs and the coupling of fatigue life with energy conversion efficiency during thermomechanical cycling. We review the existing literature and provide an in-depth analysis of the underlying mechanisms, highlighting the importance of considering the thermomechanical coupling and energy-based criteria in predicting the fatigue life of SMAs.

**Introduction:**
SMAs undergo a reversible martensitic transformation, which is accompanied by a hysteresis loop in the stress-strain curve. The area of this loop represents the energy dissipated during the transformation, which affects the fatigue life and energy conversion efficiency of the alloy. The phase transformation hysteresis effect is influenced by various factors, including the alloy composition, microstructure, and thermomechanical loading conditions.

**Literature Review:**

These criteria consider the energy dissipated during the transformation and provide a more accurate prediction of the fatigue life compared to traditional stress- or strain-based criteria.
