**Title:** Balancing Therapeutic Response Thresholds with Seizure Risk Safety Boundaries in rTMS Treatment for Depression

**Abstract:**
Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) is a non-invasive brain stimulation technique used to treat depression, particularly treatment-resistant depression (TRD). While rTMS has shown efficacy in improving depressive symptoms, its therapeutic response is highly variable, and seizure risk remains a concern. This report reviews the current literature on rTMS treatment for depression, focusing on the indications for balancing therapeutic response thresholds with seizure risk safety boundaries.

**Introduction:**
Depression is a prevalent and debilitating mental health disorder, with a significant proportion of patients experiencing treatment-resistant depression (TRD) [1]. rTMS has emerged as a promising treatment option for TRD, with high-frequency stimulation of the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) being the most commonly used protocol [2]. However, the therapeutic response to rTMS is highly variable, and seizure risk remains a concern, particularly in patients with a history of seizures or those taking medications that lower the seizure threshold [3].

**Therapeutic Response Thresholds:**
The therapeutic response to rTMS is influenced by various factors, including stimulation frequency, intensity, and duration, as well as individual patient characteristics, such as age, sex, and symptom severity [4]. High-frequency rTMS (10-20 Hz) has been shown to be effective in improving depressive symptoms, particularly in patients with TRD [5]. However, the optimal stimulation parameters for maximizing therapeutic response while minimizing seizure risk remain unclear.

**Seizure Risk Safety Boundaries:**
The risk of seizure associated with rTMS is estimated to be low, with a standardized seizure risk of 7 in 100,000 treatment sessions [6]. However, certain factors can increase the risk of seizure, including a history of seizures, use of medications that lower the seizure threshold, and concurrent use of antidepressants [7]. The FDA has established guidelines for safe rTMS use, including maximum train duration limits and intensity levels [8].

**Balancing Therapeutic Response Thresholds with Seizure Risk Safety Boundaries:**
To balance therapeutic response thresholds with seizure risk safety boundaries, clinicians must carefully consider individual patient factors, including medical history, concurrent medications, and symptom severity. The use of supervised machine learning algorithms can help predict treatment response and identify patients at high risk of seizure [9]. Additionally, the use of personalized rTMS protocols, such as theta-burst stimulation (TBS), may help optimize therapeutic response while minimizing seizure risk [10].

**Conclusion:**
rTMS is a promising treatment option for TRD, but its therapeutic response is highly variable, and seizure risk remains a concern. By carefully considering individual patient factors and using personalized rTMS protocols, clinicians can balance therapeutic response thresholds with seizure risk safety boundaries, optimizing treatment outcomes while minimizing risks. Further research is needed to establish optimal stimulation parameters and to develop more effective strategies for predicting treatment response and minimizing seizure risk.
