## Introduction

The formation of destination cognition through the interaction of virtual previews and on-site visits is a complex process that has garnered significant attention in the tourism industry. With the advent of virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) technologies, tourists can now experience destinations remotely, influencing their travel intentions and behaviors. This report aims to provide an in-depth analysis of how virtual previews and on-site visits interact to form destination cognition, with a focus on the role of VR and AR technologies.

## Literature Review

Previous studies have shown that virtual experiences can significantly enhance travel intention by offering immersive, virtual experiences that promote destination appeal and accessibility [1]. The use of VR devices creates more immersive and authentic virtual environments that stimulate real-world emotional states and feelings [2]. Visual stimulation has a stronger impact on tourist emotions compared to other aspects such as auditory and tactile [3]. The integration of AI and immersive digital platforms is revolutionizing the way visitors interact with cultural instruments and practices, enabling personalized, adaptive, and immersive experiences [4].

The cognitive image of a destination has a significant positive effect on tourists' behavioral intentions [5]. Destination image is the antecedent of tourists' behavioral intentions and the starting point for determining tourism decisions [6]. The formation of destination image is influenced by various factors, including prior destination experience, online information search behavior, and social media [7].

## Theoretical Framework

The theoretical framework for this study is based on the Information Systems (IS) Success Model, which posits that system quality, information quality, and user satisfaction are the key to digital system success [8]. Let $D$ be the destination cognition, $V$ be the virtual preview, $O$ be the on-site visit, $E$ be the enjoyment, $I$ be the immersion, and $S$ be the system quality. The relationships between these variables can be represented by the following equations:

$$D = \beta_1 V + \beta_2 O + \epsilon$$
$$E = \beta_3 V + \beta_4 O + \epsilon$$
$$I = \beta_5 V + \beta_6 O + \epsilon$$
$$S = \beta_7 V + \beta_8 O + \epsilon$$

where $\beta_1, \beta_2, \beta_3, \beta_4, \beta_5, \beta_6, \beta_7, \beta_8$ are the coefficients, and $\epsilon$ is the error term.
