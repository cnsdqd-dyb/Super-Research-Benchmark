**Title:** Optimizing Home Management Strategy for Chronic Heart Failure: Balancing NYHA Functional Classification Thresholds with Quality of Life Improvements

**Abstract:**
Chronic heart failure (CHF) is a complex and debilitating condition that affects millions of people worldwide. The New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification system is widely used to assess the severity of heart failure and guide treatment decisions. However, there is a need to balance NYHA classification thresholds with quality of life improvements in the management of CHF. This report reviews the current literature on home management strategies for CHF, with a focus on optimizing NYHA functional classification thresholds and improving quality of life.

**Introduction:**
CHF is a leading cause of hospitalization and mortality worldwide, with significant economic and social burdens [1]. The NYHA functional classification system is a widely used tool for assessing the severity of heart failure, with four classes ranging from no limitation of physical activity (Class I) to symptoms of heart failure at rest (Class IV) [2]. However, there is a need to balance NYHA classification thresholds with quality of life improvements in the management of CHF.

**Methods:**
A comprehensive literature review was conducted to identify studies that evaluated home management strategies for CHF, with a focus on optimizing NYHA functional classification thresholds and improving quality of life. The search included studies published in English between 2010 and 2022, using databases such as PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science.

**Results:**
The literature review identified several studies that evaluated the effectiveness of home management strategies for CHF, including telemedicine, remote patient monitoring, and personalized treatment plans [3-6]. These studies demonstrated significant improvements in NYHA functional classification, quality of life, and hospitalization rates. For example, a study published in the Journal of Telemedicine and Telecare found that a telemedical care program significantly improved NYHA functional classification and reduced hospitalization rates in patients with CHF [7].

**Discussion:**
The results of this literature review suggest that home management strategies for CHF can be optimized by balancing NYHA functional classification thresholds with quality of life improvements. Personalized treatment plans, telemedicine, and remote patient monitoring can improve NYHA functional classification, quality of life, and hospitalization rates in patients with CHF. The use of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning algorithms can also enhance the effectiveness of home management strategies for CHF [8].
