**Introduction**

Glucocorticoids are synthetic analogs of natural steroid hormones produced by the adrenal cortex. They are commonly used to treat various inflammatory and immunologic conditions, including asthma, rheumatoid arthritis, and inflammatory bowel disease [1]. However, long-term use of glucocorticoids can lead to adrenal suppression, a condition where the adrenal glands are unable to produce sufficient cortisol [2]. Adrenal suppression can result in a range of symptoms, including fatigue, weakness, and hypotension, and can increase the risk of infections and other complications [3].

2. **Duration of treatment**: Longer durations of treatment with glucocorticoids increase the risk of adrenal suppression [6]. Treatment for more than 3-4 weeks is associated with a higher risk of adrenal suppression [7].
3. **Formulation and route of administration**: Different formulations and routes of administration of glucocorticoids can affect the risk of adrenal suppression. For example, oral glucocorticoids are more likely to cause adrenal suppression than inhaled or topical glucocorticoids [8].
4. **Frequency and timing of administration**: Administering glucocorticoids in multiple doses throughout the day can increase the risk of adrenal suppression [9]. Evening doses of glucocorticoids can suppress the normal early morning surge of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) secretion, resulting in greater adrenal suppression [10].

**Individual Patient Characteristics**

Individual patient characteristics, such as age, sex, and underlying medical conditions, can also influence the risk of adrenal suppression [11].
