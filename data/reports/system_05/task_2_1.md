**Abstract**

In high-speed serial data streams, dispersion compensation is crucial to maintain signal integrity and ensure reliable data transmission. However, the computational complexity of dispersion compensation algorithms can be a significant bottleneck. We review various dispersion compensation techniques, including dispersion compensating fibers (DCF), fiber Bragg gratings (FBG), electronic dispersion compensation (EDC), and digital filters. **Introduction**

Optical fiber communication systems are widely used for high-speed data transmission due to their high bandwidth and low attenuation. However, dispersion, which causes pulse broadening, is a major limitation in these systems. Dispersion compensation is essential to maintain signal integrity and ensure reliable data transmission. The optimal trade-off between computational complexity and signal integrity constraints is critical in designing efficient dispersion compensation algorithms.

**Dispersion Compensation Techniques**

Several dispersion compensation techniques are available, including:

1. **Dispersion Compensating Fibers (DCF)**: DCFs have negative dispersion, which can be used to compensate for the positive dispersion of the transmission fiber.
2. **Fiber Bragg Gratings (FBG)**: FBGs can be used to compensate for dispersion by reflecting specific wavelengths.
3. **Electronic Dispersion Compensation (EDC)**: EDC uses digital signal processing to compensate for dispersion.
4. **Digital Filters**: Digital filters can be used to compensate for dispersion by filtering out the distorted signals.

**Modulation Formats**

The choice of modulation format can significantly impact dispersion compensation. Return-to-zero (RZ) and non-return-to-zero (NRZ) are two common modulation formats used in optical fiber communication systems. RZ modulation format has a higher peak power and a higher signal-to-noise ratio, making it more suitable for high-speed data transmission.
