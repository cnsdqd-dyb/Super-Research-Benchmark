**Abstract**

Metabolic syndrome (MS) is a clustering of various metabolic risk factors associated with insulin resistance, and its prevention and management are crucial for reducing the risk of chronic diseases such as type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Weight loss is a key component of MS management, but it must be balanced against the risk of malnutrition. This report reviews the current evidence on the nutritional prevention of MS, with a focus on the clinical goal of weight loss and the risk of malnutrition when adjusting diet based on insulin resistance.

**Introduction**

Metabolic syndrome is a complex condition characterized by a cluster of metabolic risk factors, including central obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and insulin resistance [1]. The prevalence of MS is increasing worldwide, and it is estimated that almost 25% of the world's population has MS [2]. Insulin resistance is a key component of MS, and it is characterized by a reduced response to insulin, leading to hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia [3].

**Weight Loss and Malnutrition Risk**

Weight loss is a key component of MS management, and it can be achieved through a combination of dietary changes and increased physical activity [4]. However, weight loss must be balanced against the risk of malnutrition, particularly in individuals with a high risk of malnutrition such as the elderly and those with chronic diseases [5]. Malnutrition can lead to a range of health problems, including weakness, fatigue, and increased risk of infections [6].

**Dietary Approaches to Weight Loss and Insulin Resistance**

Several dietary approaches have been shown to be effective for weight loss and improving insulin resistance, including low-carbohydrate diets, low-fat diets, and Mediterranean-style diets [7-9]. Low-carbohydrate diets have been shown to be particularly effective for weight loss and improving insulin resistance in individuals with MS [10-12]. However, these diets may also increase the risk of malnutrition, particularly if they are not well-planned [13].
