**Introduction**

The macroscopic fundamental diagram (MFD) is a graphical representation of the relationship between traffic flow, density, and speed in a traffic network [1]. It is a powerful tool for understanding the behavior of urban traffic networks and has been widely used in traffic engineering and planning. However, the MFD is typically based on linear or simplified assumptions about the relationship between density and velocity, which may not accurately capture the complex behavior of traffic flow near phase transition points.

**Background**

The fundamental diagram of traffic flow is a diagram that gives a relation between road traffic flux (vehicles/hour) and the traffic density (vehicles/km) [2]. A macroscopic traffic model involving traffic flux, traffic density, and velocity forms the basis of the fundamental diagram. The MFD can be used to predict the capability of a road system or its behavior when applying inflow regulation or speed limits.

Several models have been proposed to describe the relationship between traffic flow, density, and velocity, including the Greenshields, Greenburg, Underwood, and Northwestern models [3]. These models are based on different assumptions about the relationship between density and velocity and have been widely used in traffic flow modeling. However, they have limitations in capturing nonlinear behavior, particularly near phase transition points.

**Nonlinear Density-Velocity Characteristics**

Nonlinear density-velocity characteristics refer to the complex relationships between traffic density and velocity that arise near phase transition points. These relationships are characterized by abrupt changes in traffic flow behavior, such as the transition from free flow to congested flow [4]. Nonlinear density-velocity characteristics can be captured using more advanced models, such as the Colombo phase transition model [5], which describes the transition between free flow and congested flow using a set of coupled partial differential equations.

Nonlinear behavior can lead to abrupt changes in traffic flow, which can affect the accuracy of MFD predictions [6].
