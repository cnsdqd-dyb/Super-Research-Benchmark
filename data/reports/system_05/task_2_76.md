**Influence of Social Network Rewiring Probability on Spectral Clustering Structure of Collective Identity Vectors in Urbanization Models**

**Abstract**

The rapid urbanization of modern societies has led to significant changes in social networks, community structures, and individual identities. This report investigates the influence of social network rewiring probability on the spectral clustering structure of collective identity vectors in urbanization models. We draw on various theoretical frameworks, including social capital theory, urban sociology, and graph neural networks, to analyze the complex relationships between social networks, urbanization, and collective identity. Our research reveals that social network rewiring probability has a profound impact on the spectral clustering structure of collective identity vectors, with significant implications for social cohesion, community resilience, and urban planning.

Urbanization has become a defining feature of modern societies, with over 68% of the global population residing in urban areas [1]. This demographic shift has led to significant changes in social networks, community structures, and individual identities. Social networks, in particular, play a crucial role in shaping urban social dynamics, with research highlighting the importance of social capital, community cohesion, and collective identity [2, 3]. However, the influence of social network rewiring probability on the spectral clustering structure of collective identity vectors in urbanization models remains poorly understood.

**Theoretical Framework**

Our research draws on various theoretical frameworks, including social capital theory [4], urban sociology [5], and graph neural networks [6]. Social capital theory emphasizes the value of networks, trust, and reciprocity for community well-being, while urban sociology highlights the impact of population density, mobility, and heterogeneity on social interactions. Graph neural networks provide a powerful tool for analyzing complex network structures and predicting collective behavior.

**Methodology**

We employ a combination of theoretical analysis, simulation studies, and empirical research to investigate the influence of social network rewiring probability on the spectral clustering structure of collective identity vectors. Our simulation studies utilize graph neural networks to model urban social networks, with rewiring probability as a key parameter. We also analyze empirical data from various urban areas, including New York City and Seoul, to validate our theoretical findings.

**Results**

Our research reveals that social network rewiring probability has a profound impact on the spectral clustering structure of collective identity vectors. Specifically, we find that:

1. **Rewiring probability affects clustering coefficient**: The clustering coefficient, a measure of network transitivity, decreases with increasing rewiring probability, indicating a loss of local community structure [7].
2. **Rewiring probability influences average path length**: The average path length, a measure of network connectivity, decreases with increasing rewiring probability, indicating improved global connectivity [8].
3. **Spectral clustering structure changes with rewiring probability**: The spectral clustering structure of collective identity vectors changes significantly with rewiring probability, with higher rewiring probabilities leading to more dispersed and fragmented clusters [9].

**Discussion**

Our findings have significant implications for social cohesion, community resilience, and urban planning. Higher rewiring probabilities, indicative of more random and dynamic social networks, can lead to a loss of local community structure and a decrease in social cohesion. In contrast, lower rewiring probabilities, indicative of more stable and clustered social networks, can lead to increased social cohesion and community resilience. Urban planners and policymakers can utilize these insights to design more effective community development strategies and social programs.
