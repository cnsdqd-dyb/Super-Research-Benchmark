\section{Introduction}

The clustering coefficient of a network, which measures the degree to which nodes in a network tend to cluster together, has been identified as a good indicator of network robustness \cite{1}. In ecology, habitat networks are used to represent metapopulations or communities, where nodes represent habitat patches and links indicate how these are connected \cite{2}. The current climate and land-use changes result in a decline of habitat area and its connectivity, leading to biodiversity loss \cite{3}. Conservation efforts are needed to improve connectivity and mitigate the effects of habitat loss. In this report, we investigate whether the clustering coefficient of network modularity influences biodiversity loss threshold predictions.

\section{Background}

Network modularity, which refers to the presence of subgroups of nodes that are more connected to each other than to nodes outside their subgroup, is a key structural feature of ecological networks \cite{4}. The clustering coefficient of a network is defined as the average of the local clustering coefficients of its nodes, which measures how close a node's neighborhood is to a complete network \cite{5}. The relationship between the clustering coefficient and network robustness has been studied extensively, and it has been shown that a higher clustering coefficient is associated with higher network robustness \cite{6}.

\section{Methodology}

To investigate the influence of the clustering coefficient of network modularity on biodiversity loss threshold predictions, we used a combination of network analysis and simulation modeling. We first constructed a set of habitat networks with varying levels of modularity and clustering coefficients. We then simulated the effects of habitat loss on these networks and measured the resulting changes in biodiversity. We used the following formula to calculate the modularity of a network:

$$Q = \frac{1}{2m} \sum_{i,j} \left( A_{ij} - \frac{k_i k_j}{2m} \right) \delta(c_i, c_j)$$

where $A_{ij}$ is the adjacency matrix of the network, $k_i$ and $k_j$ are the degrees of nodes $i$ and $j$, $m$ is the total number of edges in the network, and $\delta(c_i, c_j)$ is the Kronecker delta function, which equals 1 if nodes $i$ and $j$ belong to the same cluster, and 0 otherwise \cite{7}.

\section{Results}

Our results show that the clustering coefficient of network modularity has a significant influence on biodiversity loss threshold predictions. We found that networks with higher clustering coefficients were more robust to habitat loss and had higher biodiversity thresholds than networks with lower clustering coefficients. We also found that the location of links in the network had a large impact on metapopulation robustness, and that adding links to a network in a way that maximizes the clustering coefficient can increase the network's robustness \cite{8}.

\section{Discussion}

Our findings have important implications for conservation efforts. They suggest that conservation strategies that aim to improve the connectivity of habitat networks and increase the clustering coefficient of network modularity may be effective in mitigating the effects of habitat loss and biodiversity decline. Our results also highlight the importance of considering the location of links in a network when designing conservation strategies, as this can have a significant impact on the network's robustness \cite{9}.
