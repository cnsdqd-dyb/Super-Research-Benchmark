## Abstract

The introduction of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) has sparked a debate about the potential benefits and risks associated with their implementation. One of the key features of CBDCs is the use of smart contracts, which enable programmability and automation of various aspects of the currency. This report examines the question of whether CBDC smart contract policy tools can balance money supply control and risk isolation, or if programmable money logic risks amplifying policy accidents.

## Introduction

CBDCs are digital forms of currency issued by central banks, which can be used for various purposes, including retail payments, wholesale settlements, and monetary policy implementation. The use of smart contracts in CBDCs allows for the automation of various processes, such as payment clearing and settlement, and can also enable the implementation of monetary policy tools, such as interest rates and reserve requirements.

## Literature Review

The literature on CBDCs and smart contracts highlights the potential benefits of programmable money, including increased efficiency, reduced costs, and improved financial inclusion [1]. However, it also notes the potential risks associated with the use of smart contracts, including the risk of cyber attacks, data breaches, and unintended consequences of automated decision-making [2].
