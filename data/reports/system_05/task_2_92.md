**What Determines the Authentication Intensity Hierarchy in Account Security Protection?**

**Abstract**

The authentication intensity hierarchy in account security protection is a critical aspect of ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of sensitive information. This report provides an in-depth analysis of the factors that determine the authentication intensity hierarchy, including the types of authentication factors, authentication methods, and assurance levels. We also examine the role of multi-factor authentication, phishing-resistant methods, and passwordless authentication in enhancing account security.

**Introduction**

Authentication is the process of verifying the identity of a user, device, or system. The authentication intensity hierarchy refers to the level of assurance that an authentication method provides, with higher levels indicating greater confidence in the authenticity of the user or device. The hierarchy is determined by the type and number of authentication factors used, as well as the assurance level of the authentication method.

**Types of Authentication Factors**

There are three primary types of authentication factors: knowledge factors (something you know), possession factors (something you have), and inherence factors (something you are) [1]. Knowledge factors include passwords, PINs, and security questions, while possession factors include security keys, tokens, and mobile devices. Inherence factors include biometric authentication methods such as fingerprints, facial recognition, and voice recognition.

**Authentication Methods**

Authentication methods can be categorized into three main types: single-factor authentication (SFA), multi-factor authentication (MFA), and passwordless authentication. SFA uses a single factor, such as a password or PIN, to authenticate a user. MFA uses two or more factors, such as a password and a biometric scan, to provide an additional layer of security. Passwordless authentication uses alternative methods, such as security keys or biometric authentication, to eliminate the need for passwords.

**Assurance Levels**

The assurance level of an authentication method refers to the level of confidence that the method provides in the authenticity of the user or device. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) defines three assurance levels: Authenticator Assurance Level (AAL) 1, AAL 2, and AAL 3 [2]. AAL 1 provides some assurance that the claimant controls an authenticator bound to the subscriber's account, while AAL 2 provides high confidence that the claimant controls authenticator(s) bound to the subscriber's account. AAL 3 provides very high confidence that the claimant controls authenticator(s) bound to the subscriber's account.

**Multi-Factor Authentication**

MFA is a critical component of the authentication intensity hierarchy, as it provides an additional layer of security by requiring two or more factors to authenticate a user. MFA can be implemented using a variety of methods, including passwords, biometric authentication, and security keys. The use of MFA can significantly reduce the risk of unauthorized access to sensitive information.

**Phishing-Resistant Methods**

Phishing-resistant methods, such as FIDO2 security keys and Windows Hello for Business, provide a high level of assurance that the user is who they claim to be. These methods use cryptographic techniques to prevent phishing attacks and provide a secure way to authenticate users.

**Passwordless Authentication**

Passwordless authentication methods, such as security keys and biometric authentication, eliminate the need for passwords and provide a secure way to authenticate users. These methods use alternative factors, such as possession or inherence factors, to verify the user's identity.

**Conclusion**

In conclusion, the authentication intensity hierarchy in account security protection is determined by the type and number of authentication factors used, as well as the assurance level of the authentication method. MFA, phishing-resistant methods, and passwordless authentication are critical components of the hierarchy, providing an additional layer of security and reducing the risk of unauthorized access to sensitive information. By understanding the factors that determine the authentication intensity hierarchy, organizations can implement effective authentication methods to protect their sensitive information.

**Mathematical Model**

The authentication intensity hierarchy can be represented mathematically using the following equation:
