This report explores the complex relationship between these two concepts, examining the impact of tourism on local cultures and traditions. Cultural tourism has become a significant aspect of the tourism industry, with many destinations promoting their unique cultural heritage to attract visitors [1]. Folk activities, such as traditional rituals and festivals, are an essential part of this cultural heritage, offering tourists an opportunity to experience the local culture firsthand. However, the increasing popularity of cultural tourism has raised concerns about the impact of tourism on local cultures and traditions, particularly with regards to ritual integrity and tourist interaction comfort [2].

**The Concept of Authenticity:**
Authenticity is a crucial aspect of cultural tourism, referring to the genuine and traditional nature of cultural practices and artifacts [3]. However, the concept of authenticity is complex and can be subjective, depending on the perspectives of both local communities and tourists [4]. Our research highlights the importance of authenticity in maintaining ritual integrity, as it ensures that cultural practices are preserved and respected.

**Cultural Sensitivity and Community Involvement:**
Cultural sensitivity and community involvement are essential in maintaining ritual integrity while ensuring tourist comfort [5]. Local communities must be involved in the development and management of cultural tourism initiatives, ensuring that their cultural heritage is respected and preserved [6]. Tourists must also be educated about the cultural significance of folk activities and the importance of respecting local traditions and customs [7].

**The Boundary between Ritual Integrity and Tourist Interaction Comfort:**
Establishing a boundary between ritual integrity and tourist interaction comfort requires a nuanced approach, considering the perspectives of both local communities and tourists. This boundary must be flexible, allowing for the evolution of cultural practices while maintaining their authenticity and cultural significance [8]. Our research suggests that this boundary can be established through the implementation of sustainable tourism practices, such as controlled visitor flows, respectful protocols, and equitable economic distribution [9].
