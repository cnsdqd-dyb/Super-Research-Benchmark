**How are data quality and attribution uncertainty handled in climate agreement verification using remote sensing?**

## Abstract

Climate agreements rely on accurate and reliable data to verify the effectiveness of mitigation efforts. Remote sensing technologies play a crucial role in providing data for climate monitoring and verification. However, data quality and attribution uncertainty are significant challenges in using remote sensing for climate agreement verification. This report reviews the current state of data quality and attribution uncertainty in remote sensing-based climate monitoring and verification, and discusses the methods and techniques used to address these challenges.

## Introduction

Climate change is a pressing global issue, and accurate monitoring and verification of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions are essential for evaluating the effectiveness of climate mitigation efforts [1]. Remote sensing technologies, such as satellite and airborne sensors, provide valuable data for climate monitoring and verification [2]. However, data quality and attribution uncertainty are significant challenges in using remote sensing for climate agreement verification [3].

## Data Quality

Data quality is a critical aspect of remote sensing-based climate monitoring and verification. The quality of remote sensing data can be affected by various factors, including sensor characteristics, atmospheric conditions, and data processing algorithms [4]. To address these challenges, various data quality assessment and control methods are used, such as data validation, calibration, and verification [5].

## Attribution Uncertainty

Attribution uncertainty refers to the uncertainty associated with attributing observed changes in the climate system to specific causes, such as human activities or natural variability [6]. In remote sensing-based climate monitoring and verification, attribution uncertainty can arise from various sources, including data quality issues, model uncertainties, and limitations in understanding the underlying climate processes [7].

## Methods for Addressing Data Quality and Attribution Uncertainty

Several methods and techniques are used to address data quality and attribution uncertainty in remote sensing-based climate monitoring and verification. These include:

1. **Data fusion**: Combining data from multiple sources, such as satellite and ground-based measurements, to improve data quality and reduce uncertainty [8].
2. **Machine learning**: Using machine learning algorithms to improve data quality, reduce noise, and enhance the accuracy of remote sensing data [9].
3. **Uncertainty quantification**: Quantifying the uncertainty associated with remote sensing data and models to provide a more accurate representation of the climate system [10].
4. **Model calibration**: Calibrating models using field measurements to reduce uncertainties and improve the accuracy of remote sensing-based climate monitoring and verification [11].

## Case Studies

Several case studies demonstrate the application of remote sensing technologies for climate monitoring and verification. For example, the Climate TRACE project uses satellite data and machine learning algorithms to estimate GHG emissions from various sources, including oil and gas production, shipping, and aviation [12]. Another example is the use of remote sensing data for monitoring forest cover changes and estimating carbon emissions from deforestation and land degradation [13].

## Conclusion

Data quality and attribution uncertainty are significant challenges in using remote sensing for climate agreement verification. However, various methods and techniques are available to address these challenges, including data fusion, machine learning, uncertainty quantification, and model calibration. By applying these methods and techniques, remote sensing technologies can provide accurate and reliable data for climate monitoring and verification, supporting the evaluation of climate mitigation efforts and the development of effective climate policies.
