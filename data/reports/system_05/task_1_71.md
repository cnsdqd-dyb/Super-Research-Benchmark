**How Uncertainty Propagation in Cumulative Effect Frameworks Affects Environmental Permit Threshold Setting**

## Abstract

Cumulative effect frameworks are used to assess the combined impact of multiple human activities and natural processes on the environment. However, uncertainty propagation in these frameworks can significantly affect environmental permit threshold setting. This report reviews the current state of knowledge on cumulative effect assessment and uncertainty propagation, and explores how uncertainty affects the setting of environmental permit thresholds.

## Introduction

Cumulative effects, also referred to as cumulative environmental effects and cumulative impacts, are changes to the environment caused by the combined impact of past, present, and future human activities and natural processes \cite{1}. Cumulative effects assessment (CEA) is a sub-discipline of environmental impact assessment (EIA) that evaluates the collective effects of actions on the environment \cite{2}. However, CEA is challenging due to inadequate environmental baseline data, complex ecological processes, and the large scale at which human activities occur \cite{3}.

## Uncertainty Propagation in Cumulative Effect Frameworks

Uncertainty propagation in cumulative effect frameworks arises from various sources, including:

1. **Random and systematic uncertainty**: Uncertainty in data and models used to predict cumulative effects \cite{4}.
2. **Knowledge uncertainty**: Limited understanding of ecological processes and relationships between stressors and effects \cite{5}.
3. **Model uncertainty**: Uncertainty in the choice of models and parameters used to predict cumulative effects \cite{6}.

These uncertainties can propagate through the cumulative effect framework, affecting the accuracy of predictions and the setting of environmental permit thresholds.

## Effects of Uncertainty Propagation on Environmental Permit Threshold Setting

Uncertainty propagation in cumulative effect frameworks can affect environmental permit threshold setting in several ways:

1. **Threshold setting**: Uncertainty in cumulative effect predictions can lead to uncertain threshold values, making it challenging to set effective permit thresholds \cite{7}.
2. **Permit approval**: Uncertainty in cumulative effect predictions can lead to uncertain permit approval decisions, potentially resulting in the approval of permits that may exceed environmental thresholds \cite{8}.
3. **Environmental risk**: Uncertainty in cumulative effect predictions can lead to uncertain environmental risk assessments, potentially resulting in inadequate protection of the environment \cite{9}.

## Mathematical Representation of Uncertainty Propagation

The uncertainty propagation in cumulative effect frameworks can be mathematically represented using probability theory and statistics. For example, the uncertainty in cumulative effect predictions can be represented using a probability distribution, such as a normal distribution:

$$P(C \leq c) = \int_{-\infty}^{c} \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} dx$$

## Conclusion

Uncertainty propagation in cumulative effect frameworks can significantly affect environmental permit threshold setting. It is essential to quantify and manage uncertainty in cumulative effect predictions to ensure effective environmental protection. This can be achieved by using robust models, data, and statistical methods to predict cumulative effects and set permit thresholds.
