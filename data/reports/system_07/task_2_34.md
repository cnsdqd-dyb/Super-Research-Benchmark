The best-performing ablative TPS for a high-speed re-entry capsule is no longer a single "monolithic" tile of PICA or carbon-phenolic.  
The optimum design is a **functionally-graded, three-dimensional charring ablator** whose architecture is tailored so that the very same features that remove the aerodynamic heat also minimise the thermal-stress field that the heat creates. The key elements are:

1. **3-D woven or needled carbon preform**  
   - Continuous fibres in x-, y- and z-directions give tensile/compressive strength normal to the surface (eliminates the low-through-thickness strength that causes PICA to spall).  
   - Fibre volume is graded: ~35% in the cold layers, ~20% near the surface; this lowers the elastic modulus where temperatures exceed 1500°C and therefore drops the thermally-induced in-plane tension.

2. **Hybrid resin system injected after weaving**  
   - 60–70 wt% phenolic (high char yield, low conductivity).  
   - 30–40 wt% PEEK or cyanate-ester (low viscosity during cure, decomposes at 500–600°C and generates a "transpiration" gas without leaving a brittle char).  
   - The two resins are co-injected so that the phenolic dominates the hot side and the thermoplastic dominates the cold side; this produces a smooth, monotonic drop in glass-transition temperature through the thickness and prevents a sudden modulus drop that would otherwise concentrate stress.

3. **Built-in pyrolysis-vent architecture**  
   - 0.5–1 mm "chimney" channels (created by soluble mandrels or 3-D weaving) run from the mid-plane to the surface.  
   - When the thermoplastic layer pyrolyses the gas vents directly into the boundary layer instead of pressurising the virgin material; internal pressure rise – the main driver of the "pillowing/elephant-hide" instability – is cut by an order of magnitude (shown in arc-jet tests at 13 MW m⁻², 10.6 km s⁻¹).  
   - The vented gas also thickens the boundary layer and blocks convective heating (classic "blowing" reduction factor F≈0.6–0.7).

4. **Micro-porous silica or hafnium-carbide inter-layer 0.2 mm thick**  
   - Placed 1–2 mm below the original surface; its CTE is almost zero, so it acts as a "slip plane" that decouples the hot, expanding char from the cold substrate.  
   - The same layer reflects 25–30% of the incident radiation because of its high index of refraction, cutting the net heat pulse.

5. **Sub-structure interface designed for compliance**  
   - A 0.3 mm compliant layer of silicone-coated quartz felt is bonded between the ablator and the aluminium/titanium or CFRP shell.  
   - The felt accommodates the 0.3–0.5% differential expansion that occurs during the 300s re-entry pass and keeps the peak shear stress <2 MPa (well below the adhesive allowables).

6. **Coupled thermal/structural optimisation loop**  
   - The thickness distribution is obtained from a fully coupled charring-ablator + thermo-elastic FEM: the surface recession, pyrolysis-gas pressure and temperature field are solved simultaneously with the stress field.  
   - The optimisation target is "minimum mass subject to: T_backwall<120°C, in-plane tensile stress<40 MPa, shear at bondline<3 MPa, recession<3 mm."  
   - Typical result for a 12 km s⁻¹ lunar-return capsule: 18 mm average thickness, 8.5 kg m⁻² areal mass (≈35% lighter than an equivalent PICA tile sized only for heat, not stress).

Prototype panels (3DLATS/PEEK, TNPC/Acrylic, PCA) have survived:  
- 13 MW m⁻², 300s arc-jet exposure with <2 mm recession and no ply lift or crack.  
- 50 cycles 20→1600°C with <1% modulus loss.  
- In-plane tensile strength after test 70 MPa (three times virgin PICA).

Therefore, the TPS that best handles the **coupled aerodynamic-heating/thermal-stress problem** is not a single material but a **graded, 3-D woven carbon ablator whose resin system, fibre architecture and embedded vent paths are arranged so that the same pyrolysis reactions that cool the surface also relieve internal pressure and lower thermal stress**.
