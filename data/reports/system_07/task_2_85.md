### 🔍 **Key Mechanisms of Uncertainty Amplification**

#### 1. **Temporal Lags in Soil-Vegetation Responses**
- **Soil carbon dynamics** respond slowly to changes in temperature, moisture, and vegetation inputs.
- **Vegetation shifts** (e.g., drought-induced dieback or CO₂ fertilization) can take years to decades to fully manifest.
- These **lags are poorly constrained** in models, leading to **wide spreads in long-term projections**.

> *"Adding vertical resolution or microbial dynamics... increased the projection uncertainty... more than 10 times larger than that in the conventional model."*  
> — *Nature Communications (2018)*

#### 2. **Nonlinear and State-Dependent Feedbacks**
- Soil moisture-vegetation feedbacks are **nonlinear** and **state-dependent**, meaning small changes in initial conditions can lead to **large divergence in outcomes**.
- Models differ widely in how they represent **vegetation sensitivity to dryness**, **soil respiration**, and **nutrient cycling**, especially under **warming and drought stress**.

> *"Current climate models have a large spread in their representation of vegetation response to dryness... with potentially large impacts on carbon fluxes."*  
> — *NASA Technical Report (2023)*

#### 3. **Parameter and Structural Uncertainty**
- **Microbial soil models**, which are more mechanistically realistic, introduce **greater uncertainty** due to **more parameters and feedback loops**.
- **DGVMs (Dynamic Global Vegetation Models)** show **large spreads** in carbon flux sensitivity to environmental drivers, especially for **land carbon sinks** (S<sub>LAND</sub>).

> *"The uncertainties are much more pronounced for S<sub>LAND</sub>... due to the lack of a compensating emissions/removals effect."*  
> — *PMC Study on Global Carbon Budget Imbalance*

### 📊 **Quantitative Impact**
- In CMIP6 models, **land carbon sink uncertainty dominates** the spread in future atmospheric CO₂ projections.

### ✅ **Conclusion**
The **lagged, nonlinear, and poorly observed** nature of soil-vegetation feedbacks **introduces deep structural uncertainty** into global carbon budget models. These feedbacks **do not just add noise** — they **amplify divergence over time**, especially under high-emission scenarios. Improving **observational constraints** and **model structure** (e.g., microbial dynamics, nutrient coupling) is **critical for reducing long-term projection uncertainty**.
