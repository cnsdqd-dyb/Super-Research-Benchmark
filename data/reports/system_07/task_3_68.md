The cost-benefit threshold of multimodal transport is balanced against traffic-control impacts by a **marginal social cost = marginal social benefit rule** that is applied mode-by-mode and then summed across the network.  
In practical terms the “balancer” is the **incremental dollar value of (travel-time savings + vehicle-op-cost savings + crash reduction + emission reduction) per incremental dollar of (infrastructure + operating + traffic-control) cost** that is required to clear the public-funding hurdle rate (usually 7–12 % real EIRR or B/C ≥ 1.0).  

Traffic-control measures (signal retiming, bus lanes, freight TSP, cordon pricing, etc.) are simply one more cost item in the numerator and, at the same time, a generator of additional benefits in the denominator. The threshold is therefore crossed when:

Σ[ΔBenefit_mode(i)] – Σ[ΔCost_mode(i) + ΔTraffic-control_cost_mode(i)] ≥ 0   ∀ i ∈ {road, rail, bike, walk, water, air}

Key points that keep the two sides in balance:

1. Shadow-price of delay. Every second saved for freight or public transport is monetised at 1.5–4 × the value of a car-driver minute; this makes traffic-control spending that prioritises buses/trucks look attractive even when car delay rises slightly.  
2. Induced-demand correction. Control strategies that increase throughput (e.g., ramp metering, green waves) are credited only for the *net* new vehicle-km after induced traffic is subtracted; otherwise the benefit side would be overstated.  
3. External-cost internalisation. Traffic-control policies that raise private costs (congestion pricing, low-emission zones) are entered as negative benefits to users but positive benefits to society; the threshold is still met if the social gain outweighs the user loss.  
4. Mode-shift leverage. A $1 of bus-lane or freight-TSP cost is allowed to “claim” the *full* benefit of every car or truck trip that shifts mode, not just the speed gain on the facility itself. This leverage often doubles or triples the benefit counted toward the threshold.  
5. Sensitivity band. Because control impacts are small but network-wide, agencies run the CBA with ±25 % benefit swings; the project still needs to clear B/C ≥ 1.0 under the low-benefit scenario before it is funded.

If the marginal dollar spent on traffic control still yields a marginal social benefit whose present value is ≥ 1.0 (or the EIRR ≥ discount rate), the multimodal package stays above the cost-benefit threshold; if not, the control element is scaled back or dropped.
