In glucocorticoid (GC) research, the balance between **anti-inflammatory efficacy** and **adrenal suppression risk** is governed by a **dose–duration–potency triad**, modulated by **circadian timing** and **dissociation of trans-repression from trans-activation**.

Key points that quantify and mechanistically explain this balance:

1. Threshold doses  
   - **≥ 4–6 mg prednisone-equivalent/day** (≈ 15–25 mg hydrocortisone) taken for **≥ 3–4 weeks** already carries a measurable risk of HPA-axis suppression.  
   - Above **20–30 mg prednisone-equivalent/day** the probability rises steeply; at **> 7.5 mg/day** virtually all patients develop biochemical suppression if exposure is prolonged.

2. Duration matters more than total cumulative dose  
   - **< 14 days** of high-dose GC: suppression is usually reversible within days.  
   - **> 3 weeks**: atrophy of the zona fasciculata begins and recovery may take months.

3. Potency and half-life  
   - Long-acting, high-potency molecules (dexamethasone > betamethasone > methylprednisolone) produce deeper, longer ACTH suppression per milligram than short-acting hydrocortisone or cortisone.  
   - Equivalent anti-inflammatory doses therefore carry **different adrenal risks** (e.g., 0.5 mg dexamethasone ≈ 4 mg prednisone for inflammation, but dexamethasone suppresses the HPA axis for > 36 h vs 18–24 h for prednisone).

4. Timing and split-dosing  
   - **Single morning dose** (mimicking the physiological cortisol peak) minimizes evening ACTH suppression.  
   - **Divided doses or night-time dosing** flatten the circadian ACTH surge and increase adrenal shutdown.

5. Molecular dissociation (research frontier)  
   - Classic side-effects (metabolic, bone, muscle, skin) are driven by **trans-activation** of glucocorticoid-responsive genes.  
   - Anti-inflammatory benefit is largely mediated by **trans-repression** of NF-κB/AP-1.  
   - **Selective glucocorticoid receptor agonists (SEGRAs)** and **dissociated compounds** (e.g., ZK 216348, CpdA) are designed to enrich trans-repression relative to trans-activation, aiming to keep anti-inflammatory dose–response while shifting the adrenal suppression curve to the right.  
   - So far, complete separation has not been achieved; some suppression still occurs because ACTH inhibition itself is trans-repression dependent.

6. Clinical translation  
   - Use the **lowest effective dose** for the **shortest duration**, preferably as a **once-daily morning dose**.  
   - Consider **alternate-day therapy** when feasible for long-acting GCs.  
   - Taper slowly after **≥ 3 weeks** of ≥ 10 mg prednisone-equivalent/day to allow HPA recovery.  
   - Evaluate axis recovery (ACTH-stimulation test) before complete withdrawal in high-risk patients.

In short, the balance is not a single number but a **dynamic interplay**:  
**“Anti-inflammatory effect rises linearly with log-dose, whereas adrenal suppression risk rises sigmoidally once the physiological cortisol ceiling (~25 mg hydrocortisone/day) is exceeded for more than a few weeks.”**  
Optimizing this curve—through timing, tapering, potency choice, and future dissociated ligands—is the central goal of current GC research.
