Heparin dosing on ECMO is not a “set-and-forget” infusion; it is a dynamic, hour-to-hour titration that tries to keep the circuit from clotting while letting the patient stop bleeding.  
The balance is struck by combining (1) a **tiered dosing algorithm**, (2) **multi-test monitoring**, and (3) **daily re-evaluation of the patient’s bleeding vs thrombotic risk**.

1. Start with a **risk-stratified target**, not a single “normal” range  
   - **Standard-risk patient**: aim for the ELSO “therapeutic” window  
     – anti-Xa 0.3–0.7 IU/mL (preferred)  
     – aPTT 45–80 s or ACT 180–220 s  
   - **High bleeding risk** (post-surgical, trauma, intracranial hemorrhage): accept a **“low-dose” or “heparin-free” tier**  
     – anti-Xa 0.1–0.3 IU/mL or aPTT 35–50 s / ACT 120–140 s  
     – circuit kept >4 L/min flow and heparin-bonded tubing; add visco-elastic tests (TEG/ROTEM) to confirm hypocoagulability  
   - **Active bleeding**: hold heparin until source controlled; monitor circuit for rising ΔP and falling D-dimer trend as early clotting clues.

2. Use **≥2 complementary tests**; no single assay is reliable in ECMO  
   – **anti-Xa** (gold standard for heparin activity) – daily, or q12 h if unstable  
   – **aPTT or ACT** – q6 h for rapid feedback (but affected by factor VIII, fibrinogen, thrombocytopenia)  
   – **TEG/ROTEM** – when bleeding or when anti-Xa/aPTT disagree; look for prolonged R-time or low clot strength  
   – **AT-III** – keep >50 %; if low and anti-Xa persistently low, give 50 IU/kg AT-III concentrate rather than just pushing more heparin

3. **Titration rules** (example used in many centres)  
   – **Initial**: 50–100 IU/kg bolus at cannulation only if no bleeding  
   – **Infusion**: 7–20 IU/kg/h (lower end for high-risk, higher end for obese or hyper-inflammatory states)  
   – **Adjustment**:  
     – anti-Xa <0.2 IU/mL → ↑10–20 %  
     – anti-Xa 0.2–0.3 IU/mL → ↑5–10 %  
     – anti-Xa 0.7–0.9 IU/mL → ↓10–20 %  
     – anti-Xa >0.9 IU/mL or any bleeding → hold 30–60 min, restart 20–30 % lower  
   – Re-check anti-Xa 4–6 h after every change; aPTT/ACT in between.

4. **Daily “anticoagulation huddle”** (intensivist, perfusionist, pharmacist, nurse)  
   – Review 24-h chest-tube output, Hb drop, platelet count, fibrinogen, circuit ΔP, oxygenator function, leg/DVT ultrasound  
   – Decide whether to remain on current tier, move up (thrombotic signals) or down (bleeding signals)

5. **Special situations**  
   – **Heparin resistance** (needs >35 000 IU/24 h to reach anti-Xa 0.3): check AT-III, give concentrate if <50 %; if still resistance, switch to bivalirudin 0.5 mg/kg bolus → 0.5–2.5 µg/kg/h (monitor aPTT 50–80 s or ECT)  
   – **HIT**: stop heparin, start argatroban 0.2–1 µg/kg/h (aPTT 50–80 s) or bivalirudin as above  
   – **Platelets <50 k or fibrinogen <100 mg/dL**: transfuse before escalating heparin dose; low clot substrate can masquerade as “heparin failure”

By repeatedly asking “What is the **dominant risk today**—clot or bleed?” and adjusting the target range, infusion rate, and even the anticoagulant itself, clinicians can keep the circuit patent without letting the patient exsanguinate.
