The mechanism is the **disconfirmation-of-expectations model**.

In this model, the customer compares the actual performance of the product or service with the performance level he or she expected when the purchase was planned.  
- If performance falls short of expectations → negative disconfirmation → dissatisfaction.  
- If performance matches expectations → simple confirmation → neutral or moderate satisfaction.  
- If performance exceeds expectations → positive disconfirmation → high satisfaction/delight.

Thus, satisfaction judgments are formed by the direction and size of the gap between the prior expectation standard and the post-experience perception.
