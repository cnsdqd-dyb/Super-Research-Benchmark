In secondary prevention of coronary heart disease (CHD), **LDL-C target achievement and lifestyle modification are not competing strategies—they are synergistic, but medication adjustment is prioritized when adherence to either pillar is suboptimal**. The balancing act is governed by three principles:

---

### 1. **LDL-C target hierarchy is non-negotiable**
- **ESC 2023 / ISS 2022**  
  – **< 55 mg/dL (1.4 mmol/L)** after ACS or in any patient with a **recurrent event within 2 years** (“extreme-risk” tier).  
  – **< 70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L)** for all other established CHD.  
  – **≥ 50 % reduction from baseline** is required even if baseline LDL-C is already low.

- **Drug ladder is algorithmic**  
  1. **Maximum-tolerated high-intensity statin** (atorvastatin 40–80 mg or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg) **started in-hospital**—no waiting for diet trial.  
  2. **Re-check LDL-C at 4–6 weeks**; if above target → **add ezetimibe 10 mg**.  
  3. Still above target → **add PCSK9-mAb (alirocumab/evolocumab) or inclisiran**; the latter is favoured when visit adherence is poor (q-6-month injection).  
  4. **Triple pill packs** (statin/ezetimibe/bempedoic acid) are now used up-front in very-high-risk Asian or familial hyper-cholesterolaemia patients to compress time-to-target.

---

### 2. **Lifestyle modification is co-prescribed, not deferred**
- **“30-30-5” rule** used in most EU prevention clinics:  
  – **30 g fibre/day, 30 min moderate activity ≥ 5 days/week, < 5 % weight loss if BMI > 25 kg/m²**.  
- **Smoking cessation** remains the single intervention with an immediate 30–40 % reduction in recurrent MI; nicotine-replacement + varenicline doubles 1-year quit rates compared with counselling alone.  
- **Mediterranean-diet pattern** adds an extra 20–25 % relative risk reduction **on top of statin therapy** (PREDIMED subgroup with prior CHD).

**Crucially, lifestyle success is never awaited before drug escalation**; instead, dietitians and physiotherapists see the patient **while** the lipid ladder is being climbed.

---

### 3. **Adherence gaps trigger faster drug intensification, not “more counselling first”**
Real-world data show **≥ 30 % drop-off** for both lifestyle and drug adherence by 6 months post-ACS. The response is asymmetric:

| Adherence Problem | Action |
|-------------------|--------|
| **Statin intolerance (myalgia, ↑LFT)** | Switch to rosuvastatin 5 mg twice weekly → titrate, or use alternate-day atorvastatin; if still not tolerated → **ezetimibe monotherapy → add PCSK9 or inclisiran** (no muscle side-effects). |
| **Poor diet/exercise adherence** | Continue dietary visits, **but** if LDL-C remains > 55 mg/dL, **proceed immediately to step 3 (PCSK9/inclisiran)** rather than “repeat diet trial”. |
| **Infrequent follow-up (rural, elderly)** | **Inclisiran or PCSK9 auto-injector** given in nurse-led clinic q-6 months; once LDL-C is at target, lifestyle coaching can be delivered by phone/app. |
| **Poly-pharmacy confusion** | Fixed-dose combination pills (e.g., atorvastatin/ezetimibe 40/10 mg) improve 12-month adherence by 20 % vs two separate pills. |

---

### 4. **Shared decision aid keeps the two tracks visible**
A one-page “traffic-light” print-out given to every patient:

- **Green**: LDL-C < 55 mg/dL → continue current Rx + lifestyle.  
- **Amber**: 55–70 mg/dL → reinforce lifestyle, **but** add ezetimibe now.  
- **Red**: > 70 mg/dL → PCSK9/inclisiran **this visit**, lifestyle coach booked within 2 weeks.

This prevents the common error of **endless lifestyle “counselling loops”** while the patient remains exposed to recurrent events.

---

### Key take-home
Lifestyle modification is **mandatory and permanently promoted**, yet **LDL-C targets are met pharmacologically first and fast**; poor adherence to either pillar simply accelerates the next drug step rather than postponing it.
