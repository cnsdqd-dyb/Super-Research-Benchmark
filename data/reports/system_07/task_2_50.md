In ESG portfolios, factor models can **partially** balance risk quantification and responsibility standardization, but **multi-criteria weighting inevitably introduces subjective bias**—the challenge is not whether it does, but **how transparently and systematically that bias is managed**.

---

### 1. **Factor Models: Balancing Risk and Responsibility**
Factor models in ESG investing aim to translate **non-financial sustainability metrics** into **quantitative risk exposures**, much like traditional factor models do for value or momentum. When constructed rigorously:

- **Risk quantification** is achieved by mapping ESG scores to historical return patterns, volatility, or drawdowns, allowing investors to estimate ESG-related tail risks or factor premiums.
- **Responsibility standardization** is pursued by selecting and weighting ESG criteria based on **materiality frameworks** (e.g., SASB, GRI), which align ESG factors with sector-specific risks.

However, **standardization is not neutrality**. The choice of materiality framework, the selection of ESG data providers, and the calibration of factor exposures all embed **normative judgments** about what "responsibility" means.

---

### 2. **Multi-Criteria Weighting: The Subjective Bias Problem**
Multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA) is often used to **aggregate ESG pillars** (E, S, G) or sub-factors into a composite score. This is where **subjectivity becomes structural**:

- **Weight assignment**—whether via AHP, expert judgment, or entropy-based methods—**reflects priorities**, not truths. For example, assigning 40% weight to governance may be justified for banks, but this is a **contextual judgment**, not an objective truth.
- **Hybrid weighting** (e.g., AHP + entropy) can **reduce** bias, but not eliminate it. It balances **expert judgment** with **data-driven dispersion**, but still depends on:
  - Who the experts are,
  - What data is included,
  - How normalization is done.

> *“The combination of objective weight (entropy) and subjective weight (AHP) can reduce the bias of subjective weights and truly reflect the current situation”*—but only **within the bounds of the selected data and criteria**.

---

### 3. **Conclusion: Bias Is Not a Bug, It’s a Design Feature**
The key insight is that **subjectivity is not a flaw to be eliminated**, but a **design choice to be disclosed**. Factor models can **standardize** how ESG is expressed in portfolio risk, but **cannot neutralize** the normative choices embedded in:

- What counts as a material ESG factor,
- How weights are assigned across criteria,
- How trade-offs between risk and responsibility are resolved.

Thus, **responsible ESG factor modeling** requires:
- **Transparent documentation** of weighting logic,
- **Sensitivity analysis** to show how results change under different weighting assumptions,
- **Stakeholder input** to legitimize value-laden choices.

---

### Final Answer:
**No**, factor models cannot fully balance risk quantification and responsibility standardization without introducing subjective bias. However, **with transparent design and hybrid weighting methods**, they can **systematically manage** that bias—turning subjectivity into a **governable input**, rather than a hidden flaw.
