The Jordan form of a social-stratification (mobility) matrix is the coordinate-free “X-ray” that tells you exactly where the flow of people, capital or status can – and cannot – equilibrate in the long run.  
In a counter-globalisation field the state space is no longer a single, well-mixed national labour market; it is a patchwork of partially decoupled regions, credential islands, visa walls and currency blocks.  The resulting stochastic matrix P is still row-stochastic, but it is only *block*-irreducible: inside every block the chain is ergodic, yet the whole graph contains transient gateways and almost-absorbing “trap” states.  The Jordan canonical form of P (or of the generator L = P – I if one works in continuous time) exposes these structural faults and therefore *delimits* the ergodic boundary in the following precise way.

1.  Eigenvalue 1 and its Jordan blocks  
   - The number of Jordan blocks for λ = 1 equals the number of *communicating classes that are final* (no outgoing edges).  
   - If every such block is 1 × 1, every final class is *exactly* ergodic: the chain forgets its initial distribution inside that island.  
   - A Jordan block of size k > 1 for λ = 1 means that the final island is only *k-1-step* ergodic: Cesàro convergence still occurs, but the period of political or seasonal gate-closing leaves a k-cycle in the ranking process; the limit is no longer stationary in the strict sense.

2.  Eigenvalues on the unit circle |λ| = 1, λ ≠ 1  
   These come from periodic visa quotas, crop cycles or academic hiring seasons.  Each such eigenvalue appears in a Jordan block whose size tells you how many consecutive periods the cycle can *amplify* inequality before the envelope of the trajectory is damped by within-class mixing.  Counter-globalisation literature calls these “Braudel cycles”; the Jordan block size is the *Braudel depth* of that cycle.

3.  Eigenvalues strictly inside the disc |λ| < 1  
   The size of the largest Jordan block associated with the *second-largest* eigenvalue gives the *mixing height* of the transient population.  If the block is large, a rank-1 perturbation – say a new trade corridor or a sudden closure – can push the second eigenvalue outward and move the ergodic boundary: a formerly transient region becomes almost absorbing, or vice-versa.  Hence the Jordan *condition number* κ(S) = ‖S‖‖S⁻¹‖ of the similarity transform that diagonalises P on the non-unit eigenvalues is the *rigidity coefficient* of the stratification order.  A high κ(S) means that small policy shocks can tilt the long-run distribution of status.

4.  The *ergodic boundary* itself  
   Formally, let Λ₁ be the set of eigenvalues whose Jordan blocks are of size > 1 or that lie on the unit circle.  The ergodic boundary is the affine hyper-plane in logarithmic coordinates  
   ∂Erg = { log ρ : ρ is a left eigenvector of P for some λ ∈ Λ₁ }.  
   Any initial distribution whose projection on the generalized eigenspace of Λ₁ is non-zero can *never* converge to a time-independent rank distribution; instead it traces a persistent limacon in the simplex Δⁿ⁻¹.  Counter-globalisation flows that start on opposite sides of ∂Erg will *never* overlap; they are stratified *forever* even though every individual trajectory is almost surely finite.

5.  Policy reading  
   - If you want to *erase* the boundary you must collapse every Jordan block of size > 1 for λ = 1 to size 1 (open a permanent relocation channel between every pair of final classes).  
   - If you want to *defend* a local privilege you must keep or enlarge the Jordan blocks that belong to the privileged island (issue time-limited, non-renewable visas so that the block size equals the visa duration).  

Hence the Jordan form is not a mere spectral curiosity; it is the *cartography of permanent inequality*.  The sizes and positions of its blocks draw the exact coastline between the region of the state space where history dies out (ergodicity) and the region where history is frozen into castes (non-ergodicity).  In counter-globalisation flow fields that coastline is the battle-line between mobile labour and entrenched status.
