The black hole information paradox does **not necessarily** require a *restructuring* of causality in quantum mechanics, but it **does force us to confront the limits of our current notions of causality**, especially when gravity is involved.

Here’s a concise breakdown:

---

### 1. **What the paradox really challenges**
The paradox arises because **unitarity** (a core tenet of quantum mechanics) appears to be violated during black hole evaporation: a pure quantum state collapses into a mixed state, implying **information loss**. This contradicts the expectation that quantum evolution should be **reversible** and **information-preserving**.

---

### 2. **Causality is not directly violated—yet**
Standard quantum mechanics **does not violate causality**, even in the presence of entanglement or nonlocal correlations (as in Bell experiments). The paradox, however, **introduces tension** between:
- **Locality** (no influence outside the light cone),
- **Unitarity** (information must be preserved),
- **Causality** (no influence from the future to the past).

In semiclassical gravity, **information appears to be trapped behind the event horizon**, and **Hawking radiation** seems thermal and **uncorrelated** with the initial state. This suggests that **information cannot escape without violating locality or causality**.

---

### 3. **Where causality becomes questionable**
Some proposed resolutions **do** imply **mild or effective violations of causality**, such as:
- **Information leakage** over the lifetime of the black hole, possibly **non-locally** or **acausally** (e.g., due to quantum gravity effects).
- **Black hole complementarity**, which suggests that **information is both inside and outside** the black hole, depending on the observer—but this **risks violating the no-cloning theorem** and **causal consistency**.
- **Firewalls**, which propose that **the event horizon is not a smooth region**, but a **violent, information-rich boundary**, again challenging **locality and causality**.

---

### 4. **Modern view: Causality is strained, not broken**
Recent developments (e.g., **Page curve reconstruction**, **holography**, **quantum error correction**) suggest that **information is preserved**, but **not in a way that respects classical causality**. Instead, **information is encoded non-locally**—possibly across the horizon or in the boundary theory (as in AdS/CFT).

This **does not require restructuring causality in quantum mechanics**, but it **does imply that causality in quantum gravity is more subtle**, possibly **emergent**, and **not strictly local**.

---

### ✅ Final Answer:
**No**, the black hole information paradox does not *necessitate* a restructuring of causality in quantum mechanics **per se**, but it **reveals that causality in quantum gravity is not fundamental in the same way as in QFT or classical relativity**. It must be **reinterpreted or emergent**, not strictly local, and possibly **nonlinear in observer-dependent ways**.
