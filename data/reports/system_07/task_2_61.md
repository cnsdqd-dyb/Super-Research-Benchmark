The shock index (SI = HR/SBP) is used in triage exactly because it is **more sensitive than HR or BP alone** to occult hypoperfusion, yet its **poor specificity keeps it from dictating automatic, resource-intensive action**.  In practice this is turned into a two-step safeguard:

1. **Screening step (high sensitivity)**  
   - Any SI ≥ 0.7–0.9 “flags” the patient for closer scrutiny (repeat vitals, lactate, place in high-acuity queue).  
   - Because the negative-predictive value is excellent (≈ 95%), a normal SI reliably rules out early shock and prevents over-testing.

2. **Confirmation step (add specificity)**  
   - The elevated SI is combined with clinical context (mechanism, skin perfusion, mentation, urine output, lactate, point-of-care ultrasound) before activating massive-transfusion protocols, central lines, or ICU beds.  
   - Modified versions (MSI, RASI, DSI, age-SI) can be used in special populations to reduce false positives.

Thus the index **widens the net early** but **never triggers therapy by itself**; downstream clinical appraisal prevents over-intervention.
