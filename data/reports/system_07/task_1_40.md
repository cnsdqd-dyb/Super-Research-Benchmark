The balance between anti-tumor activation and autoimmune risk in immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) research is governed by a **threshold model of immune activation**, which is maintained by **peripheral tolerance mechanisms** and **co-inhibitory signaling pathways**.

### Key Mechanisms:

1. **Co-inhibitory Receptors (e.g., CTLA-4, PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3)**  
   These receptors act as brakes on T cells. They maintain immune homeostasis by:
   - **Suppressing excessive T cell activation** in healthy tissues.
   - **Promoting T cell exhaustion** in tumors, which prevents immune overactivation but also allows tumor escape.

   Blocking these checkpoints (e.g., with anti-PD-1 or anti-CTLA-4 antibodies) **releases the brakes**, enhancing anti-tumor immunity but also **lowering the threshold for autoimmunity**.

2. **Regulatory T Cells (Tregs)**  
   Tregs express high levels of CTLA-4 and help maintain tolerance by:
   - **Suppressing autoreactive T cells**.
   - **Modulating antigen-presenting cells (APCs)** to reduce co-stimulation.

   ICI therapies can reduce Treg function, which contributes to both **enhanced anti-tumor responses** and **loss of self-tolerance**, increasing the risk of immune-related adverse events (irAEs).

3. **Antigen-Presenting Cells and Tolerance Switches**  
   Recent findings (e.g., EPO-EPOR signaling in cDC1 dendritic cells) show that certain pathways **actively program tolerance** by promoting Treg activation. Disrupting these pathways (e.g., by blocking EPO signaling) can **convert tolerogenic DCs into immunostimulatory ones**, tipping the balance toward autoimmunity or tumor rejection depending on context.

4. **Threshold Model of Immune Activation**  
   This model proposes that immune responses are triggered only when **stimulatory signals exceed a certain threshold**. ICIs lower this threshold, making the immune system more sensitive to both **tumor antigens** and **self-antigens**, thereby increasing the risk of autoimmune reactions.

---

### Summary:
The balance is maintained by **co-inhibitory signaling, Treg activity, and tolerogenic antigen presentation**. ICIs disrupt this equilibrium by **releasing T cell suppression**, which enhances anti-tumor immunity but also **lowers the activation threshold**, increasing the likelihood of autoimmune toxicity. This dual effect is the central challenge in optimizing ICI therapies.
