Distinguishing a true WIMP-nucleus scattering event from a neutrino that does exactly the same thing is the "neutrino-floor" problem: both give a single, low-energy nuclear recoil with no accompanying activity.  
Because the kinematics of the recoil itself are almost identical, the separation has to come from something that is different for the two incident particles.  
Experiments exploit four classes of differences.

1. **Angular structure of the flux in the lab**  
   - WIMPs arrive from the direction in which the Solar system moves through the Galactic halo (≈ 220 km s⁻¹ toward the Cygnus constellation).  
     Their flux is therefore a ∼ 10% dipole modulation on top of an otherwise isotropic background, and the recoil direction is strongly peaked opposite to that "wind".  
   - Solar neutrinos come from the Sun; atmospheric and diffuse-supernova neutrinos are isotropic.  
     Hence the recoil direction distribution is either Sun-pointing (for ⁸B ν) or completely isotropic, but never dipole-modulated in the Galactic frame.  

   A detector that can reconstruct the 3-D direction of the recoiling nucleus (gaseous TPC, nuclear-emulsion, diamond-NV strain mapping, etc.) therefore gives an almost background-free WIMP sample.  
   Even without full 3-D tracking, the annual rotation of the WIMP-wind direction produces a 5–7% cosine modulation in rate (June vs December) that neutrinos do not imitate; precise modulation searches already set limits in Xe/Ar TPCs and will become decisive once the rate is ≳ a few events yr⁻¹ ton⁻¹.

2. **Material dependence of the cross section**  
   - Spin-independent WIMP scattering scales roughly as A² (coherent over the whole nucleus).  
   - Coherent ν-nucleus scattering is also A², but the weak charge is (N – (1–4 sin²θ_W)Z), so the ratio of rates in two different targets is fixed by the Standard Model.  
   - Spin-dependent WIMP scattering is set by the net nucleon spin of the chosen isotope and can be large for odd-A targets such as ¹⁹F or ¹²⁹Xe, whereas neutrinos have no axial-vector coupling to the spin.  

   Running two very different detectors (e.g. Xe + Si, or Xe + fluorinated gas) therefore gives a "material-baseline" test: the WIMP hypothesis predicts a definite ratio of event rates, while the neutrino-only hypothesis predicts a different, SM-fixed ratio. Deviations or consistencies can separate or confirm a signal, sometimes improving the sensitivity by an order of magnitude (Phys. Rev. D 89, 023524).

3. **Recoil-energy shape when more than one neutrino component contributes**  
   The total neutrino background is the sum of pp, ⁷Be, ⁸B, hep, atmospheric and DSNB spectra.  
   Changing the analysis threshold changes the relative weight of these components, so the shape of the "neutrino-only" spectrum is known and threshold-stable.  
   A WIMP of a given mass produces a nuclear-recoil spectrum that falls off more sharply with energy than the summed neutrino continuum.  
   Joint fits of several threshold settings (or of two detectors with different thresholds) therefore give a χ² discriminator: the neutrino-only hypothesis must fit all data with one normalisation, whereas adding a WIMP mass+ cross-section term can be required by the data.

4. **Auxiliary signatures that tag the neutrino side**  
   - ν-e elastic scattering gives an electron recoil. Detectors with < 0.1% electron-recoil mis-identification (dual-phase Xe/Ar TPCs, cryogenic semiconductor, noble-gas bubble chambers) can keep this background from leaking into the nuclear-recoil box, but the same events can be used to measure the absolute solar-neutrino flux in situ.  
   - If the ν flux is known, the expected CEvNS rate is fixed; observing the predicted number of nuclear recoils with the correct energy and direction distribution would confirm that any excess is not neutrinos.  
   - Neutrons from muon spallation sometimes accompany atmospheric ν production. Active veto shields (water-Cherenkov, Gd-loaded scintillator) tag the muon and any subsequent neutron capture, providing an independent neutron rejection factor.

Putting it together, the only *irreducible* background is coherent ν-nucleus scattering that happens to give a recoil indistinguishable in energy and isolated topology.  
Directional information (either event-by-event or through annual modulation) is the most powerful handle; material-baseline comparisons and precise spectral-shape fits supply complementary discrimination.  
No single technique is background-free, but the combination already allows present-generation Xe TPCs to sit *at* the ⁸B neutrino floor, and next-generation directional or multi-target experiments expect to probe a factor 10–50 below it without confusion.
