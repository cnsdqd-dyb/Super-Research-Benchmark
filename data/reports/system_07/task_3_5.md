In trauma-focused CBT (Prolonged Exposure, Cognitive Processing Therapy, etc.) the therapist has to keep two dials in the green zone at the same time:

- **Dial 1 – Fear activation**: the memory has to be “switched on” (≥ 50% of peak pre-treatment SUDS, visible physiological arousal, hot emotions in session) so that new inhibitory learning can occur.  
- **Dial 2 – Drop-out risk**: the moment the patient’s perceived coping capacity is exceeded, avoidance behaviours escalate and the probability of unilateral termination rises steeply (observed rates 25-40% for CPT/PE vs. < 10% for gentler variants such as Written Exposure).

Balancing the two dials is done by calibrating the “therapeutic window” (Ogden et al.) so that activation is high enough for emotional processing but never higher than the client can tolerate with the skills available at that moment.  
The levers clinicians use are:

1. **Graduated, client-controlled exposure**  
   - Start with imaginal or in-vivo items that provoke 40-60 SUDS, not the top of the hierarchy.  
   - Let the patient decide when to stop the first imaginal “re-tell”; subsequent passes are lengthened only when within-session habituation ≥ 20% is observed.

2. **Real-time arousal monitoring**  
   - 30-second SUDS pings during imaginal exposure; heart-rate or skin-conductance telemetry when available.  
   - If SUDS climbs > 80 and stays there for > 2 min without decline, therapist pauses, inserts grounding, reappraises safety, then decides whether to continue or step back.

3. **Pre-exposure safety set-up**  
   - One-session emotion-regulation module (diaphragmatic breathing, brief grounding) is taught *before* the first imaginal exposure; it is *not* used to avoid emotion but as an emergency brake.  
   - Written “coping plan” (who to call, what to do, emergency numbers) reduces anticipatory threat and increases willingness to stay in treatment.

4. **Cognitive load management**  
   - First imaginal sessions keep narrative focus on “facts + sensory details”; higher-order meaning-making (worst moments, guilt appraisals) is added only when within-session arousal reliably falls ≥ 20%.  
   - This prevents overwhelming shame or dissociation that predicts drop-out (see PMC10826797: avoidance coding ≥ 2 on 0-3 scale doubled odds of early exit).

5. **Between-session dose spacing**  
   - If post-session SUDS remains > 30 for > 48 h or if intrusion spikes > 50%, the next exposure is postponed one session and a stabilisation/psycho-education session is inserted.  
   - Data from CPT trials show this “soft landing” policy cut early drop-out from 40% to 24% without reducing post-treatment effect size (d = 1.9→1.8).

6. **Therapist stance variables**  
   - Explicitly frame exposure as “training the brain that the memory is *not* dangerous now”, not as “reliving”.  
   - Use contingent encouragement, not coercion; patients who feel *forced* show 2.3× higher odds of dropping out (Steil 2021).

7. **Early-warning analytics**  
   - Session-2 process codes (physiological distress ≥ 2 + cognitive processing ≥ 2) predict 85% of completers; high avoidance (≥ 2) + low distress (≤ 1) predicts 74% of drop-outs.  
   - When the risk profile is detected, therapist can insert an extra preparation session or shift to a briefer written-exposure protocol (6% drop-out) while retaining trauma focus.

**Bottom line**: the “fear activation threshold” is not a fixed number; it is dynamically titrated so that the peak *in-session* SUDS is roughly 20-30 points above baseline but never exceeds the client’s *a priori* tolerated ceiling (agreed in the formulation session). Continuous measurement, client control, and rapid fallback options keep the exposure potent enough to produce corrective learning yet safe enough to prevent the avoidance cascade that drives treatment discontinuation.
