The **convexity of responsibility attribution functions** plays a subtle but powerful role in determining the **stability boundary of moral judgments** in AI decision spaces. Here's how:

---

### 🔍 1. **What is a responsibility attribution function?**
It maps **features of an AI decision** (e.g., autonomy level, outcome severity, human oversight) to a **degree of moral responsibility** assigned to agents (developers, users, regulators, etc.).

---

### 📈 2. **Convexity in this context**
A **convex** responsibility function implies **diminishing marginal responsibility** as AI autonomy increases.  
- **Concave** would imply **accelerating responsibility** with autonomy.  
- **Linear** implies **proportional responsibility**.

---

### ⚖️ 3. **Stability boundary of moral judgments**
This is the **threshold** beyond which small changes in AI behavior or context lead to **large, discontinuous shifts** in moral judgment (e.g., from "developer fault" to "user fault").

---

### 🔗 4. **How convexity determines this boundary**
- **Convex functions** **flatten** at high autonomy → **responsibility plateaus**, making **judgments stable** even as AI behavior varies.
- **Concave or non-convex functions** **sharpen** at high autonomy → **responsibility spikes**, leading to **unstable judgments** (small changes → big blame shifts).
- **Inflection points** in non-convex functions create **instability regions** where **moral consensus collapses**.

---

### 🧠 5. **Analogy**
Think of convexity like a **"moral damping" mechanism**:  
- **High convexity** = **smooth, stable** moral judgments.  
- **Low convexity** = **jagged, unstable** judgments, prone to **flip** under small perturbations.

---

### ✅ Summary
> **Convexity in responsibility attribution acts as a stabilizer**: it determines how **gradually or abruptly** moral responsibility shifts across agents as AI autonomy increases.  
> **Higher convexity → smoother transitions → more stable moral judgments** in AI decision spaces.
