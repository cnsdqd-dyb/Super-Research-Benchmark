Reconciling hemodynamic thresholds with myocardial-protection constraints in a patient who needs an aortic-valve replacement (AVR) is essentially an exercise in “how much load can I impose on a hypertrophied, pressure-overloaded left ventricle while it is ischaemic and arrested?”  
The problem is framed by three hard facts:

1. The valve must be replaced once the stenosis reaches a **mean gradient ≥ 40 mm Hg**, **Vmax ≥ 4 m/s**, or **AVA ≤ 1.0 cm²** (or indexed ≤ 0.6 cm²/m²).  
2. A hypertrophied LV (wall thickness ≥ 15 mm or mass ≥ 120 g/m²) has **reduced ischaemic tolerance**; every extra minute of cross-clamp or every mmHg fall in coronary perfusion during arrest is translated into more troponin leakage and a higher chance of primary-graft failure.  
3. The same hypertrophy is what **creates** the high gradient; once the valve is opened the ventricle is suddenly decompresses and the sub-endocardium that was kept “open” by the high intracavitary pressure is now at the mercy of whatever perfusion pressure you can deliver **during the arrest**.

How surgeons/anaesthetists integrate these realities is summarised below.

-------------------------------------------------
1. Pre-clamp phase – “load” the protection, not the ventricle
-------------------------------------------------
- Keep **mean aortic root pressure ≥ 70 mm Hg** until the clamp goes on; below this the hypertrophied muscle starts to lose O₂ supply reserve.  
- Avoid reflex bradycardia (HR < 55) – the hypertrophied LV depends on rate to maintain cardiac output because diastolic filling time is already shortened by reduced compliance.  
- Give **1000 IU heparin / kg** immediately before clamp; any delay between clamp and cardioplegia delivery increases ischaemic time exponentially in AS.  

-------------------------------------------------
2. Cardioplegia strategy – “overdose” in both amount and route
-------------------------------------------------
- **Antegrade + retrograde** delivery is **mandatory** in AS; antegrade alone is insufficient because the elevated LV end-diastolic pressure collapses the sub-endocardial vessels during arrest.  
- **Cold blood cardioplegia (4–8 °C)** is preferred to HTK; a multicentre study of 314 AS patients showed HTK tripled the odds of post-operative myocardial injury (OR 3.06, 95 % CI 1.63–5.77).  
- **Initial dose**: 15–20 mL/kg antegrade at 200 mm Hg root pressure, then 5 mL/kg every 15 min retrograde; keep myocardial temp ≤ 12 °C.  
- **Topical** slush is added if wall thickness > 18 mm or cross-clamp expected > 90 min.  

-------------------------------------------------
3. Cross-clamp time – the only variable you still control
-------------------------------------------------
- Every **extra 10 min** of ischaemia increases PMI odds by **13 %** (OR 1.13 per 10 min).  
- Therefore **“threshold” haemodynamics are not recalibrated**, but the **decision to replace is brought forward** once the gradient is ≥ 40 mm Hg **and** the LV mass is already > 120 g/m², because waiting longer only increases myocardial oxygen demand without improving tolerance.  
- If the gradient is **discordant** (AVA < 1 cm² but mean ΔP < 40 mm Hg) and EF < 50 %, dobutamine stress is performed **inside the operating theatre** with the perfusionist on standby; if contractile reserve is absent, AVR proceeds **immediately** under the same protection protocol to avoid further ischaemic insult.  

-------------------------------------------------
4. Reperfusion phase – “decompress first, reperfuse second”
-------------------------------------------------
- **Controlled reperfusion** is started with the valve still open (vent vented) to prevent sudden LV distension when coronary flow is restored.  
- **Initial perfusion pressure 50–60 mm Hg for 3 min**, then step-wise to 70–80 mm Hg; this avoids oedema of the sub-endocardium that was previously protected by the high intracavitary pressure.  
- **Wean from bypass only after**  
  – LV wall motion score improves to ≤ 1.5/4,  
  – Troponin-I peak < 10× upper reference,  
  – Pulmonary artery diastolic pressure < 20 mm Hg.  

-------------------------------------------------
Bottom line
-------------------------------------------------
The haemodynamic thresholds for severe AS are **not relaxed**; instead the **myocardial-protection protocol is intensified** (blood cardioplegia, antegrade+retrograde, strict ≤ 90 min clamp, topical cooling, controlled reperfusion) so that the hypertrophied ventricle can survive the obligatory ischaemic interval.
