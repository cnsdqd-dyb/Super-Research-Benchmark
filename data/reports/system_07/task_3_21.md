The short answer is: **only partially and under specific structural conditions**.  
Value networks *can* buffer consumer-culture contagion against certain economic perturbations, but the protection is topology-dependent, threshold-sensitive, and disappears once the shock is large enough to alter the cultural “infection” threshold itself.

Below is the longer, evidence-based answer.

--------------------------------------------------
1.  What we mean by the three moving parts
--------------------------------------------------
a.  **Value network**  
    A directed, weighted graph whose nodes are consumers, tastemakers, platforms, payment rails, advertisers, etc., and whose edges carry two flows:  
    -  *economic value* (price signals, discounts, affiliate fees, resale margins, staking rewards, …)  
    -  *cultural value* (status, identity, narrative, meme).  
    The network is *nested* and *core-periphery* at the same time: a small set of high-degree “culture cores” (luxury houses, KOLs, NFT whales) is tightly interconnected, while the bulk of consumers forms a sparse periphery that is only loosely linked to the core but heavily clustered among themselves (TikTok micro-communities, sneaker forums, etc.).

b.  **Economic parameter perturbation**  
    Any exogenous change that alters the *marginal utility/cost* of adopting the cultural good:  
    -  disposable-income shocks (inflation, layoffs)  
    -  liquidity shocks (BNPL rate hikes, crypto-margin calls)  
    -  relative-price shocks (counterfeit flood, tariff on Italian luxury leather).  

c.  **Contagion dynamics of consumer culture**  
    A non-linear threshold model (LTD) on the same graph: a consumer flips from “non-adopter” to “adopter” when the *weighted share* of adopting neighbours exceeds an individual threshold φᵢ that itself depends on the economic parameters above (higher φᵢ when the good becomes more expensive or the budget tighter).

--------------------------------------------------
2.  What the literature already knows
--------------------------------------------------
-  **Network topology governs shock amplification**  
    Core-periphery financial networks amplify asset-price collapses because fire-sales propagate through the densely connected core (SNU working-paper experiment, 2023).  
    The same mechanism applies to *cultural fire-sales*: when core influencers suddenly dis-adopt (because their economic threshold φᵢ spiked), the periphery experiences a *cultural price collapse*—the meme loses status value—leading to mass exit.

-  **Clustering gives “loss-containment” … up to a point**  
    Henriet et al. (firm-network simulations) show that higher clustering of peripheral nodes *positively* correlates with post-shock output recovery.  
    Translated: tightly-knit sub-communities (sneakerheads, K-pop stans) can keep the culture alive even when the global core is burning, *provided* the shock is *outside* their cluster.  
    Once the shock hits *inside* the cluster, the same density accelerates collapse because everyone is linked to the now-bankrupt hub.

-  **Nestedness is a double-edged sword**  
    Nested networks (country-product, mutualistic, luxury-brand–consumer) are robust to *random* node removal but fragile to *targeted* removal of the most generalist (highest-degree) nodes (Staniczenko et al., 2017).  
    In consumer culture the “generalists” are the mega-influencers and luxury flagship stores; an economic perturbation that knocks them out (e.g., a 40 % LVMH profit warning) produces a *nestedness-collapse* that is faster than in random networks.

-  **Threshold feedback breaks robustness**  
    In LTD models the final cascade size is discontinuous in the threshold distribution.  
    If the economic shock shifts φᵢ only a few percent, the system can stay in the sub-critical regime; but once the *mean* φ crosses the critical value θ*(G) imposed by the graph, the adoption share jumps from “most” to “few” almost instantly (see Yagan & Ozdaglar, 2021).  
    No amount of redundant wiring can restore the *cultural equilibrium* because the *rule of adoption itself* has changed.

--------------------------------------------------
3.  Synthesis: when do value networks resist?
--------------------------------------------------
Robustness emerges **only when all of the following hold simultaneously**:

1.  The shock is **localised outside the core** and **outside the largest nested block**.  
2.  The peripheral clustering is **high enough** to let micro-communities self-fund the cultural signal (drop-outs compensate by increasing in-group status production).  
3.  The economic perturbation is **temporary** and **small relative to the income-effect elasticity** of the cultural good (luxury bags → low elasticity, NFT PFPs → high).  
4.  There exist **alternative economic edges** that re-price the good without losing narrative coherence (grey-market resale, rental, fractional ownership, “dupes” that still reference the original meme).

Once the perturbation is **systemic** (inflation + rate hike + unemployment) or **targets the core**, the same topological features (core-periphery, nestedness, clustering) *reverse* into contagion accelerators.  
The value network does **not** absorb the shock; it **transmits** it, and the cultural contagion dies.

--------------------------------------------------
4.  Bottom line
--------------------------------------------------
Value networks are **not structurally robust** to economic parameter perturbations in the contagion dynamics of consumer culture.  
They offer **contingent resilience** that can be designed *ex-ante* (increase peripheral modularity, diversify core economic dependencies, embed counter-cyclical status rewards), but **cannot be assumed *ex-post*** once the shock hits the system’s cultural hubs.
