### 1. **Thermal Spectrum and Information Loss**
In Hawking’s original derivation, black hole radiation is **thermal**, implying that the emission spectrum is characterized solely by temperature. This leads to the **black hole information paradox**: a pure quantum state collapsing into a black hole appears to evolve into a mixed thermal state upon evaporation, violating unitarity.

---

These correlations **carry information** about the initial state of the black hole, suggesting a mechanism for information preservation.

---

### 3. **Holographic Duality and Unitary Evolution**
Holographic duality (e.g., AdS/CFT) posits that a gravitational system in the bulk (like a black hole) is **dual to a non-gravitational quantum field theory** on the boundary. In this dual description:

- **Unitarity is preserved** by construction.
- The black hole’s evaporation corresponds to **unitary evolution** in the boundary theory.
- The **non-thermal spectrum** in the bulk reflects **microscopic details** of the boundary theory, ensuring that **information is not lost** but encoded in subtle correlations.

---

### 4. **Constraints Imposed by Holography**
The holographic principle **constrains the number of degrees of freedom** in the black hole system. These constraints:

- Limit the **entropy** and **information capacity** of the black hole.
- Imply that **any radiation emitted must encode information** consistent with the boundary theory’s unitary evolution.
- Favor **non-thermal spectra** that include **correlations**, aligning with the requirement that the full quantum state remains pure.

---

### 5. **Summary**
The non-thermal nature of Hawking radiation is not just a correction—it is a **necessary feature** to satisfy the **unitarity constraints** imposed by holographic duality. These deviations from thermality encode the **information-preserving structure** required by the dual boundary theory, offering a resolution to the information paradox within the holographic framework.
