Key indications that keep rTMS inside the “sweet spot” – i.e., strong enough to lift mood but far enough below seizure-provoking levels – are summarised below.  They are framed as four practical questions that clinicians weigh for every candidate.

1. Degree of therapeutic urgency / treatment-resistance  
   - FDA-clearance and all major guidelines reserve rTMS for Major Depressive Episode that has failed ≥1 (Canada) or ≥2 (FDA) adequate antidepressant trials.  
   - Efficacy plateaus if illness-duration >5–7 yr, so earlier use is favoured once the resistance threshold is crossed.  
   - Baseline MADRS ≥24–28 or HAMD-21 ≥20 predicts a clinically meaningful 6- to 8-point drop with standard HF-left-DLPFC protocols, justifying exposure to any risk.

2. Individual seizure-threshold (ST) and how far above it we must stay  
   - Resting motor threshold (RMT) is the safety ruler.  Therapeutic dose is set at 100–120 % RMT for HF-rTMS and 90–110 % for LF-rTMS.  
   - ST is lowered (i.e., risk raised) by:  
     – Epilepsy history, cortical stroke, TBI, malformations, active alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal, sleep-deprivation, some antipsychotics (clozapine) or antibiotics (quinolones).  
     – Female sex, youth (<18 yr) and low-iron status also modestly reduce ST.  
   - If any of these are present, the benefit–risk calculus demands either:  
     a) use of low-frequency (≤1 Hz) right-sided stimulation (≈0.02 % seizure rate) instead of HF-left, or  
     b) keep stimulus intensity ≤100 % RMT, inter-train interval ≥20 s, and total pulses/session ≤1 200 (traditional) or ≤600 per block (theta-burst).

3. Choice of protocol with built-in safety margin  
   - Standard 10 Hz left-DLPFC: 3 000–4 000 pulses at 110 % RMT → seizure ≈0.01–0.03 %.  
   - Theta-burst (iTBS): 600 pulses in 3 min, intensity 80–120 % RMT → ≈0.02 %; safe provided blocks are separated by ≥15 min and total pulses ≤1 800/day.  
   - Low-frequency (1 Hz) right-DLPFC: virtually no seizures in >5 000 MDD patients; ideal when epilepsy, TBI or high medications lower ST.  
   - Accelerated paradigms (e.g., 50 000 pulses/5 days) are FDA-cleared for TRD but only with iTBS at 90 % RMT and hourly breaks; push dose or shorten pause and risk climbs.

4. Monitoring rules that keep the patient in the safe envelope  
   - Re-measure RMT every 5–10 sessions; a ≥5 % drop is a biological early-warning of rising excitability – lower the intensity or switch to LF.  
   - Mandatory ear-plugs, seated position, post-session 15-min observation.  
   - Written stop-rules: any myoclonic jerks, déjà-vu, or sudden mood shift → abort, re-check RMT and EEG if necessary.  
   - For patients with known epilepsy, ensure anticonvulsant adherence and avoid rTMS within 24 h of seizure; use LF-right or ≤100 % RMT iTBS, and document informed consent that quotes 1–3 % seizure risk per session.

Bottom line  
rTMS is indicated whenever MDD is moderate-severe and pharmacotherapy has failed, provided the planned protocol keeps stimulus parameters below the individually-determined seizure threshold.  Low-frequency right-sided or spaced theta-burst protocols allow even patients with epilepsy, TBI or poly-pharmacy to gain the antidepressant benefit while holding seizure risk under ≈1 %.
