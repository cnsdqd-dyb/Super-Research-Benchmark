The way in which the different environmental themes are weighted inside the Ecological-Footprint (EF) arithmetic is hard-wired into the headline “number of planets” indicator that most policy makers and media quote. Because the weights are:

1. fixed ex-ante (the carbon-uptake land assumption dominates the result),  
2. not open to user adjustment in the standard Global Footprint Network (GFN) tables, and  
3. aggregated into a single hectare-equivalent before any results are released,

they act as a non-transparent, non-alterable normative layer that is already embedded in the data set that enters policy discussions. Once the EF value is published it is impossible to dis-aggregate it and test how a different set of priorities (e.g., biodiversity, water scarcity, soil erosion) would change the ranking of countries, sectors or products. This has three direct consequences for policy assessment:

- Counter-factual analysis is blocked. A government that wants to know “what would our footprint be if we valued water or biodiversity twice as heavily as CO₂?” cannot answer the question with the published EF accounts; the weighting step is irreversible.  
- Sensitivity checks required by statistical standards (e.g. the 2021 System of Environmental-Economic Accounting—Ecosystem Accounting) cannot be performed, so the indicator fails the “analytical transparency” criterion that Eurostat and OECD apply before an indicator is admitted into formal country reviews.  
- Policy packages that target non-carbon pressures (nitrogen, habitat fragmentation, fish stock depletion) appear artificially ineffective because their weights are small in the pre-set formula, biasing cost-effectiveness rankings toward carbon-centred instruments such as forestation or bio-energy.

In short, the EF’s ex-ante, implicit, non-modifiable weight allocation is not just a methodological footnote; it is a structural feature that prevents the indicator from being used in the counter-factual, “what-if” exercises that modern policy appraisal frameworks (impact assessment, green budgeting, SDG follow-up) require.
