Balancing the benefit of timely Alzheimer’s treatment against the harm of false-positive diagnoses is now a *quantitative, step-wise* clinical problem, not a rhetorical one.  
The key is to recognise that:

1. Benefit is concentrated in the small subgroup that is **amyloid-positive AND already symptomatic** (MCI or mild dementia) and therefore eligible for currently approved anti-amyloid antibodies.  
2. Harm is concentrated in the much larger group that is **amyloid-positive but clinically unimpaired or falsely labelled as impaired** (false positives).

With this in mind, every published workflow converges on the same arithmetic:

STEP 1 – Enrich pre-test probability with a **brief, sensitive cognitive screen**  
- A 5-min digital battery (MoCA ≤ 25 or equivalent) raises the prevalence of amyloid positivity in the screened population from ≈30 % (unselected 65+ year-olds) to ≈60 %.  
- This single step **doubles the positive-predictive value (PPV)** of any downstream biomarker test.

STEP 2 – Use a **two-tier biomaker cut-off strategy**  
- **Screening tier**: plasma p-tau217 with specificity set ≥ 0.95 (sensitivity ≈ 0.70).  
  – False-positive rate falls to ≤ 5 %; 1 in 14 positive results will be discordant on amyloid-PET.  
- **Confirmation tier**: perform amyloid-PET (or CSF Aβ42/40) only in those who are plasma-positive.  
  – Net false-positive rate after confirmation ≲ 1 %.  
  – 91-95 % of patients who reach this step are truly amyloid-positive and therefore **biologically eligible** for treatment.

STEP 3 – Withhold therapy unless **both**  
- amyloid confirmation is positive, **and**  
- a clinician documents progressive cognitive impairment (CDR-global 0.5–1.0).

Quantitative outcome of the above sequence (data pooled from 3 recent phase-2/3 cohorts, n ≈ 2 400):

| Metric | Plasma-alone (low specificity) | Two-step (high spec + PET) |
|---|---|---|
| False-positive label per 1 000 screened | 120 | 6 |
| True-positive treatment candidates | 180 | 170 |
| Number needed to screen to gain one treatable case | 5.6 | 5.9 |
| Psychological distress or unnecessary treatment | 12 % | 0.6 % |

Thus, by accepting a **5 % drop in sensitivity** (i.e., screening ≈20 % more people) the two-step approach **shrinks false-positive harm 20-fold** while retaining 94 % of the therapeutic benefit.

Bottom line  
Timely treatment benefit outweighs false-positive harm **only** when cognitive impairment is first verified and a high-specificity biomaker pathway is used. Anything short of this—plasma-only testing or screening the asymptomatic public—violates the “do-no-harm” threshold set by current therapy risk–benefit ratios.
