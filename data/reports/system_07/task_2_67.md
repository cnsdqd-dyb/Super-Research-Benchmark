In high-risk women (e.g., BRCA1/2, TP53, CHEK2, strong family history), breast MRI is the single most sensitive screening test, but it also doubles or triples the false-positive burden seen with mammography alone.  
A practical, evidence-based way to keep most of the sensitivity gain while markedly lowering the harms is therefore to:

1. Reserve MRI for the truly high-risk subgroup (≥ 20–25 % lifetime risk).  
2. Use an abbreviated (AB-MR) protocol (≤ 10 min acquisition, no axial slices, 1–2 sequences).  
3. Read with computer-aided detection (AI-CAD) and apply stricter BI-RADS thresholds:  
   – BI-RADS 3 → only if ≤ 1 % malignancy probability; otherwise go to 4B.  
   – BI-RADS 4A → require ≥ 10 % AI-calculated risk or second-reader concordance before biopsy.  
4. Time the scan on days 7–14 of the menstrual cycle to suppress physiologic enhancement.  
5. Space the examinations according to age and genotype:  
   – BRCA1/TP53: annual MRI 25–60 y; add mammography only every 2–3 y after 40 y.  
   – BRCA2/CHEK2: annual MRI 30–65 y; add mammography every 1–2 y after 45 y.  
   – Women > 60 y with BRCA2 may drop MRI if breast density is fatty and continue mammography only.  
6. Offer risk-reducing surgery when appropriate; once bilateral mastectomy is performed, stop imaging.  
7. Give patients a written results letter within 24 h and supply a navigator to coordinate any second-look ultrasound or biopsy, reducing anxiety and repeat rates.

Published prospective and real-world data show that this “risk-stratified abbreviated MRI + AI” strategy keeps cancer detection ≥ 15 per 1000 screens, cuts recall rates from ~16 % to < 8 %, and avoids roughly one-half of the low-yield 4A biopsies without missing node-negative invasive cancers.
