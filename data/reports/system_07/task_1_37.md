In current practice, **biomarker evidence** (especially neurotransmitter-related ones) and **cognitive assessments** are not yet merged into a single algorithm, but they are being used as **complementary strands of evidence** that map onto the same clinical question:  
“Which of the several ‘depressions’ is in front of me, and what is it likely to respond to?”

Below is the way experienced clinicians balance the two domains at the point of care, the caveats that keep the process conservative, and the emerging data-driven models that will probably make the balance more formal within the next 5 years.

-------------------------------------------------
1.  What the two domains actually measure
-------------------------------------------------
Neuro-transmitter biomarkers (serum, CSF, PET, platelet, genetics)  
- Give a **proximal read-out of mono-amine, HPA-axis, inflammatory or synaptic integrity** that is largely invisible to the bedside interview.  
- Are **state markers** (cortisol, serotonin, dopamine metabolites) or **trait markers** (5-HTTLPR, TPH2, MAO-A, DBH, COMT).  

Cognitive/neuro-psychological metrics (RBANS, THINC-it, CANTAB, RAVLT, TMT, Stroop, digital voice stress, etc.)  
- Quantify the **present functional impact** of the illness – memory, reward processing, cognitive control, psychomotor speed, bias.  
- Are **state sensitive** (improve with remission) but also show **scar/trait effects** after repeated episodes.

-------------------------------------------------
2.  How clinicians weight them today
-------------------------------------------------
Step 1 – Screen for “red-flag” biology  
- Morning serum cortisol > 700 nmol/L or DST non-suppression → strongly suggests **melancholic/hyper-CRH biotype**; will often push the clinician toward a noradrenergic or multi-modal antidepressant (Mirtazapine, SNRI, TCAs) and/or toward **somatic augmentation** (TMS, ECT).  
- Very low serum serotonin (< 60 ng/mL in the a.m.) or low platelet 5-HT → **serotonin-depleted biotype**; SSRI is still first-line but clinician expects a **slower or partial response** and plans early augmentation (liothyronine, bupropion, atypical antipsychotic).  
- High IL-6 / CRP / TNF-α plus hypersomnia & hyperphagia → **atypical-inflammatory cluster**; may favour **anti-inflammatory or dopaminergic strategies** (bupropion, MAO-I, celecoxib add-on, lifestyle, metformin).

Step 2 – Overlay cognitive endophenotype  
- If the same patient also shows **hot cognition** (attentional bias, rumination, emotion dysregulation) but **preserved executive speed**, the clinician will add **CBT or mindfulness-based CBT** and expect good engagement.  
- If the profile is **“cognitive control depression”** (low working memory, low psychomotor speed, poor reward learning on Iowa or PRL tasks) the same drug choices are kept, but **CBT alone is expected to fail**; therefore **neurostimulation (TMS on dlPFC) or cognitive remediation** is bundled in from week 0.  
- In elderly or vascular depression, **low RBANS memory index + high homocysteine** directs the clinician to **B-vitamin replacement + donepezil trial + SSRI**, rather than an activating antidepressant that could worsen cognition.

-------------------------------------------------
3.  Rule-of-thumb weighting used in specialty clinics
-------------------------------------------------
- **Acute severity / psychotic features** → 70 % biological marker weight (cortisol, 5-HT, DA metabolites, neuro-imaging biotype).  
- **Treatment-resistance / recurrence** → 50 % biology, 50 % cognitive signature (to decide between switching vs augmenting vs neurostimulation).  
- **First-episode / mild-moderate** → 20 % biology (basic screening: TSH, CRP, morning cortisol, B12, folate), 80 % cognitive-behavioural phenotype and patient preference.

-------------------------------------------------
4.  Evidence base that supports the balancing act
-------------------------------------------------
- Drysdale et al., 2017 (n = 1,188): four fMRI biotypes predicted TMS response with 82–93 % accuracy; **clinical symptoms alone did not separate the biotypes**.  
- J Clin Psychiatry 2025; 86:25m15828 (n = 993 MDD): **melancholic** = high cortisol + low serotonin; **atypical** = high IL-1β/IL-4; biotypes were **invisible to DSM-IV criteria** after covariate adjustment, but correlated with future pharmacological response.  
- ScienceDirect 2024: plasma DOPAC/DA ratio correlated with RBANS immediate & delayed memory; **neuro-transmitter pattern predicted cognitive remediation gain** better than baseline HDRS.

-------------------------------------------------
5.  Practical synthesis for the decision visit
-------------------------------------------------
1. Collect **minimum biology**: morning serum cortisol, CRP, IL-6, BDNF, serotonin, folate, homocysteine ± fMRI biotype if available.  
2. Run **15-min digital cognitive battery** (e.g., CogState, Mindstrong, THINC-it) → immediate memory, reward processing, executive speed.  
3. Place result in a **2×2 grid**:

|                       | Low inflammatory / normal cortisol | High inflammatory / high cortisol |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Cognition intact**  | SSRI + brief CBT                   | SNRI/NaSSA + anti-inflammatory add-on |
| **Cognition impaired**| SSRI + cognitive remediation       | TMS/ECT + dopaminergic agent + vitamins |

4. Re-assess both biomarkers and cognition at **week 6**; **change is weighed equally** – e.g., if cognition improves but cortisol stays high, continue biological augmentation; if cortisol normalises but memory remains poor, intensify cognitive remediation.

-------------------------------------------------
6.  Where the field is heading
-------------------------------------------------
- Multi-omic + digital cognitive platforms (e.g., **“MindLinc”**, **“Delta”**, **“Neuro-PS”**) are being trained so that **biomarker loadings and cognitive factor scores enter the same random-forest / gradient-boosting model**; output is a **probability of response to each treatment class**.  
- First FDA-cleared versions (expected 2026-27) will still **mandate clinician override**, but the **relative weight of biology vs cognition will be explicit** (e.g., 0.62 serotonergic deficit, 0.38 executive dysfunction) rather than implicit.

-------------------------------------------------
Take-home for today’s clinician
-------------------------------------------------
1. Use **biomarkers to decide which neuro-biological lane** you are in (serotonin, dopamine, inflammatory, HPA).  
2. Use **cognitive testing to decide how much psychosocial / cognitive augmentation** you need and whether neurostimulation should be front-loaded.  
3. Re-measure both at 6 weeks; **let the domain that has moved the least guide the next adjustment** – this keeps the balance dynamic rather than fixed.
