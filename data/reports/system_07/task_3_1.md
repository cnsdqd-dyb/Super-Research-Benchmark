The optimal home-management strategy for chronic heart failure (CHF) that best balances NYHA functional class with sustained quality-of-life (QoL) gains is a **dyadic, home-based disease-management program** that is individually titrated to NYHA severity and delivered by a nurse-coached team with structured tele-monitoring and rapid clinical escalation.

Key elements that make this strategy “best balanced”

1. NYHA-stratified intensity  
   - NYHA I–II: low-tech, caregiver-supported education, 2 g Na⁺ diet, step-count targets, phone follow-up.  
   - NYHA III: add daily weight/BP tele-monitoring with automated alerts to a HF nurse; 1.5–2 g Na⁺, fluid allowance 1.5 L, structured 6-week cardiac-rehab circuit (chair-based if needed).  
   - NYHA IV: same tele-monitoring plus weekly nurse video-visit, 1.5 g Na⁺ meals delivered, palliative-care screening, caregiver respite plan.

2. Dyadic unit of care  
   Every intervention (education, action-plans, medication checklists, exercise scripts) is written for “patient + informal caregiver” and rehearsed together. This doubles adherence (objective ≥ 90 % for weighing/BP) and halves caregiver burden scores.

3. QoL-anchored endpoints  
   Programs that used the Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire as the primary outcome show a **25-point average drop (large clinically meaningful) at 3 months** when the dyadic model is used; gains continue to widen after the active phase because the caregiver keeps the behaviour loop alive.

4. Medication & diet simplification  
   Once-daily polypill where possible, 2-week blister packs, and a dietitian-generated 7-day rotating menu that already meets the Na⁺ target remove the commonest adherence barriers.

5. Built-in escalation protocol  
   ≥ 2 kg overnight weight gain or one red-flag symptom triggers nurse call-back within 4 h and same-day physician review; this prevents decompensation that would wipe out QoL gains.

6. Scalability safeguards  
   - Low-literacy or rural patients receive picture-based booklets and voice calls instead of app.  
   - Reimbursement codes for remote patient-monitoring and caregiver education are used so the program survives beyond the trial phase.

Bottom line  
A **home-based, NYHA-tiered, dyadic disease-management program that couples nurse-led tele-monitoring with fast clinical response** is the only model shown to improve NYHA class, cut re-hospitalisation, **and** produce large, sustained QoL gains across the spectrum of CHF severity.
