In Integrated Information Theory (IIT), **subjective experience entropy** is not quantified in the same way as thermodynamic or Shannon entropy. Instead, it is approached through the lens of **information geometry**, where the **shape of the space of possible system states**—and how those states are discriminated—determines the **quality and quantity** of conscious experience.

Here’s how this works:

---

### 1. **Subjective Experience as Informational Structure**
IIT posits that **conscious experience** is identical to **integrated information** (Φ), where:

- **Quantity** of consciousness is measured by **Φ**, the amount of cause–effect structure that is **integrated** (i.e., not decomposable into independent parts).
- **Quality** of experience (qualia) is captured by the **shape** of the **qualia space**—a high-dimensional geometric object formed by the **informational relationships** among system elements.

---

### 2. **Entropy in IIT: Not Shannon, but Relative**
IIT uses **relative entropy** (Kullback–Leibler divergence) to quantify how much **cause–effect information** a system **discriminates**:

\[
H[p \| q] = \sum_i p_i \log_2 \frac{p_i}{q_i}
\]

- \( p \): probability distribution of system states **with** a mechanism in place.
- \( q \): probability distribution **without** the mechanism (i.e., the "null" or unconstrained case).

This **relative entropy** reflects how much **uncertainty is reduced** by the system’s causal structure—**not** uncertainty in the observer’s mind, but in the **system’s own causal constraints**.

---

### 3. **Subjective Entropy as Causal Discrimination**
In this context:

- **Subjective experience entropy** is **not** a measure of **uncertainty** in the traditional sense.
- It is a measure of **how much causal structure** the system **discriminates**—i.e., how many **distinct cause–effect states** it can **distinguish** from others.
- The **geometry** of this discrimination space—how these distinctions are **arranged** and **integrated**—defines the **shape of the experience**.

---

### 4. **Information Geometry of Qualia**
In IIT’s **information geometric** framework:

- Each **mechanism** contributes a **point** in a **qualia space**.
- The **entire set of mechanisms** forms a **convex hull**—a geometric object.
- The **shape** of this object encodes the **quality** of experience.
- The **entropy** here is **not of the states**, but of the **causal distinctions**—how **sharply** the system **differentiates** its own possible past and future states.

---

### 5. **Summary: Entropy as Discrimination, Not Uncertainty**
| Concept | Traditional Entropy | IIT Subjective Entropy |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| **Type** | Shannon or thermodynamic | Relative entropy (KL divergence) |
| **Meaning** | Uncertainty or disorder | Causal discrimination |
| **Role** | Measures missing information | Measures **integrated distinctions** |
| **Geometry** | Not geometric | Embedded in **qualia space** |
| **Subjectivity** | Observer-dependent | System-internal, **observer-independent** |

---

### Final Answer:
In IIT, **subjective experience entropy** is quantified via **relative entropy** over the **cause–effect structure** of a system. This entropy is **not** about uncertainty, but about **how much causal discrimination** the system performs. The **geometry** of this discrimination—how distinctions are **integrated** and **shaped**—defines the **qualia**, i.e., the **subjective experience itself**.
