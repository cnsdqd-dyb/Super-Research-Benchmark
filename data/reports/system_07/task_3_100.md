Genome-editing ethics can rebuild the foundations of cross-species justice only if it first dismantles the conceptual scaffolding that keeps “justice” a human-members-only club.  
The following moves are not add-ons to the old charter; they re-engineer the charter itself.

1. Re-anchor moral status in sentience + ecological role, not species membership  
   • Drop the assumption that moral worth rises with genomic distance from *Homo sapiens*.  
   • Replace it with a sliding-scale metric: capacity to suffer, social complexity, ecological keystone function, and future-regarding interests (e.g., migratory routes, cultural behaviours in whales or elephants).  
   • Result: a prairie dog colony or a coral reef can count as a “moral community” whose genetic future may not be overwritten without justification stronger than we currently demand for human subjects.

2. Shift from “do no harm” to “no cross-species domination”  
   • Traditional bioethics asks “will this edit hurt the individual animal?”  
   • Trans-species justice asks “does this edit perpetuate a power asymmetry that allows one species to unilaterally re-write the evolutionary destiny of another?”  
   • Example: CRISPR-ing pigs for xenotransplantation is reframed not as a welfare question alone, but as a case of *hermeneutical injustice*—pigs never got to contest the meaning of their own genome.

3. Create “Genomic Sovereignty” zones  
   • Analogous to Indigenous land rights: populations that display culture, language-like signals, or multi-generational kinship structures gain legal standing over germline edits in their species.  
   • Enforcement: an international trustee body (mixed human & non-human representation via guardian proxies) can veto or delay edits that would erase intra-specific genetic lineages.

4. Embed a “future-voice” test in risk assessment  
   • Before any environmental release (gene-drive mosquitoes, coral heat-tolerance, etc.) the project must model how the least-represented future stakeholders—offspring, non-target species, ecosystem processes—would plausibly consent if they could.  
   • The modelling is adversarial: teams are funded to argue *for* the silenced parties, similar to a public-defender system.

5. Replace “human dignity” with “relational dignity”  
   • Human-dignity talk quietly smuggles in species supremacy.  
   • Relational dignity says value emerges from the *quality of co-existence* among lineages. Editing is permissible only if it increases the range of flourishing trajectories open to *all* affected lineages, not just the engineer’s.

6. Institutionalise “Genomic Reparations”  
   • Where past domestication or near-extinction was driven by human genetic projects (lab mice, industrial chickens, certain dog breeds), fund counter-edits that restore ancestral traits critical for wild competence, even at economic loss to owners.  
   • This begins to treat prior genomic colonisation as a debt, not a sunk cost.

7. Practise “epistemic humiliation” rituals  
   • Every major grant application for cross-species editing must include a public colloquium where the lead researchers present the strongest *non-anthropocentric* objections advanced by critics, followed by a recorded moment of silence for unknown harms.  
   • The ritual is not symbolic; the transcript becomes part of the regulatory dossier and can trigger re-evaluation.

8. Accept a moratorium on *aesthetic* or *convenience* edits in sentient non-humans  
   • Glow-fish, micro-pigs, colour-morph snakes, hypoallergenic cats—all suspended until the above governance structures are operational.  
   • The line between therapy and ornament is policed by the same bodies that police human enhancement, preventing a genetic ghetto of “living toys.”

If these moves are institutionalised, the default question stops being “How do we minimise human risk?” and becomes “Would we still undertake this edit if the evolutionary narrative were co-authored by every lineage whose DNA we touch?”  
Only when the answer can sincerely be “yes” will genome editing have rebuilt justice on non-anthropocentric footings.
