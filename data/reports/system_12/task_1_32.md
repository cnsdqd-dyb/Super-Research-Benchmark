**Unfractionated heparin (UFH) remains the first-line anticoagulant during ECMO support to mitigate the high risk of thromboembolism in the extracorporeal circuit, while strategies aim to minimize bleeding complications, which occur in up to 30-50% of cases.[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf)[[2]](https://journals.lww.com/asaiojournal/fulltext/2022/03000/2021_elso_adult_and_pediatric_anticoagulation.1.aspx)** 

**Initiation and Maintenance Dosing**  
Dosing is highly individualized due to variable pharmacokinetics (half-life ~60-90 min in adults, shorter in pediatrics), influenced by factors like age, critical illness, and antithrombin (AT) levels; no universal fixed doses are mandated by guidelines.[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf) Common practices include:  
- **Loading bolus**: 50-100 units/kg IV at cannulation (often lower, e.g., 20-50 units/kg in pediatrics or high-bleed-risk cases).[[3]](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2022.884063/full)  
- **Maintenance infusion**: Start at 10-30 units/kg/hour (or ~20-40 units/kg/hour per some centers), titrated via protocol.[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8062644)[[5]](https://ecmo.icu/daily-care-anticoagulation-bleeding)  

**Monitoring Assays and Targets**  
Frequent lab monitoring (q1-6 hours initially, then q6-24 hours if stable) guides adjustments, using a multimodal approach combining plasma-based assays (for heparin effect) and whole-blood tests (for global hemostasis). No single assay is perfect; anti-Xa is increasingly favored for specificity to UFH activity, less affected by confounders like fibrinogen or inflammation.[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf)[[2]](https://journals.lww.com/asaiojournal/fulltext/2022/03000/2021_elso_adult_and_pediatric_anticoagulation.1.aspx) Targets (institutional variation noted):  
| Assay | Adult Target | Pediatric Target | Notes |  
|-------|--------------|------------------|-------|  
| **Anti-Xa** | 0.3-0.7 U/mL | 0.3-0.7 U/mL (adjust for age) | Preferred; correlates best with heparin dose; q6-12h.[[6]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950133425000114)[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf) |  
| **aPTT** | 1.5-2.5x baseline (~50-80 s) | Age-adjusted (e.g., 50-70 s) | Common but variable; q6-12h.[[5]](https://ecmo.icu/daily-care-anticoagulation-bleeding) |  
| **ACT** | 180-220 s | 180-220 s | Point-of-care for trends; q1-2h; less reliable/specific.[[7]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10281775) |  
| **VHA (TEG/ROTEM)** | No fixed; heparinase CT ratio ~1.0-1.5 | Similar | Daily/PRN for bleeding/thrombosis; guides transfusions.[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf) |  

**Dose Adjustment Protocols**  
- **Titration**: If anti-Xa <0.3 U/mL, increase infusion 10-20%; if >0.7 U/mL, decrease 10-20% and recheck in 2-4 hours. Adjust ACT target by 10-20 s accordingly. For discordant results (e.g., low anti-Xa/normal aPTT), increase dose; repeat labs.[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf)  
- **Additional labs**: Platelets (>50k non-bleeding, >100k bleeding), fibrinogen (>1.5-2 g/L), AT (daily), free Hb (daily for hemolysis), circuit delta P/visual inspection.[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf)  
- **AT supplementation**: If AT <50-80% and refractory to max UFH, give concentrate (dose: [desired - current] × wt × 1.4 IU/kg); may lower UFH needs but not routine.[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf)  

**Balancing Thromboembolism vs. Bleeding Risks**  
ECMO activates coagulation (shear stress, foreign surfaces), risking circuit clots/emboli, but bleeding exceeds thrombosis frequency. Management is **tailored/multidisciplinary**:[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf)  
- **Thrombosis prevention**: Therapeutic anticoagulation unless contraindicated; monitor for clots (rising delta P >20 mmHg/L/min, hemolysis, d-dimer). Escalate dose or change oxygenator/PRBCs if needed. VA-ECMO often requires stricter targets than VV due to emboli risk.  
- **Bleeding mitigation**:  
  - Hold UFH 4-6h (up to 12-24h) for active bleed/surgery; restart only after hemostasis >12h.  
  - Lower targets (e.g., aPTT 50-70s → 40-60s) or fixed low-dose (~500 U/h, no effect) in high-risk (post-op, trauma, platelets <50k).[[5]](https://ecmo.icu/daily-care-anticoagulation-bleeding)  
  - Reverse with protamine (1 mg/100 U UFH); transfuse PRBCs (Hb >7-10 g/dL), platelets, cryo/FFP; antifibrinolytics (TXA).[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf)  
- **Risk stratification**: High bleed (recent surgery/trauma): Minimal/no heparin initially. High thrombosis (stagnant flow, HIT): Alternatives like bivalirudin. Use VHA for hyper/hypocoagulability. Emerging: Lower-intensity protocols (e.g., anti-Xa 0.2-0.4) reduce bleeding without excess clots per some studies.[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40061555)  

Local protocols, hematology input, and center expertise are essential; ongoing trials explore ultra-low/no-heparin strategies.[[1]](https://www.elso.org/Portals/0/files/pdf/2021_ELSO_Adult_and_Pediatric_Anticoagulation.1.pdf)
