**In proton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC) membrane electrode assemblies (MEAs), catalyst layer (CL) thickness presents a fundamental tradeoff: thinner CLs (typically <5–10 μm) reduce mass transfer resistance by shortening diffusion paths for O₂ and H₂, enabling higher power densities (>1–2 A/cm²) at low Pt loadings (e.g., 0.05–0.125 mgPt/cm²), but they increase susceptibility to flooding, ohmic losses, and degradation, compromising durability.[[1]](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1506248)[[2]](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/ad8267) Thicker CLs (>10–20 μm) accommodate higher Pt loadings for better durability (e.g., ECSA retention >90% after accelerated stress tests) and initial performance but elevate mass transport losses (e.g., higher oxygen transport resistance, OTR), limiting peak power.[[3]](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/1945-7111/acb8df)[[4]](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/14/21/7299)**

**This balance is managed through targeted strategies that decouple performance from thickness, often aiming for ultra-thin CLs (3–7 μm) with DOE targets like >1 W/cm² at 0.7 V and 8,000 h durability.[[1]](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1506248) Key approaches include:**

1. **Microstructural Engineering**: Design hierarchical porosity (nano-pores for ionomer/flooding control, micro-pores for gas diffusion) to lower tortuosity and Knudsen diffusion limits. This reduces pressure-independent OTR by 20–50% in thin CLs, boosting high-current density by minimizing local O₂ starvation.[[5]](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/se/d5se00028a)

2. **Ordered/Structured Catalyst Layers (OCLs)**: Vertically aligned carbon nanotubes (VACNTs) or semi-ordered structures replace random conventional CLs (CCLs), cutting OTR >35% at 0.05–0.1 mgPt/cm², achieving specific power >3.5 W/mgPt (vs. CCLs), and retaining 99% ECSA post-stress for durability. These enable ultra-low loadings without transport penalties.[[5]](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/se/d5se00028a)

3. **Ionomer and Interface Optimization**: Tune ionomer-to-carbon (I/C) ratio (0.8–1.2) and use short-side-chain ionomers for better proton conductivity without pore blockage. Gradient ionomer distribution (higher near membrane) minimizes resistance in thin layers.[[6]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666546824001058)[[7]](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adma.202306387)

4. **Advanced Catalysts and Supports**: High-ECSA nanostructured supports (e.g., N-doped spherical carbons) maximize Pt utilization (>90%) at low loadings/thin CLs, reducing agglomeration for durability. Alloys/core-shell Pt enhance stability.[[8]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-33892-8)

5. **Water and Transport Management**: Pair thin CLs with optimized gas diffusion layers (GDLs)/microporous layers (MPLs) via hydrophobicity tuning, higher cathode pressure, or anode water back-diffusion to combat flooding (e.g., 5x current gain at low T).[[1]](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1506248) Fabrication like electrospray ensures uniform thin films.

6. **Modeling and Holistic Optimization**: Use machine learning or pore-scale simulations for composition/thickness tailoring, predicting tradeoffs (e.g., peak power at ~5 μm).[[6]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666546824001058)[[9]](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports-physical-science/fulltext/S2666-3864(25)00156-0)

**Recent advances (2023–2025) emphasize OCLs and microstructures for >1 W/cm² at <0.1 mgPt/cm² with HDV durability (180k cycles), shifting toward commercialization.[[10]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2667109325000417)[[5]](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/se/d5se00028a)**
