**No direct empirical studies or models precisely address the robustness of "value networks" (typically business ecosystems or cultural value-sharing structures) against economic parameter perturbations (e.g., price shocks, income changes, inflation) in the contagion dynamics of consumer culture (e.g., spread of trends, materialism, or brand adoption via social influence).[[1]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-26961-x)**

**However, drawing from analogous research in network science, financial contagion, and cultural diffusion, the answer is a qualified yes: robust value networks *can* resist such perturbations under certain structural conditions, though vulnerabilities exist.**

### Key Concepts
- **Value Networks**: These are interconnected systems of actors (individuals, firms, communities) exchanging tangible/intangible value (e.g., cultural norms, brands, social status). In consumer culture, they underpin shared values like status-seeking or sustainability that drive behaviors.[[2]](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/43/4/509/2630502)[[3]](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/11/12/3249)
- **Contagion Dynamics**: Modeled via SIR/SEIR-like processes on networks, where "infection" represents adoption/spread of consumer behaviors (e.g., luxury buying as social contagion). Transmission depends on network ties and parameters like adoption probability (β, influenced by affordability).[[4]](https://academic.oup.com/book/32692/chapter/270956851)
- **Economic Parameter Perturbations**: Shifts in β/γ (recovery rate) due to shocks (e.g., recession lowers β by reducing purchasing power).
- **Robustness**: Network-level resilience, measured by percolation thresholds, giant component size post-perturbation, or dynamical stability (e.g., maintaining supercritical spread).[[5]](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Robustness-of-multiplex-networks-to-perturbations-Shown-are-changes-represented-by_fig3_310460248)[[6]](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/209215/1/hicl-2014-18-361.pdf)

### Evidence from Analogous Domains
1. **Network Structure Enables Resistance**:
   - Scale-free or small-world value networks (high-degree hubs like influencers) lower the epidemic threshold for contagion, making spread more persistent despite parameter drops (e.g., 20-50% β reduction in recessions). Random economic shocks (uniform parameter hits) are resisted better than targeted ones (e.g., sector-specific inflation).[[7]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531925000649)[[5]](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Robustness-of-multiplex-networks-to-perturbations-Shown-are-changes-represented-by_fig3_310460248)
   - Modularity (e.g., sub-communities in fashion-sharing networks) localizes perturbations, preventing global collapse of consumer trends.[[3]](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/11/12/3249)

2. **Financial Contagion Parallels**:
   - In banking networks, robust structures (dense core-periphery) limit shock propagation; a 10-30% leverage perturbation rarely triggers cascades if assortativity is high.[[8]](https://bengolub.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/fragilitysurvey.pdf)[[9]](https://www.jstor.org/stable/48622432) Similarly, consumer value networks (e.g., aspirational luxury circles) maintain contagion via non-price factors like status signaling during downturns.

3. **Consumer Culture Examples**:
   - Sharing economy value networks diffuse sustainable consumption resiliently, restructuring around shocks via co-creation (e.g., freecycling booms in recessions).[[10]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0019850120308750)[[2]](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/43/4/509/2630502)
   - Brand value networks in social media sustain diffusion through homophily, buffering economic volatility.[[11]](https://sk.sagepub.com/hnbk/edvol/the-sage-handbook-of-social-media-marketing/chpt/4-consumer-ties-social-media-consumer-culture-social)

### Limitations and Risks
- **Vulnerabilities**: Targeted perturbations (e.g., luxury tax hikes hitting hubs) or low-redundancy networks amplify contagion shifts, flipping stable equilibria to bistable (e.g., thrift contagion overtakes consumerism).[[12]](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23036v1)
- **Dynamic Feedback**: Economic shocks can restructure networks (e.g., reduced interactions), eroding robustness over time.[[13]](https://hammer.purdue.edu/ndownloader/files/28968618)
- **Empirical Gaps**: Most studies focus on financial/supply chains; consumer-specific models (e.g., Bass diffusion on networks) rarely test economic perturbations explicitly.

**In summary, yes—robust value networks (high clustering, redundancy) can stabilize contagion dynamics against moderate economic perturbations by preserving critical spread properties, fostering resilient consumer culture propagation. Extreme shocks or fragile structures (e.g., sparse ties) may overwhelm this.**[[6]](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/209215/1/hicl-2014-18-361.pdf)
