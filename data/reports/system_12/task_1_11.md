**The polymer coating (or drug carrier matrix) on drug-eluting stents (DES) primarily controls the drug elution rate.[[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24533457)[[2]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1043661808000224)[[3]](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1517/17425247.2014.888054)[[4]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2005.03.069)**

This balance addresses two key risks post-stenting: **thrombosis** (clot formation on exposed struts due to delayed endothelial coverage) and **restenosis** (excessive smooth muscle cell proliferation forming neointima). Anti-proliferative drugs like sirolimus, everolimus, paclitaxel, or zotarolimus inhibit both, but prolonged exposure delays endothelial cell regeneration, raising late stent thrombosis risk.[[5]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6307786)[[6]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/atvbaha.107.144220)[[7]](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2025.1622235/full)

The polymer matrix governs release kinetics through:
- **Composition and properties**: Biodegradable (e.g., PLLA) vs. durable polymers; hydrophilicity, thickness, and drug-polymer affinity dictate diffusion/degradation rates.[[8]](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2013177211A1/en)
- **Release profiles**: Often biphasic—rapid initial "burst" (e.g., 30-80% in days 1-30) prevents acute thrombosis/proliferation, followed by sustained/slow release (complete by 90 days) to minimize healing delay.[[9]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306987707004744)[[10]](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/medicaldevices/article/7/1/011005/376504/Development-and-Evaluation-of-Drug-Eluting-Stent)[[11]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S259000642501141X)
- **Advanced designs**: Abluminal reservoirs for vessel-wall targeting (reducing luminal drug exposure), bioresorbable polymers (expose bare metal post-elution for endothelial adhesion), or polymer-free micropores.[[12]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352906720300105)

This tuning ensures early anti-thrombotic efficacy while promoting timely re-endothelialization (e.g., full coverage by 30-90 days in optimized stents), validated in preclinical/clinical studies.[[13]](https://wwwold.iac.cnr.it/~pontrell/pub/pharm_res2022_dualdrug.pdf)[[14]](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/282461/2/282461.pdf)[[15]](https://cdt.amegroups.org/article/view/6827/html) Dual-drug or pro-healing coatings (e.g., with EPC-capturing CD34 antibodies) further refine this.[[16]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8746980)
