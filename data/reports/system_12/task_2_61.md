**The Shock Index (SI), calculated as heart rate (HR) divided by systolic blood pressure (SBP), is a simple, rapid triage tool (normal range: 0.5–0.7; elevated >0.7–1.0) that outperforms isolated vital signs for detecting compensated (occult) shock.**[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6698590)[[2]](https://reference.medscape.com/calculator/599/shock-index)

**In compensated shock—common in early trauma, hemorrhage, or sepsis—SBP may remain normal due to vasoconstriction and catecholamine surge, while HR rises disproportionately; SI captures this imbalance earlier than hypotension alone, signaling hypovolemia before decompensation.**[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6698590)[[3]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3628475)

**For organ perfusion, SI correlates with markers like hyperlactatemia (lactate ≥4 mmol/L), a surrogate for tissue hypoperfusion; thresholds like SI >0.68 predict it sensitively at triage, guiding fluids, vasopressors, or escalation without awaiting labs.**[[4]](https://www.cureus.com/articles/386138-diagnostic-accuracy-of-triage-shock-index-in-predicting-hyperlactatemia-in-patients-with-sepsis-in-the-emergency-department.pdf)[[3]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3628475)

**This balances early recognition (high sensitivity for mortality, transfusion needs, progression to septic shock) against over-intervention risk via its strong negative predictive value: normal SI (e.g., <0.7) reliably rules out severe disease (like elevated lactate or poor outcomes), safely deprioritizing low-risk patients and reducing unnecessary resuscitation.**[[3]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3628475)[[5]](https://westjem.com/original-research/shock-index-and-early-recognition-of-sepsis-in-the-emergency-department-pilot-study.html)

**Elevated SI prompts targeted intervention but risks false positives (lower specificity from confounders like age, beta-blockers, or pain); mitigation includes higher thresholds (>1.0–1.2 for specificity), variants (e.g., modified SI with mean arterial pressure, age-adjusted SI), clinical integration (e.g., with NEWS, capillary refill, mentation), and serial monitoring to confirm trends.**[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6698590)[[6]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10039710)

**Overall, SI enhances triage efficiency in resource-limited EDs by flagging compensated shock (preventing undertriage <5%) while high NPV curbs over-triage (<35% ideal), prioritizing interventions based on perfusion risk without reflexive over-resuscitation.**[[6]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10039710)
