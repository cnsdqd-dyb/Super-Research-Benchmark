**Adaptive algorithms in regulatory compliance (RegTech) systems can successfully balance comprehensive coverage with agility, provided robust model risk management (MRM), governance, and continuous validation are in place. Machine learning (ML) overfitting does not inevitably create systemic risks or regulatory blind spots; these are mitigable challenges rather than inherent flaws.[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)[[2]](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2025/model-risk-management.html)**

### Successes in Balancing Coverage and Agility
ML-driven RegTech excels at processing vast datasets in real-time, detecting subtle patterns in areas like AML, KYC, fraud, and transaction monitoring—enhancing **coverage** by reducing false positives (e.g., sophisticated models flag breaches proactively) while delivering **agility** through adaptive learning that evolves with new threats like money mule networks or emerging laundering tactics.[[3]](https://www.proxymity.io/views/the-future-of-compliance-emerging-regtech-trends)[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf) For instance, tools like India's MuleHunter.ai achieve 95% accuracy in mule account detection across 15+ banks, scaling efficiently without proportional human input and enabling near-instant interventions.[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf) 2025 trends highlight ML's role in predictive compliance, automating reporting, and adapting to regulatory shifts, with 65% of Indian financial AI adoption focused on fraud detection.[[3]](https://www.proxymity.io/views/the-future-of-compliance-emerging-regtech-trends)[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)

Unsupervised ML (e.g., isolation forests, clustering) complements supervised methods (e.g., random forests, gradient boosting) to cover known and novel risks, minimizing blind spots via behavioral analytics and graph neural networks for network analysis.[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)

### Risks: Overfitting, Blind Spots, and Systemic Concerns
Overfitting occurs when models memorize training data, failing on unseen scenarios like economic shifts or adversarial attacks, leading to false negatives and blind spots (e.g., unrepresentative data ignoring qualitative context or underrepresented groups).[[4]](https://www.flagright.com/post/the-biases-and-blind-spots-of-risk-scoring-algorithms) Biases from historical data create feedback loops, amplifying discrimination or economic exclusion, while "black box" opacity erodes trust and regulatory scrutiny.[[4]](https://www.flagright.com/post/the-biases-and-blind-spots-of-risk-scoring-algorithms)[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)

The EBA's 2025 report flags RegTech failures in 70% of supervised EU firms (e.g., fintechs), where poor governance—fragmented controls, untested ML outputs, and over-reliance on off-the-shelf tools—creates vulnerabilities like regulatory arbitrage or AI-driven threats, potentially leading to fines (e.g., FCA's £29m penalties).[[5]](https://www.protechtgroup.com/en-us/blog/regtech-compliance-failures-what-the-eba-report-reveals) Systemic risks include procyclicality (herd-like model failures) and widened access gaps, but these stem from implementation flaws, not ML inherently.[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)

### Proven Mitigations: Making Balance Achievable
Overfitting and blind spots are addressed through:
- **MRM Frameworks**: Iterative lifecycles with independent validation (accuracy, bias checks via AUC-ROC, F1 scores), stress testing, and drift monitoring; random forests inherently reduce overfitting.[[2]](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2025/model-risk-management.html)[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)
- **Explainability & Fairness**: Tools like SHAP/LIME for transparency; data auditing, resampling, and diverse sources (e.g., alternative data).[[4]](https://www.flagright.com/post/the-biases-and-blind-spots-of-risk-scoring-algorithms)
- **Human-in-the-Loop & Governance**: Hybrid systems for context; RBI's FREE-AI mandates policies, audits, and reporting; EBA urges "single source of truth" for controls with expert oversight.[[5]](https://www.protechtgroup.com/en-us/blog/regtech-compliance-failures-what-the-eba-report-reveals)[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)
- **Agile Practices**: Continuous re-validation triggers, unsupervised ML for unknowns, and scalable inventories for coverage without rigidity.[[2]](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2025/model-risk-management.html)

Only 35% currently validate bias fully, but adoption of these (e.g., NASSCOM toolkits) is rising, proving feasibility.[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)

In summary, RegTech ML thrives when governed proactively—delivering agile, comprehensive compliance without inevitable pitfalls. Industry momentum (e.g., 583+ AI tools in finance) and regulatory evolution affirm this balance is not just possible but increasingly standard by 2026.[[3]](https://www.proxymity.io/views/the-future-of-compliance-emerging-regtech-trends)[[1]](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2025/11/bridging-innovation-and-compliance-machine-learning-models-in-fcc.pdf)
