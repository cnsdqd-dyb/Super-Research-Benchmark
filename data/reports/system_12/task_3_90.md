**The mechanism is known as **disconfirmation** (or expectation disconfirmation), a core process within **Expectation Confirmation Theory (ECT)**—also called the Expectancy-Disconfirmation Theory.[[1]](https://open.ncl.ac.uk/theories/14/expectation-confirmation-theory)[[2]](https://www.gapbodhitaru.org/res/articles/(10-18)%20THE%20EXPECTATION%20CONFIRMATION%20THEORY%20A%20SERVICE%20PERSPECTIVE.pdf)**

In ECT, originally proposed by Richard L. Oliver in 1980, satisfaction evaluations arise from a cognitive comparison between **pre-purchase or planning expectations** (formed from prior knowledge, advertising, word-of-mouth, or trip/service planning) and the **actual post-purchase experience or perceived performance**.[[1]](https://open.ncl.ac.uk/theories/14/expectation-confirmation-theory)[[3]](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/Digital-Library/volume-9-issue-9/4743-4750.pdf)

This comparison produces a **gap** or disconfirmation in three forms:
- **Positive disconfirmation**: Actual experience exceeds expectations → satisfaction.
- **Negative disconfirmation**: Actual experience falls short → dissatisfaction.
- **Confirmation**: Actual matches expectations → neutral satisfaction (or mild positivity).[[1]](https://open.ncl.ac.uk/theories/14/expectation-confirmation-theory)

The theory applies widely in consumer behavior, services marketing, tourism (e.g., vacation planning vs. reality), and public services, where "planning expectations" specifically refer to anticipations built during preparation phases.[[1]](https://open.ncl.ac.uk/theories/14/expectation-confirmation-theory)

This differs slightly from the SERVQUAL Gap Model (focused on service quality gaps, with Gap 5 as expectations vs. perceptions), but ECT directly addresses the psychological satisfaction formation via this gap.[[4]](https://forms.app/en/blog/gap-model-of-service)[[5]](https://openstax.org/books/principles-marketing/pages/11-3-the-gap-model-of-service-quality)
