**The phase transformation hysteresis effect in shape memory alloys (SMAs), such as NiTi, arises during reversible martensitic transformations (austenite ↔ martensite) under thermomechanical cycling. This manifests as a characteristic loop in stress-strain or strain-temperature curves, representing irreversible energy dissipation as heat due to friction between variants, twinning, and microstructural irreversibilities.[[1]](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/appliedmechanicsreviews/article/74/4/040801/1145362/Fatigue-of-Shape-Memory-Alloys-With-Emphasis-on)[[2]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1369702117305497)**

**This hysteresis directly couples fatigue life and energy conversion efficiency:**

- **Energy Conversion Efficiency**: In applications like actuators, heat engines, or energy harvesters, efficiency (η ≈ mechanical work output / thermal or mechanical input) is reduced by hysteresis losses. The dissipated energy per cycle (W_hys, the loop area) represents inefficiency—typically 5-20% loss in conventional SMAs. Lower hysteresis boosts η, as seen in low-hysteresis SMAs (LH-SMAs) or supercompatible designs.[[1]](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/appliedmechanicsreviews/article/74/4/040801/1145362/Fatigue-of-Shape-Memory-Alloys-With-Emphasis-on)[[3]](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/11/9/1387)

- **Fatigue Life**: Functional fatigue (degradation of recoverable strain) and structural fatigue (crack initiation/propagation) are driven by accumulated damage from phase transformations. Energy-based fatigue criteria model this via dissipated hysteresis energy: fatigue life (N_f) ≈ C / W_hys, where C is a material constant. Higher W_hys accelerates damage (e.g., martensite stabilization, dislocations), shortening N_f; lower W_hys extends it, often by orders of magnitude in engineered SMAs.[[4]](https://www.academia.edu/6542897/Cyclic_behavior_and_energy_approach_to_the_fatigue_of_shape_memory_alloys)[[5]](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fde12971e6df2c6793a2c00732887c0a909ac5a)[[6]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230955896_Fatigue_analysis_of_shape_memory_alloys_Energy_approach)

**The coupling**: Both metrics inversely scale with hysteresis magnitude. Greater hysteresis dissipates more energy per cycle, lowering efficiency *and* hastening damage accumulation (shorter N_f). Conversely, minimized hysteresis (via precipitates, grain refinement, or supercompatibility) enhances both—e.g., supercompatible NiTi shows unprecedented fatigue resistance (>10^6 cycles) and higher actuation efficiency.[[2]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1369702117305497)[[1]](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/appliedmechanicsreviews/article/74/4/040801/1145362/Fatigue-of-Shape-Memory-Alloys-With-Emphasis-on) Thermomechanical coupling (self-heating from dissipation) amplifies this during high-rate cycling.[[7]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295846180_Energy-based_fatigue_model_for_shape_memory_alloys_including_thermomechanical_coupling)

This shared dependence on W_hys explains the observed trade-off resolution in advanced SMAs, enabling high-efficiency, long-life devices.[[8]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332388268_Stored-energy-based_fatigue_criterion_for_shape_memory_alloys)
