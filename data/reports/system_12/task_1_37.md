In distinguishing depression subtypes—such as cognitive, circuit-based, or neuroanatomical variants—biomarker evidence for neurotransmitters (e.g., serotonin/5-HT, norepinephrine/NA, dopamine/DA) is integrated with cognitive assessments through multimodal, data-driven approaches in research settings, though routine clinical use remains limited by biomarker accessibility and reproducibility.[[2]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576961)

**Neurotransmitter Biomarker Evidence**: Direct measurement (e.g., CSF/plasma metabolites like homovanillic acid for DA or 3-methoxy-4-hydroxyphenylglycol for NA; PET/SPECT for receptors/transporters) reveals subtypes with distinct profiles, such as serotonergic/dopaminergic/GABAergic dysregulation (Subtype 1) versus glutamatergic/endocannabinoid/serotonergic imbalances (Subtype 2), often tied to network alterations in cognitive control regions.[[2]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576961) Low baseline levels predict antidepressant nonresponse (e.g., SSRIs for low 5-HT), while DA/NA deficits link to motivational/decision-making impairments; systematic reviews confirm subtypes with/without NT disturbances.

**Cognitive Assessments**: Standardized batteries (e.g., IntegNeuro for executive function, response inhibition; Go/No-Go tasks) identify ~27% of major depressive disorder (MDD) cases as a "cognitive biotype" with pronounced deficits (e.g., 0.5–0.8 SD below norms), persistent post-treatment, and poor response to standard antidepressants (e.g., sertraline; remission 38.8% vs. 47.7%).[[3]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2806103) Circuit biotypes (e.g., attention hypoconnectivity or cognitive hyperactivation) further stratify via task-evoked fMRI and behavioral tests, correlating with errors/reaction times in attention/executive domains.[[4]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03057-9)

**Balancing for Clinical Decision Making**: 
- **Stratification**: NT biomarkers provide mechanistic targets (e.g., DA/5-HT for Subtype 1 → monoamine-focused therapy; glutamate for Subtype 2 → NMDA modulators), while cognitive tests quantify functional impact (e.g., executive deficits mediating symptom nonresponse) and rule out confounders like pseudo-dementia.[[2]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576961)[[3]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2806103)
- **Treatment Guidance**: Cognitive biotype → prioritize pro-cognitive agents (e.g., vortioxetine) or remediation; circuit biotypes predict differential outcomes (e.g., cognitive hyperactivation → venlafaxine, 64% response; hyperconnectivity → behavioral therapy).[[4]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03057-9)[[3]](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2806103) Multimodal integration (e.g., low DA biomarker + executive deficits → bupropion augmentation) enhances precision, reducing trial-and-error (30–40% inadequate treatments), though validated in trials like EMBARC rather than standard care.[[2]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576961)
- **Challenges/Limitations**: NT biomarkers are indirect/invasive; cognitive tests are practical but influenced by mood. Future: Combine with fMRI/genomics for robust subtyping.
