**In tourist destination decision-making, online reviews and past memories are primarily integrated during the information search and evaluation stages of the multi-stage travel planning process, as outlined in established consumer behavior models adapted for tourism (e.g., need recognition → information search → evaluation of alternatives → choice → post-choice).**[[1]](https://www.cabidigitallibrary.org/doi/pdf/10.1079/9780851990231.0003)[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254379214_Travel_Decision-Making_From_the_Vantage_Point_of_Perceived_Risk_and_Information_Preferences)

**Past memories contribute through "internal information search," where travelers retrieve stored knowledge from autobiographical memory, prior trips, personal preferences, and experiences to form an initial set of potential destinations.**[[3]](https://digitalcommons.georgiasouthern.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1039&context=jamt)[[4]](https://open.clemson.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1273&context=all_dissertations) This process is cognitive, low-effort, and often comes first, relying on heuristics like "I've enjoyed beach vacations before" to narrow options.[[5]](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sogokanko/01/0/01_127/_pdf)[[6]](https://sc.edu/study/colleges_schools/hrsm/faculty-staff/pdfs/xiang_li_documents/cruise_passengers_decision-making_processes_07.pdf)

**Online reviews, as user-generated content (UGC) from sites like TripAdvisor or Google, form a key part of "external information search," providing vicarious experiences, ratings, photos, and social proof to fill gaps in personal knowledge.**[[7]](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstreams/2ff2f375-ad11-450f-a3ae-3c9607113259/download)[[8]](https://norma.ncirl.ie/4046/1/nikhilprabha.pdf) External search is triggered when internal recall is insufficient (e.g., for new destinations) or to reduce perceived risks like uncertainty about quality.[[9]](http://archives.marketing-trends-congress.com/2017/pages/PDF/170.pdf)[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254379214_Travel_Decision-Making_From_the_Vantage_Point_of_Perceived_Risk_and_Information_Preferences)

### Key Ways They Integrate:
1. **Sequential Flow**: Internal search usually precedes and influences external search. Memories guide what reviews are sought (e.g., focusing on family-friendly aspects if past trips succeeded there), acting as a filter for relevance.[[5]](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sogokanko/01/0/01_127/_pdf)[[6]](https://sc.edu/study/colleges_schools/hrsm/faculty-staff/pdfs/xiang_li_documents/cruise_passengers_decision-making_processes_07.pdf) Reviews then validate or challenge memories, such as confirming a destination's changes since a last visit.[[10]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272291555_Tourist_Destination_and_Experience_Choice_A_Choice_Experimental_Analysis_of_Decision_Sequence_Effects)

2. **Comparative Evaluation**: In the alternative evaluation stage, travelers blend both: past memories provide a benchmark (e.g., "Was Paris as romantic as I remember?"), while reviews offer updated, aggregated insights. This forms an "evoked set" of viable destinations. Positive reviews can override negative memories, especially for low-experience travelers; experienced ones weigh memories more heavily.[[3]](https://digitalcommons.georgiasouthern.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1039&context=jamt)[[11]](https://www.goldenratio.id/index.php/grsse/article/download/433/727)

3. **Moderating Factors**:
   - **Traveler Expertise**: Novices rely more on reviews (external-dominant); repeat visitors or experts prioritize memories (internal-dominant).[[12]](https://etda.libraries.psu.edu/files/final_submissions/26276)
   - **Risk Perception**: High uncertainty boosts external review-seeking to supplement memories.[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254379214_Travel_Decision-Making_From_the_Vantage_Point_of_Perceived_Risk_and_Information_Preferences)
   - **Digital Shift**: UGC like reviews has amplified external search in the digital age, but memories retain primacy for emotional or nostalgic decisions (e.g., revisits).[[13]](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/81003/1/JCU_81003_Agarwal_2023_thesis.pdf)[[14]](https://espace.library.uq.edu.au/view/UQ:286096/Pearce_and_Packer_2013.pdf)

**Empirical taxonomies classify strategies as internal-heavy (memory-reliant), external-heavy (review-focused), or hybrid, with hybrids most common for complex choices.**[[3]](https://digitalcommons.georgiasouthern.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1039&context=jamt) Studies confirm both significantly influence choice, with reviews gaining prominence post-digital era but memories anchoring loyalty and personalization.[[10]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272291555_Tourist_Destination_and_Experience_Choice_A_Choice_Experimental_Analysis_of_Decision_Sequence_Effects)[[15]](https://www.emerald.com/whatt/article/11/6/696/457187/Consumer-perception-of-tourist-experience-through)
