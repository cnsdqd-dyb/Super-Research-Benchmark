**Effective population size (Ne) plays a critical role in modulating genetic load—the burden of deleterious mutations that reduce mean population fitness—especially during range contractions driven by climate change, such as habitat loss at trailing range edges.[[1]](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1007450)[[2]](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.22.609253v1.full)**

### Key Mechanisms
1. **Range Contraction Reduces Ne**: Climate-induced habitat fragmentation and loss (e.g., warming trailing edges) lead to smaller census sizes, bottlenecks, isolation, and reduced gene flow. This sharply lowers Ne, the demographic parameter reflecting vulnerability to drift.[[3]](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1913596117)

2. **Impact of Small Ne on Genetic Load**:
   - **Weaker Purifying Selection**: In large-Ne populations, natural selection efficiently removes deleterious alleles. Small Ne strengthens genetic drift, increasing the fixation probability of mildly deleterious mutations, thus elevating genetic (or mutational) load.[[1]](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1007450)[[2]](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.22.609253v1.full)
   - **Drift-Selection Balance**: Load rises as Ne falls below critical thresholds (e.g., ~500–5,000), where drift dominates. Simulations show mutation load increases modestly with declining Ne, but edge effects amplify it via serial bottlenecks.[[4]](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pgen.1007450)
   - **No Core Rescue**: Unlike expansions with gene flow from diverse cores, contractions lack migrant influx, prolonging high load and slowing fitness recovery.[[1]](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1007450)

3. **Consequences for Adaptation and Persistence**:
   - Higher load reduces fitness, inbreeding depression, and adaptive potential, heightening extinction risk under ongoing stress.[[2]](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.22.609253v1.full)
   - Empirical patterns: Small-Ne edge populations (e.g., in *Vitis arizonica*, Arabidopsis) show elevated deleterious variants, predicted to worsen with future contractions despite some range shifts.[[5]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11926394)[[2]](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.22.609253v1.full)
   - Models predict slight load increases (e.g., 3–5% per individual by 2100) tied to low Ne, dispersal limits, and climate, but variance rises at edges.[[2]](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.22.609253v1.full)

### Evidence from Studies
- **Range Shift Models**: Faster contractions/shifts yield smaller front/rear Ne, maximizing load via drift; slower ones allow more mutations but better selection.[[1]](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1007450)
- **Empirical Genomics**: Ne is the top predictor of load; low-Ne edges accumulate harmful variants via drift during historical/future climate-driven shifts.[[2]](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.22.609253v1.full)
- **Past Analogues**: Quaternary warming caused Ne declines and diversity loss, mirroring projected contractions.[[3]](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1913596117)

In summary, declining Ne during range contraction directly amplifies genetic load by favoring drift over selection, compounding climate vulnerability. Conservation should prioritize connectivity to bolster Ne.[[6]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41437-023-00610-z)
