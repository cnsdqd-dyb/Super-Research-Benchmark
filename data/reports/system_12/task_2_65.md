**High-risk proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) is the primary indication for panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) laser treatment, as it best balances the prevention of lesion progression—such as vitreous hemorrhage, tractional retinal detachment, and neovascular glaucoma—with the risks of treatment-related visual field defects.[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)[[2]](https://eyewiki.org/Panretinal_Photocoagulation)**

### Key Definitions and Evidence
- **High-risk PDR characteristics** (from the Diabetic Retinopathy Study [DRS] and American Academy of Ophthalmology [AAO] Preferred Practice Pattern [PPP] 2024):[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)
  - Neovascularization of the disc (NVD) occupying ≥1/4 to 1/3 disc area (equivalent to standard photograph 10A).
  - Any vitreous or preretinal hemorrhage with NVD < standard photograph 10A.
  - New vessels elsewhere (NVE) ≥1/2 disc area with vitreous/preretinal hemorrhage.
  - Neovascularization anywhere near the optic disc or within 1 disc diameter, moderate severity, or with hemorrhage.
  
  PRP is strongly recommended (Level I+, moderate/high-quality evidence) as expeditious treatment, typically full scatter PRP (1200–1600 spots, 0.1-second duration, half-burn width, ≥2 disc diameters from fovea).[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf) The DRS showed PRP halves the risk of severe visual loss (SVL; <5/200) from 26% to 11% at 2 years and 44% to 20% at 4 years in high-risk PDR, preventing progression in ~90% of cases.[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)[[2]](https://eyewiki.org/Panretinal_Photocoagulation)

### Balancing Benefits vs. Risks
- **Benefits**: Regresses neovascularization by ablating ischemic retina (reducing VEGF), slashing SVL risk by >50% at 12 months and halting progression to blinding complications (Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study [ETDRS]; Level I++ evidence).[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)[[2]](https://eyewiki.org/Panretinal_Photocoagulation)
- **Risks**: Substantial peripheral visual field constriction (e.g., -422 dB mean loss vs. -23 dB with anti-VEGF; Protocol S), night vision loss, delayed dark adaptation, choroidal neovascularization, and transient macular edema exacerbation. Risks accumulate over sessions and time (ETDRS post-hoc; Protocol S 5-year data).[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)
- **Why high-risk balances?** Progression risk is imminent and severe (HR=3.97 for worse prognosis; Protocol S), outweighing field loss, especially in type 2 diabetes or poor anti-VEGF adherence.[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)

### Other Indications (with More Deferral to Minimize Risks)
- **Very severe nonproliferative DR (NPDR) or non-high-risk PDR**: Early PRP discretionary (e.g., ≥2 of four 4-2-1 ETDRS characteristics: hemorrhage/MA in 4 quadrants, venous beading in 2, IRMA in 1), as ~50% progress to high-risk PDR within 1 year. Defer if patient compliance allows close monitoring (every 2–4 months); benefits in type 2 diabetes (SVL/vitrectomy reduced 50%).[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)[[2]](https://eyewiki.org/Panretinal_Photocoagulation)
- **Severe NPDR**: Generally defer until high-risk develops (ETDRS), avoiding overtreatment and field loss in lower-risk cases.[[2]](https://eyewiki.org/Panretinal_Photocoagulation)

### Anti-VEGF as Alternative/Adjunct (Often Preferred to Spare Fields)
Anti-VEGF (e.g., ranibizumab, aflibercept; Protocol S, CLARITY) is noninferior to PRP for visual acuity at 2–5 years, with superior short-term VA (+2.8 vs. +0.2 letters), less field loss, fewer vitrectomies (4% vs. 15%), and lower new DME (9% vs. 28%).[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)
- **PRP over anti-VEGF**: High-risk PDR needing rapid control, poor follow-up (anti-VEGF requires monthly visits initially), access/cost barriers.
- **Anti-VEGF first**: Reliable patients, concurrent center-involved DME (20/32 or worse), to minimize field risks.
- **Combination**: Persistent neovascularization/DME or angle neovascularization.

**In summary, treat high-risk PDR promptly with PRP (or anti-VEGF ± PRP) for optimal risk-benefit; defer earlier PDR/NPDR with vigilant monitoring to avoid unnecessary field defects.**[[1]](https://www.aao.org/Assets/811c9cb7-279d-4b3d-9cca-032191e4891c/638749627918470000/diabetic-retinopathy-ppp-pdf)[[2]](https://eyewiki.org/Panretinal_Photocoagulation)
