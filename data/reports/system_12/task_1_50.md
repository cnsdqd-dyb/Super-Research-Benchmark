This question combines niche concepts from dynamical systems theory (fractal dimensions of attractors), cultural evolution (cultural attractors as stable cognitive or social patterns),[[1]](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/1151999) fractal models in cultural analysis,[[2]](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2242848) and evolutionary biology (branch points as splits in phylogenetic trees), but no peer-reviewed literature links them causally in this specific manner.

### Key Concepts and Gaps:
- **Cultural attractors**: In cultural evolution theory, these are stable forms or ideas that cultural systems gravitate toward due to cognitive biases and social transmission (e.g., from cognitive anthropology). Some recent work explores "cultural attractor landscapes" with fractal-like self-similarity across scales for information flow in cumulative culture.[[1]](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/1151999) However, "cultural *identity* attractors" isn't a standard term, and their fractal dimensions aren't quantified or tied to evolution.
  
- **Fractal dimensions**: Used to measure complexity in strange attractors (e.g., in chaos theory) or cultural patterns (e.g., Maya settlements or consciousness models).[[3]](http://www.fau.edu/~ctbrown/The%20Fractal%20Geometry%20of%20Ancient%20Maya%20Settlement.pdf) Speculative extensions exist to thought patterns or AI-human interactions showing fractal attractors in meaning-space,[[4]](https://intuitmachine.medium.com/the-gravity-of-meaning-how-ai-revealed-the-strange-attractors-that-shape-human-thought-f950044a6210) but not for cultural identity.

- **Branch points and evolutionary trajectories**: Common in biology (e.g., genetic phylogenies) or brain evolution (global vs. local neural changes).[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32594640) In culture/memetics, trajectories describe idea spread, but "global-local" ones (e.g., glocalization) don't reference fractal-driven branch points.

No studies model cultural dynamics where attractor fractality predicts evolutionary splits. This phrasing resembles speculative interdisciplinary synthesis (e.g., chaos in psyche or fractal culture models)[[4]](https://intuitmachine.medium.com/the-gravity-of-meaning-how-ai-revealed-the-strange-attractors-that-shape-human-thought-f950044a6210)[[2]](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2242848) rather than empirical science. If it's from an unpublished theory, it lacks verification. For real cultural evolution modeling, look to agent-based simulations or Bayesian attractors, not fractals deterministically dictating branches.
