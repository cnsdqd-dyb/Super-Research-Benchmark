**Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA), particularly comprehensive or multimodal CBA frameworks, is the key mechanism that balances the cost-benefit threshold of multimodal transport against traffic control impacts.[[1]](https://www.vtpi.org/multimodal_planning.pdf)[[2]](https://www.vtpi.org/tranben.pdf)[[3]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282666369_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY)**

In transportation planning, the "cost-benefit threshold" typically refers to the minimum benefit-cost ratio (e.g., BCR >1) or net present value (NPV >0) where a project becomes economically viable, often over 15–40 years with discount rates like 2–4%.[[2]](https://www.vtpi.org/tranben.pdf)[[3]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282666369_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY) Multimodal transport (integrating walking, cycling, transit, and vehicles) involves higher upfront costs (e.g., infrastructure for priority lanes) but yields benefits like reduced vehicle miles traveled (VMT, 3–6× per passenger-mile shifted), congestion relief ($0.10–$0.30/vehicle-mile), parking savings ($2+/trip), and safety/emission reductions.[[1]](https://www.vtpi.org/multimodal_planning.pdf)[[2]](https://www.vtpi.org/tranben.pdf)

**Traffic control impacts**—such as congestion, delays, reliability losses, and capacity constraints (e.g., LOS D–F ratings at 500–2,300 veh/hr/lane)—are monetized within CBA as costs (e.g., bus wear =1.5–4.5 car equivalents, delay values $15–$65/hour for trucks/autos) or benefits (e.g., mode shifts reducing peak loads, car-deterrence at $0.45/transit-km).[[1]](https://www.vtpi.org/multimodal_planning.pdf)[[2]](https://www.vtpi.org/tranben.pdf)[[4]](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop15034/ch4.htm) Tools like multimodal transport models (e.g., Bern tram study, MATSim simulations) forecast these by simulating demand shifts, generated/induced traffic, headways, reliability (e.g., bus delays 3.5–8%), and capacity (e.g., trams free up road space vs. buses).[[3]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282666369_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY)

**How CBA balances them:**
- **User/operator benefits**: Time/comfort savings (e.g., 10.9M CHF/year), revenue (+10.6M CHF/year), capacity gains (+3.8M CHF/year).[[3]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282666369_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY)
- **Externalities**: Environment/safety (+5.9M CHF/year), equity (non-drivers 20–40% of users), health (active modes).[[1]](https://www.vtpi.org/multimodal_planning.pdf)
- **Threshold adjustment**: Sensitivity tests (±30% inputs) yield BCRs like 2.1–3.9 (tram > bus); accessibility metrics (jobs reachable) amplify by 4× vs. speed-based LOS; green hierarchies prioritize modes to self-regulate congestion.[[1]](https://www.vtpi.org/multimodal_planning.pdf)[[2]](https://www.vtpi.org/tranben.pdf)[[3]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282666369_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY)

Complementary approaches like **Level of Service (LOS) for multimodal modes**, **accessibility analysis**, or **Cost-Benefit Threshold (CBT)** in disruption scenarios (e.g., RaaS for rail failures, intervening if CBT >1) refine this, but CBA integrates them holistically.[[1]](https://www.vtpi.org/multimodal_planning.pdf)[[5]](https://theses.hal.science/tel-05057201v1/file/TH2025UEFL2004.pdf) Examples: Calgary transit BCR=1.8; Bern tram=2.8.[[2]](https://www.vtpi.org/tranben.pdf)[[3]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282666369_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY_APPLICATION_OF_MULTIMODAL_TRANSPORT_MODELS_FOR_COST_BENEFIT_ANALYSIS_TRAM_REGION_BERN_STUDY)
