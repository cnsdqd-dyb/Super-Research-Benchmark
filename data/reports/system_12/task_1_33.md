**In secondary prevention of coronary heart disease (CHD), also termed atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) or post-acute coronary syndrome (ACS), guidelines emphasize a multimodal approach combining aggressive LDL-cholesterol (LDL-C) lowering via medications with comprehensive lifestyle modifications to minimize recurrence risk (e.g., major adverse cardiovascular events [MACE]).[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024) Both are pursued concurrently without substitution—lifestyle addresses broader risks (e.g., obesity, hypertension, inflammation), while medications provide dose-dependent LDL-C reduction (≈22% relative MACE reduction per 39 mg/dL drop).[[3]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305897)**

### LDL-C Targets
Current guidelines (2025 ACC/AHA ACS, aligned with prior AHA/ACC and ESC/EAS) recommend:
- **≥50% LDL-C reduction** from baseline using high-intensity statin therapy for all patients.[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024)
- **<70 mg/dL (<1.8 mmol/L)** as the primary threshold; further lowering to **<55 mg/dL (<1.4 mmol/L)** is reasonable (Class 2a) for very high-risk patients (e.g., diabetes, multivessel CAD, recurrent events, polyvascular disease, CKD, or persistent LDL-C ≥100 mg/dL despite statin + ezetimibe).[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024)[[4]](https://wa-provider.kaiserpermanente.org/static/pdf/public/guidelines/ascvd-secondary.pdf)
- No LDL-C is "too low"—very low levels (<30-40 mg/dL) are safe and associated with lowest MACE risk; do not de-escalate therapy.[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024)

ESC 2025 aligns closely, targeting <55 mg/dL for very high-risk CHD, with <40 mg/dL (<1.0 mmol/L) after recurrent events.[[3]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305897)

### Stepwise Medication Adjustments
1. **Initiate immediately post-event**: High-intensity statin (e.g., atorvastatin 40-80 mg or rosuvastatin 20-40 mg daily) ± ezetimibe 10 mg for early risk reduction (benefits emerge within weeks).[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024)
2. **Monitor**: Fasting lipid panel 4-8 (optimally 4-12) weeks post-initiation/adjustment, then every 3-12 months.[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)[[3]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305897)
3. **If LDL-C not at goal**:
   - **Confirm adherence** to current regimen (up to 50% discontinue statins within 1 year).[[4]](https://wa-provider.kaiserpermanente.org/static/pdf/public/guidelines/ascvd-secondary.pdf)[[3]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305897)
   - Maximize statin (if tolerated), then add ezetimibe (additional 15-25% reduction; IMPROVE-IT trial evidence).[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)
   - If ≥70 mg/dL (or 55-69 in high-risk): Add PCSK9 inhibitor (e.g., evolocumab/alirocumab: 50-60% further reduction; FOURIER/ODYSSEY evidence) or inclisiran; consider bempedoic acid for statin-intolerant.[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024)[[4]](https://wa-provider.kaiserpermanente.org/static/pdf/public/guidelines/ascvd-secondary.pdf)
4. **Statin intolerance** (e.g., myalgias): Re-challenge with lower dose/alternate statin; use ezetimibe ± bempedoic acid/PCSK9i early.[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)

### Lifestyle Modifications (Foundational & Concurrent)
- **Core elements** (AHA Life’s Essential 8): Mediterranean/DASH diet (plant-based, fish 2x/week, limit saturated fats/red meat), ≥150 min/week moderate aerobic + resistance exercise, smoking cessation, weight management (BMI <25), ≤1-2 alcohol drinks/day.[[4]](https://wa-provider.kaiserpermanente.org/static/pdf/public/guidelines/ascvd-secondary.pdf)[[3]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305897)
- **Cardiac rehabilitation (CR)**: Refer all (in-person/home-based); improves adherence, lipids, fitness, and reduces MACE by 20-30%.[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)

### Balancing Medication Adjustments with Lifestyle Adherence
- **No trade-offs**: Lifestyle non-adherence does *not* justify delaying/escalating meds—both are independently additive for recurrence risk reduction (lifestyle: multifactorial; meds: LDL-specific). Poor lifestyle adherence signals need for intensified support (e.g., counseling, CR), not de-intensification.[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024)[[4]](https://wa-provider.kaiserpermanente.org/static/pdf/public/guidelines/ascvd-secondary.pdf)
- **Patient-centered algorithm**:
  1. **Assess both at every visit**: Adherence barriers (cost, side effects, knowledge gaps, nocebo); use tools like AUDIT-C for alcohol, BMI/exercise logs.[[4]](https://wa-provider.kaiserpermanente.org/static/pdf/public/guidelines/ascvd-secondary.pdf)
  2. **Shared decision-making**: Discuss costs, preferences (e.g., injectables vs. pills), residual risk; early post-discharge pathways (EHR alerts, remote follow-up).[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024)
  3. **Prioritize early action**: Hospital initiation + 4-week follow-up captures 40-50% needing combo therapy; CR reinforces dual adherence.[[1]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309)
- **Outcomes**: Sequential vs. simultaneous intensification delays benefits (e.g., SWEDEHEART: early combo > monotherapy); real-world gaps (only 30-50% at goal) underscore adherence focus, yielding substantial MACE reduction when optimized.[[2]](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2025.04.024)

This approach minimizes recurrence by maximizing LDL-C lowering (pharmacologic) alongside holistic risk factor control (lifestyle), tailored via ongoing monitoring.[[3]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305897)
