**Antipsychotic medications primarily control positive symptoms of schizophrenia (e.g., hallucinations, delusions) through dopamine D2 receptor blockade in the mesolimbic pathway, where dopaminergic hyperactivity is implicated.[[1]](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/publication/the-four-dopamine-pathways-relevant-to-antipsychotics-pharmacology-2096)** This reduces excessive dopamine signaling in limbic regions like the nucleus accumbens.[[1]](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/publication/the-four-dopamine-pathways-relevant-to-antipsychotics-pharmacology-2096)

**However, D2 blockade in the nigrostriatal pathway—responsible for motor control—leads to extrapyramidal side effects (EPS) such as dystonia, parkinsonism, akathisia, and tardive dyskinesia by disrupting normal dopamine transmission in the striatum.[[1]](https://psychopharmacologyinstitute.com/publication/the-four-dopamine-pathways-relevant-to-antipsychotics-pharmacology-2096)** First-generation (typical) antipsychotics like haloperidol achieve strong, sustained D2 occupancy (>80% in striatum), effectively controlling positive symptoms but at high EPS risk due to slow dissociation kinetics (e.g., haloperidol: ~38 minutes for 50% dissociation).[[3]](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.10.1984-a)

**Second-generation (atypical) antipsychotics balance this through two main mechanisms:**

1. **Rapid ("fast-off") dissociation from D2 receptors:** Atypicals bind transiently to D2 receptors ("hit-and-run"), dissociating quickly (often <60 seconds for 50% dissociation, e.g., clozapine: 15s; quetiapine: 16s; aripiprazole: 52s), allowing physiological dopamine pulses to resume between bindings.[[3]](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.10.1984-a) This spares nigrostriatal motor function (less sustained blockade) while mesolimbic hyperactivity is controlled by intermittent occupancy at therapeutic levels (typically 60-80% D2 occupancy).[[3]](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.10.1984-a) In contrast, slow-off drugs like risperidone (~27 min) or olanzapine (~17 min) carry higher EPS risk, though olanzapine's anticholinergic effects can mitigate this.[[3]](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.10.1984-a)

2. **Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor antagonism (often with higher affinity than D2):** In the nigrostriatal pathway, 5-HT2A blockade on dopaminergic terminals disinhibits dopamine release, increasing endogenous dopamine to compete with the antipsychotic at D2 receptors, reducing net blockade and EPS. In the mesolimbic pathway, hyperdopaminergia predominates, so D2 blockade persists for symptom control, with less serotonergic modulation.

**Additional factors include partial agonism (e.g., aripiprazole stabilizes rather than fully blocks D2) and dose optimization to stay below EPS-threshold occupancy (~80%).[[3]](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.10.1984-a)** This pathway-selective modulation allows atypicals to effectively treat positive symptoms with markedly lower EPS incidence compared to typicals.
