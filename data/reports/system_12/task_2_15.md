**Multi-objective optimization-based resource scheduling algorithms, such as the Multi-Objective Optimization-based Edge Computing Network (MOOECN) algorithm, effectively address this challenge.[[1]](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/19/10837)**

MOOECN models the edge resource scheduling problem as a **Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process (MOMDP)** and employs **multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL)** with **proximal policy optimization (PPO)**. This dynamically optimizes conflicting objectives like task transmission latency (for latency-sensitive, real-time processing) and energy consumption (for energy efficiency and long-term reliability).[[1]](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/19/10837)

Key aspects include:
- **Resolving the latency-energy conflict**: It achieves Pareto-optimal trade-offs by minimizing both latency and energy simultaneously, e.g., average delay of 18.7s and energy of 135.6J in experiments.[[1]](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/19/10837)
- **Real-time processing**: Supports "ultra-low-latency computing services" and "real-time responsiveness for mobile devices" via edge offloading and dynamic allocation.[[1]](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/19/10837)
- **Long-term reliability**: Delivers "long-term optimized strategies," high task completion rates (93.2%), and robust performance in heterogeneous/dynamic edge nodes, ensuring sustained high rewards.[[1]](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/19/10837)

It evaluates edge nodes using entropy weight methods for unified computing power metrics, enabling efficient allocation across nodes.[[1]](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/19/10837)

Similar hybrid/metaheuristic strategies like FFDDE (First-Fit Decreasing with Differential Evolution) also balance energy, makespan (latency), and reliability via task consolidation. These approaches outperform traditional heuristics in edge environments with constrained nodes.[[3]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12715522)[[4]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10586-024-04893-7)
