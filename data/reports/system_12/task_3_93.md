**Quantum non-locality does not literally reconstruct or enable classical information transfer in distributed cognitive systems, due to fundamental physical constraints, but it inspires analogous models and hardware mimics in research on cognition and computing.[[1]](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/quantum-material-mimics-non-local-brain-function)[[2]](http://backreaction.blogspot.com/2020/05/understanding-quantum-mechanics-3-non.html)**

### Quick Primer on Key Concepts
- **Quantum non-locality**: Arises from quantum entanglement, where distant particles exhibit correlated outcomes upon measurement (e.g., Bell violations). Crucially, the **no-signaling** (or no-communication) theorem proves it cannot transmit usable classical information faster than light or without a classical channel—correlations appear "instantaneous" but are unusable for signaling.[[2]](http://backreaction.blogspot.com/2020/05/understanding-quantum-mechanics-3-non.html)[[3]](https://scholarworks.sjsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=4646&context=etd_theses)
- **Distributed cognitive systems**: Refer to cognition emerging across multiple agents, components, or environments (e.g., brain networks, lab teams with tools, swarms, or AI ensembles), where information integrates non-centrally via interactions like neural signals or communication.[[4]](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/518771)[[5]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/246844118_Model-Based_Reasoning_in_Distributed_Cognitive_Systems)

### Why It Doesn't Directly "Reconstruct" Information Transfer
No mainstream scientific evidence supports quantum non-locality as the physical mechanism for information transfer in biological or artificial cognitive systems. Brains operate classically (electrochemical signals at ~100 m/s), and decoherence rapidly destroys quantum effects in warm, wet environments.[[6]](https://d-nb.info/1207756695/34) Claims otherwise (e.g., in fringe consciousness theories like Orch-OR or "quantum mind") remain unproven and face decoherence critiques.[[7]](https://www.bosco-bellinghausen.de/post/quecs-theory-by-bosco-bellinghausen)

### Analogous and Inspirational Roles
Researchers draw parallels in three ways:
1. **Quantum Cognition (Metaphorical Modeling)**: Quantum formalism (without physical quanta) models "non-local" cognitive effects like contextuality (decisions depend on question order) or interference (preferences violate classical probability). E.g., entanglement analogies describe joint judgments in groups, reconstructing distributed decision-making as non-separable states. This "reconstructs" info integration mathematically, not physically.[[8]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0303264721001246)[[9]](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsta/article/383/2309/20240381/355798/From-quantum-cognition-to-conceptuality)[[6]](https://d-nb.info/1207756695/34)
2. **Neuromorphic Hardware Mimics**: Quantum materials exhibit "non-local" responses (stimuli at one site affect distant sites via shared pathways), mimicking brain-like distributed processing for efficient, low-energy computation. A 2023 nickelate film study showed electrical signals creating persistent conductive "memories" across non-adjacent electrodes, enabling pattern recognition without full wiring—like cortical layers integrating info non-locally.[[1]](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/quantum-material-mimics-non-local-brain-function) This hardware "reconstructs" brain transfer for AI.
3. **Speculative Theories**: Some propose non-local consciousness (e.g., QUECS or implicate order models) where entanglement enables holistic info access in distributed minds, but these lack empirical support and violate no-signaling.[[7]](https://www.bosco-bellinghausen.de/post/quecs-theory-by-bosco-bellinghausen)[[10]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388821958_The_importance_of_the_law_of_Quantum_Nonlocality_in_human_Consciousness_Subconsciousness)
