**Yes, standardized processes of carbon accounting generally improve information symmetry in investment decisions.**[[1]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jiec.13574)

### Key Concepts
- **Carbon accounting standardization** refers to uniform methodologies (e.g., GHG Protocol, ISSB/IFRS S2 standards, or regulatory mandates like SEC climate disclosure rules) for measuring, reporting, and verifying corporate greenhouse gas emissions.[[2]](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/04/11/2022-06342/the-enhancement-and-standardization-of-climate-related-disclosures-for-investors)[[3]](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/28/2024-05137/the-enhancement-and-standardization-of-climate-related-disclosures-for-investors)
- **Information symmetry in investment decisions** means investors have access to reliable, comparable data on climate risks/opportunities, reducing asymmetry (where firms know more than investors), adverse selection, and inefficient capital allocation.[[4]](https://davidmytton.blog/standardizing-carbon-accounting)[[5]](https://pollution.sustainability-directory.com/question/how-can-standardized-metrics-reduce-pollution)

### Evidence from Research and Practice
Empirical studies show that carbon disclosures—particularly those detailing accounting methods—reduce information asymmetry, as measured by bid-ask spreads, analyst forecast accuracy, or trading volume. Standardization amplifies this by enabling cross-firm comparability, which voluntary or inconsistent reporting lacks.[[1]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jiec.13574)[[6]](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/59435/1/J%20of%20Industrial%20Ecology%20-%202024%20-%20Steindl%20-%20Carbon%20disclosures%20and%20information%20asymmetry%20Empirical%20evidence%20on%20the.pdf)[[7]](https://www.scienpress.com/Upload/JAFB/Vol%2014_4_4.pdf)

- **Direct impacts**:
  - Firms with detailed carbon accounting descriptions (a step toward standardization) exhibit lower information asymmetry, helping investors assess climate efforts.[[1]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jiec.13574)
  - Higher climate performance (often tied to robust accounting) correlates with reduced asymmetry.[[8]](https://publications.aaahq.org/jiar/article/24/1/13/12903/Impact-of-Corporate-Climate-Change-Performance-on)
  - Standardized frameworks like CDP or stock exchange ESG guidance distinguish genuine sustainability efforts, lowering investor uncertainty.[[9]](https://figshare.mq.edu.au/ndownloader/files/59738816)

- **Mechanisms**:
  | Mechanism | Effect on Symmetry |
  |-----------|---------------------|
  | **Comparability** | Uniform metrics allow benchmarking across firms, reducing "apples-to-oranges" issues.[[4]](https://davidmytton.blog/standardizing-carbon-accounting) |
  | **Transparency** | Mandatory disclosure of methods builds trust and signals quality.[[5]](https://pollution.sustainability-directory.com/question/how-can-standardized-metrics-reduce-pollution) |
  | **Verification** | Standards enable audits, mitigating greenwashing risks.[[10]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395904719_Carbon_Assurance) |
  | **Market efficiency** | Symmetric info supports better pricing of climate risks, attracting green investments.[[11]](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/20/9272) |

- **Regulatory context**: U.S. SEC rules (2024) mandate standardized climate disclosures to enhance investor info on material risks, explicitly addressing asymmetry.[[3]](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/28/2024-05137/the-enhancement-and-standardization-of-climate-related-disclosures-for-investors) Similar effects seen in EU CSRD and China's carbon trading pilots.[[12]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521925006222)

### Caveats
While evidence is positive, effects vary by context (e.g., stronger in high-emission sectors or emerging markets with high baseline asymmetry).[[13]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-024-05697-w) Full standards are emerging (ISSB lacks carbon-specific ones yet), so detailed voluntary descriptions already help, but uniformity would strengthen impacts.[[1]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jiec.13574)

In summary, standardization reduces opacity in carbon data, empowering investors and fostering efficient markets.[[4]](https://davidmytton.blog/standardizing-carbon-accounting)
