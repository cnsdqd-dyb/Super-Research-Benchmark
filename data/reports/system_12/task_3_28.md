**No single set of "institutional guarantees" universally mandates long-term value chains in creative industries (often termed Cultural and Creative Industries or CCIs, encompassing sectors like arts, film, music, design, and publishing) to balance economic benefits (e.g., GDP contributions, jobs, exports) with cultural heritage preservation (e.g., diversity of expressions, intangible heritage). Instead, a multi-layered framework of international conventions, national policies, IP regimes, and support mechanisms provides the foundation, emphasizing sustainability across value chains—from creation and production to distribution and consumption.[[1]](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/111a11b5ff7ce45ffbe4b78c7e96ee5d-0460012024/original/Understanding-Cultural-and-Creative-Industries-and-Designing-Approaches-for-its-Development-A-Guide.pdf)**

### Key International Frameworks
These treaties set binding or normative standards ratified by many countries, promoting CCIs as dual economic-cultural assets:

- **UNESCO's 2005 Convention on the Protection and Promotion of the Diversity of Cultural Expressions**: Adopted by 157 parties (as of 2025), it explicitly recognizes cultural goods/services as having both economic value and non-economic cultural significance. Objectives include protecting/promoting diversity against homogenization (e.g., via globalization/digital platforms), creating conditions for cultures to flourish, and fostering sustainable CCIs development. Key provisions:
  - Article 7: States must support creators/content providers through measures like preferential treatment, infrastructure, and market access to strengthen value chains.
  - Article 9: Promotes international solidarity (e.g., via the International Fund for Cultural Diversity, IFCD) for capacity-building in developing countries, ensuring long-term economic viability without eroding heritage.[[2]](https://www.unesco.org/en/diversidad-expresiones-culturales)[[3]](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000388847)
  - Balances via "cultural exception" principle, allowing policies (e.g., quotas, subsidies) that prioritize heritage over pure trade liberalization.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_the_Protection_and_Promotion_of_the_Diversity_of_Cultural_Expressions)

- **UNESCO's 2003 Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage**: Complements by protecting living heritage (traditions, crafts) integrated into CCIs value chains, with state obligations for inventories, education, and sustainable practices.[[1]](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/111a11b5ff7ce45ffbe4b78c7e96ee5d-0460012024/original/Understanding-Cultural-and-Creative-Industries-and-Designing-Approaches-for-its-Development-A-Guide.pdf)

- **WIPO Copyright Treaty and Berne Convention**: Administered by World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO), these ensure automatic IP protection for creative works, enabling royalties and licensing that sustain creators economically while preserving cultural outputs in value chains.[[1]](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/111a11b5ff7ce45ffbe4b78c7e96ee5d-0460012024/original/Understanding-Cultural-and-Creative-Industries-and-Designing-Approaches-for-its-Development-A-Guide.pdf)

### Regional and National Policies
- **EU Cultural and Creative Sectors Strategy**: Supports interlinked value chains (e.g., heritage, performing arts) via funding (Creative Europe program), digital single market rules, and data harmonization. Emphasizes resilience, green transitions, and heritage integration for 3-4% EU GDP contribution.[[5]](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/cultural-and-creative-industries_en)
- **National CCI Strategies** (e.g., World Bank-guided): Many countries (Kenya, South Africa, Jordan) establish ministries/commissions for CCI mapping, IP enforcement (e.g., Kenya Copyright Board), gender-inclusive funds (e.g., HEVA Fund), and councils for advocacy. These formalize informal sectors, provide finance/skills training, and integrate CCIs into sustainable development plans.[[1]](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/111a11b5ff7ce45ffbe4b78c7e96ee5d-0460012024/original/Understanding-Cultural-and-Creative-Industries-and-Designing-Approaches-for-its-Development-A-Guide.pdf)

### Operational Mechanisms for Long-Term Value Chains
- **Collective Management Organizations (CMOs)**: Entities like SACEM (France) or PRS for Music (UK) collect/distribute royalties, ensuring creators benefit lifelong from works, balancing economic returns with heritage continuity.
- **Public-Private Dialogues and Incubators**: Facilitate market access, digital tools, and green practices (e.g., Estonia's Sustainable Development Commission).[[1]](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/111a11b5ff7ce45ffbe4b78c7e96ee5d-0460012024/original/Understanding-Cultural-and-Creative-Industries-and-Designing-Approaches-for-its-Development-A-Guide.pdf)
- **Data and Monitoring**: Satellite accounts (e.g., Kenya Film Industry) track value chain impacts for evidence-based policies.[[1]](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/111a11b5ff7ce45ffbe4b78c7e96ee5d-0460012024/original/Understanding-Cultural-and-Creative-Industries-and-Designing-Approaches-for-its-Development-A-Guide.pdf)

### Challenges and Effectiveness
These guarantees promote sustainability but face gaps like weak IP enforcement in the Global South, digital disruptions, and fragmentation. Success depends on implementation: e.g., UNESCO's IFCD has funded 100+ projects for CCI value chains.[[1]](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/111a11b5ff7ce45ffbe4b78c7e96ee5d-0460012024/original/Understanding-Cultural-and-Creative-Industries-and-Designing-Approaches-for-its-Development-A-Guide.pdf) Overall, they shift CCIs from short-term exploitation to resilient ecosystems, contributing 3-6% global GDP while safeguarding diversity.[[6]](https://www.thepolicycircle.org/minibrief/the-creative-economy)
