**The classification boundary for problem severity in after-sales service response is primarily defined by the degree of impact on the customer's business operations, system functionality, data integrity, and availability of workarounds or alternatives.[[1]](https://en.ucloudstack.com/after-service)[[2]](https://uptimerobot.com/knowledge-hub/monitoring/severity-levels-explained)[[3]](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/learn/incident-severity-levels.html)**

In after-sales service contexts (e.g., cloud platforms, hardware support, or equipment maintenance), severity levels—often labeled S1/Level 1 (critical), S2/Level 2 (high), S3/Level 3 (medium), and S4/Level 4 (low)—are distinguished using clear thresholds:[[1]](https://en.ucloudstack.com/after-service)[[4]](https://marketplace.huaweicloud.com/intl/portal/mkp/end-user-license-agreement/96a5168e-d9fa-494e-9c0f-2885343908a5)

- **Critical (Level/S1)**: Total system paralysis, service outage, business stoppage, data loss, or core functions unavailable with no alternatives. Boundaries: No workaround; affects entire platform or production.[[1]](https://en.ucloudstack.com/after-service)
- **High (Level/S2)**: Major degradation, potential breakdown risk, partial outages, or non-critical data issues; temporary workarounds exist. Boundary: Core functions impacted but recoverable short-term.[[1]](https://en.ucloudstack.com/after-service)
- **Medium (Level/S3)**: Minor performance issues, non-core component faults, or substandard functions without business halt. Boundary: Core services unaffected; development/test impacts only.[[1]](https://en.ucloudstack.com/after-service)
- **Low (Level/S4)**: Cosmetic issues, consultations, or enhancement requests. Boundary: No faults or service disruption.[[1]](https://en.ucloudstack.com/after-service)

These boundaries determine response SLAs (e.g., 30 mins for L1 in premium service), escalating from low-impact queries to emergency interventions.[[1]](https://en.ucloudstack.com/after-service) In broader frameworks like IT service management, severity often uses an **impact-urgency matrix** (e.g., high impact + high urgency = critical), focusing on user count affected, revenue loss, and resolution feasibility.[[5]](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/kpis/severity-levels)[[6]](https://incident.io/blog/designing-your-incident-severity-levels)

This ensures prioritized resource allocation in after-sales scenarios like equipment repair or software support.[[4]](https://marketplace.huaweicloud.com/intl/portal/mkp/end-user-license-agreement/96a5168e-d9fa-494e-9c0f-2885343908a5)
