**Virtual reality (VR) narratives fundamentally reconstruct the perceptual boundaries of traditional space-time—characterized in media like film or literature by fixed frames, linear progression, and passive spectatorship—through immersive embodiment, user agency, and spatial-temporal fragmentation, fostering a sense of "presence" where users feel inhabitors rather than observers.[[1]](https://inria.hal.science/hal-03654937v1/file/VRIC_2022_paper_3497.pdf)[[2]](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2023.1155700/full)**

### Reconstruction of Spatial Boundaries
Traditional narratives confine space to a proscenium (e.g., screen edges or page layout), imposing a distant, voyeuristic gaze. VR shatters this via **360-degree envelopment and embodied navigation**, where users turn their heads, walk (or teleport), and interact kinesthetically, expanding perception into a spherical, habitable "world" with permeable inside/outside edges. For instance, semi-transparent realms and portals blur figure-ground distinctions, evoking a "perceptual buzz" that confuses virtual with bodily-felt space, as in works like *Osmose* and *Ephémère*. Breath- or balance-driven movement further grounds the body in VR space, creating a "body-in-technology" that mediates real-virtual synthesis, often inducing disorientation or "drifting" as habitual reflexes clash with digital depth.[[4]](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02614367.2024.2379419)

**Spatial montage** redefines editing: users' movements trigger zone transitions (e.g., wormholes), fragmenting environments into reactive "editing cells" bounded by physical play space, contrasting cinema's passive cuts. This grants narrative agency, where proximity or gaze activates elements, reconstructing space as dynamic and user-puppeteered.[[1]](https://inria.hal.science/hal-03654937v1/file/VRIC_2022_paper_3497.pdf) In *RealityMedia*, architectural layouts (rotundas, galleries) enable serendipitous exploration and manipulation (e.g., force graphs), turning space into a non-linear "writing/reading" arena that remediates museums or books.[[2]](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2023.1155700/full)

### Reconstruction of Temporal Boundaries
Conventional time is sequential and clock-bound. VR **merges personal (intuitive, embodied) and impersonal (remembered) time**, condensing durations—e.g., a 6-minute video feels 4–30 minutes via hazy flow and memorable "flashes"—prioritizing affective peaks over linearity.[[4]](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02614367.2024.2379419) Cycles (seasons, dawns) and gaze-triggered transformations introduce flux, where immersion duration summons witherings or blooms, evoking Bergsonian duration tied to perception rather than metric time.

**Discontinuous temporality** via interactivity resolves the "narrative paradox": movements induce ellipses, flashbacks, or branches, modulating presence through breaks that reveal mediation, unlike seamless cinematic flow. Teleportations simulate "time travel," blending eras without cuts, as users navigate interwoven threads at their pace.[[1]](https://inria.hal.science/hal-03654937v1/file/VRIC_2022_paper_3497.pdf)[[2]](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2023.1155700/full)

### Interplay and Broader Implications
Space-time in VR fuse via **sensory-motor loops**: locomotion drives montage rhythm, embodiment synchronizes physical/virtual clocks, and presence fluctuations (immersion to distance) deepen engagement. This reconstructs perception as holistic—blurring real/virtual, self/world—enabling novel storytelling like emergent narratives or spatial meaning-making, though risking disorientation.[[1]](https://inria.hal.science/hal-03654937v1/file/VRIC_2022_paper_3497.pdf) Ultimately, VR elevates the user to co-author, transforming rigid boundaries into fluid, participatory continua.[[4]](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02614367.2024.2379419)
