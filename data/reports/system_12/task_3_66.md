**Yes, kinetic control steps in extraction models significantly affect the process economics of rare earth element (REE) separation from waste sources such as electronic waste, magnets, coal byproducts, or phosphogypsum.[[1]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02393-7)[[2]](https://objects.lib.uidaho.edu/etd/pdf/Lyon_idaho_0089N_10815.pdf)**

### Key Reasons and Mechanisms:
- **Kinetic Limitations in Solvent Extraction (SX)**: REE SX, the dominant industrial method, often faces slow kinetics due to diffusion barriers, interfacial reactions, and extractant dependencies (e.g., PC88A). Extraction rates follow complex rate laws (e.g., quasi-first-order in REE^{3+}, second-order in extractant dimers), leading to depletion at the aqueous-organic interface without convection.[[1]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02393-7)[[3]](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jpcc.5c06366) Equilibrium models assume instant partitioning but overlook these, underestimating residence times (2-3 minutes needed for equilibrium in mixer-settlers).[[2]](https://objects.lib.uidaho.edu/etd/pdf/Lyon_idaho_0089N_10815.pdf)

- **Impact on Process Design**:
  - **Equipment and Staging**: Slow kinetics require larger mixer-settler volumes, more stages (hundreds for adjacent REEs like Pr/Nd due to low separation factors), and longer transients to steady-state, inflating capital costs (capex).[[2]](https://objects.lib.uidaho.edu/etd/pdf/Lyon_idaho_0089N_10815.pdf)
  - **Operating Costs (Opex)**: Higher reagent (extractant, acid, saponifier), solvent, and energy use; e.g., pH control via NaOH/NH4OH adds waste and costs. Impure waste feeds exacerbate kinetics via emulsions or impurities.[[1]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02393-7)[[2]](https://objects.lib.uidaho.edu/etd/pdf/Lyon_idaho_0089N_10815.pdf)

- **Economic Quantifications and Improvements**:
  - SX contributes up to 30% of environmental/economic impacts via solvent/acid use; kinetic enhancements (e.g., magnetic fields boosting rates 18x) cut these, enabling fewer stages and greener recycling (current REE recycle ~1%).[[1]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02393-7)
  - Kinetic models optimize flowsheets (e.g., MATLAB/SIMULINK predicting 99% Nd purity in 12-18 stages), reducing stages/reflux and integration risks for waste-derived feeds.[[2]](https://objects.lib.uidaho.edu/etd/pdf/Lyon_idaho_0089N_10815.pdf)

In summary, ignoring kinetics in models leads to inefficient, costly designs; incorporating them via rate laws or enhancements improves yield, cuts costs, and supports sustainable waste recovery.[[3]](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jpcc.5c06366)
