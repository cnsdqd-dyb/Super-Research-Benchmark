**Primary FDA-cleared indication for rTMS in depression**: Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (rTMS) is indicated for adults with Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) per DSM criteria who have failed **at least one adequate antidepressant trial** (minimal effective dose and duration) in the current episode, typically while medication-free during treatment.[[1]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/guidance-documents-medical-devices-and-radiation-emitting-products/repetitive-transcranial-magnetic-stimulation-rtms-systems-class-ii-special-controls-guidance) This targets treatment-resistant depression (TRD), balancing robust efficacy against low risks; expansions include adolescents aged 15-21 as an adjunct to antidepressants.[[2]](https://ir.neuronetics.com/news-releases/news-release-details/neurostarr-advanced-therapy-receives-fda-clearance-first-line)

**Therapeutic response thresholds**: Clinical response is defined as **≥50% reduction** from baseline on validated scales (e.g., HAM-D, MADRS, QIDS-SR, PHQ-9), with remission as scores ≤7-10 (e.g., HAM-D ≤7). In TRD, meta-analyses/RCTs show active rTMS response rates of **40-60%** and remission **~30%** (vs. sham ~10-30% response), often evident by weeks 2-3 but requiring a full 4-6 week acute course (20-36 sessions, 5/week). Predictors of better response include shorter episode duration, higher baseline severity, and absence of psychosis/anxiety; non-response (<25-50% improvement by week 4) prompts discontinuation or protocol switch.

**Seizure risk safety boundaries**: Overall incidence is **<0.1% per session** (~0.003-0.3/10,000 sessions; 0.07-0.7/1,000 patients), lower with figure-8 coils (~0.08-0.14/1,000 patients) vs. H-coils (~0.4-5.6/1,000).[[4]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1935861X21001182) **Absolute contraindications**: Head/neck ferromagnetic implants (e.g., aneurysm clips, cochlear implants), DBS/VNS, history of seizures/epilepsy.[[1]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/guidance-documents-medical-devices-and-radiation-emitting-products/repetitive-transcranial-magnetic-stimulation-rtms-systems-class-ii-special-controls-guidance) **Relative risks** (managed via screening): Meds (e.g., bupropion, TCAs), sleep deprivation, alcohol/TBI history, electrolyte imbalance—increased ~2-10x but still <1-2% even in epilepsy patients.

**Balancing response thresholds with safety**: The risk-benefit favors rTMS in **non-psychotic TRD after ≥1 failed antidepressant**, excluding seizure-prone patients (via TASS screening, neurologist consult if needed), as **50%+ response rates** (NNT ~6-8) vastly outweigh <0.1% seizure risk—far safer than ECT (~0.1-1% seizures/session) or untreated MDD suicidality. Use FDA-cleared protocols (10-18 Hz left DLPFC, 120% motor threshold [MT], ≤3,000 pulses/session, short trains/≥20-26s ITI per Rossi/NINDS guidelines) with weekly MT checks, session monitoring (EMG/EEG/visual), and emergency readiness.[[1]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/guidance-documents-medical-devices-and-radiation-emitting-products/repetitive-transcranial-magnetic-stimulation-rtms-systems-class-ii-special-controls-guidance) Informed consent emphasizes this; not first-line routinely but viable adjunct/early intervention in select MDD.[[2]](https://ir.neuronetics.com/news-releases/news-release-details/neurostarr-advanced-therapy-receives-fda-clearance-first-line)
