**No, the dynamic evolution of privacy preferences does not inherently destabilize the optimal solution of social welfare functions in multi-agent decision theory.**[[1]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396137804_Social_Welfare_Maximization_in_Noncooperative_Dynamical_Systems_With_Privacy-Preserving_Incentive_Mechanism)[[2]](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/20.500.12932/39338/thesis_report_amwanjesa_6641016.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

In multi-agent decision theory—encompassing areas like game theory, mechanism design, and multi-agent planning—social welfare functions aggregate agents' utilities to identify Pareto-efficient or welfare-maximizing outcomes.[[3]](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/93-1/Papers/060.pdf)[[4]](https://ai.vub.ac.be/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Radulescu_PhD.pdf) Privacy preferences influence agents' utilities, particularly in settings involving data sharing, consensus, or resource allocation (e.g., online social networks or cooperative planning).[[5]](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/chen23e/chen23e.pdf)[[6]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266289788_A_Survey_of_Privacy_in_Multi-agent_Systems)

While privacy preferences can evolve dynamically—through mechanisms like social diffusion, evolutionary games, or learning in multi-agent systems—no reviewed literature indicates this evolution destabilizes welfare optima.[[2]](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/20.500.12932/39338/thesis_report_amwanjesa_6641016.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)[[7]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320178548_Community-Structured_Evolutionary_Game_for_Privacy_Protection_in_Social_Networks) Instead:

- **Privacy-preserving mechanisms stabilize welfare maximization**: Recent work designs incentive schemes for noncooperative dynamical systems to achieve weighted social welfare optima while protecting privacy, showing convergence to stable equilibria.[[1]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396137804_Social_Welfare_Maximization_in_Noncooperative_Dynamical_Systems_With_Privacy-Preserving_Incentive_Mechanism)
- **Evolutionary and diffusion models promote protection**: Frameworks model privacy strategy evolution (e.g., via community-structured games or information diffusion) and demonstrate stable strategies like evolutionary stable states (ESS), aiding welfare-aligned outcomes without instability.[[2]](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/20.500.12932/39338/thesis_report_amwanjesa_6641016.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)[[7]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320178548_Community-Structured_Evolutionary_Game_for_Privacy_Protection_in_Social_Networks)
- **Negotiation and planning handle dynamics**: Agent-based negotiation for data sharing respects evolving privacy preferences while optimizing normalized social welfare; differential privacy in planning ensures task completion (welfare proxy) without preference-induced instability.[[8]](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/9453e36b-cb0c-4481-b5ff-6296e00f5eae-MECA.pdf?abstractid=4987022&mirid=1)[[5]](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/chen23e/chen23e.pdf)

Preferences may be unstable at the individual level (privacy paradox), but multi-agent frameworks incorporate this via adaptive incentives, obfuscation, or consensus protocols, tracking optima over time.[[9]](https://www.heinz.cmu.edu/~acquisti/papers/economics-privacy-oxford.pdf)[[10]](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fmic.20210100) Destabilization would require chaotic or adversarial evolution unmitigated by design, which is not the norm in studied models.[[11]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0005109820304519)
