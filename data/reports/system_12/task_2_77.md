**The "value translation mechanism" appears to be a conceptual framework primarily discussed in cultural studies and AI/media theory, particularly in the context of Stuart Hall's encoding/decoding model applied to global technologies.**[[1]](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01634437251360381)

In this framework—exemplified in analyses of generative AI and digital culture—it addresses the inherent tension where **local encoding** (culturally specific meanings, epistemologies, oral traditions, and symbolic systems from marginalized or indigenous contexts) clashes with **international (or global) aesthetics** (dominant, hegemonic representations from high-resource languages like English, which prioritize universalized norms, visual styles, and ideologies).[[1]](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01634437251360381)

### How It Adjusts the Tension:
The mechanism operates as a **mediating process of adaptation and negotiation**, functioning across encoding (production) and decoding (interpretation) stages:

1. **Negotiated Re-encoding**: Local values are not erased but selectively "translated" into hybrid forms compatible with global platforms. For instance, indigenous African oral aesthetics (performance-based emotions, community interactions) are reformatted into data patterns that AI can process, blending them with dominant visual/textual norms to avoid marginalization.[[1]](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01634437251360381)

2. **Decoding Resistance and Customization**: Users or creators engage in "oppositional" or "negotiated" decoding, reinterpreting global outputs locally. This creates feedback loops where AI tools adapt, reducing erasure of local knowledge by incorporating diverse datasets or prompts that prioritize cultural specificity.[[1]](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01634437251360381)

3. **Hybrid Aesthetic Outcomes**: It balances the pull toward universal appeal (e.g., sleek, English-centric AI-generated art) with local authenticity, producing "glocal" outputs—like decolonial programming or transcultural visuals—that gain international legibility without fully assimilating.[[2]](https://prezi.com/p/zh_qp9stxar0/transcultural-aesthetics-negotiating-global-memory-and-local-identity)

This prevents the "status quo" reinforcement of hegemonic aesthetics while enabling local voices to circulate globally, though challenges persist due to data imbalances.[[1]](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01634437251360381) Similar dynamics appear in design, fashion (e.g., China-chic hybrids), and translation studies, where cultural values are stylized for broader appeal.[[3]](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/126015156.pdf)
