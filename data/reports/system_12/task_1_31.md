**Hemodynamic thresholds for severe aortic stenosis (AS) are standardized in major guidelines to guide valve replacement decisions, primarily based on aortic valve area (AVA ≤1.0 cm² or indexed AVAi ≤0.6 cm²/m²), mean transvalvular gradient (≥40 mmHg for high-gradient severe AS), and peak velocity (V_max ≥4.0 m/s), with adjustments for flow states.[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)[[2]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000923)**

- **High-gradient severe AS**: Mean gradient ≥40 mmHg, V_max ≥4 m/s, AVA ≤1 cm²; intervention (SAVR or TAVR) is Class I recommended for symptomatic patients (LOE A/B) and considered in select asymptomatic cases with low procedural risk and adverse features (e.g., V_max >5 m/s, rapid progression, elevated BNP >3x upper limit, LVEF 50-55%) (Class I/IIa, LOE A/B).[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)[[2]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000923)
- **Low-flow, low-gradient (LFLG) AS** (SVi ≤35 mL/m²):
  - **Reduced LVEF (<50%)**: Confirm true severe vs. pseudo-severe via dobutamine stress echo (DSE; AVA remains ≤1 cm² with gradient increase); Class I for symptomatic confirmed severe (LOE B).[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)[[2]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000923)
  - **Paradoxical (preserved LVEF ≥50%)**: Confirm with projected AVA at normal flow rate, CT calcium score (>2000 AU men, >1200 AU women), or DSE; Class I/IIa for symptomatic confirmed severe (LOE B).[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)
  - **Normal-flow LG AS** (SVi >35 mL/m²): Integrative multimodality imaging; intervention Class IIa if confirmed severe (LOE B).[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)

**Myocardial protection constraints arise primarily from pressure overload-induced concentric LV hypertrophy (LVH), driven by elevated transvalvular pressures/gradients, which increases myocardial oxygen demand, subendocardial ischemia risk (due to supply-demand mismatch, elevated LVEDP compressing coronaries), and fibrosis.**[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)[[2]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000923) Hypertrophied myocardium has reduced ischemia tolerance: higher vulnerability to perioperative ischemia-reperfusion injury, poor antegrade cardioplegia penetration (due to intramural pressure gradients), and uneven protection during SAVR cross-clamp time.[[3]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4157473)

**Reconciliation occurs via multidisciplinary Heart Team evaluation (Class I, LOE C), integrating hemodynamic severity with myocardial/ischemic constraints:**[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)[[2]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000923)
- **Preoperative staging**: Assess LVH (wall thickness, mass via echo/CMR), subclinical dysfunction (GLS <16-18%, fibrosis on LGE CMR), biomarkers (elevated BNP/NT-proBNP signals decompensation), exercise testing (for symptoms/hemodynamic intolerance, e.g., BP drop >20 mmHg), and CAD (CCTA/coronography for ischemia).[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)
- **Risk stratification**: STS-PROM/EuroSCORE II (>4-8% high risk), frailty (e.g., Fried index), comorbidities; severe LVH elevates SAVR risk, favoring TAVR (avoids CPB/cardioplegia, Class I for ≥75 years/high-risk, LOE A).[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)
- **Intervention timing/mode**: Benefits of AVR (symptom relief, LV mass regression, ischemia reduction, survival gain) outweigh risks in symptomatic severe AS (Class I); early AVR in asymptomatic low-risk with adverse myocardial features (Class IIa). TAVR preferred for LVH/high ischemia risk (shorter recovery, no aortic clamp); SAVR with optimized protection (retrograde cardioplegia, frequent infusions) for low-risk/younger patients.[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)[[2]](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000923)
- **Threshold nuances**: High gradients (e.g., ≥60 mmHg) signal advanced LVH/ischemia but still warrant intervention if symptomatic; LFLG may underestimate severity but confirmed cases benefit similarly; no absolute LVH/gradient cutoff defers surgery—net benefit assessed individually.

AVR reverses remodeling, reduces gradients/LVH, and improves ischemia tolerance long-term, justifying intervention despite perioperative constraints.[[1]](https://inavalverhd.inaheart.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-ESC-EACTS-Guidelines-for-the-Management-of-Valvular-Heart-Disease.pdf)
