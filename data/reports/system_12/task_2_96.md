Oral history transmission— the verbal passing down of stories, traditions, myths, and lived experiences within a community—serves as a vital bridge between cultural authenticity and tourist understanding by delivering genuine, unfiltered narratives directly from cultural custodians, fostering emotional connections, and enabling interactive learning without commodifying or diluting the heritage.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666957924000235)

### Preserving and Conveying Authenticity
Unlike scripted tours or static exhibits, oral histories originate from the living voices of community elders, storytellers, or guides, ensuring the transmission remains true to its cultural roots. This "frontstage" authenticity—where locals share personal or ancestral knowledge—avoids the pitfalls of staged performances, allowing tourists to encounter the culture as it is practiced internally.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666957924000235) For instance, in Indigenous Australian tourism, practices like "yarning" (conversational storytelling rooted in oral traditions) draw on traditional custodial knowledge to craft experiences that reflect genuine cultural protocols, countering Westernized tourist imagery.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666957924000235)

### Enhancing Tourist Comprehension and Engagement
Oral transmission humanizes abstract histories, making them relatable through vivid anecdotes, emotions, and sensory details that resonate universally. Tour guides skilled in oral history, for example, create empathy by weaving narratives of historical truth and spiritual values, transforming passive sightseeing into immersive value-sharing that deepens tourists' cultural insights.[[3]](https://ijmehd.com/index.php/ijmehd/article/view/115) Folk narratives in regions like Northeast India further exemplify this: by valorizing local traditions through storytelling, they engage tourists while transmitting identity to younger generations, revitalizing heritage without erasure.[[4]](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1374&context=jti)

### Interactive and Adaptive Dynamics
The dialogic nature of oral history invites questions and adaptations to tourists' perspectives, bridging comprehension gaps in real-time. This interactivity builds trust and emotional bonds, as seen in cultural heritage tours where storytellers blend historical facts with personal flair, shaping perceptions of richness and legitimacy.[[5]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363938325_Storytelling_in_Cultural_Heritage_Tourism_and_Community_Engagement) Challenges persist, such as knowledge management and avoiding exploitation, but benefits include community empowerment, preservation incentives via tourism revenue, and mutual understanding that respects authenticity.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666957924000235)

In essence, oral history transforms tourism from superficial consumption to meaningful cultural exchange, sustaining authenticity while demystifying it for outsiders.
