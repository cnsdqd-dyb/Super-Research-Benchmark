**Histopathological assessment of GVHD activity versus quiescence or resolution, particularly per NIH Consensus Pathology Working Group criteria, guides this balance.**

In GVHD management (acute or chronic, post-allogeneic HSCT), immunosuppressants control donor T-cell attack on host tissues but delay immune reconstitution and heighten infection risk. Serial biopsies from affected organs (e.g., skin, GI tract, liver, mucosa) evaluate response and inform adjustments: persistent active features signal ongoing GVHD requiring sustained or intensified suppression to prevent flares, while resolution supports tapering to promote donor immune recovery and lower prolonged immunosuppression risks.[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4304105/)

### Key Histopathological Guides (NIH 2014 Pathology Consensus)
- **Active/Ongoing GVHD** (continue/escalate immunosuppression):
  - Apoptosis: ≥1 apoptotic body/fragment per biopsy fragment/piece (GI crypts/epithelium, skin basal layer, mucosa); higher thresholds (e.g., >3 in oral) for stronger evidence.
  - Lymphocytic inflammation/exocytosis, vacuolar/interface dermatitis (skin), bile duct damage/cholestasis (liver), crypt destruction (GI).
  - Categories: "Likely GVHD" (clear features, no confounders) or "Possible GVHD" (features + alternatives like infection/drug toxicity, e.g., MMF).

- **Quiescent/Resolved GVHD** (taper immunosuppression):
  - Fibrosis/sclerosis, ductopenia (liver), crypt distortion/Paneth cell loss (GI), epidermal atrophy (skin) **without** apoptosis/inflammation.
  - Category: "Not GVHD" (no features or only chronic sequelae).
  - Supports taper after clinical response, as histological improvement may lag (e.g., months in liver).[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4304105/)

### Balancing Considerations
- **Exclude Mimickers**: Biopsies rule out infection (e.g., CMV IHC), drugs (e.g., MMF apoptosis + eosinophils), preventing unnecessary immunosuppression.
- **Timing/Monitoring**: Protocol-driven serial biopsies (e.g., every 3-6 months or at taper steps) correlate with clinical staging (NIH organ scores) and biomarkers; high-grade activity (e.g., Lerner 4) predicts poor outcomes, favoring caution.[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4304105/)
- **Organ-Specific**: GI (apoptosis threshold key); skin/liver (inflammation + damage); integrate with symptoms/PFTs/labs for global severity.

This approach minimizes over-suppression (infection/poor reconstitution) while avoiding under-suppression (GVHD progression), per NIH guidelines used in trials/clinical practice.
