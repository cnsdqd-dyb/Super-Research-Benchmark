**Optimization of radial (or diametral) clearance in the bearing surfaces.[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4303792)[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245133260_Lubrication_and_wear_modelling_of_artificial_hip_joints_A_review)[[3]](https://eor.bioscientifica.com/view/journals/eor/1/2/2058-5241.1.000004.xml)**

In the tribological design of artificial joints (e.g., hip or knee replacements), radial clearance—the difference between the femoral head and acetabular cup radii—is precisely optimized (typically 20–100 μm depending on materials and diameter) to balance lubrication film formation and wear particle generation.[[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15109756)[[5]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301679X19305109)[[6]](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4442/8/7/72)

**Why this manages the competition:**
- **Lubrication film formation**: Adequate clearance enables elastohydrodynamic lubrication (EHL) or mixed regimes, promoting hydrodynamic entrainment and squeeze-film effects. Per Hamrock-Dowson EHL theory, film thickness \(h_{\min}\) scales positively with equivalent radius (influenced by clearance) and entrainment speed but negatively with load. Optimal clearance (e.g., <50 μm in CoC hips) sustains \(h_{\min} > 0.02–0.1\) μm during gait, separating asperities via synovial fluid (non-Newtonian, protein-rich).[[7]](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4442/10/10/238)[[8]](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/prediction-of-lubricating-film-thickness-in-uhmwpe-hip-joint-repl)[[9]](https://www.ors.org/transactions/48/1015.pdf)
- **Wear particle generation**: Excessive clearance reduces effective radius, thinning films (\(h_{\min}\) drops), increasing asperity contact, adhesion/abrasion, and particle release (e.g., UHMWPE debris or metal ions). Insufficient clearance risks edge loading, misalignment, squeaking, or third-body abrasion from trapped particles.[[10]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4107799)[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245133260_Lubrication_and_wear_modelling_of_artificial_hip_joints_A_review)

**Durability benefits**: Models (deterministic/stochastic EHL) show optimal clearance (e.g., 40–60 μm in MoM hips) cuts predicted wear by 50–80% via thicker films (\(\lambda > 1\)) and reduced contact stress.[[7]](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4442/10/10/238)[[11]](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/101427/3/Predictive%20wear%20modeling%20of%20the%20articulating%20metal.pdf)

**Biocompatibility benefits**: Fewer/smaller submicron particles (e.g., <0.1 vol% reduction in PE debris) limit osteolysis, inflammation, and aseptic loosening from macrophage activation.[[12]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5367615)[[7]](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4442/10/10/238)

This is informed by computational models (EHL with ISO gait loads), in vitro tests, and clinical data, often combined with material choices (e.g., CoC for harder pairings) and larger heads (≥28 mm) for enhanced entrainment.[[7]](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4442/10/10/238)[[3]](https://eor.bioscientifica.com/view/journals/eor/1/2/2058-5241.1.000004.xml)
