**Abbreviated breast MRI (AB-MRI) protocols, often used as supplemental screening alongside mammography, represent a key strategy endorsed by organizations like the ACR for high-risk women (lifetime risk >20%).[[1]](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/3158166/Narrative)[[2]](https://www.jacr.org/article/S1546-1440(23)00334-4/fulltext)**

MRI offers superior sensitivity (71–100%) for detecting cancers in high-risk groups compared to mammography (25–58%) or ultrasound (33–52%), enabling earlier detection. However, its lower specificity (81–98%) results in high false-positive rates (e.g., ~80 per 1,000 screens), leading to unnecessary biopsies, anxiety, costs, and overdiagnosis of indolent lesions.[[4]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1903986)

**AB-MRI addresses this by streamlining protocols** (e.g., fewer sequences, focusing on pre- and early post-contrast images), cutting scan time to ~10 minutes (vs. 30–40 for full MRI), interpretation time, and costs while retaining non-inferior sensitivity and comparable specificity/cancer detection rates in high-risk screening.[[5]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1546144016309401#!)[[6]](https://academic.oup.com/jbi/article/3/2/196/6054295)[[7]](https://www.moffitt.org/for-healthcare-professionals/clinical-programs-and-services/breast-oncology-program/treatments-services/abbreviated-breast-mri) ACR rates both full and AB-MRI as "Usually Appropriate" for supplemental screening in high-risk women, regardless of density.[[1]](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/3158166/Narrative)

**Emerging adjuncts further optimize the balance:**
- **Prediction models** (e.g., from DENSE trial data): Integrate clinical factors (age, BMI, family history, prior biopsies) and MRI features (BI-RADS, kinetics) to triage positives, averting ~45% of false-positive recalls and ~21% of benign biopsies without missing cancers (AUC 0.88).[[8]](https://www.rsna.org/media/press/i/2278)[[9]](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.2021210325) Applicable to high-risk/dense breast contexts.
- **Risk- and age-adapted intervals**: Intensive MRI (e.g., every 6 months) for young BRCA carriers, transitioning to annual/biennial with mammography in older women to curb cumulative FPs/overdiagnosis.[[10]](https://www.ajmc.com/view/optimizing-screening-mri-for-women-with-high-risk-of-breast-cancer)

**NCCN/ACR guidelines recommend annual mammography + MRI starting ~ages 25–30 for highest-risk women (e.g., BRCA1/2), balancing high detection with multimodal specificity gains.**[[11]](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/breastcancerscreening-patient.pdf)[[2]](https://www.jacr.org/article/S1546-1440(23)00334-4/fulltext) AB-MRI + models make this sustainable, minimizing harms while prioritizing sensitivity.
