**The therapeutic index (or therapeutic ratio) of glucocorticoids.**

In glucocorticoid research, the therapeutic index quantifies the balance between dose-dependent anti-inflammatory efficacy and adverse risks like adrenal (HPA axis) suppression. Both effects exhibit strong dose-response relationships: anti-inflammatory benefits (via GR-mediated transrepression of pro-inflammatory transcription factors like NF-κB/AP-1 and transactivation of anti-inflammatory genes) increase with higher receptor occupancy and doses (e.g., prednisone equivalents >5 mg/day), while adrenal suppression—via negative feedback on CRH/ACTH leading to pituitary atrophy and reduced cortisol—correlates similarly (strong risk above 5 mg/day prednisone, or cumulative >0.5 g over weeks).[[1]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279156/)[[2]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279156/#:~:text=Glucocorticoid%20Therapy%20and%20Adrenal%20Suppression)

This index is the separation between efficacy and toxicity dose-response curves. Traditional glucocorticoids have a narrow index due to overlapping curves, limiting high-dose use. Research improves it via:[[3]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279156)[[2]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279156/#:~:text=Glucocorticoid%20Therapy%20and%20Adrenal%20Suppression)

- **Pharmacokinetics**: Low oral bioavailability, lung retention, high protein binding, rapid clearance (e.g., inhaled ciclesonide enhances topical:systemic ratio, reducing suppression).[[1]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279156/)
- **Selective GR modulators (SEGRAs/dissociated steroids)**: Favor transrepression for anti-inflammation over transactivation-linked sides (e.g., ZK216348, CpdA); full dissociation is challenging but improves index.[[2]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279156/#:~:text=Glucocorticoid%20Therapy%20and%20Adrenal%20Suppression)
- **Regimens**: Alternate-day or morning dosing mimics circadian rhythm, minimizing suppression vs. split/evening doses.[[1]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279156/)

Potency rankings (e.g., dexamethasone potency 30x cortisol) further inform this balance, as higher-potency agents risk more suppression per anti-inflammatory effect.[[2]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279156/#:~:text=Glucocorticoid%20Therapy%20and%20Adrenal%20Suppression)
