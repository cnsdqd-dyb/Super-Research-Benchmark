**Yes, partition coefficients (also known as distribution coefficients, denoted as \(K\) or \(D\)) in solvent extraction significantly affect the economic feasibility of urban mining recovery.**[[1]](https://www.onlinescientificsresearch.com/articles/revolutionizing-urban-mining-solvent-extraction-for-sustainable--metal-recovery-from-electronic-waste.pdf)[[2]](https://wasteroadmap.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/32-UKZN-Final_Dissertation_Honours_Bridgemohan_S.pdf)[[3]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393170115_Revolutionizing_Urban_Mining_Solvent_Extraction_for_Sustainable_Metal_Recovery_from_Electronic_Waste)[[4]](https://www.911metallurgist.com/blog/solvent-extraction)

Urban mining involves recovering valuable metals (e.g., copper, rare earth elements, precious metals) from electronic waste (e-waste) or waste electrical and electronic equipment (WEEE) using processes like hydrometallurgy, where solvent extraction plays a central role after leaching.[[1]](https://www.onlinescientificsresearch.com/articles/revolutionizing-urban-mining-solvent-extraction-for-sustainable--metal-recovery-from-electronic-waste.pdf)[[3]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393170115_Revolutionizing_Urban_Mining_Solvent_Extraction_for_Sustainable_Metal_Recovery_from_Electronic_Waste) The partition coefficient quantifies the ratio of a metal's concentration in the organic (solvent) phase to the aqueous phase at equilibrium (\(K = \frac{[M]_{org}}{[M]_{aq}}\)), directly governing extraction efficiency and selectivity.[[2]](https://wasteroadmap.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/32-UKZN-Final_Dissertation_Honours_Bridgemohan_S.pdf)[[4]](https://www.911metallurgist.com/blog/solvent-extraction)

### How Partition Coefficients Impact Economics:
- **Extraction Efficiency and Stages Required**: High \(K\) values (e.g., >10) enable near-complete metal transfer in 1–2 counter-current stages, minimizing equipment (mixer-settlers), solvent inventory, and energy use. Low \(K\) requires more stages, increasing capital (CAPEX) and operating expenses (OPEX).[[4]](https://www.911metallurgist.com/blog/solvent-extraction)
- **Selectivity and Purity**: In complex e-waste leachates with multiple metals, high separation factors (derived from \(K\) ratios) reduce co-extraction of impurities, lowering downstream purification costs and improving metal yield/value.[[2]](https://wasteroadmap.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/32-UKZN-Final_Dissertation_Honours_Bridgemohan_S.pdf)[[5]](https://fardapaper.ir/mohavaha/uploads/2022/01/Fardapaper-Evaluation-of-Solvent-Recovery-Options-for-Economic-Feasibility-through-a-Superstructure-Based-Optimization-Framework.pdf)
- **Solvent Consumption and Recovery**: Favorable partitioning reduces solvent losses (e.g., via entrainment) and enables efficient recycling, critical for cost-effectiveness in low-margin recycling vs. primary mining.[[5]](https://fardapaper.ir/mohavaha/uploads/2022/01/Fardapaper-Evaluation-of-Solvent-Recovery-Options-for-Economic-Feasibility-through-a-Superstructure-Based-Optimization-Framework.pdf)
- **Overall Process Viability**: Solvent extraction's success in urban mining hinges on optimized extractants (e.g., D2EHPA, Cyanex) yielding high \(K\), making recovery sustainable and competitive. Poor coefficients render processes uneconomical due to high costs relative to metal values.[[1]](https://www.onlinescientificsresearch.com/articles/revolutionizing-urban-mining-solvent-extraction-for-sustainable--metal-recovery-from-electronic-waste.pdf)[[4]](https://www.911metallurgist.com/blog/solvent-extraction)

For instance, in copper hydrometallurgy (applicable to e-waste), solvent extraction must economically concentrate dilute solutions; low \(K\) prohibits profitability if leaching costs exceed gains.[[4]](https://www.911metallurgist.com/blog/solvent-extraction) Advances in green solvents (e.g., ionic liquids) target improved \(K\) for e-waste, enhancing feasibility.[[6]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949839225001026)

In summary, favorable partition coefficients are essential for efficient, low-cost urban mining, directly influencing whether recovery competes with virgin ore extraction.
