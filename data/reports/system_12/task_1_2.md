**Local chemical ordering (LCO) or short-range order (SRO) in high-entropy alloys (HEAs) significantly modulates the interaction between dislocation motion and twin boundary (TB) energy during plastic deformation through several interconnected mechanisms.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092583882204484X)**

**1. Tuning of TB Energy via Stacking Fault Energy (SFE):**  
TB energy is intrinsically linked to SFE, as TBs consist of coherent stacking faults. SRO alters local atomic arrangements (e.g., reducing unfavorable Cr-Cr pairs in CrCoNi MEAs), which disrupts preferred bonds during fault formation. This raises intrinsic (γ_ISF) and extrinsic (γ_ESF) SFEs—e.g., from -42.9 mJ/m² to +30 mJ/m² for γ_ISF in CrCoNi with increasing SRO—leading to heterogeneous TB energies. Lower local SFEs promote nanotwinning (TB formation as barriers), while higher SFEs stabilize TBs but suppress twinning nucleation, influencing dislocation dissociation and partial dislocation activity.[[3]](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevMaterials.8.053602)

**2. Pinning and Sluggish Dislocation Motion at TBs:**  
TBs act as horizontal slip paths for dislocations, but LCO structures (e.g., Ni-based in CoCrNi) pin dislocations directly on TB planes, severely restricting their extension and mobility. This pinning creates a coupled strengthening effect, where LCO and TBs together inhibit dislocation propagation more effectively than either alone (e.g., hardness increases up to 31.79% in CoCrNi). SRO-induced chemical fluctuations ruggedize the energy landscape, elevating activation barriers for dislocation glide, cross-slip, or transmission through TBs.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092583882204484X)[[4]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2238785424025973)

**3. Dynamic LCO Evolution and Deformation Coupling:**  
During deformation, LCO develops dynamically (e.g., interstitial-driven in FCC HEAs at cryogenic temperatures), transitioning dislocation slip from planar (pinned by nanoscale LCO domains) to wavy via elevated local SFE. This interacts with TBs through twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP), where LCO refines twins/ε-martensite, boosts work hardening, and hinders strain localization by blocking dislocation entanglement.[[5]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adq6398)

**Overall, LCO/SRO enhances TB resistance to dislocation penetration by increasing energy barriers and pinning sites, promoting synergistic strengthening, higher twinning propensity in some systems, and superior ductility-strength balance in HEAs.[[1]](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092583882204484X)**
