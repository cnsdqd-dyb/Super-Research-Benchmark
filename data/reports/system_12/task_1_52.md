**No direct empirical or established theoretical framework in political science, international relations (IR), or global governance literature confirms that "time-lag effects of national sovereignty parameters" specifically influence the "chaotic attractor structure" of global governance systems, as these exact terms do not appear in relevant studies.[[1]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD1031010.pdf)[[2]](https://www.garretwilson.com/essays/internationalrelations/complex-world)**

Chaos theory has been applied metaphorically and mathematically to IR and global systems since the 1990s, treating interstate interactions (e.g., arms races, power balances) as nonlinear dynamical systems with attractors—stable sets in phase space where system trajectories converge.[[3]](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/81223505.pdf)[[1]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD1031010.pdf) Key examples include:

- **Saperstein's models** of bipolar/tripolar systems, where nations' "devotion to arms" evolves via discrete-time equations with built-in one-year lags (e.g., \(X_{n+1}\) depends on \(X_n, Y_n\)):
  \[
  X_{n+1} = \frac{1}{4}(1 - \alpha)Y_n + \frac{1}{4}(1 - Y_n)
  \]
  \[
  Y_{n+1} = \frac{1}{4}(1 - \beta)X_n + \frac{1}{4}(1 - X_n)
  \]
  Parameters like \(\alpha, \beta\) (response sensitivities) and \(\epsilon\) (coupling) determine attractor structure: low values yield stable attractors (negative Lyapunov exponents, e.g., peace); high values trigger bifurcations to chaotic attractors (positive exponents, sensitivity to initial conditions, war-prone instability).[[1]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD1031010.pdf) Multipolar extensions show expanded chaotic regions, relevant to fragmented global governance.[[1]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD1031010.pdf)

- **Other nonlinear models**, like cellular automata for balance-of-power dynamics or conflict models reconciling realism/liberalism, exhibit multiple attractors (stable multipolar orders, limit cycles) sensitive to parameters and initial conditions, with path dependence explaining IR surprises (e.g., Soviet collapse).[[2]](https://www.garretwilson.com/essays/internationalrelations/complex-world)[[4]](https://www.jstor.org/stable/174507)

**Relevance to the question**:
- **Time-lag effects**: Discrete recursions simulate lags (e.g., delayed responses in arms races), altering dynamics toward chaos, as in delay-induced bifurcations common in nonlinear systems.[[1]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD1031010.pdf)
- **National sovereignty parameters**: Not explicitly defined, but analogous to low coupling \(\epsilon\) (high sovereignty = minimal interdependence, larger stable regions) or response coefficients. Increasing interdependence (reducing sovereignty) expands chaotic attractors.[[1]](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/AD1031010.pdf)
- **Global governance**: Models imply chaotic attractors in multipolar/anarchical systems, but global institutions (e.g., UN) are underexplored—sometimes viewed as chaos-control mechanisms stabilizing orbits.[[2]](https://www.garretwilson.com/essays/internationalrelations/complex-world)[[5]](https://www.ipsa.org/wc/panel/chaos-paradigm-international-law-embracing-non-linearity-anarchical-world)

**Theoretical implication**: Yes, varying sovereignty-like parameters with time-lags could reshape attractors (e.g., from stable bipolarity to chaotic multipolarity), but this remains speculative, limited to stylized simulations rather than validated global governance models. Real-world applications are mostly qualitative (butterfly effect in geopolitics) or policy-oriented (avoiding unstable parameter regimes).[[6]](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/geopolitics-and-chaos-theory-how-the-butterfly-effect-shapes-mena-and-beyond)[[7]](https://journal.iag.ir/article_108150_en.html) Further quantitative modeling is needed for rigorous confirmation.
