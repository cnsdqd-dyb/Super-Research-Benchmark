# Markov Chains and Prediction Reliability

A **Markov chain** is a stochastic model where the next state depends only on the current state, not the full history.  In formal terms, the probability of transitioning to the next state is fully determined by the present state (and not by any earlier states) ([journals.plos.org](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0288928#:~:text=A%20Markov%20chain%20is%20a,a%20full%20introduction%20to%20Markov)).  This “memoryless” property means that, under a Markov assumption, one can predict an activity sequence using fixed transition probabilities for each state.  In a stationary environment, a correctly specified Markov chain can yield reliable next-state forecasts because it captures all relevant information in the current state ([journals.plos.org](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0288928#:~:text=A%20Markov%20chain%20is%20a,a%20full%20introduction%20to%20Markov)).

## Non-Stationarity and Changing Attributes

However, **changing socio-economic attributes** (like income levels, employment patterns, or social norms) can invalidate the stationarity assumption of a simple Markov model.  A homogeneous Markov chain holds the transition matrix constant over time.  If the underlying context shifts, the original transition probabilities may no longer apply, and a fixed model will produce biased or outdated predictions.  In practice, one can use a **time-varying (non-homogeneous) Markov chain** where the transition probabilities evolve.  For example, Zha *et al.* (2024) applied a time-varying Markov-chain model to three decades of urban activity data and found it **“generate[d] daily activity diaries with high accuracy”**, comparable to more complex models ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214367X2300162X#:~:text=that%20daily%20activity%20patterns%20at,any%20specific%20person%20are%20not)).  This shows that accounting for temporal or contextual changes in the transition structure can restore reliability.  If such drift is ignored, predictive accuracy will typically deteriorate (an effect similar to **concept drift** in machine learning).

## Incorporating Socioeconomic Attributes

In many applications, analysts incorporate socio-demographic factors directly into the model.  For instance, clustering methods often associate typical activity patterns with respondents’ socioeconomic profiles ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417415001657#:~:text=2009%2C%20Shoval%20and%20Isaacson%2C%202007a%29,subpopulation%20who%20shares%20similar%20socio)).  If personal attributes change or vary across subpopulations, one might extend the Markov model by treating these attributes as part of the state or as covariates.  A **Hidden Markov Model (HMM)** or a covariate-dependent Markov chain can capture latent or observable factors alongside the immediate activity state.  In effect, the “state” in the Markov chain is expanded to encode socioeconomic context.  The cited study found that when socio-economic data are available, one can **extrapolate patterns to subpopulations with similar backgrounds** ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417415001657#:~:text=2009%2C%20Shoval%20and%20Isaacson%2C%202007a%29,subpopulation%20who%20shares%20similar%20socio)), implying that ignoring such attributes would leave out important predictive information.  In summary, if activities strongly depend on changing attributes, omitting them will reduce reliability.

## Conclusion

In short, the Markovian nature of activity sequences is only as reliable as the stationarity of the system.  If the Markov assumption truly holds and all relevant information is encoded in the current state (including any socio-economic context), then prediction performance will remain strong.  Conversely, shifting socioeconomic conditions break that assumption and require adaptation.  Fortunately, Markov-based models can remain robust: for example, Chong *et al.* (2019) reported that a Markov-chain forecasting strategy was “resistant to changes occurring” in the time series ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417419303033#:~:text=universality%20in%20relation%20to%20the,example%2C%20strategies%20following%20the%20trend)), meaning that with the right formulation the model can adapt to trends or evolving distributions.  In practice, the solution is to allow transitions to vary or to include socio-economic covariates.  By using time-varying transition matrices or hidden-state extensions, one can maintain high predictive accuracy even as socio-economic factors change ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214367X2300162X#:~:text=that%20daily%20activity%20patterns%20at,any%20specific%20person%20are%20not)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417415001657#:~:text=2009%2C%20Shoval%20and%20Isaacson%2C%202007a%29,subpopulation%20who%20shares%20similar%20socio)).

**Answer:** Changes in socio-economic attributes will affect prediction reliability unless the Markov model is updated to account for those changes (for example via time-varying transition probabilities or incorporating socio-economic variables) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214367X2300162X#:~:text=that%20daily%20activity%20patterns%20at,any%20specific%20person%20are%20not)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417415001657#:~:text=2009%2C%20Shoval%20and%20Isaacson%2C%202007a%29,subpopulation%20who%20shares%20similar%20socio)).  A static (homogeneous) Markov chain will lose accuracy under such drift, whereas an adaptable model can remain reliable ([journals.plos.org](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0288928#:~:text=A%20Markov%20chain%20is%20a,a%20full%20introduction%20to%20Markov)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417419303033#:~:text=universality%20in%20relation%20to%20the,example%2C%20strategies%20following%20the%20trend)).
