In practice, “admission stratification” schemes treat supplier promotion much like setting a cut‐off score.  The system first measures each supplier’s performance (e.g. quality, delivery, cost metrics) and then defines a **threshold** (performance boundary) that separates those who will be promoted from those who will not.  In other words, the boundary is simply the minimum performance level a supplier must reach to move up a tier.  This is very similar to how job applicants need a certain cut‐off score on a test to advance: only suppliers above that benchmark are admitted to the higher category ([www.testpartnership.com](https://www.testpartnership.com/blog/impact-cut-off-scores-recruitment.html#:~:text=Cut,poor%20performers%20on%20that%20assessment)). 

How the boundary is chosen depends on how many suppliers the system wants to promote.  For instance, if the goal is to promote roughly the top 10% of suppliers, one might set the threshold at the 90th percentile of performance.  By doing so, only the “cream of the crop” – those scoring above 90% – are elevated ([www.testpartnership.com](https://www.testpartnership.com/blog/impact-cut-off-scores-recruitment.html#:~:text=When%20you%20set%20high%20cut,likely%20to%20be%20extremely%20highly)).  Equivalently, if capacity or policy demands promoting a fixed number of suppliers, the boundary is placed so that exactly that number of top performers qualify.  In short, the performance boundary is determined by examining the distribution of supplier scores and selecting a cut‐off point that yields the desired promotion rate – much like setting an exam pass mark or hiring cut‐off to balance candidate quality and quantity ([www.testpartnership.com](https://www.testpartnership.com/blog/impact-cut-off-scores-recruitment.html#:~:text=Cut,poor%20performers%20on%20that%20assessment)) ([www.testpartnership.com](https://www.testpartnership.com/blog/impact-cut-off-scores-recruitment.html#:~:text=When%20you%20set%20high%20cut,likely%20to%20be%20extremely%20highly)). 

**References:** Conceptually, the boundary acts like a “cut‐off score” in selection systems ([www.testpartnership.com](https://www.testpartnership.com/blog/impact-cut-off-scores-recruitment.html#:~:text=Cut,poor%20performers%20on%20that%20assessment)).  In practice, administrators simply choose the performance threshold (e.g. a percentile) so that the intended number or percentage of top suppliers (for example, the top 10%) qualify for promotion ([www.testpartnership.com](https://www.testpartnership.com/blog/impact-cut-off-scores-recruitment.html#:~:text=When%20you%20set%20high%20cut,likely%20to%20be%20extremely%20highly)).
