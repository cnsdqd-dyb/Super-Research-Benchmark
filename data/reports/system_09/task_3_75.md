# Temporal weighting in seller reputation systems

Seller reputation systems commonly **weight feedback by recency** so that older ratings have less influence.  In practice, designers apply a time-decay or “rating correction” function to gradually dim the impact of old feedback.  This reflects the intuition that a seller’s performance can change over time (e.g. a store can improve or decline), so *“time leeches value from reputation”* ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=users%2C%20remember%20to%20account%20for,value%20in%20the%20same%20interval)).  For example, Randy Farmer (a reputation-system expert) suggests that a digital camera’s rating (resolution) might lose half its weight each year, whereas a restaurant’s rating might only lose about 10% per year ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=users%2C%20remember%20to%20account%20for,value%20in%20the%20same%20interval)).  In other words, the decay rate is chosen based on how quickly the underlying quality typically changes – fast for volatile products, slow for stable services.

**Common decay models** include linear, exponential, and windowed approaches.  A simple method is **exponential decay**: assign each rating a weight $w(t)=e^{-\lambda t}$ (where $t$ is the age), so older reviews count less.  The decay constant $\lambda$ (or equivalently a half-life) is set so that the reputation “forgets” at the desired pace (e.g. half-weight in 1 year).  Another approach is a **fixed time window**: only feedback from the past $T$ months is counted (older feedback is dropped).  For example, eBay effectively uses a rolling 12-month window – *“Seller’s feedback percentages are based on the last 12 months of received feedback”* ([community.ebay.com](https://community.ebay.com/t5/Buying/Feedback-rating-table/td-p/34663570#:~:text=On%20a%20seller%27s%20,signed%20up%20to%20use%20ebay)).  In this windowed method, feedback beyond one year ceases to affect the score.  

There are also hybrid methods.  One can continuously re-scale all past ratings linearly over time (subtracting a fixed percentage per year) or recalculating as new ratings come in.  In practice, systems often choose whichever method best balances accuracy and efficiency.  For instance, the building-reputation.com guidance notes linear decay (“decrease by a fixed percentage per unit time”), windowed recalc (keeping only the latest feedback values in a rolling window), or time-limited recalculation at query time ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=Here%20are%20some%20specific%20algorithms,a%20reputation%20score%20over%20time)) ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=%2A%20Window)).

## Determining the decay rate

The exact **temporal function** (its form and rate) is usually set by policy or tuning experiments.  Platforms may pick a decay based on domain knowledge (e.g. “half‐life of 1 year for electronics, slower for durable goods”) ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=users%2C%20remember%20to%20account%20for,value%20in%20the%20same%20interval)).  Alternatively, the parameters can be learned from data: one can optimize the decay factor so that the time-weighted reputation best matches actual outcomes or sales trends.  For example, one patent describes splitting products into categories and running a grid search for the optimal decay parameter (between 0 and 1) in each category ([patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20180211269A1/en#:~:text=,each%20of%20the%20following%20categories)).  In that analysis, a value nearer 1 means feedback stays relevant longer (slow decay), while a value near 0 means cold older data quickly vanishes ([patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20180211269A1/en#:~:text=,each%20of%20the%20following%20categories)).  In essence, stable categories get high decay-factors (slow discounting), and volatile ones get lower factors (fast discounting).  The platform designer chooses or tunes these based on how quickly seller quality historically has shifted. 

In summary, the **temporal rating-correction function** is a chosen decay or weighting function (often exponential or sliding-window) that de-emphasizes older ratings. Its form and rate are determined by the marketplace’s needs: either by rule (e.g. eBay’s 12-month window) or by empirical tuning (optimizing a decay constant per seller category) ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=users%2C%20remember%20to%20account%20for,value%20in%20the%20same%20interval)) ([patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20180211269A1/en#:~:text=,each%20of%20the%20following%20categories)).  The goal is to give recent feedback more influence, while still reflecting a seller’s past history with diminishing weight.
