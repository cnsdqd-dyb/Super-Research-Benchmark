# Destination Cognition and Image Destination cognition (often called *destination image*) refers to the mental representation – the set of beliefs, impressions, and expectations – that a traveler holds about a place ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/12/22/9413#:~:text=concept%20,their%20acquaintance%20with%20the%20particular)).  It encompasses what people know or think they know (cognitive component) and how they feel about the destination (affective component). This cognitive image critically guides travel decisions: travelers form an initial image from information sources, and then evaluate their actual experience against this pre-formed image (Pike 2002) ([www.scribd.com](https://www.scribd.com/document/770020018/a-comparison-of-pre-visit-beliefs-and-projected-visual-images#:~:text=This%20depends%20on%20whether%20the,Scholars%20investigated%20the%20role)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212571X20301128#:~:text=which%20travellers%20base%20their%20evaluations,creation%20experiences)). 

## Virtual Previews: Building Initial Expectations  
Modern travelers often encounter a destination first through digital media – websites, social media posts, videos, or immersive VR/AR tours. These **virtual previews** powerfully shape the initial cognitive image. For example, immersive VR tours create strong *mental imagery*: users report vivid simulations of “being there,” which raises hedonic expectations of how happy a visit would be ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261517721000790#:~:text=destination%20either%20in%20traditional%20still,previous%20experience%20with%20the%20destination)).  Research shows that viewing a destination in VR produces higher levels of mental imagery and positive emotion forecasting than static images, which in turn increases the intention to visit ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261517721000790#:~:text=destination%20either%20in%20traditional%20still,previous%20experience%20with%20the%20destination)).  In short, visually rich previews become a template of the destination in the traveler’s mind.  

Virtual content provides selective cues (scenic views, cultural highlights, activities) that travelers weave into beliefs. According to cue-consistency theory, people rely on multiple cues to form a coherent image ([www.wisdomlib.org](https://www.wisdomlib.org/science/journal/sustainability-journal-mdpi/d/doc1846318.html#:~:text=Sustainability%202024%20%2C%2016%20%2C,highlighting%20its%20central%20role%20in)) ([www.wisdomlib.org](https://www.wisdomlib.org/science/journal/sustainability-journal-mdpi/d/doc1846318.html#:~:text=consistency%20theory%20proposes%20that%20alignment,theory%20is%20crucial%2C%20as%20information)).  When virtual previews use high-quality visual and auditory cues, they enhance the sense of “presence” and create a compelling initial image ([www.wisdomlib.org](https://www.wisdomlib.org/science/journal/sustainability-journal-mdpi/d/doc1846318.html#:~:text=consistency%20theory%20proposes%20that%20alignment,theory%20is%20crucial%2C%20as%20information)).  Conversely, if online information is sparse or inconsistent, travelers may form a vague or even negative image. In practice, well-designed virtual tours set precise expectations.  Indeed, studies find that adding a virtual tour (e.g. of an Airbnb property) can substantially increase guests’ satisfaction with the real stay by aligning expectations and reality ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387940550_Do_Virtual_Tours_Really_Enhance_Customer_Satisfaction_with_Physical_Experiences_A_Quasi-Experimental_Study_on_Airbnb#:~:text=satisfaction%20of%20the%20physical%20experiences,across%20online%20and%20offline%20channels)). 

Importantly, the image formed during virtual preview tends to persist.  Kim et al. (2021) note that a *positive destination image created virtually “is not likely to dissipate immediately after the user leaves the virtual world.”*  In other words, the salient mental picture carries over into reality and boosts the traveler’s motivation to visit ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355830823_Virtual_Tours_Encourage_Intentions_to_Travel_and_Willingness_to_Pay_via_Spatial_Presence_Enjoyment_and_Destination_Image#:~:text=Similarly%2C%20the%20positive%20destination%20image,virtual%20preview%20of%20the%20destination)).  Thus, virtual previews effectively seed the cognitive image: they make the destination familiar, desirable, and cognitively “real” before any physical travel. 

## On-Site Visits: Multi-Sensory Confirmation and Learning  
When travelers finally visit the destination, they gather the richer, multi-sensory input that virtual media could only partially convey.  A live visit provides not just sights and sounds, but also smells, tastes, touch, and direct social encounters. Tourism research stresses that *“tourism engagements encompass both visual and non-visual aspects such as sound, smell, taste, and touch”* ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/18/8030#:~:text=Tourism%20engagements%20encompass%20both%20visual,20)).  In fact, multi-sensory stimuli are crucial to creating memorable experiences ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/18/8030#:~:text=Tourism%20engagements%20encompass%20both%20visual,20)).  This embodied engagement gives depth to the cognitive image. For example, feeling the sea breeze, tasting local cuisine, or conversing with residents adds information that was absent from the preview. These real inputs **enrich or amend** the mental model built from the preview.

The on-site experience thus serves as a litmus test of expectations.  Travelers inevitably compare what they imagined (pre-visit image) to what they actually encounter. If on-site reality matches the mental image, the traveler’s cognition is confirmed and strengthened; travelers feel satisfied that their expectations were met. If the real experience falls short, this creates *expectation disconfirmation*, leading to disappointment and a downward revision of the destination image ([www.scribd.com](https://www.scribd.com/document/770020018/a-comparison-of-pre-visit-beliefs-and-projected-visual-images#:~:text=This%20depends%20on%20whether%20the,Scholars%20investigated%20the%20role)) ([www.wisdomlib.org](https://www.wisdomlib.org/science/journal/sustainability-journal-mdpi/d/doc1846318.html#:~:text=consistency%20theory%20proposes%20that%20alignment,theory%20is%20crucial%2C%20as%20information)).  Conversely, if the trip exceeds the preview (for example, a modest promo video understates a beautiful scene), the actual experience can *augment* the cognitive image, making it even more positive than anticipated. 

## Interaction: Integrating Virtual and Real Information  
Destination cognition ultimately forms through the **feedback loop** between virtual previews and physical visits. Pre-travel images guide how tourists perceive and interpret on-site experiences, and the new information from the trip updates their image of the place. As one study notes, *“At the destination, tourist evaluations are based on pre-conceived imagery of what they would experience”* ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212571X20301128#:~:text=which%20travellers%20base%20their%20evaluations,creation%20experiences)).  In effect, the pre-visit image becomes the benchmark against which on-site cues are judged.  Cue-consistency theory predicts that when virtual cues and actual experience align, tourists feel confident and consistent; if cues conflict, they feel cognitive dissonance ([www.wisdomlib.org](https://www.wisdomlib.org/science/journal/sustainability-journal-mdpi/d/doc1846318.html#:~:text=consistency%20theory%20proposes%20that%20alignment,theory%20is%20crucial%2C%20as%20information)). 

For example, if a virtual tour prominently features a sunny beach, a cloudy and crowded beach on the day of visit may jar the traveler’s expectations, weakening their cognitive image of the destination (and possibly their satisfaction).  Many DMOs therefore strive for accurate virtual portrayals so that guests’ expectations will be met or surpassed ([www.scribd.com](https://www.scribd.com/document/770020018/a-comparison-of-pre-visit-beliefs-and-projected-visual-images#:~:text=This%20depends%20on%20whether%20the,Scholars%20investigated%20the%20role)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387940550_Do_Virtual_Tours_Really_Enhance_Customer_Satisfaction_with_Physical_Experiences_A_Quasi-Experimental_Study_on_Airbnb#:~:text=satisfaction%20of%20the%20physical%20experiences,across%20online%20and%20offline%20channels)).  Empirical evidence supports this: Liu and Roehl (2025) found that having a virtual tour of a rental increased customers’ satisfaction with the actual stay by improving “advertising accuracy,” i.e. by aligning online previews with offline reality ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387940550_Do_Virtual_Tours_Really_Enhance_Customer_Satisfaction_with_Physical_Experiences_A_Quasi-Experimental_Study_on_Airbnb#:~:text=satisfaction%20of%20the%20physical%20experiences,across%20online%20and%20offline%20channels)). 

Over time, this interaction produces a refined cognitive schema. The traveler integrates the stable elements (geography, attractions) from the virtual image with the nuanced, affective elements (ambience, personal meaning) from the visit.  The final destination cognition is thus both *induced* (shaped by mediated images) and *acquired* (shaped by direct experience). If the integration is positive, it builds a strong, favorable destination image; if negative, it warps the cognition or may even deter future visits. Notably, the cognitive component (knowledge/beliefs) often anchors the affective response: knowing that a beach is scenic or that people are friendly leads to corresponding feelings and intentions ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/12/22/9413#:~:text=concept%20,their%20acquaintance%20with%20the%20particular)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212571X20301128#:~:text=Mavragani%2C%202015%20%3B%20Prayag%2C%202009,creation%20experiences)). 

## Conclusion  
In summary, destination cognition forms through a dynamic interplay of virtual preview and on-site experience. Digital previews plant the initial mental image – a bundle of expectations and simulated sensations – by leveraging vivid imagery and presence ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261517721000790#:~:text=destination%20either%20in%20traditional%20still,previous%20experience%20with%20the%20destination)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355830823_Virtual_Tours_Encourage_Intentions_to_Travel_and_Willingness_to_Pay_via_Spatial_Presence_Enjoyment_and_Destination_Image#:~:text=Similarly%2C%20the%20positive%20destination%20image,virtual%20preview%20of%20the%20destination)). The actual visit either confirms those images or forces an update: real sensory data and social context validate or adjust what was imagined ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/18/8030#:~:text=Tourism%20engagements%20encompass%20both%20visual,20)) ([www.wisdomlib.org](https://www.wisdomlib.org/science/journal/sustainability-journal-mdpi/d/doc1846318.html#:~:text=consistency%20theory%20proposes%20that%20alignment,theory%20is%20crucial%2C%20as%20information)). Consistent, immersive previews thus create stronger initial images (and travel intent ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0261517721000790#:~:text=destination%20either%20in%20traditional%20still,previous%20experience%20with%20the%20destination))), but the enduring cognition depends on how well the real experience matches or re-shapes those expectations. In this way, each visitation cycle – preview, travel, memory – continuously refines the traveler’s cognitive image of the destination, shaping their satisfaction and future travel decisions ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212571X20301128#:~:text=which%20travellers%20base%20their%20evaluations,creation%20experiences)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387940550_Do_Virtual_Tours_Really_Enhance_Customer_Satisfaction_with_Physical_Experiences_A_Quasi-Experimental_Study_on_Airbnb#:~:text=satisfaction%20of%20the%20physical%20experiences,across%20online%20and%20offline%20channels)).
