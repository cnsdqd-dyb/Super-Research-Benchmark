# Node Selection Strategy in Word-of-Mouth Propagation Networks

Marketing leverage in word-of-mouth networks relies on a **seeding strategy guided by network influence**. In other words, firms pick a set of initial “seed” individuals whose activation will trigger the largest cascade of sharing.  In practice, this means identifying and targeting the most **influential or well-connected nodes** in the social graph.  The goal is to choose seeds that **maximize the spread** of information: as one review notes, the major concern “focuses on finding a set of seeds such that the expected number of individuals activated from this seed set is maximized” ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378437117310609#:~:text=Currently%2C%20the%20major%20concern%20on,23)).  Empirical studies confirm that targeting **hubs** – people with many social contacts – dramatically boosts reach.  For example, Hinz et al. (2011) advise “choosing highly connected people as seed,” and find that seeding these well-connected hubs can make a campaign *“up to eight times more successful”* than unguided targeting ([www.redalyc.org](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4717/471767357009/html/#:~:text=Businesses%20should%20use%20social%20networking,Empirical%20results)).  This influence-maximization (or viral seeding) approach explicitly picks nodes by their centrality or connectivity in the network ([www.redalyc.org](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4717/471767357009/html/#:~:text=Businesses%20should%20use%20social%20networking,Empirical%20results)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378437117310609#:~:text=Currently%2C%20the%20major%20concern%20on,23)).

- **Influence-Maximization (Seeding):**  Treat the network as a diffusion process and use algorithms to pick seed nodes whose activation yields the largest cascade. In this framework, one explicitly selects the smallest set of initial adopters to maximize the final number of influenced people ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378437117310609#:~:text=Currently%2C%20the%20major%20concern%20on,23)).  
- **High-Centrality Targets:**  Use network metrics (degree, eigenvector, etc.) to identify social **“hubs.”** These highly connected individuals reach many others directly, so activating them rapidly spreads information. Studies show that targeting best-connected people (high‐degree nodes) is far more effective than random targeting ([www.redalyc.org](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4717/471767357009/html/#:~:text=Businesses%20should%20use%20social%20networking,Empirical%20results)) ([www.redalyc.org](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4717/471767357009/html/#:~:text=Several%20authors%20have%20analyzed%20the,actors%20further%20from%20the%20network)). For instance, marketing theory often classifies actors into hubs (many ties) and fringes (few ties) ([www.redalyc.org](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4717/471767357009/html/#:~:text=Several%20authors%20have%20analyzed%20the,actors%20further%20from%20the%20network)); seeding campaigns focus on those hubs.  
- **Bridges and Connectors:**  It’s also useful to include **bridging nodes** (high betweenness centrality) who link different communities. By targeting these connectors, the campaign can spread across network clusters rather than staying within one group ([www.redalyc.org](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4717/471767357009/html/#:~:text=Several%20authors%20have%20analyzed%20the,actors%20further%20from%20the%20network)).  
- **Empirical Evidence:**  The combination of these principles has been validated in practice.  Choosing top influencers dramatically improves campaign outcomes: well-executed seeding strategies yield several-fold higher propagation than uninformed choices ([www.redalyc.org](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4717/471767357009/html/#:~:text=Businesses%20should%20use%20social%20networking,Empirical%20results)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378437117310609#:~:text=Currently%2C%20the%20major%20concern%20on,23)).

In summary, node selection is driven by an **influence-based seeding strategy**: use social-network analysis to pick the “seed” people who can most quickly and widely propagate the message.  Typically these are the individuals with highest connectivity or influence in the word-of-mouth network ([www.redalyc.org](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/4717/471767357009/html/#:~:text=Businesses%20should%20use%20social%20networking,Empirical%20results)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378437117310609#:~:text=Currently%2C%20the%20major%20concern%20on,23)).
