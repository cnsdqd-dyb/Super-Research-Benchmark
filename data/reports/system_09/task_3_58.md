# Effective population size and genetic load under range contraction

The **effective population size** (Ne) is a key parameter in population genetics: it roughly equals the number of breeding individuals that contribute genes to the next generation.  Under idealized conditions, neutral genetic diversity scales as 4Neμ (μ = mutation rate) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-28927-z#:~:text=effective%20population%20size%20and%20the,than%201%2C%20drift%20is%20the)), so a smaller Ne implies lower genetic diversity.  **Genetic load (L)** is the reduction in mean fitness of a population due to deleterious alleles ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=In%20the%20simplest%20models%20of,Under)).  Range contraction (e.g. from climate change) typically **reduces Ne** — populations become smaller and more fragmented ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/63f472525b1b43d975e5abad0a37d045#:~:text=WASHINGTON%20%28AP%29%20,to%20future%20threats%20%202)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11526952/#:~:text=inbreeding%2C%20which%20leads%20to%20further,2020)) — and this has well-understood effects on genetic load. 

- **Increased genetic drift in small Ne:**  When Ne drops, genetic drift overwhelms selection for many mutations.  Roughly, if |4Ne s| < 1 (s = selection coefficient), even somewhat deleterious alleles behave almost neutrally ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-28927-z#:~:text=effective%20population%20size%20and%20the,than%201%2C%20drift%20is%20the)).  In small populations, drift randomly fixes deleterious mutations that would be purged in large populations.  This **“drift load”** means that mildly deleterious alleles accumulate to higher frequency or fixation when Ne is small ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=s,drift%20load%20is%20driven%20by)).  For example, theory predicts and data confirm that long-term small-Ne populations tend to carry a higher proportion of weakly deleterious variants – i.e. elevated genetic load ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=population%20size%20on%20the%20coalescent,elevated%20measures%20of%20genetic%20load)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=s,drift%20load%20is%20driven%20by)). 

- **Inbreeding and “hidden” load:**  Smaller Ne also means more inbreeding (matings among relatives).  Many deleterious mutations are recessive or partially recessive, so they can be **“masked”** in large, outbred populations (carried in heterozygotes).  In small Ne, inbreeding causes those recessive deleterious alleles to become homozygous and reduce fitness.  This **inbreeding load** is normally unexpressed in a random-mating population, but as inbreeding rises it “unmasks” deleterious alleles and lowers mean fitness ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=is%20the%20total%20possible%20load,inbreeding%20load%20in%20different%20ways)).  In short, small Ne exposes cryptic load: recessive deleterious mutations that linger in the gene pool now affect individuals’ fitness. 

- **Purging vs accumulation:**  Importantly, population genetics theory and empirical studies suggest that very strongly deleterious alleles are often purged even in small populations (they are exposed and selected against), whereas **mildly deleterious alleles tend to accumulate** when Ne is low ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=populations%20we%20surveyed,Overall)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=s,drift%20load%20is%20driven%20by)).  For example, whole-genome studies of a declining quail showed that the small, inbred subpopulation had **fewer** very harmful mutations (they had been “purged” or lost) but a **higher realized load** overall because many weakly deleterious alleles had drifted to homozygosity ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=populations%20we%20surveyed,Overall)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=lost%20due%20to%20drift,simultaneously%20considering%20deeper%20demographic%20histories)).  In effect, large populations “carry” a hidden load of rare recessive deleterious alleles, but small Ne makes that load manifest and raises the **realized genetic load** ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=lost%20due%20to%20drift,simultaneously%20considering%20deeper%20demographic%20histories)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=population%20size%20on%20the%20coalescent,elevated%20measures%20of%20genetic%20load)). 

- **Empirical patterns:**  Studies consistently find that populations with small or declining Ne show higher relative genetic loads.  For example, Robinson et al. note that **long-term small-Ne populations are associated with elevated measures of genetic load**, reflecting accumulation of weakly deleterious variants by drift ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=population%20size%20on%20the%20coalescent,elevated%20measures%20of%20genetic%20load)).  Likewise, the Montezuma quail study found the most isolated (lowest Ne) population had a much higher inbreeding coefficient and more slightly deleterious alleles fixed, compared to larger populations ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=lost%20due%20to%20drift,simultaneously%20considering%20deeper%20demographic%20histories)).  In practice, conservation genetics often uses Ne thresholds (e.g. Ne≈50 to avoid inbreeding depression in the short term, Ne≈500 to maintain diversity long-term ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11526952/#:~:text=inbreeding%2C%20which%20leads%20to%20further,2020))) precisely because small Ne is known to degrade genetic health.  

In the **context of climate-driven range contraction**, these effects become critical.  As ranges contract (for example, retreating to higher elevations or latitudes), the remaining populations shrink and often become fragmented.  This is already being documented: a recent meta-analysis reports that about two-thirds of studied animal and plant populations are losing genetic diversity as they contract ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/63f472525b1b43d975e5abad0a37d045#:~:text=WASHINGTON%20%28AP%29%20,to%20future%20threats%20%202)).  Smaller, isolated populations (low Ne) will experience the above processes: **stronger drift and inbreeding**.  In practical terms, a contracting range often leaves “rear-edge” or refuge populations with very low Ne – making them prone to high genetic load.  For instance, Florida panthers lost habitat and became inbred (manifesting deleterious effects like kinked tails); introducing unrelated individuals (raising Ne) reduced inbreeding and genetic load ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/63f472525b1b43d975e5abad0a37d045#:~:text=diversity,University%20ecologist%20Stuart%20Pimm%2C%20who)).  

**Summary:**  Effective population size largely determines how genetic load changes when a species’ range contracts.  Reduced Ne (from climate-induced shrinkage and fragmentation) intensifies drift and inbreeding, allowing mildly harmful alleles to fix and recessive deleterious mutations to express, thus increasing genetic load ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=s,drift%20load%20is%20driven%20by)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=lost%20due%20to%20drift,simultaneously%20considering%20deeper%20demographic%20histories)).  Although very deleterious alleles may be purged in tiny populations, the net result of a shrinking Ne is usually higher realized genetic load and lower mean fitness, making populations more vulnerable unless mitigated by gene flow or management ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=lost%20due%20to%20drift,simultaneously%20considering%20deeper%20demographic%20histories)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933137/#:~:text=population%20size%20on%20the%20coalescent,elevated%20measures%20of%20genetic%20load)).
