# Translating Animal Potency to Clinical Efficacy for Multi-Target APDs

In practice, one predicts a new multi‐target antipsychotic’s clinical efficacy by linking its in vivo potency in animals to its human receptor-binding profile.  First, one measures the drug’s dose–response in standard rodent models of antipsychotic activity (e.g. amphetamine- or PCP-induced hyperlocomotion, conditioned avoidance, or catalepsy assays).  These animal ED₅₀ values reflect the concentration needed to engage the relevant targets (primarily dopamine D₂, and often serotonin 5-HT₂A, etc.) enough to see an effect.  In parallel, one knows the drug’s affinities (K_i or K_d) at human receptors.  The key is to convert the effective animal dose into a *target occupancy* in humans.  Receptor occupancy (RO) is a unifying metric: clinically effective antipsychotics typically occupy ~65–80% of D₂ receptors in patients ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9020768/#:~:text=of%20these%20compounds%20in%20patients,However%2C%20partial%20agonist%20antipsychotics%20behave)) without causing excessive EPS, and this “therapeutic window” turns out to be similar across species ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6590357/#:~:text=example%20is%20the%20dopamine%20D2,in%20the%20pharmacotherapy%20of%20antipsychotics)).  Thus the strategy is to ask, “At the dose that showed efficacy in rodents, what occupancy would the drug achieve at human D₂ (and other) receptors?”  One uses the in vitro K_i values in the occupancy equation (occupancy = C/(C+K_d)) to map drug concentration (or dose) to percent receptor binding.  

**Key steps in prediction (balancing curves):** 
- **Measure animal potency on each target-driven assay.**  For example, find the ED₅₀ that suppresses psychostimulant-induced activity (primarily mediated by D₂) or the ED₅₀ in a 5-HT₂A-linked assay. These give the in vivo potency curves (percent effect vs dose) for each target.  
- **Convert in vivo concentration to human occupancy.**  Estimate the free brain (or blood) level at the ED₅₀.  Then compute the expected human receptor occupancy at that concentration using the human K_i: **RO = C/(C + K_i)**.  For D₂, we know ~65% occupancy is needed for effect ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9020768/#:~:text=of%20these%20compounds%20in%20patients,However%2C%20partial%20agonist%20antipsychotics%20behave)).  For example, if the ED₅₀ dose yields a brain level equal to the human D₂K_i, then RO ≈50%; one would then adjust upward to hit ~70% (in the efficacious range) in man.  
- **Apply known occupancy thresholds.**  PET studies establish that good response requires ≥65% D₂ RO, with higher RO causing more EPS ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9020768/#:~:text=of%20these%20compounds%20in%20patients,However%2C%20partial%20agonist%20antipsychotics%20behave)).  Many animal assays similarly require reaching a threshold occupancy for effect ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4821196/#:~:text=All%20antipsychotics%20to%20date%20act,Although%20individual)).  Therefore, one targets clinical doses that give RO in the same window.  If the calculated dose (from animals) yields, say, only 50% human D₂ RO, one anticipates suboptimal efficacy; if it yields >80% RO, risk of side-effects rises.  The same logic can apply to other receptors if thresholds are known (e.g. 5-HT₂A blockade may augment efficacy or reduce EPS).  
- **Balance across multiple targets.**  A true “multi-target” profile means the clinical dose should engage *all* relevant receptors at efficacious levels.  Concretely, one overlays the occupancy curves for each receptor (scaled by its human K_i) against dose.  If the drug’s affinity for one receptor is much higher, it will reach occupancy earlier; balancing means adjusting so that at the chosen dose, each target is broadly in its therapeutic occupancy range.  For example, if a drug has 5-HT₂A K_i ten times lower than D₂, then at the dose that hits ~70% D₂ RO it might already saturate 5-HT₂A.  One checks that neither curve is too far apart: the *relative* affinities (the so-called affinity ratios) should be chosen so that, at therapy, dopamine and serotonin targets are both adequately engaged.  In practice this often requires PK/PD modeling or simple ratio scaling of the ED₅₀ values by the ratio of K_i’s.  

By this “balancing” procedure, the predicted clinical dose is one where the animal-derived dose–response curves align with the human Ki-based occupancy curves.  In effect, we ensure the compound reaches ~70% D₂ blockade *and* the desired level of 5-HT₂A (or other) blockade simultaneously – replicating the multi­receptor signature of a known effective antipsychotic.  This approach is grounded in the well-established relationship between binding profile and clinical action: for instance, a high 5-HT₂A/D₂ affinity ratio is linked to efficacy on negative symptoms ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8639018/#:~:text=binding%20affinity%20profiles%20is%20decisive,to)), and potent D₂ affinity to control of positive symptoms.  If the translational occupancy model shows the drug occupying all its targets in the efficacious range (similar to benchmark atypicals), one predicts it will also be efficacious in patients ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6590357/#:~:text=example%20is%20the%20dopamine%20D2,in%20the%20pharmacotherapy%20of%20antipsychotics)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4821196/#:~:text=All%20antipsychotics%20to%20date%20act,Although%20individual)). 

**References:** This strategy relies on receptor‐occupancy principles (e.g. ∼65–80% D₂ RO in humans for effect ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9020768/#:~:text=of%20these%20compounds%20in%20patients,However%2C%20partial%20agonist%20antipsychotics%20behave))) and on the classic correlation between in vitro affinity and clinical potency ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4821196/#:~:text=All%20antipsychotics%20to%20date%20act,Although%20individual)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8639018/#:~:text=binding%20affinity%20profiles%20is%20decisive,to)), extended to the full polypharmacological profile of the drug.
