A practical solution is a **two‐tier sentinel surveillance system** combining fast syndromic reporting with targeted lab confirmation.  In this design, a network of outpatient clinics (or sentinel providers) **rapidly report counts of ILI cases** (e.g. weekly percentages of patients with fever + cough) while **simultaneously collecting respiratory specimens from a subset of those cases** for laboratory testing.  The symptom‐based reports give the earliest epidemiological signal (often with only 1–2 day delays), and the lab results provide the diagnostic accuracy.  This balances speed (using clinical ILI case reports) against specificity (lab confirmation of true influenza).  

- **Sentinel syndromic reporting:**  Designated clinics report ILI counts quickly (often weekly) using a standard case definition.  Such data are timely and sensitive to outbreaks, even though many non‐influenza viruses may also cause ILI.  (For example, over 80% of children’s ILI workups in one African sentinel network found viruses other than influenza ([stacks.cdc.gov](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/51617/cdc_51617_DS8.xml#:~:text=Methods%E2%80%82%20Sentinel%20surveillance%20was%20established,multiple%20respiratory%20viruses%20using%20RT%E2%80%90PCR)).)  WHO and CDC recommend this approach to monitor when and where “flu‐like” activity is rising ([www.emro.who.int](https://www.emro.who.int/health-topics/influenza/influenza-surveillance.html#:~:text=Influenza%20surveillance%20is%20conducted%20through,Sentinel%20surveillance%20is%20a)) ([stacks.cdc.gov](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/51617/cdc_51617_DS8.xml#:~:text=Methods%E2%80%82%20Sentinel%20surveillance%20was%20established,multiple%20respiratory%20viruses%20using%20RT%E2%80%90PCR)).  

- **Virological confirmation:**  In parallel, those sites collect specimens from some ILI patients and test them in a lab (or with point-of-care rapid tests).  This yields the true influenza positivity rate.  Rapid antigen or PCR tests have higher specificity (~90–95%) than symptoms alone (often <70%) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3649292/#:~:text=The%20majority%20of%20US%20primary,as%20our%20alternative%20surveillance%20measure)), and results can be available the same day.  For example, using rapid POC tests in a clinic network detected outbreaks a median ~16 days before official surveillance peaks ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3649292/#:~:text=influenza%20test%20in%20the%20University,and%20twice%20as%20likely%20to)).  In practice, weekly ILI reports give a “heads up” of rising flu activity, and then the lab results confirm the diagnosis and circulating strains.

Overall, a **sentinel ILI network with built‐in lab testing** is the standard design to achieve both objectives.  It provides near-real-time alerting from clinical data and maintains accuracy through specimen testing ([www.emro.who.int](https://www.emro.who.int/health-topics/influenza/influenza-surveillance.html#:~:text=Influenza%20surveillance%20is%20conducted%20through,Sentinel%20surveillance%20is%20a)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3649292/#:~:text=The%20majority%20of%20US%20primary,as%20our%20alternative%20surveillance%20measure)). 

**Key sources:**  WHO/CDC influenza surveillance guidelines, and published evaluations of sentinel ILI systems ([www.emro.who.int](https://www.emro.who.int/health-topics/influenza/influenza-surveillance.html#:~:text=Influenza%20surveillance%20is%20conducted%20through,Sentinel%20surveillance%20is%20a)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3649292/#:~:text=The%20majority%20of%20US%20primary,as%20our%20alternative%20surveillance%20measure)) ([stacks.cdc.gov](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/51617/cdc_51617_DS8.xml#:~:text=Methods%E2%80%82%20Sentinel%20surveillance%20was%20established,multiple%20respiratory%20viruses%20using%20RT%E2%80%90PCR)).
