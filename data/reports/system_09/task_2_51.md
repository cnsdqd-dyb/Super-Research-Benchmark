# Convex Portfolio Optimization Solvers

- **Global vs. Local Optima:**  Portfolio optimization (e.g. mean–variance, CVaR, etc.) is typically formulated as a *convex* optimization problem.  In a convex problem, **any local optimum is a global optimum** ([ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/optimizationmodelsandapplications/part/local-and-global-optima-in-convex-optimization/#:~:text=Theorem%3A%20Global%20vs,the%20optimal%20set%20is%20convex)).  This means modern convex solvers (e.g. interior-point or accelerated gradient methods) are guaranteed to converge to the global solution; there is no need for special “escape from local minima” strategies.  In practice, the solver simply follows a monotonic descent on the convex objective, ensuring global convergence ([ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/optimizationmodelsandapplications/part/local-and-global-optima-in-convex-optimization/#:~:text=Theorem%3A%20Global%20vs,the%20optimal%20set%20is%20convex)).  

- **Duality Gap as a Convergence Criterion:**  Convex solvers usually work in a *primal–dual* fashion, maintaining both primal (allocation) and dual (Lagrange multiplier) iterates.  They monitor the **duality gap** (the difference between primal and dual objective values) as a measure of optimality.  Under standard conditions (e.g. Slater’s condition), strong duality holds and the duality gap vanishes at the optimum ([grokipedia.com](https://grokipedia.com/page/Duality_gap#:~:text=primal%20%20optimum.,positive%20gap%20may%20persist%2C%20highlighting)).  Interior-point algorithms explicitly shrink this gap towards zero along the “central path,” driving convergence.  In other words, the solver decreases the duality gap each iteration until it is below a tolerance, indicating near-optimality ([grokipedia.com](https://grokipedia.com/page/Duality_gap#:~:text=primal%20optimum%2C%20regardless%20of%20convexity.,positive%20gap%20may%20persist%2C%20highlighting)).  Thus, duality-gap estimation is *not* an extra global-search step but simply the built-in convergence test of the convex method.

- **Complexity in High Dimensions:**  Tracking the dual solution and gap does introduce some extra work per iteration, but this is part of the normal primal–dual algorithm.  The overall complexity of convex solvers is still **polynomial** in the problem size ([mathoverflow.net](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/92939/can-all-convex-optimization-problems-be-solved-in-polynomial-time-using-interior#:~:text=As%20mentioned%20by%20another%20poster%2C,These%20methods%20are%20hugely)).  For example, primal–dual interior-point methods for LP/QP/SOCP/SDP are proven polynomial-time (see Nesterov–Nemirovski’s theory) ([mathoverflow.net](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/92939/can-all-convex-optimization-problems-be-solved-in-polynomial-time-using-interior#:~:text=As%20mentioned%20by%20another%20poster%2C,These%20methods%20are%20hugely)).  In high-dimensional portfolios, the dominant cost is solving the linear systems or performing matrix operations (typically $O(n^3)$ or better per iteration for $n$ variables), which is a standard algebraic cost.  Computing the dual objective (to measure the gap) is a small additional step relative to that.  In short, the duality-gap check does **not** cause an exponential blow-up in complexity – the solver’s runtime scales reasonably (often polynomially) with the number of assets and constraints ([mathoverflow.net](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/92939/can-all-convex-optimization-problems-be-solved-in-polynomial-time-using-interior#:~:text=As%20mentioned%20by%20another%20poster%2C,These%20methods%20are%20hugely)). 

**Summary:** Because portfolio allocation problems are convex, the solver inherently finds the global minimum without special local-escape heuristics ([ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/optimizationmodelsandapplications/part/local-and-global-optima-in-convex-optimization/#:~:text=Theorem%3A%20Global%20vs,the%20optimal%20set%20is%20convex)).  Duality-gap evaluation is used to confirm convergence (it goes to zero under strong duality) but remains within the solver’s normal polynomial-time operation ([grokipedia.com](https://grokipedia.com/page/Duality_gap#:~:text=primal%20%20optimum.,positive%20gap%20may%20persist%2C%20highlighting)) ([mathoverflow.net](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/92939/can-all-convex-optimization-problems-be-solved-in-polynomial-time-using-interior#:~:text=As%20mentioned%20by%20another%20poster%2C,These%20methods%20are%20hugely)). 

**Sources:** Standard convex-optimization theory confirms that “for convex problems, any locally optimal point is globally optimal” ([ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/optimizationmodelsandapplications/part/local-and-global-optima-in-convex-optimization/#:~:text=Theorem%3A%20Global%20vs,the%20optimal%20set%20is%20convex)).  Texts on interior-point methods explain that the duality gap is driven to zero along the central path ([grokipedia.com](https://grokipedia.com/page/Duality_gap#:~:text=primal%20optimum%2C%20regardless%20of%20convexity.,positive%20gap%20may%20persist%2C%20highlighting)).  Complexity analyses (e.g. Nesterov–Nemirovski) show many convex programs (LP, QP, SDP, etc.) are solvable in polynomial time ([mathoverflow.net](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/92939/can-all-convex-optimization-problems-be-solved-in-polynomial-time-using-interior#:~:text=As%20mentioned%20by%20another%20poster%2C,These%20methods%20are%20hugely)), so the dual-gap mechanism does not incur any super-polynomial burden.
