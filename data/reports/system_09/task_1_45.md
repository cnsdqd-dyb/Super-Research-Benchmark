In multi‐agent welfare maximization, one usually assumes fixed utility functions and then picks the outcome that maximizes a social welfare function (e.g. the sum or another aggregator of utilities).  Under that static‐preference paradigm, the “optimal” social outcome is a fixed target.  However, if agents’ privacy preferences themselves evolve over time, the underlying utility functions change, and hence the social welfare objective shifts.  In other words, a solution that was optimal under one set of (privacy) preferences will generally no longer maximize welfare once those preferences change.  Concretely, Lin et al. (2021) show in a duopoly pricing model that **“the level of social welfare under different privacy regulations depends on consumers’ product‐privacy preference”** ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/0718-1876/16/7/161#:~:text=making%20consumers%E2%80%99%20surplus%20higher,optimal%20under%20the%20weak%20regulation)).  By analogy, if consumers’ (or agents’) privacy valuations rise or fall dynamically, the social welfare maximum will move. 

Put simply, dynamically evolving privacy preferences make the welfare‐optimum a moving target.  If the planner or mechanism does not continually re‐compute the solution as preferences shift, the old “optimum” becomes suboptimal or unstable.  This is akin to the well-known issue of time‐inconsistency in collective choice: recent theory shows that aggregate social preferences (i.e. the social welfare ordering) can be generically time-inconsistent when agents’ tastes or discounting differ ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022053118300851#:~:text=Recent%20work%20on%20collective%20intertemporal,they%20may%20be%20time%20consistent)).  By analogy, changing privacy tastes induce a similar time‐dependence in the welfare problem.  

In practice, this means a static social welfare solution is indeed “destabilized” by evolving privacy concerns: the optimal allocation or policy must be continually updated.  Dynamic mechanism‐design frameworks explicitly address this (by re-optimizing in each period or requiring commitment over time), but a one-shot social welfare optimum will not remain stable under preference drift.  In short, yes – because social welfare functions aggregate current utilities, a change in agents’ privacy preferences changes the welfare landscape, so any prior “optimal” solution is no longer optimal absent adjustment ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/0718-1876/16/7/161#:~:text=making%20consumers%E2%80%99%20surplus%20higher,optimal%20under%20the%20weak%20regulation)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022053118300851#:~:text=Recent%20work%20on%20collective%20intertemporal,they%20may%20be%20time%20consistent)).
