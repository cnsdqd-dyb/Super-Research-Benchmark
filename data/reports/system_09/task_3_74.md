# Severity Classification in After-Sales Support

Problem severity levels (e.g. Critical/High/Medium/Low or S1–S4) are defined by the practical impact of the issue on the customer’s operations.  In practice, the “boundary” between severity classes is set by explicit criteria – typically in a support policy or SLA – that describe how badly core functions are affected.  Key factors include: who and what is impacted, whether operations stop or continue, and whether a workaround exists.  

- **Business impact:**  A complete outage of critical functions (so that the customer *cannot continue normal operations*) is classified as the highest-severity level.  For example, Severity 1 is often defined as “production use of the service is stopped or so severely impacted that the customer cannot reasonably continue business operations” ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,and%2For%20cause%20significant%20financial%20impact)). In contrast, a lesser severity (e.g. S2) is used when operations continue despite serious issues – for instance, “one or more important functions of the service are unavailable… but customer’s production use is continuing” ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,on%20the%20Customer%27s%20business%20operations)).  Thus the boundary between Critical and High is essentially whether the business is halted (S1) versus merely disrupted (S2) ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,and%2For%20cause%20significant%20financial%20impact)) ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,on%20the%20Customer%27s%20business%20operations)).
- **Scope of impact:**  How many systems or users are affected and how critical those systems are also define the boundary.  Issues that only affect a non‐critical or redundant component, or only a few users (often on a test or development system), are usually rated a lower severity ([knowledge.hitachivantara.com](https://knowledge.hitachivantara.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=242403060171622#:~:text=Severity%203%20%E2%80%93%20Minor%20Restriction)).  For example, a “Minor” (e.g. S3) problem might be a failure in a non-critical module or a glitch in one office, whereas a widespread outage or failure of core infrastructure would immediately be escalated to a higher severity.
- **Workarounds and alternatives:**  If a functional workaround or alternate solution exists, the issue is typically rated lower.  For example, Severity 4 (“Minimal”) might be defined as “an incident that has minimal impact on business operations or basic functionality” ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,basic%20functionality%20of%20the%20Service)), or “a problem that has been circumvented using an alternative solution or workaround” ([knowledge.hitachivantara.com](https://knowledge.hitachivantara.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=242403060171622#:~:text=Severity%204%20%E2%80%93%20Circumvented)).  In other words, the boundary between high and low severity often rests on whether an ad-hoc fix lets the customer carry on.
- **Formal SLA/Support definitions:**  Most companies document exact severity criteria in their service agreements.  These SLA definitions draw the lines explicitly.  For instance, one example SLA defines:  
  > **Severity 1 (Critical):** “production use of the Service is stopped or so severely impacted that the Customer cannot reasonably continue business operations” ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,and%2For%20cause%20significant%20financial%20impact)),  
  > **Severity 2 (Significant):** “one or more important functions of the Service are unavailable with no acceptable alternative… Customer’s production use is continuing but there is a serious impact” ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,on%20the%20Customer%27s%20business%20operations)).  
  Here the boundary between S1 and S2 is exactly the line between “business cannot continue” versus “operations continue with serious impact” ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,and%2For%20cause%20significant%20financial%20impact)) ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,on%20the%20Customer%27s%20business%20operations)).  Other SLAs use similar language (e.g. “system down” vs “major functionality severely restricted” ([knowledge.hitachivantara.com](https://knowledge.hitachivantara.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=242403060171622#:~:text=Severity%201%20%E2%80%93%20System%20Down)) ([knowledge.hitachivantara.com](https://knowledge.hitachivantara.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=242403060171622#:~:text=Severity%202%20%E2%80%93%20Severe%20Restriction))).  

In summary, the classification boundary for post-sales problem severity is not an arbitrary numeric threshold but is strictly defined by the agreed impact criteria in the support framework.  In practice, support engineers apply these rules (from SLAs or internal policy) to decide whether an issue is, say, Category 1 or 2.  The boundary is therefore determined by whether the problem meets the higher‐impact criteria (full outage, no workaround, critical data loss, etc.) versus the more moderate criteria (partial outage, workaround available, limited scope).  These explicit definitions – typically found in Service Level Agreements – mark exactly where one severity class ends and the next begins ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,and%2For%20cause%20significant%20financial%20impact)) ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,on%20the%20Customer%27s%20business%20operations)).

**Sources:** Service-level definitions of severity (e.g. SLAs, support guidelines) clearly delineate the criteria for each level ([creliohealth.com](https://creliohealth.com/sla-and-support-services/#:~:text=1,and%2For%20cause%20significant%20financial%20impact)) ([knowledge.hitachivantara.com](https://knowledge.hitachivantara.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=242403060171622#:~:text=Severity%201%20%E2%80%93%20System%20Down)).
