The short answer is that the **coating material and design** of the stent controls its elution profile.  In practice, the drug is embedded in a polymer (or polymer-free reservoir) on the stent surface, and the **polymer matrix acts as a diffusion barrier**.  By choosing the polymer’s chemistry (hydrophobic vs. hydrophilic, biodegradable vs. permanent), its thickness and porosity, and even adding multilayers or surface micro-reservoirs, engineers can precisely tune the release rate ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3567608/#:~:text=the%20established%20SMC%20proliferation%20and,Acharya%20and%20Park%2C%202006)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8981810/#:~:text=immunosuppressant,flexible%2C%20thin%2C%20and%20appropriate%20for)).  In fact, for diffusion-controlled release the characteristic release time scales roughly as \(L_c^2/D_c\), where \(L_c\) is the coating thickness and \(D_c\) is the drug’s diffusivity in that polymer ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4470631/#:~:text=%7Bt%7D_%7B%5Cmathrm%7B%5Ctext%7BE%7D%7D%7D%3D%5Cfrac%7B%5Cpi%20%7BL%7D_%7B%5Cmathrm%7B%5Ctext%7Bc%7D%7D%7D,two%20parameters%20defining%20a%20particular)).  Thus **thicker or more impermeable coatings slow elution** (extending drug delivery), while thinner, more porous or faster-eroding coatings speed it.  In short – coating composition and geometry (and any degradable/erosion behavior) are what “control” how fast the drug comes off the stent.  By “dialing in” these parameters, designers achieve the desired profile: an early high dose to prevent acute thrombosis, then a tapered, slower release that allows endothelial cells to repopulate the vessel lining ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3567608/#:~:text=the%20established%20SMC%20proliferation%20and,Acharya%20and%20Park%2C%202006)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8981810/#:~:text=immunosuppressant,flexible%2C%20thin%2C%20and%20appropriate%20for)).  

**Sources:** Drug-eluting stents use a polymeric carrier as a diffusional barrier; changing its thickness, chemistry and surface area directly alters release kinetics ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3567608/#:~:text=the%20established%20SMC%20proliferation%20and,Acharya%20and%20Park%2C%202006)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4470631/#:~:text=%7Bt%7D_%7B%5Cmathrm%7B%5Ctext%7BE%7D%7D%7D%3D%5Cfrac%7B%5Cpi%20%7BL%7D_%7B%5Cmathrm%7B%5Ctext%7Bc%7D%7D%7D,two%20parameters%20defining%20a%20particular)).
