# Finality vs. Partition Tolerance in BFT Consensus

Byzantine Fault-Tolerant (BFT) protocols like PBFT (and derivatives such as Tendermint) are designed to give *deterministic finality*: once a block is committed, it is irrevocably final.  In practice, this means these protocols choose consistency/finality over availability under network faults.  This trade-off is governed by the CAP theorem: a system can only fully guarantee two of Consistency, Availability, and Partition-tolerance ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=The%20well,For%20example)).  PBFT-style consensus effectively opts for **Consistency** and (to the extent possible) **Partition tolerance**, sacrificing **Availability** (liveness) when the network splits ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=adopted%20in%20PBFT%2C%20PBFT%20is,weakens%20the%20level%20of%20availability)) ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=are%20also%20restricted%20by%20the,new%20perspective%2C%20ensuring%20fault)).  

- **CAP trade-off:**  The CAP theorem states that a distributed system cannot simultaneously have strong consistency, high availability, and tolerance to network partitions ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=The%20well,For%20example)).  PBFT-based blockchains emphasize consistency (finality) and tolerate up to 1/3 malicious or offline nodes, but during a true network *partition* they will typically halt rather than risk diverging views ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=adopted%20in%20PBFT%2C%20PBFT%20is,weakens%20the%20level%20of%20availability)) ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=are%20also%20restricted%20by%20the,new%20perspective%2C%20ensuring%20fault)).  In effect they implement *CP* in CAP terms.   
- **Strong Finality:**  In a PBFT network, once a block is committed it is final and immutable.  There are no forks or chain reorganizations as in probabilistic consensus.  For example, Hyperledger Sawtooth’s PBFT documentation explicitly notes that “blocks committed by nodes are final, so there are no forks in the network” ([sawtooth.splinter.dev](https://sawtooth.splinter.dev/docs/1.2/pbft/architecture.html#:~:text=network%20can%20tolerate%20a%20certain,no%20forks%20in%20the%20network)).  This is excellent for financial use-cases (no waiting for confirmations), but it means a system cannot continue to produce conflicting blocks on different sides of a network split.  
- **Behavior under Partition:**  If the network is partitioned, a PBFT consensus will sacrifice availability: nodes in each partition cannot make progress unless one partition has a majority (2f+1) of replicas.  In practice, this means new commits stop until the partition heals or the view-change process resolves the leader issue.  This preserves safety/consistency (so finality is never violated) but at the cost of stalling availability ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=adopted%20in%20PBFT%2C%20PBFT%20is,weakens%20the%20level%20of%20availability)) ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=The%20well,For%20example)).  

In summary, BFT consensus in permissioned “financial” blockchains **cannot both** be fully partition-tolerant and continuously available *and* maintain instant finality.  To ensure final, fork-free commits, BFT systems yield to the CAP theorem by losing liveness under partitions ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=adopted%20in%20PBFT%2C%20PBFT%20is,weakens%20the%20level%20of%20availability)) ([sawtooth.splinter.dev](https://sawtooth.splinter.dev/docs/1.2/pbft/architecture.html#:~:text=network%20can%20tolerate%20a%20certain,no%20forks%20in%20the%20network)). 

## PBFT View-Change and Communication Complexity

PBFT achieves consistency through a multi-phase vote among all replicas.  During **normal operation** (no leader failure), each new proposal incurs $O(n^2)$ message broadcasts (each of the $n$ nodes gossips to the others).  In terms of cryptographic authenticators (signatures or MACs), this can be as large as $O(n^3)$ per proposal ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=tors%20,conveying)).  When a primary is suspected faulty or unresponsive, PBFT triggers a **view-change**: all replicas broadcast their state (prepared certificates) so a new leader can be elected.  This adds another round of all-to-all communication, further multiplying the message complexity.  In effect, multiple back-to-back view-changes can **explode** the communication cost far beyond $O(n^2)$.  

- **Message blow-up:**  In fact, analyses show PBFT’s communication can reach $O(n^3)$ or worse.  The HotStuff paper notes that a single PBFT proposal already has “a communication footprint of $O(n^3)$ authenticators” ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=tors%20,conveying)).  If up to $O(n)$ sequential view-changes occur before consensus, the *total* authenticator count can reach $O(n^4)$ in the worst case ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=tors%20,conveying)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=ted%20if)).  In other words, continuous leader changes turn PBFT into a network-intensive protocol.  
- **View-change bottleneck:**  Each view-change phase requires every replica to send its last accepted state to the new proposer, and to gather $2f+1$ matching replies.  This all-to-all pattern is a known performance bottleneck.  In practice, any prolonged instability (for example, a prolonged partial partition) can trigger repeated view changes and severely degrade throughput.  Recent work explicitly notes that view-change processes are a “performance bottleneck” in BFT protocols ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09184#:~:text=blockchain,VCO%29%20model)).  
- **Scaling limits:**  For small consortiums (e.g. a dozen bank nodes), this overhead can be tolerated.  But as $n$ grows, the quadratic (or worse) messaging means throughput falls off rapidly.  Indeed, permissioned-blockchain benchmarks show PBFT-based ordering has much higher latency and lower scalability than simpler CFT (crash-fault-tolerant) protocols, because of its $O(n^2)$–$O(n^3)$ messaging costs ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2096720922000549#:~:text=consensus%20algorithms%20boosted%20performance%20in,and%20finality%20instead%20of%20efficiency)).  

**Mitigations:**  To reduce the view-change overhead, modern BFT designs incorporate cryptographic tricks and pipelining.  For example, HotStuff achieves *linear* view-change: after GST (synchronization), a correct leader only needs to send $O(n)$ authenticators (via a threshold signature) to reach consensus, even including leader replacement ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=designated%2C%20sends%20only)).  This cuts the worst-case per-view-change cost to $O(n)$ messages (and $O(n^2)$ total in cascading failures) instead of PBFT’s $O(n^2)$–$O(n^3)$.  Similarly, protocols like SBFT or Tendermint use methods (threshold signatures + batching) to cap PBFT’s $O(n^3)$ blowup down to $O(n^2)$ or $O(n)$ in common cases. 

However, a plain PBFT-style protocol **without** these refinements still carries heavy costs. Its view-change mechanism, while ensuring safety under partial synchrony, indeed magnifies communication complexity.  In summary, BFT consensus *can* deliver final blocks (as desired for finance) but only by sacrificing availability during partitions ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=adopted%20in%20PBFT%2C%20PBFT%20is,weakens%20the%20level%20of%20availability)), and PBFT’s view-change makes handling failures or partitions quite expensive ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=tors%20,conveying)) ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09184#:~:text=blockchain,VCO%29%20model)).  In practice this means careful tuning (small replica sets, reliable networking, or optimized BFT variants) is needed to balance performance vs. finality in a financial blockchain. 

**Key Takeaways:**  CAP/FLP theory guarantees the tension: BFT systems give instant finality at the cost of availability under partitions ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=The%20well,For%20example)) ([www.alibabacloud.com](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/from-distributed-consensus-algorithms-to-the-blockchain-consensus-mechanism_595315#:~:text=adopted%20in%20PBFT%2C%20PBFT%20is,weakens%20the%20level%20of%20availability)). PBFT ensures safety and finality, but its view-change procedure involves $O(n^2$–$n^4)$ messaging ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=tors%20,conveying)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=ted%20if)), which limits scalability. Modern protocols mitigate this messaging blowup (e.g. HotStuff’s linear view-change ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334577305_HotStuff_BFT_Consensus_with_Linearity_and_Responsiveness#:~:text=designated%2C%20sends%20only))), but the fundamental trade-off remains: finality **competes** with partition tolerance in BFT blockchain designs.
