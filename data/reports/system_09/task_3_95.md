**Quantum radical-pair magnetoreception.**  Migratory birds are thought to sense Earth’s field via a light-induced *radical pair* in a cryptochrome protein in the retina ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetoreception#:~:text=on%20migratory%20birds%20%20provide,frequency%20interference%2C%20unlike%20a)).  In this mechanism, a blue-light photon excites a flavin cofactor (FAD) and an electron hops from a nearby tryptophan, creating two unpaired electrons (a “radical pair”) whose spins start out in a singlet/triplet quantum superposition ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetoreception#:~:text=identified%20capable%20of%20supporting%20radical,acid%20to%20the%20FAD%20molecule)) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetoreception#:~:text=on%20migratory%20birds%20%20provide,frequency%20interference%2C%20unlike%20a)).  The very weak geomagnetic field (tens of μT) slightly alters the energies of the singlet vs triplet spin states, so that over time the spin superposition oscillates and changes the yield of singlet versus triplet recombination reactions.  The key quantum element is that the two spins remain *coherent* (entangled) long enough for the Earth’s field to influence their state.  In fact, experiments show the avian compass is exquisitely sensitive (and easily disrupted by weak radio-frequency fields), consistent with a delicate quantum *radical-pair* process ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetoreception#:~:text=on%20migratory%20birds%20%20provide,frequency%20interference%2C%20unlike%20a)).  

**Coherence timescales.**  Remarkably, theoretical and experimental studies suggest that the spin coherence in bird cryptochromes lasts on the order of tens to hundreds of microseconds ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3725#:~:text=superposition%20and%20entanglement%20are%20sustained,such%20quantum%20phenomena%20to%20endure)).  This is far longer than would be expected for a random “warm and wet” molecule, and is just long enough for the weak geomagnetic field to appreciably mix the singlet and triplet states before the pair recombines.  For example, one quantum-information analysis found that “superposition and entanglement are sustained… for at least tens of microseconds” in the avian compass ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3725#:~:text=superposition%20and%20entanglement%20are%20sustained,such%20quantum%20phenomena%20to%20endure)), exceeding coherence times in comparable synthetic systems.  This suggests that birds’ proteins provide an environment that partly **shields the radical pair from rapid decoherence**, allowing spin superposition to persist on biologically useful timescales.  

**Mechanisms prolonging coherence (quantum Zeno effect).**  The radical-pair system has an inbuilt mechanism to balance coherence and “measurement.”  As the spins evolve, their eventual *recombination* into reaction products acts like a continuous, spin-dependent measurement.  In quantum terms, the spin-dependent reaction (singlet vs triplet recombination) plays the role of frequent observations, which can slow the apparent spin dynamics via a **quantum Zeno effect**.  In fact, theory shows that this effect *naturally leads to very long spin-coherence lifetimes*, with no fine-tuning of parameters ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.2646#:~:text=geomagnetic%20field%20will%20have%20a,robustness%20of%20this%20sensory%20mechanism)).  In other words, each time the radical pair tries to evolve its spin state, the possibility of recombination “probes” the state and resets it, effectively extending the duration of coherence.  (Recent work even suggests that the intrinsic molecular chirality of cryptochrome can *reinforce* this Zeno effect ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01519#:~:text=first%20time%2C%20one%20generating%20coherence,universal%20enhancer%20of%20sensitivity%20or)), further favoring longer coherence.)  In practice, this means the birds’ compass exploits a regime where recombination is neither instantaneous (which would kill coherence) nor so slow that the spin can forget its quantum phase; it is just right to let the weak field act before decoherence “observes” it.

**Role of decoherence and environmental tuning.**  Although decoherence is often seen as destructive, in the avian compass it is partly *essential* to produce a usable signal. The spin singlet or triplet nature finally must collapse into a chemical outcome that the nervous system can detect.  Crucially, biological design appears to allow only the *mild* decoherence that comes from the radical pair’s own hyperfine couplings, while suppressing more rapid environmental noise.  One detailed analysis finds that decoherence with internal nuclear spins (arising from hyperfine interactions) actually “induces new structure in the spin dynamics without materially affecting the compass action,” whereas uncontrolled environmental decoherence would “completely disrupt” the compass ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.1327#:~:text=distinctive%20effects%20of%20nuclear%20and,other%20hand%2C%20completely%20disrupts%20it)).  In other words, built-in spin–nuclear interactions help mix the singlet/triplet populations (which is needed for sensitivity) without immediately collapsing the state, but outside perturbations (e.g. random molecular collisions or thermal noise) are minimized by the protein’s structure.  Some researchers even argue that a moderate amount of decoherence may *amplify* the weak magnetic effect to macroscopic scales ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.4179#:~:text=Contrary%20to%20the%20usual%20picture,of%20importance%20for%20biological%20systems)) — for example, by helping drive the system toward one reaction outcome or another in a field-dependent way. 

**Balancing act (“Goldilocks” regime).**  In summary, the avian compass operates in a finely tuned “sweet spot” between too much coherence and too much decoherence.  The cryptochrome’s radical pair spins stay coherent just long enough (microseconds) for Earth’s magnetic field to influence their quantum state ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3725#:~:text=superposition%20and%20entanglement%20are%20sustained,such%20quantum%20phenomena%20to%20endure)), thanks in part to a quantum Zeno–like slowing of decay ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.2646#:~:text=geomagnetic%20field%20will%20have%20a,robustness%20of%20this%20sensory%20mechanism)).  Eventually the spins decohere — via hyperfine interactions and spin-selective chemistry — to yield a directional chemical signal.  This delicate balance is borne out by experiments: the compass fails if either coherence is destroyed (e.g. by radio-frequency fields ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetoreception#:~:text=on%20migratory%20birds%20%20provide,frequency%20interference%2C%20unlike%20a))) or if the recombination is too rapid.  Thus birds appear to have evolved magnetoreceptor molecules that keep quantum spin superpositions alive on just the right timescale, using the very decohering interactions (measurements) that ultimately read out the signal.  In short, Nature has dialed in a “Goldilocks” regime where quantum coherence and decoherence coexist in dynamic balance to enable magnetoreception ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.2646#:~:text=geomagnetic%20field%20will%20have%20a,robustness%20of%20this%20sensory%20mechanism)) ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.1327#:~:text=distinctive%20effects%20of%20nuclear%20and,other%20hand%2C%20completely%20disrupts%20it)).

Analysis of decoherence reveals that some spin–environment coupling shapes the compass signal without destroying it, whereas stronger noise would be fatal ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.1327#:~:text=distinctive%20effects%20of%20nuclear%20and,other%20hand%2C%20completely%20disrupts%20it)) ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.4179#:~:text=Contrary%20to%20the%20usual%20picture,of%20importance%20for%20biological%20systems)).  These findings together support a picture of a finely tuned balance between superposition and decoherence in the bird’s magnetic sense.
