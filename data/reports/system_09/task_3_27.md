# Jordan Canonical Form and Ergodicity in Social Mobility Models 

The terminology in the question spans linear algebra, sociology, and globalization—all very different fields. In mathematics, **Jordan canonical form** (or Jordan normal form) is a standard way to represent any square matrix as a block-diagonal matrix of its eigenvalues (with 1’s on superdiagonals for nontrivial blocks) ([statlect.com](https://statlect.com/matrix-algebra/Jordan-form#:~:text=A%20matrix%20is%20said%20to,its%20other%20entries%20are%20zeros)).  In particular, if you have any matrix *A*, there exists an invertible *P* such that *P⁻¹AP = J*, where *J* is its Jordan form.  Each Jordan block corresponds to one eigenvalue of *A* (possibly repeated), and the block has that eigenvalue on the diagonal and ones on the superdiagonal within the block ([statlect.com](https://statlect.com/matrix-algebra/Jordan-form#:~:text=A%20matrix%20is%20said%20to,its%20other%20entries%20are%20zeros)).  In practice, Jordan form is used in linear algebra to analyze the structure of linear systems and matrix powers, but it is **not typically used in social science** literature. Social stratification usually refers to hierarchical class or status categories, not a literal matrix that one diagonalizes. 

The notion of a **“social stratification matrix”** is not standard.  However, sociologists do sometimes use matrices to model social mobility. For example, *intergenerational mobility tables* (often contingency tables of parent vs. child social class) can be viewed as transition matrices of a Markov chain ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8294650/#:~:text=statistical%20view%2C%20mobility%20tables%20are,15%3B%20White%201963)).  As Song (2020) notes, *“mobility tables are equivalent to a single transition matrix of a Markov chain, which describes the transition probability of moving from one social class to another in one generation step”* ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8294650/#:~:text=statistical%20view%2C%20mobility%20tables%20are,15%3B%20White%201963)).  In that interpretation, each entry *p_{ij}* of the matrix gives the probability that an individual in class *i* will be in class *j* in the next generation.  If we imagine a “social stratification matrix” as such a transition matrix *P*, then it is a **stochastic matrix** (rows sum to 1) encoding mobility between classes ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8294650/#:~:text=statistical%20view%2C%20mobility%20tables%20are,15%3B%20White%201963)).  Social scientists rarely compute the Jordan form of this matrix, but if one did, it would reveal its eigenvalues and the structure of its invariant subspaces. 

In the **Markov chain** context, *ergodicity* has a clear meaning. A finite-state Markov chain is *ergodic* if it is irreducible (one strongly connected component) and aperiodic.  In such a case the chain has a unique stationary distribution and long-run averages converge to ensemble averages ([intro.quantecon.org](https://intro.quantecon.org/markov_chains_II.html#:~:text=,a%20unique%20stationary%20distribution%2C%20while)).  More precisely, as Sargent & Stachurski explain, *“an irreducible Markov chain guarantees the existence of a unique stationary distribution, while an ergodic Markov chain generates time series that satisfy a version of the law of large numbers.”* ([intro.quantecon.org](https://intro.quantecon.org/markov_chains_II.html#:~:text=,a%20unique%20stationary%20distribution%2C%20while)).  In other words, if every social class communicates (directly or indirectly) with every other, there is a unique long-run class distribution and the system is ergodic. Conversely, if the chain splits into disconnected blocks (e.g. some subgroups never mix), then there are multiple invariant distributions (one per closed class), and the system is **not** ergodic.  

Globalization is often described in terms of **multidirectional flows** (of people, goods, information) that transcend borders ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/296679557_Conceptualizing_globalization_in_terms_of_flows#:~:text=,and%20being%20hard%20to%20stop)).  Ritzer (2010) characterizes globalization as involving *“growing multidirectional flows of people, objects, places and information”* along with *“structures they encounter … that are barriers to, or expedite, those flows.”* ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/296679557_Conceptualizing_globalization_in_terms_of_flows#:~:text=,and%20being%20hard%20to%20stop)).  By contrast, *“counter-globalization”* suggests forces or policies that **impede** or reverse those flows (for example, re-established national barriers or protectionist measures).  The phrase **“counter-globalization flow fields”** does not have a formal definition in economics or physics; it seems metaphorical.  If taken literally, one could imagine a vector field on a social graph that represents how anti-globalization forces (like migration restrictions) push people back or block flows. But in practice this term is not found in the literature. 

Putting it all together *speculatively*:  Suppose we model social stratification and mobility with a transition matrix *P*.  Its Jordan form *J* would reveal how many invariant components the system has (via the multiplicity of eigenvalue 1, for example).  In an **ergodic** regime (full mixing), *P* is irreducible, so it has a single eigenvalue λ=1 of algebraic multiplicity 1; in Jordan form there would be one 1×1 block with value 1 on the diagonal (plus other blocks for eigenvalues <1) ([statlect.com](https://statlect.com/matrix-algebra/Jordan-form#:~:text=A%20matrix%20is%20said%20to,its%20other%20entries%20are%20zeros)).  If “counter-globalization flows” introduce barriers, *P* might become reducible: the society essentially splits into subpopulations with no transitions between them.  In that case *P* has multiple eigenvalues λ=1 (one for each disconnected subchain), and its Jordan form *J* would display multiple Jordan blocks each with 1 on the diagonal.  This jump from one 1-block to many 1-blocks in the Jordan form corresponds to the loss of a unique steady-state distribution – i.e. crossing an *“ergodicity boundary.”*  In other words, one could *interpret* the ergodicity boundary as the point at which the largest eigenvalue (λ=1) goes from simple to repeated, and the Jordan form begins to reflect multiple invariant classes. 

- **Globalization flows vs barriers:**  Globalization is characterized by interconnected flows ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/296679557_Conceptualizing_globalization_in_terms_of_flows#:~:text=,and%20being%20hard%20to%20stop)).  “Counter” flows would act like barriers that disconnect parts of the chain. If these “fields” block connections between classes, the transition matrix breaks into blocks.  
- **Markov-ergodic threshold:**  If *P* remains fully connected (irreducible), there is one steady state (system ergodic) ([intro.quantecon.org](https://intro.quantecon.org/markov_chains_II.html#:~:text=,a%20unique%20stationary%20distribution%2C%20while)).  Once barriers split the network, each block has its own steady state (chain not ergodic).  
- **Jordan form indication:**  The Jordan canonical form of *P* would thus show the connectivity: a single Jordan block for λ=1 indicates full ergodicity, while multiple unit-eigenvalue blocks indicate fragmentation (loss of ergodicity).  
- **Delimiting “ergodicity boundary”:**  Conceptually, the “boundary” is where the transition happens. In the Jordan form, it is delimited by the change in structure of the λ=1 blocks.  

However, it is important to emphasize that **no formal framework or source explicitly uses “Jordan form of social stratification matrix” or “ergodicity boundary” in this way**. The above reasoning is purely hypothetical. Put simply, the question’s phrasing does not correspond to any standard analysis. In practice, though, these concepts are not linked in the literature, so any “delimitation” of ergodicity by Jordan blocks remains an analytic metaphor rather than a documented phenomenon.
