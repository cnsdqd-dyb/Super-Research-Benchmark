The macroscopic fundamental diagram (MFD) – the network‐level flow–density (or speed–density) relation – is highly sensitive to the assumed speed–density (v–k) curve.  In particular, if the v–k relationship is strongly nonlinear (as it usually is in real traffic), then the MFD is no longer a single smooth curve.  Near the critical (phase‐transition) density, small changes in density can cause large, abrupt changes in average speed (and hence flow).  In practice this means that the “free‐flow” and “congested” regimes overlap and the MFD develops a hysteresis loop or scatter (i.e. for the same density there are two possible flows depending on traffic state).  Empirical studies confirm this: when traffic is vehicle‐uniform and stable, a tight MFD can be observed, but during transitions (onset or dissipation of congestion) the data “loop” and show multiple flows at a given density ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856411000620#:~:text=congested%20regime%20,fine%20structures%20in%20the%20scatter%E2%80%9D)) ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=81402#:~:text=function,a%20given%20value%20of%20density)). 

In concrete terms, **near phase transitions the prediction error increases**.  A simple (e.g. triangular or linear) MFD fit will either under‐ or over‐predict the peak flow, because the nonlinearity causes the true peak to skew and the downstream branch to fall off more steeply (the so‐called “capacity drop”).  In other words, assuming a single‐valued FD “suppresses” the finer multi‐valued behavior at the transition ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856411000620#:~:text=congested%20regime%20,fine%20structures%20in%20the%20scatter%E2%80%9D)).  As Zhang (2003) and others note, averaging into one curve “suppress[es] finer traffic dynamics” such as multiple metastable states ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856411000620#:~:text=congested%20regime%20,fine%20structures%20in%20the%20scatter%E2%80%9D)).  In practice this means that predictions of network flow (or travel time) based on a nominal MFD will be misleading around the transition: the same density can correspond to either a high‐flow (free‐flow) or low‐flow (congested) state.  Researchers have therefore observed *hysteresis* and *capacity drop* in empirical MFDs – signs of underlying nonlinearity – which simple MFD models cannot predict without extra state information ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856411000620#:~:text=congested%20regime%20,fine%20structures%20in%20the%20scatter%E2%80%9D)) ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=81402#:~:text=function,a%20given%20value%20of%20density)). 

In summary, strongly nonlinear speed–density characteristics make the MFD **multi‐regime** near the critical point.  Predictions that ignore this (for example, using a fixed functional form) will either miss or smear out the phase transition.  Instead, one must allow for multiple branches or include variables like density variance to capture the true network behavior.  Failing to do so leads to biased estimates of capacity and underestimation of flow loss during congestion onset (and vice versa during recovery).  Empirical evidence shows that accurately capturing these nonlinearities is essential for precise MFD prediction around the phase‐change region ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856411000620#:~:text=congested%20regime%20,fine%20structures%20in%20the%20scatter%E2%80%9D)) ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=81402#:~:text=function,a%20given%20value%20of%20density)).

**Answer Summary:** Nonlinear v–k curves cause MFDs to bifurcate or hysteresis near critical densities, so single‐valued MFD predictions break down at phase transitions.  Small density changes cause large flow/speed shifts, leading to multiple flows for one density (hysteresis loops) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0965856411000620#:~:text=congested%20regime%20,fine%20structures%20in%20the%20scatter%E2%80%9D)) ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=81402#:~:text=function,a%20given%20value%20of%20density)).  Thus, ignoring the nonlinearity yields large errors in predicted flows and capacities near the transition point.
