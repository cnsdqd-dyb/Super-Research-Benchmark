# Eigen-Decomposition of Punishment Dynamics in RJ Games

In a restorative-justice “game” model one can represent how players punish each other by a **punishment matrix** $P$ (an $n\times n$ matrix of punishment weights).  Each column (or row, depending on convention) of $P$ encodes how much one agent’s action provokes punishments from others.  The eigenvectors of $P$ form a basis of **modes** or patterns of punishment distribution.  Concretely, if $P v = \lambda v$, then $v$ is an eigenvector and $\lambda$ its eigenvalue.  Intuitively, $v$ represents a pattern of social disturbance (a “direction” in the space of possible punishment states), and each timestep of the game scales that disturbance by $\lambda$. 

### Stability via Eigenvalues (Lyapunov Criterion)  
Stability of the social equilibrium (the “harmony” state) is governed by these eigenvalues.  In linear stability theory (Lyapunov’s method), an equilibrium is stable if all eigenvalues of the Jacobian (here, the punishment matrix $P$ or its derivative) lie in the stable region.  For a continuous-time analog, stability requires all eigenvalues have negative real parts; for a discrete-time or iterative model, stability requires all eigenvalues have magnitude less than 1.  In either case, any perturbation along the direction of an eigenvector will shrink if $|\lambda|<1$ (or Re$\,\lambda<0$) and grow if $|\lambda|\ge1$ (or Re$\,\lambda>0$).  Thus **each eigenvector indicates a potential “mode” of social disharmony**, and its eigenvalue tells us whether a small conflict in that direction will die out or amplify.  As a standard result notes, *“to check if a point … is asymptotically stable, we check if all eigenvalues … are [in the stable region]”* ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064152/#:~:text=From%20Lyapunov%27s%20theorem%2C%20it%20is,conditions%20of%20the%20eight%20points)).  In sum: if every eigenvalue of $P$ is subcritical, then the restorative justice process pushes any disturbance down the social-harmony gradient back to equilibrium ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064152/#:~:text=From%20Lyapunov%27s%20theorem%2C%20it%20is,conditions%20of%20the%20eight%20points)). If any eigenvalue crosses the critical threshold, the associated eigenvector yields an unstable direction that grows and destabilizes harmony.

### Social Harmony Gradients and Eigenmodes  
What is the **social harmony gradient** in this context?  One can think of social harmony as a “potential” or aggregate measure of cooperation in the system, whose gradient points toward disagreement or conflict.  Eigenvectors of $P$ correspond to principal axes of this conflict space.  For example, a uniform eigenvector (all entries equal) might represent a broad consensus mode, whereas a highly skewed eigenvector highlights tension concentrated in a subgroup.  If the dominant eigenvector (largest $|\lambda|$) favors equitable outcomes (punishments spread evenly), then the system tends to relax smoothly toward harmony along that mode.  But if the dominant eigenvector concentrates punitive weight on one faction, a small perturbation in that faction’s direction will be amplified, pulling the system “downhill” on the harmony landscape and triggering systemic conflict.  In Markov-chain terms, when the punishment matrix is row-stochastic, its principal eigenvector (eigenvalue 1) is the stationary distribution and all other eigenvalues have $|\lambda|<1$ ([studylib.net](https://studylib.net/doc/28100362/social-and-economic-networks#:~:text=a%20nonnegative%20right,useful%20is%20in%20examining%20the)), so disturbances vanish.  By analogy, when all non-principal eigenvalues of the (normalized) punishment matrix lie inside the unit circle, any social discord inevitably decays and the harmony gradient is stable; if not, one or more eigenmodes will cause runaway disharmony ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064152/#:~:text=From%20Lyapunov%27s%20theorem%2C%20it%20is,conditions%20of%20the%20eight%20points)) ([studylib.net](https://studylib.net/doc/28100362/social-and-economic-networks#:~:text=a%20nonnegative%20right,useful%20is%20in%20examining%20the)).

### Summary of Eigenvector Effects  
- **Eigenvector directions**: Each eigenvector of the punishment matrix is a fundamental pattern of how punishments (and thus conflict) can be distributed across the community.  
- **Eigenvalue conditions**: If the associated eigenvalue has $|\lambda|<1$ (or negative real part), that mode is *dissipative* — a small disharmony (down the social-harmony gradient) along that vector will shrink over time ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064152/#:~:text=From%20Lyapunov%27s%20theorem%2C%20it%20is,conditions%20of%20the%20eight%20points)).  If $|\lambda|\ge1$, that mode is *explosive*, so any discord along it will grow, undermining harmony ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064152/#:~:text=From%20Lyapunov%27s%20theorem%2C%20it%20is,conditions%20of%20the%20eight%20points)).  
- **Dominant eigenvector role**: The principal eigenvector (largest $|\lambda|$) often dominates the long-term behavior.  If it corresponds to equitable, consensus-oriented punishments, harmony persists; if it corresponds to targeting or revenge loops, then even slight deviations can lead to escalating conflict.  
- **Gradient interpretation**: One can view the “social harmony gradient” as the slope of a Lyapunov-like potential.  The punishment matrix’s eigenvalues essentially tell us whether that potential has a local maximum (stable harmony) or saddle/unstable directions.  In summary, the spectral decomposition of $P$ directly reveals which directions of social tension will self-correct (stable) and which will intensify (unstable), thus determining the stability of the overall harmony gradient ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064152/#:~:text=From%20Lyapunov%27s%20theorem%2C%20it%20is,conditions%20of%20the%20eight%20points)) ([studylib.net](https://studylib.net/doc/28100362/social-and-economic-networks#:~:text=a%20nonnegative%20right,useful%20is%20in%20examining%20the)).

**Sources:**  The above follows standard linear stability and network theory principles.  For example, Lyapunov’s theorem asserts stability iff all eigenvalues are in the stable region ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064152/#:~:text=From%20Lyapunov%27s%20theorem%2C%20it%20is,conditions%20of%20the%20eight%20points)), and in stochastic network processes a unique eigenvector-$1$ steady state exists with all other eigenvalues $|\lambda|<1$ ([studylib.net](https://studylib.net/doc/28100362/social-and-economic-networks#:~:text=a%20nonnegative%20right,useful%20is%20in%20examining%20the)).  These results apply here by analogy, showing that the eigenstructure of the punishment matrix governs how perturbations to social harmony grow or decay.
