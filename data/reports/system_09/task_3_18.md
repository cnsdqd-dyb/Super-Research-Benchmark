# Low-Rank Local Embeddings Sharpen Principal Directions but Lose Detail

In practice, replacing high-dimensional “local” node embeddings with a low-rank approximation (e.g. truncating a singular‐value decomposition) **compresses** the cultural feature space.  By design, only the top $r$ singular vectors or principal components are retained; all smaller singular values (and their directions) are discarded.  In other words, the original embedding space is projected onto an $r$-dimensional subspace.  This projection **removes fine-grained variation** in the data.  In model terms, anything in the discarded subspace is effectively treated as zero – one “dongrads of freedom” are lost.  As noted in discussions of PCA and low-rank SVD, discarding small singular components typically eliminates noise or low‐variance features ([stats.stackexchange.com](https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/66060/does-dimension-reduction-always-lose-some-information#:~:text=%28PCA%29,it%20resembles%20a)).  Thus low-rank embeddings **smooth out** the node features: cultural nuances encoded in the discarded components are no longer present in the representation. 

# Effect on the Entropy Gradient

When one computes a gradient of a diversity-by-entropy objective with respect to the node embeddings, the low-rank projection directly affects the gradient.  Concretely, one first computes the full Euclidean gradient of the entropy with respect to the original embedding matrix, then projects that gradient into the low-rank subspace (usually by truncated SVD) before updating any parameters.  In formulaic terms, if $\nabla_X H$ is the full gradient of the entropy $H$ with respect to the embedding matrix $X$, then the **effective gradient** under a rank–$r$ constraint is 
$$\nabla_X H \;\longrightarrow\; \mathcal{P}_r(\nabla_X H)\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^r\sigma_i\,u_i v_i^T,$$ 
where $(\sigma_i,u_i,v_i)$ are the top $r$ singular triplets of $\nabla_X H$ ([www.emergentmind.com](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/low-rank-gradient-projections#:~:text=denotes%20projection%20onto%20the%20set,underlies%20canonical%20projected%20gradient%20descent)).  In other words, gradient updates are **truncated** to the principal subspace.  Any gradient components in discarded directions (small singular values) vanish.  This is exactly the mechanism of *projected gradient descent* on a rank-$r$ manifold ([www.emergentmind.com](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/low-rank-gradient-projections#:~:text=denotes%20projection%20onto%20the%20set,underlies%20canonical%20projected%20gradient%20descent)): the true entropy gradient is computed, then only its projection onto the rank-$r$ tangent space is used.  As a consequence, the *magnitude* of the gradient is typically smaller and its “shape” is dominated by the leading modes.  

# Consequences for Cultural Diversity

Because the entropy of cultural states depends on the details of the local embeddings, projecting to a low-rank subspace tends to **smooth and dilute** diversity signals.  Subtle differences between node‐profiles (cultural traits) that lived in the small-variance directions are ignored.  Roughly speaking, the entropy gradient will no longer respond to those fine‐scale variations – only the major variation captured by the top singular vectors drives the gradient.  This has two main effects on cultural‐diversity optimization: 

- **Reduced Sensitivity / Smoother Gradient:** The low-rank constraint acts like a noise filter or regularizer. It lowers the variance of the gradient by aligning updates along the largest singular directions ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6554071/#:~:text=We%20propose%20an%20estimator%20based,rank)).  In network estimation contexts, low-rank methods are known to “smooth” out noise and highlight global structure ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6554071/#:~:text=We%20propose%20an%20estimator%20based,rank)).  Here, similarly, the entropy gradient becomes smoother (less noisy) but can be weaker in magnitude, since many small fluctuations (potentially corresponding to minor cultural differences) have been lost. 

- **Possible Underestimation of Diversity:** Because discarded components may carry minor cultural features that contribute to overall diversity, the entropy computed from low-rank embeddings may be slightly **understated**.  The gradient of entropy (which for a probability vector $p$ is $-\ln p - 1$ in each component) will thus be based on a “flatter” distribution.  In effect, dimension reduction can bias the entropy gradient toward dominant modes and away from outliers or niche subcultures.  As one textbook axiom reminds us, removing dimensions via PCA or SVD (i.e. discarding small “loadings”) retains the bulk of the signal but loses possibly useful information in the discarded noise ([stats.stackexchange.com](https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/66060/does-dimension-reduction-always-lose-some-information#:~:text=%28PCA%29,it%20resembles%20a)).  In the cultural graph, that translates to military interpretion: the optimisation will focus on maximizing the diversity present in the principal subspace, but it may **miss improvements in diversity** that could only be seen in the discarded dimensions.

In summary, using a low-rank approximation of node embeddings effectively **projects the entropy gradient onto a subspace** of principal cultural directions ([www.emergentmind.com](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/low-rank-gradient-projections#:~:text=denotes%20projection%20onto%20the%20set,underlies%20canonical%20projected%20gradient%20descent)).  The gradient’s components in low-variance directions are set to zero.  This typically means a *smaller, smoother gradient* that emphasizes the main modes of variation – potentially stabilizing training but at the cost of ignoring fine-grained cultural features.  Overall, one should expect the entropy (diversity) gradient to be damped and biased toward the principal components retained by the low-rank model ([stats.stackexchange.com](https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/66060/does-dimension-reduction-always-lose-some-information#:~:text=%28PCA%29,it%20resembles%20a)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6554071/#:~:text=We%20propose%20an%20estimator%20based,rank)). 

**References:** This behavior follows standard results on low-rank matrix projections and gradient updates ([www.emergentmind.com](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/low-rank-gradient-projections#:~:text=denotes%20projection%20onto%20the%20set,underlies%20canonical%20projected%20gradient%20descent)), and is analogous to the smoothing effect observed in low-rank approximations of graph data ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6554071/#:~:text=We%20propose%20an%20estimator%20based,rank)).  Dimension reduction (PCA/SVD) intentionally drops low-variance singular directions ([stats.stackexchange.com](https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/66060/does-dimension-reduction-always-lose-some-information#:~:text=%28PCA%29,it%20resembles%20a)), so any entropy–gradient signal in those directions is lost.
