Yes – in fact one of the key purposes of carbon‐accounting standards is to level the informational playing field.  By requiring firms to measure and report their emissions in a common way, standardized frameworks make carbon data comparable and transparent, which tends to *reduce* information asymmetry for investors.  For example, the IASB’s new climate disclosure standard (IFRS S2) was explicitly developed in response to investor demand for “consistent, … comparable and verifiable” climate data ([www.ifrs.org](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/html-standards-issb/english/2023/issued/issbs2-bc.html#:~:text=BC1%20%20,these%20risks%20and%20opportunities%20are)).  Empirical studies confirm the effect: US firms that fully disclose their carbon emissions see narrower bid–ask spreads (a proxy for lower information asymmetry) compared to firms that omit or partial-report emissions ([ideas.repec.org](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/sampjp/sampj-02-2020-0046.html#:~:text=analysis%2C%20the%20authors%20find%20that,firms%20that%20provide%20incomplete%20information)).  More generally, theory and evidence suggest that robust carbon disclosure “alleviates information asymmetry between enterprises and stakeholders” ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11408138/#:~:text=conducive%20to%20alleviating%20the%20information,actively%20undertaking%20social%20responsibility%20for)), whereas weak or voluntary reporting can exacerbate gaps (allowing “greenwashing” by selectively hiding negative carbon outcomes) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521925005228#:~:text=However%2C%20some%20studies%20have%20suggested,For%20instance%2C%20they%20might)).  In short, standardized carbon-accounting processes (whether using the GHG Protocol, IFRS/CSRD rules, etc.) give all investors access to the same reliable emissions data – improving symmetry of information in investment decisions ([www.ifrs.org](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/html-standards-issb/english/2023/issued/issbs2-bc.html#:~:text=BC1%20%20,these%20risks%20and%20opportunities%20are)) ([ideas.repec.org](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/sampjp/sampj-02-2020-0046.html#:~:text=analysis%2C%20the%20authors%20find%20that,firms%20that%20provide%20incomplete%20information)).
