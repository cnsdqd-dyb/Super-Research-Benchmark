The “penalty gradient” in a merchant‐violation system is not learned by an algorithm but is defined a priori by the platform’s policy.  In practice it is a fixed (usually piecewise) function that maps the severity or frequency of a merchant’s violations to increasing penalties.  In other words, the system designers set thresholds or “nodes” – for example, every 12 points of accumulated violations triggers a penalty – and at each step the fine or sanction gets steeper.  Typically the penalties jump in tiers as violations accumulate.  For example, one platform splits product‐quality breaches into categories A/B/C and imposes fines equal to a percentage of the product’s recent sales.
