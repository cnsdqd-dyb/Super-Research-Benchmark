# Path-Selection Convergence in Power-Law (Scale-Free) Networks

- **Scale-free topology:**  Many large networks have a **power-law degree distribution** (scale-free), meaning the fraction of nodes with degree *k* scales like *k*^(-γ) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9759579/#:~:text=Scale,of%20the%20model%20is%20the)).  Such graphs feature a few very-high-degree hubs and many low-degree nodes.  
- **Slower convergence:**  The heavy-tailed connectivity tends to slow down distributed path-selection algorithms.  High-degree hubs attract most traffic, and under limited bandwidth these hubs quickly saturate.  Traffic must then detour onto longer routes, requiring more iterations to settle.  In fact, studies of iterative averaging/routing show that Barabási–Albert (power-law) graphs converge noticeably more slowly than random graphs of the same density; for example, an Erdős–Rényi graph converged ~10% faster than a BA scale-free graph with the same average degree ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9759579/#:~:text=graphs%20have%20comparable%20performance%20for,performance%20for%20equal%20average%20degrees)).  Scale-free graphs “never reach the maximum speed” of complete or very regular topologies ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9759579/#:~:text=graphs%20have%20comparable%20performance%20for,performance%20for%20equal%20average%20degrees)) – their high degree variance directly degrades convergence speed ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9759579/#:~:text=graphs%20have%20comparable%20performance%20for,performance%20for%20equal%20average%20degrees)).  
- **Effect of capacity limits:**  Introducing link/node capacity constraints amplifies this slowdown.  Since hubs carry disproportionate load, fixed capacity causes them to bottleneck quickly.  Routing must continually adjust to avoid the saturated hubs, causing additional oscillations.  In practice this means convergence time grows faster with network size in scale-free topologies than in uniform networks. (For instance, BA graphs reach only about 90% of the convergence rate of a comparable random graph ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9759579/#:~:text=graphs%20have%20comparable%20performance%20for,performance%20for%20equal%20average%20degrees)); imposing strict capacities would reduce that further.)  In summary, **power-law network structure slows path-selection convergence** under capacity limits – the larger and more skewed the network, the more iterations are needed for routes to stabilize.
