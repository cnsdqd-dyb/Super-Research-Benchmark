Adaptive, context‐aware sampling schemes – essentially only “wake up” the sensors at high rate when an abnormal event (or “change point”) is suspected and otherwise sample sparsely – give the best trade‐off.  In practice, wearable sleep monitors can run in a low‐power idle mode and then boost their sampling rate or duty cycle as anomalies are flagged.  This episodic or event‐driven approach drastically cuts energy use (studies report ~80–85% savings) while only slightly reducing detection sensitivity (~5% drop in accuracy) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5019958/#:~:text=context,classification%20accuracy%20in%20our%20experiment)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1574119224001019#:~:text=GPS,A%20similar%20approach%20based%20on)).  In short, adaptive sampling (increasing rate on anomaly cues, decreasing it in normal conditions) is shown to optimize the power vs. sensitivity balance in wearable sleep monitoring ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5019958/#:~:text=context,classification%20accuracy%20in%20our%20experiment)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1574119224001019#:~:text=GPS,A%20similar%20approach%20based%20on)).

**Answer:** Use an adaptive “episodic” (event‐driven) sampling strategy – sleep most of the time and only ramp up sampling rate when a potential anomaly is detected.  This dramatically lowers power use with minimal loss of detection sensitivity ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5019958/#:~:text=context,classification%20accuracy%20in%20our%20experiment)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1574119224001019#:~:text=GPS,A%20similar%20approach%20based%20on)).
