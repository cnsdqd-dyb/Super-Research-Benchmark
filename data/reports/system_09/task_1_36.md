One effective approach is to **coarsen or anonymize the GPS trajectories into larger geographic units** before contact‐tracing.  For example, researchers have proposed converting a person’s precise path into a sequence of larger “district” or “mesh‐block” areas.  Health authorities then only see which broad zones were visited, not the exact coordinates ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1548392423000319#:~:text=To%20address%20the%20problem%20of,of%20our%20works%20as%20follows)).  In practice this still lets them flag overlapping visits in the same area (interrupting transmission) but conceals the individual’s fine-grained route (protecting privacy).  In one study, this _district‐based_ trajectory method was shown to successfully identify likely contact locations and exposed persons from the anonymized data ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1548392423000319#:~:text=visiting%20unsafe%20MBs)), while hiding specific location points.  In short, **aggregate or obfuscate individual trajectories into coarser blocks** – enough to find exposure overlaps, yet too coarse to pinpoint exact movements – thereby balancing outbreak control with privacy ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1548392423000319#:~:text=To%20address%20the%20problem%20of,of%20our%20works%20as%20follows)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1548392423000319#:~:text=visiting%20unsafe%20MBs)).
