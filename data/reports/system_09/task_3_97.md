Experts agree that a **precautionary, adaptive governance** model – often called “anticipatory” or “responsible innovation” regulation – is needed.  In practice this means requiring synthetic‐biology developers to prove safety (reversing the burden of proof) and using **flexible, stage‐wise oversight** so that rules can evolve with new knowledge.  For example, one recent review notes that regulatory efforts should “strike a balance between [synthetic biology’s] maximum development and minimized risks,” ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405805X22000291#:~:text=people%20and%20environment%20across%20the,23%20%2C%2047)) and argues that a forward‐looking precautionary approach (with proportionate, risk‐based measures) provides a “strong theoretical guarantee for the balance between technological progress and [public/environmental] security” ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12364803/#:~:text=complexity%20of%20potential%20biotechnological%20risks,progress%20and%20social%20security%20development)).  In line with this, bodies like the OECD explicitly call for agile, adaptive governance that “complements the more precautionary approach…with a more balanced consideration of benefits” ([www.frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/synthetic-biology/articles/10.3389/fsybi.2025.1585337/abstract#:~:text=consider%20that%20the%20broader%20OECD,technologies%20that%20support%20their%20realization)).  In short, a precautionary yet flexible regulatory paradigm – one that couples strict biosafety standards with ongoing monitoring and review – is seen as the best way to safeguard ecosystem integrity without unduly stifling innovation ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405805X22000291#:~:text=people%20and%20environment%20across%20the,23%20%2C%2047)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12364803/#:~:text=complexity%20of%20potential%20biotechnological%20risks,progress%20and%20social%20security%20development)).
