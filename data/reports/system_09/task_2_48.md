# Balancing Signal vs. Costs in Practice

Alpha‐generation models in quantitative trading explicitly target **predictive patterns** (signals) in market data. In principle one *can* incorporate transaction costs into the optimization (for example by adding cost terms to the objective or via cost‐sensitive reinforcement‐learning rewards ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03051#:~:text=propose%20a%20cost,We%20theoretically%20analyze%20the))).  In practice, however, the edge from any signal is typically small (markets have very low “signal-to-noise” ratios ([education.signalpilot.io](https://education.signalpilot.io/curriculum/advanced/67-machine-learning-trading.html#:~:text=Every%20quant%20fund%20claims%20to,and%20when%20it%27s%20snake%20oil))), so high turnover quickly erodes gains.  Trading costs thus *must* be managed: many algorithmic frameworks explicitly penalize excessive turnover or embed costs into the model ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03051#:~:text=propose%20a%20cost,We%20theoretically%20analyze%20the)) ([xiaohui-victor-li.github.io](https://xiaohui-victor-li.github.io/projects/algo-trading-project/#:~:text=match%20at%20L192%20As%20shown,ask%20spread%20and%20commission%20fee)).  For instance, one practitioner group noted that “transaction cost plays a pivotal role in our portfolio… [so we] add the transaction cost factor (market impact, spreads, fees) into our optimization model” when calibrating their alpha signals ([xiaohui-victor-li.github.io](https://xiaohui-victor-li.github.io/projects/algo-trading-project/#:~:text=match%20at%20L192%20As%20shown,ask%20spread%20and%20commission%20fee)).  In short, modern quant strategies try to **balance signal strength and costs** by tuning how many trades they take on each signal, and by including cost terms in portfolio optimization. 

# Curse of Dimensionality & Noise Amplification

The catch is that **adding many predictors/features tends to amplify noise rather than reveal true signals**.  Financial markets are classic “low‐signal, high‐noise” environments ([education.signalpilot.io](https://education.signalpilot.io/curriculum/advanced/67-machine-learning-trading.html#:~:text=Every%20quant%20fund%20claims%20to,and%20when%20it%27s%20snake%20oil)).  The *curse of dimensionality* means that as the number of input features grows, the data become sparse and models overfit incidental patterns ([jfin-swufe.springeropen.com](https://jfin-swufe.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40854-022-00441-7#:~:text=irrelevant%20variables%2C%20computational%20cost%2C%20and,article%20presents%20various%20types%20of)).  In fact, a recent survey notes that “a large number of features… causes generalization performance to deteriorate owing to the curse of dimensionality” ([jfin-swufe.springeropen.com](https://jfin-swufe.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40854-022-00441-7#:~:text=irrelevant%20variables%2C%20computational%20cost%2C%20and,article%20presents%20various%20types%20of)).  Put simply, each extra feature can introduce spurious correlations that do not hold out-of-sample.  Empirical studies confirm this: for example, one analysis found that adding many non‐critical features to a time-series model for mortgage defaults *hurts* prediction, because “extended time spans may introduce noise from historical data and outdated patterns, while excessive non-critical features interfere with the model’s learning of core factors” ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00034#:~:text=%282012,feature%20selection%20and%20time%20window)).  Likewise, financial ML education cautions that markets’ nonstationarity means complex models trained on one regime often fail when conditions change ([education.signalpilot.io](https://education.signalpilot.io/curriculum/advanced/67-machine-learning-trading.html#:~:text=Every%20quant%20fund%20claims%20to,and%20when%20it%27s%20snake%20oil)).  

In sum, without strict controls the **curse of dimensionality makes alpha models fit market noise**.  Naïvely stacking hundreds of technical or fundamental features will almost certainly find flukes.  Any marginal benefit of a tiny alpha signal is usually swamped by trading friction: an empirically minuscule edge subject to cost may vanish or even reverse.  As one quant educator puts it, trading models often achieve high backtest accuracy on rich feature sets, yet “when volatility spiked… the model’s predictions became worthless” because it had overfit the calm‐market noise ([education.signalpilot.io](https://education.signalpilot.io/curriculum/advanced/67-machine-learning-trading.html#:~:text=Every%20quant%20fund%20claims%20to,and%20when%20it%27s%20snake%20oil)).

# Mitigating Overfitting: Feature Selection and Regularization

To counter the curse, robust alpha engines *must* prune or regularize their feature set.  In practice this means aggressively selecting only the strongest signals and using techniques like shrinkage or sparsity penalties.  Feature-selection surveys in finance emphasize exactly this: reducing input variables not only cuts overfitting and computation, but empirically improves trading performance ([jfin-swufe.springeropen.com](https://jfin-swufe.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40854-022-00441-7#:~:text=irrelevant%20variables%2C%20computational%20cost%2C%20and,article%20presents%20various%20types%20of)).  For instance, in a practical factor-trading example a model started with 27 fundamental predictors but a **BIC-driven stepwise selection** ended up keeping only 2 of them – and surprisingly this **raised** (not lowered) the out-of-sample CAGR from 14.7% to 19.1% ([epchan.blogspot.com](https://epchan.blogspot.com/2017/03/more-data-or-fewer-predictors-which-is.html#:~:text=the%20model%20at%20a%20time%2C,that%20in%20my%20talk%20at)), even though the Sharpe ratio stayed the same.  In other words, throwing away 25 noisy features uncovered a stronger, more robust signal.  In line with this, many quant teams routinely filter features by importance (e.g. via a tree model) or apply L1/L2 penalties to avoid high-dimensional traps ([xiaohui-victor-li.github.io](https://xiaohui-victor-li.github.io/projects/algo-trading-project/#:~:text=During%20the%20training%20procedure%2C%20to,feature%20set%20as%20prediction%20features)) ([jfin-swufe.springeropen.com](https://jfin-swufe.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40854-022-00441-7#:~:text=irrelevant%20variables%2C%20computational%20cost%2C%20and,article%20presents%20various%20types%20of)).  

Common practices include: 
- **Feature screening:** Use statistical criteria or machine learning importance scores to drop weak/unrelated predictors ([xiaohui-victor-li.github.io](https://xiaohui-victor-li.github.io/projects/algo-trading-project/#:~:text=During%20the%20training%20procedure%2C%20to,feature%20set%20as%20prediction%20features)) ([jfin-swufe.springeropen.com](https://jfin-swufe.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40854-022-00441-7#:~:text=irrelevant%20variables%2C%20computational%20cost%2C%20and,article%20presents%20various%20types%20of)).
- **Dimensionality reduction:** Combine raw signals via PCA, autoencoders or orthogonal factors to capture most variation with fewer components.
- **Ensemble & cross-validation:** Average across many small models (bagging, random subspaces) and rigorously test on hold-out or rolling windows to avoid fitting noise ([epchan.blogspot.com](https://epchan.blogspot.com/2017/03/more-data-or-fewer-predictors-which-is.html#:~:text=times%20to%20form%20K%20bags,far%2C%20but%20at%20least%20it)) ([education.signalpilot.io](https://education.signalpilot.io/curriculum/advanced/67-machine-learning-trading.html#:~:text=Every%20quant%20fund%20claims%20to,and%20when%20it%27s%20snake%20oil)).
- **Transaction-cost-aware modeling:** Integrate cost terms or turnover constraints so the model naturally favors lower-turnover signals ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03051#:~:text=propose%20a%20cost,We%20theoretically%20analyze%20the)) ([xiaohui-victor-li.github.io](https://xiaohui-victor-li.github.io/projects/algo-trading-project/#:~:text=match%20at%20L192%20As%20shown,ask%20spread%20and%20commission%20fee)). 

These methods don’t eliminate the curse, but they *balance* it: by focusing on the richest signals and penalizing complexity, the strategy avoids chasing marginal patterns that would only incur costs.  As one quant group puts it, their training pipeline “addresses the curse of dimensionality by ... filtering out the irrelevant features” according to a feature-importance threshold ([xiaohui-victor-li.github.io](https://xiaohui-victor-li.github.io/projects/algo-trading-project/#:~:text=During%20the%20training%20procedure%2C%20to,feature%20set%20as%20prediction%20features)).  

# Conclusion

In summary, alpha-extraction algorithms **must** juggle signal strength against trading costs.  In theory one can codify this balance—e.g. via cost-sensitive optimization or regularized models—but in practice the biggest pitfall is overfitting.  The “curse of dimensionality” turns out to be a real menace in finance: adding features tends to fit noise, not true alpha ([jfin-swufe.springeropen.com](https://jfin-swufe.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40854-022-00441-7#:~:text=irrelevant%20variables%2C%20computational%20cost%2C%20and,article%20presents%20various%20types%20of)) ([epchan.blogspot.com](https://epchan.blogspot.com/2017/03/more-data-or-fewer-predictors-which-is.html#:~:text=the%20model%20at%20a%20time%2C,that%20in%20my%20talk%20at)).  Therefore successful quant strategies *inevitably* constrain model complexity.  They select a handful of top predictors, shrink the rest, and embed cost penalization so that only robust signals survive costs ([xiaohui-victor-li.github.io](https://xiaohui-victor-li.github.io/projects/algo-trading-project/#:~:text=During%20the%20training%20procedure%2C%20to,feature%20set%20as%20prediction%20features)) ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03051#:~:text=propose%20a%20cost,We%20theoretically%20analyze%20the)).  Without such safeguards, any finely tuned “alpha” in a high-dimensional feature set will generally disappear amid trading frictions and market noise ([jfin-swufe.springeropen.com](https://jfin-swufe.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40854-022-00441-7#:~:text=irrelevant%20variables%2C%20computational%20cost%2C%20and,article%20presents%20various%20types%20of)) ([epchan.blogspot.com](https://epchan.blogspot.com/2017/03/more-data-or-fewer-predictors-which-is.html#:~:text=the%20model%20at%20a%20time%2C,that%20in%20my%20talk%20at)).
