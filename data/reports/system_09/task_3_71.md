# Optimal Budget Allocation in Multi-Channel Marketing 

In marketing mix models, the budget is allocated so that each channel’s *marginal* return per dollar is balanced.  In other words, you spend more on channels with higher ROI or conversion efficiency *until* their additional (marginal) benefit falls to the same level as the others ([slm.mba](https://slm.mba/mmpc-010/equi-marginal-principle-business-decisions/#:~:text=The%20optimal%20allocation%20occurs%20when,Instagram%20until%20the%20returns%20equalize)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305072984_Allocation_of_Advertising_Budget_between_Multiple_Channels_to_Support_Sales_in_Multiple_Markets#:~:text=resource%20allocation,advise%20to%20invest%20in)).  Formally, the KKT conditions for profit-maximization require equating the incremental profit contribution of each channel.  Thus the *ratio* of spending across channels is driven by their relative cost-effectiveness – channels with higher initial ROI take a larger share – tempered by diminishing returns as spend rises.

- **Balance of Marginal ROI (equi-marginal principle):**  The optimal split occurs when the marginal return on spend (often measured in incremental sales or profit per dollar) is equal across all channels ([slm.mba](https://slm.mba/mmpc-010/equi-marginal-principle-business-decisions/#:~:text=The%20optimal%20allocation%20occurs%20when,Instagram%20until%20the%20returns%20equalize)).  If one channel’s ROI is higher, shifting budget to it raises total return until its marginal ROI drops to match the others ([slm.mba](https://slm.mba/mmpc-010/equi-marginal-principle-business-decisions/#:~:text=The%20optimal%20allocation%20occurs%20when,Instagram%20until%20the%20returns%20equalize)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305072984_Allocation_of_Advertising_Budget_between_Multiple_Channels_to_Support_Sales_in_Multiple_Markets#:~:text=resource%20allocation,advise%20to%20invest%20in)).  In practice, this means solving the stage-two optimization (subject to total budget) yields an allocation where you cannot improve total outcome by moving a dollar between channels – i.e. all marginal returns are “equalized.”  
- **Channel response curves and diminishing returns:**  Each channel has a concave (often S-shaped) response curve: early spending yields strong gains that taper off.  Because of diminishing returns, the optimal model allocates just enough to each channel so that its marginal gain per dollar equals the common margin.  Channels with more efficient targeting or lower cost-per-conversion (higher elasticity) start out with higher marginal ROI, so they attract more budget until saturation.  As [Abedi et al.] note, marketing response often has threshold and saturation effects, so you invest until the point where each channel’s incremental impact is balanced ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305072984_Allocation_of_Advertising_Budget_between_Multiple_Channels_to_Support_Sales_in_Multiple_Markets#:~:text=We%20consider%20the%20allocation%20of,branch%20and%20cut%20solution%20method)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305072984_Allocation_of_Advertising_Budget_between_Multiple_Channels_to_Support_Sales_in_Multiple_Markets#:~:text=resource%20allocation,advise%20to%20invest%20in)).  
- **Practical constraints and interactions:**  Other factors can shift the ratio.  For example, some channels may require a **minimum spend** before any impact (a threshold), which forces a baseline allocation ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305072984_Allocation_of_Advertising_Budget_between_Multiple_Channels_to_Support_Sales_in_Multiple_Markets#:~:text=We%20consider%20the%20allocation%20of,branch%20and%20cut%20solution%20method)).  Budget caps or strategic mandates (e.g. brand vs. performance emphasis) can also constrain the split.  Cross-channel synergies (or cannibalization) – where one channel’s effect amplifies or substitutes for another – may alter the effective ROI curves.  In all cases, the model incorporates these constraints and then allocates funds so that the adjusted marginal returns are equalized. 

**In summary:**  The optimal allocation ratio is determined by the shape of each channel’s spend–response curve (ROI/elasticity functions) and the need to equalize marginal return.  Channels with higher initial efficiency get more budget, but the point of diminishing returns forces the allocation to balance out ([slm.mba](https://slm.mba/mmpc-010/equi-marginal-principle-business-decisions/#:~:text=The%20optimal%20allocation%20occurs%20when,Instagram%20until%20the%20returns%20equalize)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305072984_Allocation_of_Advertising_Budget_between_Multiple_Channels_to_Support_Sales_in_Multiple_Markets#:~:text=resource%20allocation,advise%20to%20invest%20in)).  Constraints (minimum spends, capacity limits, strategic goals) and channel interactions further tweak the exact split, but the underlying driver remains the balance of marginal ROI across channels.
