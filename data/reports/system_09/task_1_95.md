# Innate Continuity vs Quantum‐Gravity Discreteness  
Human “folk physics” assumes a smooth, continuous flow of time.  In everyday life (and even in infant cognition) we expect objects to persist and move along continuous paths, and we think of time as an infinitely divisible “river” ([plato.stanford.edu](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2019/entries/innateness-cognition/#:~:text=As%20adults%2C%20we%20recognize%20physical,objects%20causally%20interact%20upon%20contact)).  Classical relativity likewise treats time as a continuous coordinate.  Quantum gravity, however, suggests this picture must change. 

## Quantum Gravity’s Discrete Spacetime  
Many quantum gravity approaches predict **quantized space and time** rather than a continuum.  For example, loop quantum gravity (LQG) describes spacetime as a network of discrete volume “chunks” (spin networks) that evolve in steps. LQG predicts “spacetime is a discrete entity; space is not infinitely divisible… and time flows in a discrete pattern like the ticking of a clock” ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Theoretically%2C%20the%20most%20appealing%20aspect,like%20a%20ticking%20of%20a)).  In such theories, there is a smallest time interval (on the order of the Planck time, ~$10^{-43}$ s) and no meaning to times in between.  Causal-set theory likewise models spacetime as a finite set of discrete events with a causal order, from which usual space and time emerge.  (In extreme proposals time itself is an emergent, relational phenomenon rather than a background parameter ([www.numberanalytics.com](https://www.numberanalytics.com/blog/causal-set-theory-ultimate-guide#:~:text=,the%20causal%20relations%20between%20events)).) 

These ideas mean **our classical assumption of an infinitely divisible timeline breaks down**.  Between any two “ticks” there are literally no intermediate times.  For instance, if Planck time is the fundamental step, then one second of time would contain ~10^43 such ticks ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Time%20does%20not%20flow%20continuously,observe%E2%80%95does%20not%20play%20any%20apparent)).  At macroscopic scales this discreteness is imperceptible, so clocks and motion *appear* continuous.  But fundamentally there is a “grain” to time. 

## How Assumptions Change  
Quantum-gravitational discreteness forces us to revise several core assumptions about time:

- **No arbitrarily small durations.** There is a minimum time unit, so time is not a smooth continuum.  We cannot make time intervals smaller than a Planck tick ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Time%20does%20not%20flow%20continuously,observe%E2%80%95does%20not%20play%20any%20apparent)).  This contrasts sharply with our intuition (and classical calculus) of infinitesimal time intervals.  
- **Discrete evolution (“clock ticks”).** Physical processes at the fundamental level would proceed in jumps, not smooth sweeps.  Carried to the limit, one can think of each elementary change in the quantum spacetime network as a “tick” of a clock ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Time%20does%20not%20flow%20continuously,observe%E2%80%95does%20not%20play%20any%20apparent)).  This replaces the idea of time as a continuously flowing dimension with a picture of successive events.  
- **Emergent time.** In some formulations (e.g. canonical quantum gravity) time doesn’t appear as a fundamental variable at all but arises from correlations or entanglement between degrees of freedom.  Thus our sense of a universal clock is only approximate. (Experiments have even shown that for photons, an “internal” observer experiences time from entangled subsystems, whereas no time flows for an “external” clock ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_of_time#:~:text=In%202013%2C%20at%20the%20Istituto,DeWitt%20equation)).) 
- **Symmetries and conservation laws.**  A continuous time implies exact time-translation symmetry and energy conservation via Noether’s theorem.  If time is fundamentally discrete or emergent, one must re-examine these symmetries at the smallest scales. (How precisely energy conservation works with Planck-scale time steps is an area of research.) 

Overall, **the “smooth river of time” is replaced by a ticking clock at the deepest level**.  Our innate, continuous-time worldview becomes an approximation valid only at large scales. In reality, spacetime would be like a fine 4D lattice or network such that “between” nodes there is no time or space; continuity emerges only when one averages over many quanta.  This reshapes even basic intuitions: we must accept that at a fundamental level there are gaps in time just as in space, and our continuous experience of time is a rounded-out, emergent phenomenon  ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Time%20does%20not%20flow%20continuously,observe%E2%80%95does%20not%20play%20any%20apparent)) ([plato.stanford.edu](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2019/entries/innateness-cognition/#:~:text=As%20adults%2C%20we%20recognize%20physical,objects%20causally%20interact%20upon%20contact)).  

**Key implications include:**  
- There is a minimum Planck‐scale time unit (chronon), so the timeline has “holes” at that scale ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Time%20does%20not%20flow%20continuously,observe%E2%80%95does%20not%20play%20any%20apparent)).  
- Dynamics occur via discrete transitions (akin to clock-ticks) of the spacetime quanta ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Time%20does%20not%20flow%20continuously,observe%E2%80%95does%20not%20play%20any%20apparent)).  
- Some approaches imply time isn’t fundamental but built from an underlying causal structure (so “flow of time” is emergent).  
- Our usual calculus and continuity assumptions hold only approximately, not at Planck scales.  

In short, quantum gravity’s discrete spacetime turns the classical notion of seamless, continuous time on its head.  What we take as an endlessly smooth time coordinate is instead an effective, emergent description of a fundamentally quantized sequence of moments ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Theoretically%2C%20the%20most%20appealing%20aspect,like%20a%20ticking%20of%20a)) ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=85341#:~:text=Time%20does%20not%20flow%20continuously,observe%E2%80%95does%20not%20play%20any%20apparent)).
