In practice the best solution is a **hybrid PA architecture** that uses a high-efficiency topology with dynamic biasing plus aggressive linearization.  For example, many mm‐wave PAs today are built as Doherty or out‐phasing (LINC/EER) amplifiers **with envelope tracking (ET)** on the supply and **digital predistortion (DPD)** on the RF path.  The ET supply modulates the PA’s bias to closely follow the signal envelope – pushing the device into saturation whenever possible – so the PA runs at very high efficiency even for high‐PAPR waveforms ([www.edn.com](https://www.edn.com/use-digital-predistortion-with-envelope-tracking/#:~:text=Modulating%20the%20supply%20voltage%20dynamically%2C,performance%20with%202G%20battery%20life)).  By itself this supply modulation introduces extra distortion, but that distortion is largely removed by DPD (or other linearization).  In other words, ET recovers efficiency (approaching 50%+ even at back-off) while DPD corrects the nonlinear output.  This combination yields essentially the †optimal trade-off: the PA operates nearly in a switching/peak‐efficiency mode yet still meets linearity specs (low EVM/ACPR) after predistortion ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/13/2869#:~:text=amplifiers%20,PA%E2%80%99s%20average%20PAE%20and%20linear)) ([www.edn.com](https://www.edn.com/use-digital-predistortion-with-envelope-tracking/#:~:text=This%20efficiency%20gain%20is%20a,See%20Figure%202)).  In summary, modern mm-wave front ends typically *combine* a high-efficiency amplifier class (often in a Doherty or outphasing scheme) **with envelope-tracking biasing and digital predistortion**.  Experiments have shown that this approach restores high PAE while maintaining linearity, precisely balancing the efficiency/linearity trade-off ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/13/2869#:~:text=amplifiers%20,PA%E2%80%99s%20average%20PAE%20and%20linear)) ([www.edn.com](https://www.edn.com/use-digital-predistortion-with-envelope-tracking/#:~:text=This%20efficiency%20gain%20is%20a,See%20Figure%202)).
