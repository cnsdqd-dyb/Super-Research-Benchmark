Brand valuation frameworks achieve this by treating brand value as a *co-creative* construct that links brand symbolism with consumer psychology.  In practice such models combine metrics of a brand’s symbolic assets (heritage, prestige, narrative) with measures of consumer sentiment (awareness, associations, loyalty, identity fit).  The key mechanism is a feedback‐loop or “virtuous cycle”: as a brand accumulates symbolic capital (social value, status, meaning) it becomes a stronger identity‐anchor for consumers, which in turn amplifies the brand’s prestige and reach.  In other words, brand value models don’t just score a brand in isolation – they explicitly integrate consumer‐side data so that brand prestige (image and imagery) and consumer identification reinforce each other ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_equity#:~:text=Brand%20equity%2C%20in%20marketing%20%2C,known)) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol-intensive_brand#:~:text=A%20symbol,of%20belonging%20to%20a%20group)). By calibrating these in a single model (or weighted composite index), the frameworks balance symbolic capital and consumer bonding.
