# Graph Laplacian and Mobility Networks.  We model the social network as a weighted graph where nodes represent individuals or social groups and edge‐weights encode **mobility functions** (the ease or probability of moving between nodes).  The graph Laplacian \(L = D - W\) (with \(D\) the weighted degree matrix and \(W\) the adjacency/mobility matrix) summarizes this structure.  Its eigenvalues \(\lambda_1=0\le\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lambda_n\) capture global connectivity.  In fact, a fundamental result of spectral graph theory is that the number of zero eigenvalues of \(L\) equals the number of disconnected components.  Equivalently, the {\it second} smallest eigenvalue (the *Fiedler value* or *algebraic connectivity*) is strictly positive if and only if the network is fully connected; it becomes zero exactly at the point the graph splits into two pieces ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_connectivity#:~:text=matrix%20%20of%20,well%20connected%20the%20overall%20graph)).  

## Mobility Eigenvalues and Connectivity.  As we vary the underlying mobility parameters (for example, scaling all mobility weights by a factor \(t\)), the Laplacian spectrum shifts.  When mobility is high (weights large), the network is well‐connected (\(\lambda_2>0\)).  But as mobility decreases, \(\lambda_2\) shrinks.  The *threshold* for structural solidification is reached when \(\lambda_2\to 0\).  At that exact point the graph becomes disconnected: no paths remain between certain subgroups.  In other words, social strata have “frozen apart.”  Each zero eigenvalue now signals an isolated subnetwork (stratum).  Thus the eigen‐spectrum directly delimits the transition: before the threshold \(\lambda_2>0\) (fluid class mixing), and past the threshold \(\lambda_2=0\) (fully segregated strata) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_connectivity#:~:text=matrix%20%20of%20,well%20connected%20the%20overall%20graph)).  In practice, one often sees pre‐threshold behavior as well: a very small (but positive) \(\lambda_2\) indicates the network is almost split along a bottleneck cut.  By Cheeger’s inequality and related results, the corresponding Fiedler *vector* reveals the optimal cut that nearly disconnects the graph ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian_matrix#:~:text=Kirchhoff%27s%20theorem%20%20can%20be,the%20Laplacian%20matrix%20allows%20the)).  In social terms, this means the weakest mobility links (identified by the sign structure of the Fiedler vector) lie exactly where the society is about to stratify.

- The *algebraic connectivity* \(\lambda_2\) is the critical indicator: \(\lambda_2>0\) ⇢ one fluid network; \(\lambda_2=0\) ⇢ at least two isolated groups ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_connectivity#:~:text=matrix%20%20of%20,well%20connected%20the%20overall%20graph)).  
- More generally, the multiplicity of zero‐eigenvalues equals the number of disconnected strata.  When further eigenvalues approach zero, the network is on the verge of breaking into more pieces.  
- The Fiedler eigenvector (✕ second eigenvector) pinpoints which nodes split off first.  It approximates the sparsest cut in the graph ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian_matrix#:~:text=Kirchhoff%27s%20theorem%20%20can%20be,the%20Laplacian%20matrix%20allows%20the)).  In other words, it tells us *where* mobility fails and solidification occurs.  
- As mobility parameters cross the critical value (making \(\lambda_2\) zero), the network “solidifies” into rigid components.  These thresholds are thus read off the Laplacian spectrum: one finds the value where eigenvalues vanish to mark the onset of structural stratification.

In summary, **Laplacian eigenvalues act as spectral monitors of social connectivity**.  By analyzing the spectrum of the mobility‐weighted Laplacian, one can identify the exact point (threshold) where the network loses cohesion.  Crossing that point breaks society into non‐communicating strata – the hallmark of structural solidification.  These principles (connectivity ⇆ \(\lambda_2\) and zero‐eigenvalue counts) are well‐established in network theory ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_connectivity#:~:text=matrix%20%20of%20,well%20connected%20the%20overall%20graph)) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian_matrix#:~:text=Kirchhoff%27s%20theorem%20%20can%20be,the%20Laplacian%20matrix%20allows%20the)) and directly apply to mobility‐driven social graphs. 

**Sources:**  Graph spectral theory shows that the Laplacian’s second eigenvalue vanishes exactly when a connected network fragments ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_connectivity#:~:text=matrix%20%20of%20,well%20connected%20the%20overall%20graph)), and that the Fiedler (second) eigenvector identifies the critical cut ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian_matrix#:~:text=Kirchhoff%27s%20theorem%20%20can%20be,the%20Laplacian%20matrix%20allows%20the)).  Thus the mobility‐Laplacian spectrum precisely delimits the transition from fluid mobility to rigid stratification.
