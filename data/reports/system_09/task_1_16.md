Maintaining a tight spacecraft formation with minimal fuel means using the most “efficient” engine possible – i.e. **maximizing specific impulse** – *but only up to the point that you can still control it accurately*.  In practice this means using very high-I<sub>sp</sub> thrusters (such as tiny electric or micro-propellant jets) **for fuel efficiency**, *but* pairing them with extremely precise thrust-vectoring so that each tiny impulse goes exactly where it’s needed.  High‐I<sub>sp</sub> engines produce very low thrust, so any mis-pointing or coarse impulse-bit can quickly lead to drift and require corrective burns (which wastes propellant).  Thus the trade-off is: **go as far toward high I<sub>sp</sub> as your control precision allows**.  Push I<sub>sp</sub> higher (less propellant per Δv) only if your attitude/thruster pointing system can deliver the low thrust with the fine accuracy needed to maintain the formation.  

In short, formation flying is most energy‐efficient *when using the highest‐I<sub>sp</sub> propulsion that still delivers controllable micro‐impulses*.  For example, electric or micro-thrusters (high I<sub>sp</sub>, low thrust) are ideal for formation keeping ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/1/221#:~:text=Specific%20impulse%20I_,specific%20impulse%20is%20defined%20as)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/5/4/119#:~:text=inherently%20low%20thrust%20levels%20and,based%20options)), but only if their tiny impulses are aimed extremely precisely.  If control is too coarse, one must use richer propellant burns (lower I<sub>sp</sub>, higher thrust) to avoid constant over-corrections.  The optimum is the point where the marginal fuel saved by increasing I<sub>sp</sub> is balanced by the marginal fuel cost of correcting any thrust misalignments.  In practice this often means equipping the craft with fine-resolution microthrusters or gimbaled low‐thrust jets and pushing I<sub>sp</sub> to the highest level that the formation’s attitude control can support ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/5/4/119#:~:text=enabling%20technology%20for%20constellation,experimental%20results%20indicate%20that%20this)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/5/4/119#:~:text=inherently%20low%20thrust%20levels%20and,based%20options)). 

**Answer:**  Use the highest‐I<sub>sp</sub> (lowest-fuel) thrusters *that you can still aim and modulate very precisely*.  Energy efficiency peaks where specific impulse is maximized *subject to* the precision of thrust-vector control – i.e. when the engine’s tiny thrust pulses are still within the pointing accuracy needed to hold formation.
