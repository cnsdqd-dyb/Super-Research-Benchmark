**Quantum confinement wins:**  Strong 3D quantization “locks” carriers into discrete dot states, which both raises the modal gain and produces a very narrow emission line.  In practice this means using well‐defined, uniform (monodisperse) QDs and high‐Q cavities so that the ground‐state DOS is low and easily saturated.  As one review explains, QDs have a “delta-like” density of states that yields higher material gain and a narrowed spectrum ([www.degruyterbrill.com](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/nanoph-2020-0382/html#:~:text=separates%20electron%20and%20hole%20wave,well%20%28DWELLs%29%2C%20or)).  Thus tight confinement (small, uniform dots in multi‐layer stacks) inherently lowers the threshold current and improves spectral purity.  

- **Minimize surface traps:**  Every surface state is a non‐radiative recombination sink that siphons carriers away from the lasing transition.  In quantum‐dot lasers – especially in micro-cavities with large surface‐to‐volume ratios – surface recombination can dominate the threshold behavior ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=oe-15-12-7281#:~:text=samples%20and%20which%20were%20grown,be%20addressed%20in%20section%205)).  Optimizing J_th requires engineering the barriers and surfaces to suppress these traps.  For example, choosing materials with intrinsically low surface SRV (e.g. InAs QDs in InP or AlGaAs barriers) or applying surface passivation dramatically reduces non‐radiative loss ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/fulltext.cfm?uri=prj-3-5-B1#:~:text=current%20at%20submicrometer%20scales%2C%20very,Various%20surface%20treatments)) ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/fulltext.cfm?uri=prj-3-5-B1#:~:text=Different%20parameters%20and%20assumptions%20used,If)).  In tightly confined nanolasers any remaining surface current still raises the required carrier density (and thus threshold), so treating surfaces (passivation, dielectric coatings, etc.) is critical ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/fulltext.cfm?uri=prj-3-5-B1#:~:text=current%20at%20submicrometer%20scales%2C%20very,Various%20surface%20treatments)) ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=oe-15-12-7281#:~:text=samples%20and%20which%20were%20grown,be%20addressed%20in%20section%205)).

Together, these two mechanisms – **maximizing 3D confinement** while **minimizing surface‐state recombination** – give the lowest achievable threshold current density.  Strong confinement ensures a high differential gain and very narrow linewidth (high spectral purity) ([www.degruyterbrill.com](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/nanoph-2020-0382/html#:~:text=separates%20electron%20and%20hole%20wave,well%20%28DWELLs%29%2C%20or)) ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=oe-15-12-7281#:~:text=Microcavity%20lasers%20with%20InAs%20quantum,absorbed%20threshold%20pump%20power%20of)), and good surface passivation (or material choice) keeps nonradiative losses low so that nearly all injected current contributes to lasing.  In practice, engineers balance these by, for instance, using multi-layer QD active regions with uniform dot sizes (to exploit the discrete GS gain) and carefully engineered capping/passivation layers. ([www.degruyterbrill.com](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/nanoph-2020-0382/html#:~:text=separates%20electron%20and%20hole%20wave,well%20%28DWELLs%29%2C%20or)) ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/fulltext.cfm?uri=prj-3-5-B1#:~:text=current%20at%20submicrometer%20scales%2C%20very,Various%20surface%20treatments)) (Some groups even modulation-dope the barriers to “fill” trap states, which likewise suppresses surface recombination and lowers J_th ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/fulltext.cfm?uri=prj-3-5-B1#:~:text=coatings%20%5B15%20%5D.%20Room,2%7D%20and%20as%20low%20as)).)  

**Answer:** Design the dots and waveguide to maximize 3D quantum confinement (i.e. a high gain, narrow line from the discrete QD states) while simultaneously suppressing surface recombination (through surface passivation or choice of low‐SRV materials).  This double strategy minimizes the threshold current for a given output and yields high spectral purity ([www.degruyterbrill.com](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/nanoph-2020-0382/html#:~:text=separates%20electron%20and%20hole%20wave,well%20%28DWELLs%29%2C%20or)) ([opg.optica.org](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=oe-15-12-7281#:~:text=samples%20and%20which%20were%20grown,be%20addressed%20in%20section%205)).
