# Black Hole Information Paradox vs. Quantum Causality

The **black hole information paradox** arises because Hawking’s semiclassical calculation predicts that a pure quantum state (matter falling in) evolves into thermal Hawking radiation, apparently destroying information ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/journal/entropy/special_issues/SJ322C78CO#:~:text=In%201976%2C%20Stephen%20Hawking%20argued%2C,attempted%20to%20resolve%20this%20problem)).  This conflicts with standard quantum mechanics, which is strictly unitary (no information loss).  Naively it looks as if information that falls behind the event horizon can never escape, breaking the usual “cause precedes effect” rule: once the black hole evaporates away, there is no obvious record of the original data.  In quantum theory, unitary evolution (and relatedly no‐signaling) is tied to causality: one cannot influence spacelike-separated regions instantaneously, and time evolution is reversible.  If information were truly lost, it would effectively introduce an irreversible, non-unitary element into quantum evolution, a radical change to the theory.  

However, **most modern proposals do not require rewiring quantum causality**. In fact, many string-theory and holographic arguments assume that quantum mechanics (QM) remains intact (unitary and causal) and that new quantum-gravitational physics simply provides hidden channels to preserve information. Key ideas include:

- **Holography/AdS–CFT:** In the AdS/CFT duality, a black hole in the bulk corresponds to an ordinary thermal state in a conformal field theory on the boundary.  Since the CFT evolves unitarily, *all* information must in principle reappear in the Hawking radiation ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.104012#:~:text=The%20information%20paradox%20in%20the,a%20semiclassical%20effective%20theory%20in)).  Crucially, Lowe and Thorlacius (1999) emphasize that in this picture “no violations of causality are apparent to local observers” – the boundary theory is manifestly causal even though the bulk description requires subtle nonlocal effects to transfer information out of the hole ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.104012#:~:text=The%20information%20paradox%20in%20the,a%20semiclassical%20effective%20theory%20in)).  In other words, holography encodes interior data at infinity without any glaring faster-than-light signals.  The bulk spacetime description may duplicate degrees of freedom inside/outside the horizon, but these are resolved by quantum correlations (no observer sees causality broken) ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.104012#:~:text=The%20information%20paradox%20in%20the,a%20semiclassical%20effective%20theory%20in)).

- **Black Hole Complementarity:**  Susskind et al. (1993) proposed that an infalling observer and a distant observer see different yet consistent descriptions.  To an outside observer, the black hole evaporates via a unitary S-matrix that maps infalling states to outgoing radiation, with information smeared on a “stretched horizon” just above the event horizon ([grokipedia.com](https://grokipedia.com/page/Black_hole_complementarity#:~:text=postulates%3A%20,be%20preserved%20without%20violating%20the)).  From this point of view the horizon behaves like a hot membrane carrying microstates.  There is no observable cloning of information because no single observer can see both the inside and the outside simultaneously.  Thus unitarity is preserved—for the outside world the black hole is just another quantum system ([grokipedia.com](https://grokipedia.com/page/Black_hole_complementarity#:~:text=postulates%3A%20,be%20preserved%20without%20violating%20the)).  Crucially, complementarity maintains the normal causal structure *for each observer*: infalling observers see a smooth horizon (no drama) and cannot send messages out, while outside observers see thermal emission carrying tiny correlations. This scenario **does not require violating quantum causality**, only that quantum degrees of freedom near the horizon are highly scrambled. 

- **Fuzzball Paradigm:**  Mathur and collaborators in string theory argue that black holes are actually “fuzzballs,” complex quantum states with no traditional horizon or interior.  In this picture, an infalling shell of matter triggers a quantum transition into a fuzzball state *before* a true horizon forms.  As Mathur (2017) phrased it, the paradox can be split into a “causality problem” – in semiclassical GR the infalling matter would be trapped behind an event horizon “with no way to send its information back to infinity” unless one allows superluminal propagation – and an entanglement problem.  In the fuzzball scenario, high-energy infalling objects undergo “entropy-enhanced tunneling” into a quantum fuzzball state (a state with complicated multipole structure) outside where a classical horizon would have been ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03042#:~:text=The%20black%20hole%20information%20paradox,These%20two)).  Since there is *no event horizon* in the usual sense, the information never gets frozen behind a light cone – it can in principle emerge with the radiation.  Importantly, this resolution **uses quantum structure near the would-be horizon to preserve causality**: nothing “breaks the speed limit” or sends signals outside light cones, because the horizon itself is replaced by quantum matter/space that radiates away unitarily ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03042#:~:text=The%20black%20hole%20information%20paradox,These%20two)).

- **Soft Hair and Quantum Hair:**  Recent work emphasizes that black holes carry subtleties beyond the classical no-hair theorem.  Hawking, Perry and Strominger (2016) showed that asymptotic symmetries (the BMS supertranslations) imply an infinite set of conservation laws, forcing black holes to carry an abundance of “soft hair” – zero-energy gravitational (and gauge) modes on the horizon ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.231301#:~:text=It%20has%20recently%20been%20shown,used%20to%20give%20an%20infinite)).  These soft modes encode information about what fell in, which in turn enforces precise relations among the outgoing radiation modes ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.231301#:~:text=It%20has%20recently%20been%20shown,used%20to%20give%20an%20infinite)).  In effect, the information is stored in horizon degrees of freedom (and conserved charges at infinity) and can leak out with the radiation.  This proposal still uses conventional quantum theory – it simply discovers that more information is accessible at the boundary than previously thought.  No standard causal law is broken; rather, the resolution is that the black hole exterior is subtly correlated with its interior via these extra symmetries ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.231301#:~:text=It%20has%20recently%20been%20shown,used%20to%20give%20an%20infinite)).

Each of these approaches preserves global unitarity and adheres to ordinary causal behavior for observers.  They may imply new forms of quantum nonlocality (for example, ER=EPR conjecture equating entanglement with spacetime wormholes), but **none require explicit superluminal signaling or time-reversed influences in the standard sense**.  In AdS/CFT and complementarity, the apparent “teleportation” of information is encoded in boundary correlations; in fuzzballs and soft hair, it is encoded on the horizon.  In all cases, local observers do not see any causal loops or signals traveling outside the light cone. 

In contrast, some **radical ideas** do break with standard QM causality:  
- **Final-state projection:** Horowitz and Maldacena (2004) proposed a fixed “final state” at the singularity, effectively post-selecting the outgoing radiation to maintain unitarity.  This is akin to allowing a specific backward-in-time boundary condition.  While clever, it explicitly ties future and past boundary data and deviates from orthodox quantum evolution (it requires a kind of non-linear projection at the singularity).  
- **Objective wavefunction collapse:** Penrose and others have speculated that gravity might induce a fundamental collapse of the wavefunction, turning pure states into mixed states.  Okon & Sudarsky (2014) note that if such collapses occur, then information could disappear without contradiction, but this means giving up strict unitarity and modifying quantum dynamics.  Such collapse models entail an inherent irreversibility (and would alter cause-effect relations at a fundamental level).  
- **Hidden-variable non-equilibrium:** In pilot-wave (de Broglie–Bohm) theory, Antony Valentini has argued that if the quantum equilibrium (Born rule) were not exact, then entanglement could carry signals nonlocally.  He shows that in this “quantum non-equilibrium” one could, in principle, transmit information via entangled particles – even across an event horizon ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05374#:~:text=We%20show%20that%20quantum%20nonequilibrium,of%20an%20evaporating%20black%20hole)).  This is a severe departure from standard QM causality (it allows instantaneous information flow).  None of these speculative ideas has any experimental support; they represent how drastically one would have to change QM to *truly* violate causality.  

**Conclusion:**  The prevailing view is that solving the information paradox does **not** require a wholesale rewrite of quantum causality or abandonment of no-signaling.  Instead, most research focuses on finding subtle quantum-gravitational mechanisms that preserve information within the existing causal framework.  As Lowe and Thorlacius concluded, the holographic principle suggests *all* information returns in the radiation and “no violations of causality are apparent to local observers” ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.104012#:~:text=The%20information%20paradox%20in%20the,a%20semiclassical%20effective%20theory%20in)).  Concrete proposals (complementarity, fuzzballs, soft hair, quantum error correction in holography, etc.) all preserve unitary QM and causal consistency, at the cost of highly nontrivial correlations or new degrees of freedom at the horizon.  In short, **no established resolution has yet forced physicists to give up the normal notion of causality in quantum mechanics** ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.104012#:~:text=The%20information%20paradox%20in%20the,a%20semiclassical%20effective%20theory%20in)) ([journals.aps.org](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.231301#:~:text=It%20has%20recently%20been%20shown,used%20to%20give%20an%20infinite)).  If anything, the paradox has taught us that spacetime and quantum information are more subtly intertwined than we thought, but it so far threatens to change our ideas of gravity more than our ideas of QM causality.
