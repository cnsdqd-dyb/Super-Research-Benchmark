The key result is that an age‐structured projection matrix (a Leslie or similar matrix) can be diagonalized into eigenmodes, and the long‐term growth of the population is governed by the largest eigenvalue.  In practice one writes the projection equation as 
\[\mathbf{n}_{t+1}=A\,\mathbf{n}_t,\] 
so that formally 
\[\mathbf{n}_t=A^t\mathbf{n}_0=\sum_i c_i\lambda_i^t\,\mathbf{w}_i,\] 
where $\{\lambda_i,\mathbf{w}_i\}$ are the eigenpairs of $A$ and $c_i$ are coefficients from the initial age‐distribution.  In this decomposition each mode grows as $\lambda_i^t$.  By the Perron–Frobenius theorem for nonnegative (age‐structured) matrices, there is a **dominant eigenvalue** $\lambda_1$ (real, positive and larger in modulus than all others) with corresponding positive right–eigenvector $\mathbf{w}_1$ ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_matrix#:~:text=where%20%5Cmathbf,lambda)) ([bookdown.org](https://bookdown.org/cdorm/lefko3gentle/detan.html#:~:text=The%20asymptotic%20properties%20of%20this,also%20see%20the%20following%20conditions)).  As $t\to\infty$ the term $c_1\lambda_1^t\mathbf{w}_1$ dominates all others.  In other words, the population vector converges to the **stable age distribution** $\mathbf{w}_1$, and the total population (and any linear function of it, such as total births) grows (or declines) at a constant exponential rate $\lambda_1$.  Mathematically, one shows 
\[\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A^t}{\lambda_1^t} = \mathbf{w}_1\,\mathbf{v}_1^T,\] 
so that $\mathbf{n}_t\approx (c_1\mathbf{w}_1)\,\lambda_1^t$ for large $t$ ([vdoc.pub](https://vdoc.pub/documents/applied-mathematical-demography-4ruld8hsr7u0#:~:text=The%20dominant%20eigenvalue%20of%20A%2C,to%20w1%20%3B%20the%20rows)) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_matrix#:~:text=where%20%5Cmathbf,lambda)).  

Concretely, this means: once the population reaches its stable age structure, each time step multiplies the population (and births) by $\lambda_1$ (so successive generations reproduce geometrically by $\lambda_1^t$) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_matrix#:~:text=where%20%5Cmathbf,lambda)) ([bookdown.org](https://bookdown.org/cdorm/lefko3gentle/detan.html#:~:text=The%20asymptotic%20properties%20of%20this,also%20see%20the%20following%20conditions)).  The *left* eigenvector (reproductive value) $v_1$ gives the relative contribution of each age‐class to future reproduction, but the *right* eigenvector $w_1$ fixes the **proportions** of ages in the long run.  Any other eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ with $|\lambda_i|<\lambda_1$ only contribute “transient” modes: if they are real they decay relative to $\lambda_1^t$, and if they are complex they cause damped oscillations.  In short, the eigen‐decomposition shows that asymptotically the population (and hence its reproduction output) grows or shrinks at rate $\lambda_1$, with a fixed age‐profile given by $w_1$ ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_matrix#:~:text=where%20%5Cmathbf,lambda)) ([bookdown.org](https://bookdown.org/cdorm/lefko3gentle/detan.html#:~:text=The%20asymptotic%20properties%20of%20this,also%20see%20the%20following%20conditions)).  This directly determines all long-run reproduction functions (birth rates, net reproduction, etc.) since those quantities scale with $\lambda_1^t$ under the stable age distribution. 

**References:** In a standard Leslie (age‐structured) model the dominant eigenvalue is the intrinsic growth factor and its eigenvector the stable age distribution ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_matrix#:~:text=where%20%5Cmathbf,lambda)).  Equivalently, Smith & Caswell (2001) and others show that $A^t\to \lambda_1^t w_1v_1^T$ as $t\to\infty$, so $\lambda_1$ is the asymptotic growth rate and $w_1$ (scaled as proportions) is the final reproduction structure ([bookdown.org](https://bookdown.org/cdorm/lefko3gentle/detan.html#:~:text=The%20asymptotic%20properties%20of%20this,also%20see%20the%20following%20conditions)) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_matrix#:~:text=where%20%5Cmathbf,lambda)).  (Any sub‐dominant eigenmodes vanish in the limit.)
