Traffic scheduling in congested networks always follows the underlying QoS‐policy (priorities, weights, SLAs) that was configured for the flows.  In practice packets are first **classified** into service classes (e.g. VoIP, streaming, best‐effort) based on SLAs or DiffServ markings ([www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com](https://www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com/blog/internet-qos-and-traffic-scheduling.html#:~:text=The%20foundation%20of%20traffic%20scheduling,This%20involves)).  The scheduler then enforces these classes with its chosen algorithm.  For example, strict **priority scheduling** always serves high‐priority queues first – so under peak load low‐priority queues simply starve or drop packets ([www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com](https://www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com/blog/internet-qos-and-traffic-scheduling.html#:~:text=Cons%3A)).  In a **custom queue** setup where each class has a fixed share of buffer, the queues with smaller allocatio­n will fill up and shed packets first under congestion ([www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com](https://www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com/blog/internet-qos-and-traffic-scheduling.html#:~:text=,when%20traffic%20volumes%20are%20high)).  Likewise, **Weighted Fair Queuing** allocates bandwidth in proportion to preconfigured weights, so during overload each flow’s throughput is cut back according to its weight ([www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com](https://www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com/blog/internet-qos-and-traffic-scheduling.html#:~:text=WFQ%20ensures%20fair%20bandwidth%20distribution,even%20when%20packet%20sizes%20vary)).  

In short, the **service degradation strategy is determined by the traffic classes and their configured priorities/weights**.  The scheduler’s policy (priority vs. WFQ vs. custom queuing, etc.) and the per‐class bandwidth shares decide which services get preserved and which are throttled or dropped at peak load ([www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com](https://www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com/blog/internet-qos-and-traffic-scheduling.html#:~:text=The%20foundation%20of%20traffic%20scheduling,This%20involves)) ([www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com](https://www.computernetworkassignmenthelp.com/blog/internet-qos-and-traffic-scheduling.html#:~:text=Cons%3A)).  High‐priority or larger‐weight flows continue to get service, while lower‐priority or smaller‐weight flows bear the brunt of the degradation.
