# Spectral Radius and Stability in Digital Interaction Hypergraphs 

Stability of networked interactions often relates to eigenvalues of the underlying weight matrix.  In simple pairwise models (e.g. linear consensus or epidemic spread on a graph), one indeed finds a critical threshold involving the largest eigenvalue (spectral radius) of the adjacency or weight matrix ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)).  In particular, for an SIS‐like contagion on a network, the epidemic dies out if β/δ < 1/λ_max(A), where λ_max(A) is the spectral radius.  Recent work on *hypergraphs* (models of higher-order group interactions) shows that near a fixed point the dynamics reduce to an **effective graph projection**: a weighted adjacency matrix G that encodes the net pairwise coupling ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=this%20paper%2C%20we%20derive%20expressions,two%20dynamics%20of%20general%20interest)). In other words, **hypergraph dynamics can be analyzed via a “graph-projection”** whose weights (derived from trust or influence values) determine stability ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=this%20paper%2C%20we%20derive%20expressions,two%20dynamics%20of%20general%20interest)).  

For example, in a social‐contagion model on a hypergraph, the stability of the zero‐activity (absorbing) state depends only on the pairwise (second-order) adjacency matrix A^2.  One finds a threshold condition of the form β/δ < 1/λ_max(A^2) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)), meaning the spectral radius of that effective matrix delimits the boundary between die-out and outbreak.  This is mathematically analogous to classic results for graph contagion: the **basis of the stability condition is a largest‐eigenvalue (spectral radius) criterion ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)).** In general, the linear stability analysis decouples structural and dynamical contributions, and only the leading eigenmode (spectral radius) of the effective weight matrix enters the threshold ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=this%20paper%2C%20we%20derive%20expressions,two%20dynamics%20of%20general%20interest)).  

However, in a truly hypergraph (multi-way) setting, **stability is not governed by the trust matrix alone**.  The “trust matrix” (pairwise trust weights) contributes to the graph-projection, but higher-order interaction terms can influence dynamics in subtler ways ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=this%20paper%2C%20we%20derive%20expressions,two%20dynamics%20of%20general%20interest)). In the general framework of Iacovacci et al. (2021) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=this%20paper%2C%20we%20derive%20expressions,two%20dynamics%20of%20general%20interest)), the full hypergraph is decomposed into layers (m-uniform hypergraphs) and each layer contributes additively to the effective coupling.  In many contagion cases only the pairwise (m=2) layer matters for the absorbing state, so the trust-weighted adjacency dominates the threshold ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)). But other processes or non-linear dynamics can make higher-order terms relevant. In short, **the spectral radius of the trust matrix enters the condition for stability but does not by itself “delimit” all possible interaction forms**. One must consider the combined effective adjacency (including hyperedge effects) when determining the true stability boundary ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=this%20paper%2C%20we%20derive%20expressions,two%20dynamics%20of%20general%20interest)) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)).  

**In summary:** For linearized dynamics on a digital interaction hypergraph, the critical threshold is indeed tied to the largest eigenvalue of an appropriate weight matrix (often the pairwise “trust” adjacency) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)).  But because hypergraph interactions can involve multi-node effects, the simple trust‐matrix spectral radius is not the *sole* determinant of stability. A more complete analysis uses the graph-projection of the hypergraph (combining all orders of interaction) to identify the stability boundary ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=this%20paper%2C%20we%20derive%20expressions,two%20dynamics%20of%20general%20interest)) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)).  

**Answer:** Not by itself. The trust‐matrix spectral radius appears in the stability criterion (through the effective adjacency of pairwise interactions) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)), but hypergraph stability generally depends on the full projected structure. The largest eigenvalue of that effective matrix enters the threshold ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)), yet the trust matrix’s spectral radius alone does *not* universally delimit all stability boundaries in higher-order interaction systems ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=this%20paper%2C%20we%20derive%20expressions,two%20dynamics%20of%20general%20interest)) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00525-3#:~:text=%24%24%5Cfrac%7B%5Cbeta%20%7D%7B%5Cdelta%20%7D%3C%7B%5Cleft%28%7B%7B%7B%5CLambda%20%7D%7D%7D_%7B%5Cmax%20%7D%28%7B%7B%5Cbf%7BA%7D%7D%7D%5E%7B2%7D%29%5Cright%29%7D%5E%7B)).
