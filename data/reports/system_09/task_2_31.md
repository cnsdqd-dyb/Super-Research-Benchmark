# Impact of UV irradiation on PV backsheets  
Intense ultraviolet (UV) light can **chemically degrade** backsheet polymers, breaking C–C bonds and generating free radicals.  This leads to photo-oxidation and chain scission, which makes the material brittle and chalky ([www.lkintl.com](https://www.lkintl.com/news/backsheet-material-selection-for-harsh-climates.html#:~:text=UV%20degradation%20coupled%20with%20extreme,Selecting%20a%C2%A0backsheet%C2%A0with%20a%20thick)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9753168/#:~:text=factor%2C%20UV%20radiation%20highly%20affects,radiation%20on%20the%20lifetime%20of)).  For example, one industry report notes that “intense UV radiation breaks down polymer chains, leading to embrittlement, cracking, and loss of mechanical/insulating properties” ([www.lkintl.com](https://www.lkintl.com/news/backsheet-material-selection-for-harsh-climates.html#:~:text=UV%20degradation%20coupled%20with%20extreme,Selecting%20a%C2%A0backsheet%C2%A0with%20a%20thick)).  In practice UV aging often produces microscopic pits and “fish-scale” craters in the surface polymer layers ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=Single%20UV%20exposure%20caused%20pits,scale%20craters%20on%20the%20surface)).  These surface defects are stress concentrators that under mechanical or thermal strain quickly turn into visible cracks ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=Single%20UV%20exposure%20caused%20pits,scale%20craters%20on%20the%20surface)).  Even small cracks greatly compromise the moisture/oxygen barrier: laboratory UV aging of transparent backsheets showed that after cracking the oxygen and water-vapor transmission rates “increase[d] significantly” ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=)).  In short, prolonged UV exposure causes backsheet surfaces to yellow/chalk and become brittle, after which cracks form and sharply reduce the material’s barrier effectiveness ([www.lkintl.com](https://www.lkintl.com/news/backsheet-material-selection-for-harsh-climates.html#:~:text=UV%20degradation%20coupled%20with%20extreme,Selecting%20a%C2%A0backsheet%C2%A0with%20a%20thick)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=Single%20UV%20exposure%20caused%20pits,scale%20craters%20on%20the%20surface)).  

## Effects of humidity (moisture) cycling  
Cyclic exposure to moisture (high/low humidity or damp heat) attacks backsheets in several ways.  Water vapor is absorbed into polymers and adhesives, causing plasticization and even chemical hydrolysis in susceptible materials.  For instance, moisture can hydrolyze polyester (PET) and polyamide layers at elevated temperature, slowly cutting polymer chains.  In practice, fielded modules show that moisture and heat together lead to delamination and cracking over time.  One review notes that backsheets (especially polyamides) develop cracking after years in service and that these “physico-chemical degradation processes largely depend on temperature, moisture (humidity), and UV irradiation” ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X21005375#:~:text=However%2C%20some%20commercial%20PA,2018)).  In other words, humidity cycling itself stresses the laminate: repeated swelling and shrinkage of layers causes micro-cracks and weakens adhesive bonds at the interfaces.  Moisture-laden conditions also enable corrosive byproducts (like acetic acid from EVA) to form, which attack metal cells and sealants.  In damp-heat tests (85 °C/85% RH) or humidity-freeze cycles, backsheets often show early delamination from the EVA and a drop in peel/tensile strength.  

## Synergistic UV + humidity aging  
UV and humidity tend to **reinforce each other’s damage**.  High humidity or water condensation accelerates photo-oxidation chemistry inside the module.  Under combined UV+heat/humidity exposure, polymer degradation is dramatically worse than under UV or humidity alone.  Studies show that—for example—EVA (the encapsulant) experiences chain scission much faster under simultaneous UV and 85 % RH heat than under either stress alone ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/12/12/5208#:~:text=step,More%20precisely%2C%20a%20significant%20chemical)).  The UV creates oxidized fragments (e.g. peri-acid species) that react in the presence of moisture to break polymer chains.  This is true for backsheets too: UV-induced radicals combined with water can hydrolyze or otherwise attack the polymer matrix.  As one analysis concluded, backing films in field environments are exposed to all these stressors, and their “degradation processes largely depend on … moisture (humidity), and UV irradiation” ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X21005375#:~:text=However%2C%20some%20commercial%20PA,2018)).  In practical terms, a backsheet that is weakened by UV will soak up water faster and vice versa.  The net effect is an accelerated aging: flaking or chalking surfaces, deeper cracks, and fast loss of mechanical strength in humid climates or lab damp-heat tests.  

## Consequences for module sealing integrity  
All of the above aging modes directly **compromise the module’s seal and insulation**.  Cracks and delaminations in the backsheet create pathways for water and oxygen to enter the module.  Once the backsheet integrity is lost, moisture can invade the EVA and reach metallic parts.  This leads to corrosion on the cell grid, frame, or busbars, and often electrical leakage paths (snail trails, potential-induced degradation).  Critically, even small cracks in the backsheet film hugely increase the permeation rate of moisture ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=)).  Empirical studies show that backsheet cracking “impairs the electrical insulation” of the module: insulation (wet leakage) resistance drops sharply when cracks align with cell gaps  ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X21005375#:~:text=However%2C%20some%20commercial%20PA,2018)).  In severe cases operators report inverter ground-fault trips and insulation failures as backsheet conditions worsen.  In summary, UV and humidity cycling age a backsheet by hardening, cracking, and delaminating its polymer layers.  These degradations destroy the moisture barrier: the module’s seal integrity is lost, permitting rapid ingress of moisture that then undermines the entire module (leakage currents, corrosion, and insulation failure) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X21005375#:~:text=However%2C%20some%20commercial%20PA,2018)).
