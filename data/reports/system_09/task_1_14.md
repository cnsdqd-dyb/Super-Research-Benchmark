# Thermal runaway and propagation in Li-ion packs  
Lithium-ion thermal runaway is a self‐accelerating chain reaction in which one cell’s exothermic breakdown (SEI and electrolyte decomposition, cathode breakdown) generates intense heat and flammable gas, igniting neighboring cells.  For example, once a cell’s internal temperature exceeds ~80–150 °C it can vent hot gases and reach >800 °C, rapidly **propagating** to adjacent cells ([batterypackdesign.com](https://batterypackdesign.com/thermal-runaway-mechanisms-in-lithium-batteries#:~:text=Thermal%20runaway%20is%20an%20uncontrolled,150%C2%B0C%20depending%20on%20chemistry)).  In a battery module a single-cell failure can spread via **conductive heat, ejecta (solid fragments), and flammable gas channels**, often igniting neighbors in seconds ([batterypackdesign.com](https://batterypackdesign.com/thermal-runaway-mechanisms-in-lithium-batteries#:~:text=Single,and%20current%20for%20enhanced%20reliability)) ([batterypackdesign.com](https://batterypackdesign.com/thermal-runaway-mechanisms-in-lithium-batteries#:~:text=Once%20triggered%2C%20thermal%20runaway%20propagates,heat%20exceeding%20800%C2%B0C%20within%20milliseconds)).  In short, thermal runaway propagation is the mechanism by which one cell’s failure triggers a cascade, and it is the chief hazard that pack safety measures must interrupt.

# Electrolyte & separator stability  
Because propagation depends on materials catching fire or shorting, the **stability of the electrolyte and separator** is a first line of defense.  Conventional liquid carbonates decompose into **flammable/toxic gases (HF, POF₃, etc.)** at high temperature ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-6255/8/6/223#:~:text=methyl%20carbonate%20,%C2%B0C%2C%20and%2033%20%C2%B0C%2C%20respectively)), fueling the fire.  Similarly, a standard polyolefin separator will melt and shrink (~125–165 °C), causing internal short-circuits and triggering runaway ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949821X24000917#:~:text=high%20temperatures%2C%20but%20the%20separators,As%20the%20temperature)).  Thus safer chemistries are used to add redundancy: for example, **flame-retardant electrolytes** (e.g. organophosphate additives) raise the flash point and can *delay or even prevent full runaway*, dramatically reducing flame intensity ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-6255/8/6/223#:~:text=methyl%20carbonate%20,%C2%B0C%2C%20and%2033%20%C2%B0C%2C%20respectively)) ([www.mecc-xpower.com](https://www.mecc-xpower.com/news/safety-redundancy-design-of-rack-mounted-lit-85185376.html#:~:text=China%27s%20,experiment%20of%20a%202U%20module)).  Likewise, **high-temperature separators** (multilayer plastics or ceramic-coated PET/Al₂O₃ separators) are engineered to remain intact far above normal abuse thresholds; they physically shut down ion flow or block thermal conduction rather than bubble or shrivel ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949821X24000917#:~:text=high%20temperatures%2C%20but%20the%20separators,As%20the%20temperature)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949821X24000917#:~:text=Ceramic,improve%20the%20thermal%20stability%20and)).  In practice, a cell with a 5 μm ceramic-coated separator and a 10 % phosphate-ester fire-retardant electrolyte was shown to delay thermal runaway from ~3 min to ~15 min and cut combustion rate by 60 % – in abuse tests the cell merely smoked instead of exploding ([www.mecc-xpower.com](https://www.mecc-xpower.com/news/safety-redundancy-design-of-rack-mounted-lit-85185376.html#:~:text=China%27s%20,experiment%20of%20a%202U%20module)).  These material improvements are **built-in safety redundancies**, so that even if one layer (e.g. the separator) begins to fail, the electrolyte won’t immediately ignite or reignite adjacent cells.

# Multi-layer safety redundancy  
Understanding propagation drives a *“belt and suspenders”* approach in pack design.  In addition to stable cells, engineers add **redundant barriers and interventions** at multiple levels:  
- **Cell-level devices:** Most cells include built-in fuses or current-interrupt devices (CIDs) and vent valves.  For instance, some advanced cells use a low-melting alloy fuse that opens at ~80 °C to cut off the cell—this automatically isolates a failing cell the moment it overheats ([www.mecc-xpower.com](https://www.mecc-xpower.com/news/safety-redundancy-design-of-rack-mounted-lit-85185376.html#:~:text=South%20Korea%27s%20,cell%20without%20affecting%20module%20operation)).  The built-in vent then quickly releases gas before pressure builds, preventing an explosion.  
- **Module barriers:** Cells or sub-packs are often physically separated by thermal insulation (e.g. porous aerogel or mineral wool) and compartment walls.  For example, one design encapsulates each module in its own aerogel-lined housing and adds a fire-suppression pack (FM-200 gas) that auto-deploys if any cell exceeds ~80 °C ([www.mecc-xpower.com](https://www.mecc-xpower.com/news/safety-redundancy-design-of-rack-mounted-lit-85185376.html#:~:text=The%20combination%20of%20,only%20increased%20by%205%20%E2%84%83)).  In tests this compartmentalization **blocked flame spread completely**, with neighboring modules rising only ~5 °C ([www.mecc-xpower.com](https://www.mecc-xpower.com/news/safety-redundancy-design-of-rack-mounted-lit-85185376.html#:~:text=The%20combination%20of%20,only%20increased%20by%205%20%E2%84%83)).  Such spatial and material barriers force runaway heat into one compartment, buying time for emergency systems to act.  
- **System-level controls:** Battery Management Systems add electronic redundancy: voltage/temperature sensors and auto-shutdown circuits on each string.  If a sensor detects abnormal heat or pressure, redundant logic can disconnect the entire string or trigger external suppression.  (For example, large-stack designs often couple BMS shutdown with external ventilation or sprinklers.)  

Each layer is a redundant safety net.  The **propagation mechanism** specifically informs these choices: knowing that heat, sparks or gas from one cell can ignite neighbors means designers deliberately **break all those pathways**.  Robust separators and flame-suppressed electrolytes inhibit internal shorts and fires at the cell level ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949821X24000917#:~:text=high%20temperatures%2C%20but%20the%20separators,As%20the%20temperature)) ([www.mecc-xpower.com](https://www.mecc-xpower.com/news/safety-redundancy-design-of-rack-mounted-lit-85185376.html#:~:text=China%27s%20,experiment%20of%20a%202U%20module)), while thermal insulation and active vents interrupt heat and gas transfer at the module/pack level ([batterypackdesign.com](https://batterypackdesign.com/thermal-runaway-mechanisms-in-lithium-batteries#:~:text=Single,and%20current%20for%20enhanced%20reliability)) ([www.mecc-xpower.com](https://www.mecc-xpower.com/news/safety-redundancy-design-of-rack-mounted-lit-85185376.html#:~:text=The%20combination%20of%20,only%20increased%20by%205%20%E2%84%83)).  In practice this multi-tiered redundancy ensures that even if one safety feature fails, others will halt the chain reaction before it engulfs the pack. The bottom line: understanding how runaway propagates (through separators, electrolyte, heat and gas) guides designers to add *redundant safety measures* at every stage – from inherently safer cell materials to physical barriers and emergency shutoffs – making large lithium-ion packs far more tolerant of single-cell failures ([www.mecc-xpower.com](https://www.mecc-xpower.com/news/safety-redundancy-design-of-rack-mounted-lit-85185376.html#:~:text=China%27s%20,experiment%20of%20a%202U%20module)) ([batterypackdesign.com](https://batterypackdesign.com/thermal-runaway-mechanisms-in-lithium-batteries#:~:text=Design)).
