**Quantum non-locality:**  At its core, quantum non‐locality refers to the fact that entangled quantum systems can exhibit correlations across space that no classical local model can explain ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/9/24/5406#:~:text=Bell%E2%80%99s%20discovery%2C%20ref.%20,19%2C5%20%2C%2021%2C7%20%2C%2023)).  In Bell‐type experiments, measurements on entangled particles at distant locations show coordinated outcomes that violate classical “local realism” ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/9/24/5406#:~:text=Bell%E2%80%99s%20discovery%2C%20ref.%20,19%2C5%20%2C%2021%2C7%20%2C%2023)) ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-016-9987-9#:~:text=theory%2C%20%E2%80%9Chas%20a%20very%20surprising,likely%20not%20have%20surprised%20Bell)).  Importantly, this quantum correlation is *non‐local* in the sense that the measurement results are correlated faster than any classical signal could travel.  However – and this is crucial – these correlations **do not by themselves transmit usable information** beyond what conventional physics allows.  As Bell emphasized, “events at one place propagate to other places faster than light… in a way that we cannot use for signalling” ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-016-9987-9#:~:text=theory%2C%20%E2%80%9Chas%20a%20very%20surprising,likely%20not%20have%20surprised%20Bell)).  Any protocol using entanglement (e.g. quantum teleportation or QKD) still requires classical communication channels to complete the information transfer ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-016-9987-9#:~:text=theory%2C%20%E2%80%9Chas%20a%20very%20surprising,likely%20not%20have%20surprised%20Bell)) ([philpapers.org](https://philpapers.org/rec/BRAQP#:~:text=telepathy%20is%20a%20surprising%20application,currently%20known%20to%20the%20authors)).  In short, quantum non‐locality is a powerful resource (enabling, for example, quantum cryptography and coordination protocols) but it cannot *magically* circumvent relativity’s no‐signalling constraint ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-016-9987-9#:~:text=theory%2C%20%E2%80%9Chas%20a%20very%20surprising,likely%20not%20have%20surprised%20Bell)) ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjp/i2019-12987-0#:~:text=corresponding%20violation%20of%20the%20marginal,superluminal%20communication%20would%20be%20possible)).  

**Distributed cognitive systems:**  A “distributed cognitive system” – whether a neural network in a brain, a swarm of robots, or a network of cooperating agents – typically shares and processes information via local signals, synaptic connections or inter‐agent communication links.  In classical terms, information transfer in such a system follows finite‐speed channels, building up global cognition through successive local interactions.  Introducing quantum non‐locality into this picture suggests a fundamentally different kind of linkage: subsystems could be prepared in entangled states so that their internal states become correlated non‐locally.  In principle, this means the cognitive “state” of one node is not independent of another even without direct signals.  Notably, researchers have shown that entanglement can enable coordination in distributed tasks beyond classical possibilities.  For example, in **quantum pseudo-telepathy** games, spatially separated players sharing entangled qubits can achieve perfect coordination “with no need for communication whatsoever, which would be an impossible feat for classical players” ([philpapers.org](https://philpapers.org/rec/BRAQP#:~:text=telepathy%20is%20a%20surprising%20application,currently%20known%20to%20the%20authors)).  In these scenarios, the entangled network of nodes behaves as if it has “shared information” instantaneously, even though no conventional information has actually traveled.  

Likewise, recent theoretical work on swarm systems shows that entangled qubits can synchronize distributed actions: entangled random walks can collapse in concert across agents, and entanglement can be used to detect eavesdropping or coordinate movements without classical messaging ([www.emergentmind.com](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2210.10551#:~:text=Abstract%3A%20This%20paper%20demonstrates%20and,attacking%20the%20system%20using%20entangled)).  Such results illustrate how a *new* information‐transfer mechanism emerges when non‐locality is allowed: instead of sending bits along wires or airwaves, the network’s state is established holistically by its entangled structure.  In effect, information is “encoded” across the distributed system from the outset.  For instance, quantum teleportation uses pre-shared entanglement so that an unknown state in one part of the network can be reconstructed in another, effectively transferring that state’s information content ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-016-9987-9#:~:text=theory%2C%20%E2%80%9Chas%20a%20very%20surprising,likely%20not%20have%20surprised%20Bell)) ([philpapers.org](https://philpapers.org/rec/BRAQP#:~:text=telepathy%20is%20a%20surprising%20application,currently%20known%20to%20the%20authors)).  Superdense coding is another example where one qubit (with entanglement) can carry two bits’ worth of classical information.  These protocols **reconstruct** the channel architecture: entanglement becomes a resource that alters how knowledge flows through the network.  

However, it is vital to stress that **no physical principle has been overturned**.  Even in a quantum-connected cognitive network, actual signal transmission or conscious “messages” still respect the usual limits.  The “fast” correlations are inherently random until a measurement is made, and any controlled use of them for sending substantive information still requires classical communication ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-016-9987-9#:~:text=theory%2C%20%E2%80%9Chas%20a%20very%20surprising,likely%20not%20have%20surprised%20Bell)) ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjp/i2019-12987-0#:~:text=corresponding%20violation%20of%20the%20marginal,superluminal%20communication%20would%20be%20possible)).  In practical terms, this means that quantum non-locality doesn’t *automatically* let a brain-like network “wirelessly download” ideas from another distant brain.  Instead, it means that if such a network were physically entangled, its subsystems could respond in a highly coordinated way, faster or more coherently than via classical links alone.  Some speculative models even propose that brain regions or group‐minds might exploit transient quantum coherence to bind information non-locally, but these remain unproven and highly controversial.  

In summary, quantum non-locality can “reconstruct” the information transfer in a distributed cognitive system by replacing ordinary communication channels with entangled correlations.  Under this paradigm, information is embedded in global quantum states rather than conveyed point to point.  The system’s nodes effectively share a collective informational substrate: decisions or pattern-recognition can emerge from the entangled network as if the whole system “knew” something at once.  This has been shown theoretically to reduce communication needs (as in pseudo-telepathy protocols ([philpapers.org](https://philpapers.org/rec/BRAQP#:~:text=telepathy%20is%20a%20surprising%20application,currently%20known%20to%20the%20authors))) and to enable novel coordination modes ([www.emergentmind.com](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2210.10551#:~:text=Abstract%3A%20This%20paper%20demonstrates%20and,attacking%20the%20system%20using%20entangled)).  But crucially, any actual message-like content still propagates via conventional means.  Quantum non-locality offers a radically different infrastructure for distributed cognition – one governed by entangled superposition and correlation – yet it ultimately coexists with the usual causality constraints ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-016-9987-9#:~:text=theory%2C%20%E2%80%9Chas%20a%20very%20surprising,likely%20not%20have%20surprised%20Bell)) ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjp/i2019-12987-0#:~:text=corresponding%20violation%20of%20the%20marginal,superluminal%20communication%20would%20be%20possible)).  Thus, while it enriches and potentially accelerates the patterns of information sharing in a cognitive network, it does so without violating fundamental limits on information flow.
