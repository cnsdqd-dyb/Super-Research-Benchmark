**Electrode spacing in practice:**  Modern intracortical microelectrode arrays generally use electrodes spaced on the order of hundreds of micrometers.  For example, the classic Utah “bed‐of‐nails” array is 10×10 pins on a 4–4.2 mm² base (≈400 µm inter‐electrode pitch, yielding ~6 electrodes/mm²) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9954796/#:~:text=report%20fewer%20neurons%20and%20more,which%20are%20linked%20to%20the)).  Newer high-density probes (e.g. Neuropixels, Neuralink threads) push spacing down towards 50–200 µm (tens to hundreds of electrodes/mm²) to record individual neurons ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11057246/#:~:text=paper%2C%20the%20high%20density%20was,have%20a%20pitch%20within%20200%E2%80%89%CE%BCm)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653975/#:~:text=The%202,sandwiched%20between%20a%20pair%20of)).  In contrast, surface ECoG grids (nonpenetrating) sample local field potentials and use much coarser spacing (≈1–3 mm for macro‐contacts ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7795822/#:~:text=the%20most%20reliable%20and%20final,Clinically%2C%20subdural%20electrode)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7795822/#:~:text=in%20a%203%20%C3%97%206,the%20area%202%E2%80%933%20mm%20from)), or ≈0.5–1 mm for micro‐ECoG ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653975/#:~:text=The%202,sandwiched%20between%20a%20pair%20of))) since cortical signals blur over millimeter scales.  

**Trade‐off – density vs damage:**  Denser arrays give higher spatial resolution (more neurons/neural populations sampled) but at the cost of increased tissue injury.  Insertion of a penetrating electrode kills/dissociates neurons in its immediate vicinity – animal studies report a “dead” radius of ~100–200 µm around each shank ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9954796/#:~:text=report%20fewer%20neurons%20and%20more,which%20are%20linked%20to%20the)).  Thus, if electrodes are too close (much under ~200 µm apart), their injury zones overlap and add little new information while causing heavy scarring.  Empirically, designers often space penetrating electrodes roughly twice the injury radius (e.g. ~400 µm pitch) so that each electrode covers new tissue but total damage zones just touch ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9954796/#:~:text=report%20fewer%20neurons%20and%20more,which%20are%20linked%20to%20the)).  This corresponds to on the order of 5–25 electrodes per mm².  By comparison, pushing spacing below ~100 µm (hundreds/mm²) runs into practical limits – signals begin to correlate/crosstalk and chronic inflammatory responses accumulate ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9954796/#:~:text=report%20fewer%20neurons%20and%20more,which%20are%20linked%20to%20the)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11057246/#:~:text=match%20at%20L507%20Crosstalk%20is,of)).  

**Long‐term stability:**  Chronic studies show that overly dense arrays tend to lose signal quality faster, presumably due to compounded inflammation and glial scarring ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9954796/#:~:text=report%20fewer%20neurons%20and%20more,which%20are%20linked%20to%20the)).  In practice, an intermediate density (hundreds of microns spacing) is preferred for long-term BCIs.  For example, many “high-density” flexible cortical probes use pitches ~50–200 µm ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11057246/#:~:text=paper%2C%20the%20high%20density%20was,have%20a%20pitch%20within%20200%E2%80%89%CE%BCm)), but even these are careful not to saturate every 20–30 µm.  Surface ECoG implants with ~1–3 mm spacing have shown very stable recordings over months with virtually no neuron loss ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653975/#:~:text=The%202,sandwiched%20between%20a%20pair%20of)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7795822/#:~:text=in%20a%203%20%C3%97%206,the%20area%202%E2%80%933%20mm%20from)).  In short, a spacing on the order of a few hundred micrometers (≈10–50 electrodes/mm²) is often cited as a practical compromise: it is fine enough to sample single‐unit activity yet coarse enough to avoid massive tissue trauma ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9954796/#:~:text=report%20fewer%20neurons%20and%20more,which%20are%20linked%20to%20the)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11057246/#:~:text=paper%2C%20the%20high%20density%20was,have%20a%20pitch%20within%20200%E2%80%89%CE%BCm)). 

**Summary:**  There is no single “magic” density, but arrays spaced on the order of 0.2–0.5 mm (100–25 electrodes/mm²) are commonly used in neural prostheses to balance resolution and biocompatibility ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9954796/#:~:text=report%20fewer%20neurons%20and%20more,which%20are%20linked%20to%20the)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11057246/#:~:text=paper%2C%20the%20high%20density%20was,have%20a%20pitch%20within%20200%E2%80%89%CE%BCm)).  Denser (≈≤100 µm) layouts can improve resolution further, but at the cost of overlapping injury zones and cross-talk; sparser (≈1–3 mm) arrays reduce damage but only capture coarse field potentials ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653975/#:~:text=The%202,sandwiched%20between%20a%20pair%20of)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7795822/#:~:text=in%20a%203%20%C3%97%206,the%20area%202%E2%80%933%20mm%20from)).  Thus, aiming for a few‐hundred‐micron pitch is generally optimal for chronic BCI implants.  

**Sources:**  Acute and chronic studies of implanted arrays show neurons die within ~100–200 μm of each electrode ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9954796/#:~:text=report%20fewer%20neurons%20and%20more,which%20are%20linked%20to%20the)), and recent reviews define “high‐density” arrays as having ≤100–200 μm spacing ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11057246/#:~:text=paper%2C%20the%20high%20density%20was,have%20a%20pitch%20within%20200%E2%80%89%CE%BCm)).  ECoG work likewise uses ~1–3 mm spacing to match the ~2–3 mm spread of cortical LFPs ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653975/#:~:text=The%202,sandwiched%20between%20a%20pair%20of)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7795822/#:~:text=in%20a%203%20%C3%97%206,the%20area%202%E2%80%933%20mm%20from)). These data inform the compromise spacing above.
