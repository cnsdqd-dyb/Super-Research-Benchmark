Qualification criteria for merchant onboarding are **pre-set by industry and regulatory bodies**, not arbitrarily chosen during an audit. In practice, an acquirer’s onboarding policy and any audit of that process are based on external standards and internal risk rules.  In other words, merchants must meet the same criteria mandated by payment networks, laws, and security standards – auditors simply verify that these are applied correctly. Key sources of those standards include:

- **Payment-network rules:**  Card schemes like Visa, Mastercard, etc. publish baseline merchant qualification requirements.  These typically include providing full business/legal information (name, address, DBA, locations, etc.), financial-responsibility documentation, signed contracts, etc.  As one industry glossary notes, “card associations – Visa and Mastercard in particular – have baseline requirements that merchants must meet to become network members” ([chargebacks911.com](https://chargebacks911.com/glossary/merchant-qualification-standards/#:~:text=Card%20associations%20%E2%80%93%20Visa%20and,for%20accepting%20that%20card%20brand)).  (For example, Visa’s qualification programs explicitly spell out what mix of technology and compliance a merchant must have to qualify.)  

- **Regulatory KYC/AML laws:**  Governments and international bodies set “know your customer”/anti-money-laundering rules that drive onboarding checks.  For instance, India’s RBI Master Direction on KYC/AML dictates documentary requirements for merchants ([phicommerce.com](https://phicommerce.com/merchant-onboarding-policy/#:~:text=Phi%20Commerce%20has%20framed%20the,and%20obligations%20under%20PMLA%2C%202002)).  Global frameworks (FATF recommendations, EU/US AML directives, etc.) similarly specify merchant identity verification and due-diligence steps.  Audits will reference these rules when checking merchant files.  As one compliance guide explains, onboarding must meet “legal compliance factors, like Anti-Money Laundering (AML), Know Your Customer (KYC)…” and often vary by risk level ([medium.com](https://medium.com/the-regtech-hub/best-practices-in-merchant-onboarding-kyc-and-monitoring-917b592d550d#:~:text=While%20there%20are%20differences%20in,in%20effect%20offering%20unsecured%20loans)).  

- **Industry security standards (PCI etc.):**  The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) and related Payment Application (PA-DSS/PCI P2PE) standards impose technical qualifications.  Merchants’ payment infrastructure and data-handling must comply (e.g. no unauthorized storage of card data).  Onboarding audits will verify evidence of PCI compliance (AOC, scan reports, etc.).  For example, a payment aggregator’s policy explicitly requires reviewing a merchant’s PCI-DSS status as part of onboarding ([www.cashigo.in](https://www.cashigo.in/merchant-onboarding-policy.html#:~:text=,DSS%2C%20as%20applicable)).  In short, meeting PCI security standards is treated as a qualification requirement.  

- **Acquirer/PSP risk policies:**  Each acquirer or PSP sets its own risk-based rules as part of qualification.  This can include banned-merchant lists (prohibited business types), minimum financial thresholds, transaction volume limits, security clauses in contracts, etc.  For example, a merchant-acquirer might maintain an internal “banned businesses” list to block high-risk industries ([www.cashigo.in](https://www.cashigo.in/merchant-onboarding-policy.html#:~:text=The%20Company%20has%20adequate%20systems,both%20cases%20is%20outlined%20below)).  These internal controls become part of the audit checklist.  Auditors ensure that **all** such prescribed criteria – whether from regulation, card rules or the company’s policy – were applied before approving a merchant.  

In summary, audit “qualification standards” come from established rulebooks and laws: card network rulebooks (Visa/Mastercard requirements), financial-regulator directives (AML/KYC mandates, data laws), and recognized industry standards (PCI DSS, etc.), plus any internal underwriting policies.  Auditors determine on-board eligibility by comparing each merchant’s documentation and controls to these pre-defined standards ([chargebacks911.com](https://chargebacks911.com/glossary/merchant-qualification-standards/#:~:text=Card%20associations%20%E2%80%93%20Visa%20and,for%20accepting%20that%20card%20brand)) ([medium.com](https://medium.com/the-regtech-hub/best-practices-in-merchant-onboarding-kyc-and-monitoring-917b592d550d#:~:text=While%20there%20are%20differences%20in,in%20effect%20offering%20unsecured%20loans)) ([www.cashigo.in](https://www.cashigo.in/merchant-onboarding-policy.html#:~:text=,DSS%2C%20as%20applicable)).  Merchants that fail any of the mandated criteria do not qualify.
