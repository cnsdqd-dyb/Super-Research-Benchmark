# Influence of Rewiring Probability on Identity Clustering

In small‐world network models (Watts–Strogatz type), a **rewiring probability** \(p\) controls how many local links are replaced by random “long‐range” links.  At \(p=0\) the network is a regular lattice (high clustering, long path lengths), whereas \(p=1\) yields a completely random graph ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=In%201998%2C%20Watts%20and%20Strogatz,that%20of%20the%20Erd%C5%91s%E2%80%93R%C3%A9nyi%20model)).  This transition has a clear effect on how collective identities organize.  At **low \(p\)** the network remains locally clustered, so agents interact mostly with near neighbors.  Local communities tend to converge internally but stay distinct from each other, leading to **multiple, well‐separated identity clusters**.  In spectral terms, the similarity (or affinity) matrix of identity vectors will have several large eigenvalues and a clear spectral gap between them, reflecting those distinct groups.  By contrast, at **high \(p\)** the network’s random links bridge communities, enabling rapid global mixing.  This fosters consensus (one dominant identity) rather than fragmentation.  Klemm et al. (2003) found that increasing \(p\) in a small‐world network greatly expands the conditions under which the society reaches a single absorbing (homogeneous) state ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=There%20were%20applications%20of%20the,velocity%20to%20reach%20consensus%20is)).  Flache & Macy (2011) similarly note that, under ordinary assimilation assumptions, adding long‐range ties drives cultural convergence and reduces polarization ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254244818_Small_Worlds_and_Cultural_Polarization#:~:text=Building%20on%20Granovetter%27s%20theory%20of,range%20ties)).  In a fully randomized network the identity vectors tend to collapse into one “mega‐cluster,” so spectral clustering would yield one dominant group (or very few clusters).  

For example, one simulation study showed that a purely local (lattice) network produces much greater cultural variability (diverse identities) than a random network ([jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk](https://jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk/16/3/9.html#:~:text=match%20at%20L973%20variable%20than,based%20models%20that%20must)).  In practice, as \(p\) rises spectrally we see the leading eigenvector of the identity‐similarity matrix capture more of the variance (one global identity) and the number of significant eigenmodes falls.  The **spectral cluster structure** thus erodes: first and second eigenvalues may merge, and the usual spectral gaps between clusters shrink.  In short, higher rewiring probability tends to **merge and blur** clusters of identity, whereas lower \(p\) preserves distinct locality‐driven clusters.  This aligns with the fact that small amounts of random linking sharply decrease path lengths (Watts–Strogatz “small‐world” effect) while only modestly reducing local clustering ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=In%201998%2C%20Watts%20and%20Strogatz,that%20of%20the%20Erd%C5%91s%E2%80%93R%C3%A9nyi%20model)) ([jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk](https://jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk/16/3/9.html#:~:text=presented%20by%20Watts%20and%20Strogatz,appears%20from%20the%20local%20level)), so moderate \(p\) already begins to homogenize identities.  Overall, more rewiring means fewer, larger identity clusters in any spectral clustering of the collective identity vectors ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=There%20were%20applications%20of%20the,velocity%20to%20reach%20consensus%20is)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254244818_Small_Worlds_and_Cultural_Polarization#:~:text=Building%20on%20Granovetter%27s%20theory%20of,range%20ties)).  

**Key points:** Low rewiring (near-0) networks are highly clustered and yield many distinct identity clusters in spectral analysis, while high rewiring (near-1) networks mix identities globally so that spectral clustering finds only one or a few broad clusters.  The transition is smooth: even a small increase in \(p\) can drop the mean path length drastically (improving global mixing) before local clustering decreases much ([jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk](https://jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk/16/3/9.html#:~:text=presented%20by%20Watts%20and%20Strogatz,appears%20from%20the%20local%20level)).  Consequently, as \(p\) grows, the eigenstructure of the identity similarity matrix shifts from multiple dominant eigencomponents toward a single dominant eigenvector, reflecting the shift from polarized subgroups to consensus ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=There%20were%20applications%20of%20the,velocity%20to%20reach%20consensus%20is)) ([jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk](https://jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk/16/3/9.html#:~:text=match%20at%20L973%20variable%20than,based%20models%20that%20must)).

**Sources:** The above behavior is well known for Watts–Strogatz networks ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=In%201998%2C%20Watts%20and%20Strogatz,that%20of%20the%20Erd%C5%91s%E2%80%93R%C3%A9nyi%20model)) and has been explicitly observed in cultural diffusion models: higher rewiring promotes uniformity ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=There%20were%20applications%20of%20the,velocity%20to%20reach%20consensus%20is)) ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254244818_Small_Worlds_and_Cultural_Polarization#:~:text=Building%20on%20Granovetter%27s%20theory%20of,range%20ties)) and reduces identity variance ([jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk](https://jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk/16/3/9.html#:~:text=match%20at%20L973%20variable%20than,based%20models%20that%20must)), thus collapsing spectral cluster structure.
