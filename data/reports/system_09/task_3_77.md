# RFM stratification uses data-driven cutoffs. 

In practice, each of the R, F, and M values is divided into tiers (often 3–5 levels) by analyzing the data distribution.  A common method is **quantile binning** – e.g. split the sorted customer list into equal-sized groups (quartiles or quintiles) and assign scores 1–4 or 1–5 accordingly.  For instance, in a quintile scheme the top 20 % of customers by Recency (most recent), Frequency, or Monetary get a score of 5, the next 20 % a score of 4, and so on ([mcpanalytics.ai](https://mcpanalytics.ai/whitepapers/whitepaper-rfm-segmentation#:~:text=Quintile,This%20method%20produces%20125)).  In this way the partition boundaries fall at the 20th, 40th, 60th, 80th percentiles of each R, F, M distribution, ensuring balanced segment sizes ([mcpanalytics.ai](https://mcpanalytics.ai/whitepapers/whitepaper-rfm-segmentation#:~:text=Quintile,This%20method%20produces%20125)).  Practically this produces up to 5×5×5=125 RFM combinations, which are then consolidated into a smaller number of “customer grades” or segments.  

Another approach is **fixed thresholding**: choosing cutoffs based on business rules or cycle times rather than strict percentiles.  For example, one might decide “Recency 5 = last 14 days, score 4 = last 30 days, score 3 = 60 days”, etc., reflecting the normal purchase cycle ([telegra.ph](https://telegra.ph/RFM-Segmentation-for-CRM-A-Marketing-Consultants-Tutorial-10-02#:~:text=example%2C%20Recency%20score%205%20might,cycle%20or%20typical%20consideration%20period)).  Likewise, minimum purchase counts or spend amounts (e.g. “Monetary ≥ $500”) can be set to define top grades.  Fixed thresholds avoid shifting segments if overall purchase volume changes (especially important for seasonal businesses) ([telegra.ph](https://telegra.ph/RFM-Segmentation-for-CRM-A-Marketing-Consultants-Tutorial-10-02#:~:text=Quintiles%20are%20a%20decent%20default,cycle%20or%20typical%20consideration%20period)).  In effect the “partition boundary” for each customer grade is simply those chosen cutoff values.  

In summary, grade boundaries in RFM stratification are not arbitrary but come from the data and business context. Analysts typically use statistical rules (quartiles/quintiles, Pareto 80/20 principle, or mean/median splits) or domain knowledge (e.g. marketing-defined spend levels or time windows) to set the cutoffs ([mcpanalytics.ai](https://mcpanalytics.ai/whitepapers/whitepaper-rfm-segmentation#:~:text=Quintile,This%20method%20produces%20125)) ([telegra.ph](https://telegra.ph/RFM-Segmentation-for-CRM-A-Marketing-Consultants-Tutorial-10-02#:~:text=example%2C%20Recency%20score%205%20might,cycle%20or%20typical%20consideration%20period)).  The goal is to produce meaningful tiers (VIP, high-value, mid, low, etc.) that reflect the underlying customer value distribution. 

**Sources:** Industry best practices document how RFM scores are commonly assigned via quantiles vs. thresholds ([mcpanalytics.ai](https://mcpanalytics.ai/whitepapers/whitepaper-rfm-segmentation#:~:text=Quintile,This%20method%20produces%20125)) ([telegra.ph](https://telegra.ph/RFM-Segmentation-for-CRM-A-Marketing-Consultants-Tutorial-10-02#:~:text=example%2C%20Recency%20score%205%20might,cycle%20or%20typical%20consideration%20period)), noting that quintile-based scoring yields top-20% cutoffs and that fixed cutoffs can be aligned with business cycles and 80/20 effects.
