# Hawking radiation: thermal vs. non-thermal spectrum  
In Hawking’s original analysis, a stationary black hole emits a **thermal (Planckian) spectrum**, with temperature $T_H=\kappa/2\pi$ set by its surface gravity.  In practice, however, the actual emission deviates slightly from an ideal black‐body.  For example, **greybody factors** (wave scattering in the curved geometry) suppress certain frequencies, and including energy conservation/backreaction (the Parikh–Wilczek tunnelling picture) gives  
$$\Gamma(\omega)\sim\exp\Big[-\frac{\omega}{T_H}\Big(1-\frac{\omega}{2M}\Big)\Big],$$  
not a pure $\exp(-\omega/T_H)$ ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4434/3/2/103#:~:text=semiclassical%20approximation%20in%20the%20tunnelling,loss%20paradox%20of%20BH%20evaporation)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4434/3/2/103#:~:text=,Dirac)).  In other words, successive quanta become correlated and the spectrum is only *approximately* thermal.  These small non-thermal corrections are exactly what prevent the emitted radiation from carrying no information.  As one review notes, “Parikh and Wilczek found a probability of emission compatible with a non-thermal spectrum… important to resolve the information loss paradox” ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4434/3/2/103#:~:text=semiclassical%20approximation%20in%20the%20tunnelling,loss%20paradox%20of%20BH%20evaporation)).

## Holographic duality and unitarity constraints  
The **holographic principle** (and AdS/CFT) places a stronger demand: black hole evaporation must be unitary.  In AdS/CFT, a black hole is dual to a pure thermal state of a boundary CFT, which by definition evolves unitarily.  Exact thermal decay would imply information loss, conflicting with the dual CFT’s unitarity ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2218-1997/7/8/297?type=check_update&version=2#:~:text=conformal%20field%20theory,allow%20to%20save%20the%20unitarity)) ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-022-10382-1#:~:text=information%20paradoxes%20which%20are%20further,results%20which%20are%20consistent%20with)).  Indeed, one review emphasizes that a “semi-classical black hole [evaporating into] thermal (Hawking) radiation” violates unitarity ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-022-10382-1#:~:text=information%20paradoxes%20which%20are%20further,results%20which%20are%20consistent%20with)).  The holographic correspondence *rescues* unitarity by forcing additional structure on the emission. In effect, the boundary dual has a **finite, discrete spectrum** and quantum correlations, so the bulk radiation cannot be a featureless continuum.  This enforces subtle correlations among Hawking quanta: the radiation must carry the imprints of the black hole’s microstate.  

- **Correlated emissions:** In holographic models (e.g. large-$N$ gauge theories), the leading-order radiation looks thermal (since most operators obey the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis), but $1/N$ corrections encode small deviations.  These corrections show up as tiny shifts in the spectrum or as entanglement among modes, ensuring complete evaporation is unitary.  
- **Entanglement & the Page curve:** Holographic calculations (via Ryu–Takayanagi, replica “island” saddles, etc.) reproduce a Page curve for the radiation’s entropy.  That means the early-time spectrum can appear nearly thermal, but after the Page time the emitted quanta become increasingly correlated so that information begins to emerge. In short, **thermality breaks down** at subleading order precisely to satisfy boundary unitarity ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-022-10382-1#:~:text=information%20paradoxes%20which%20are%20further,results%20which%20are%20consistent%20with)).  

## Imprint of holographic microstates  
Physically, the non-thermal character reflects the black hole’s microscopic structure, which holography makes explicit.  In string-theory AdS models, a black hole is a condensate of many “fuzzball” or brane states rather than an ideal smooth horizon.  Radiation from each microstate slightly differs, so the *ensemble* appears nearly thermal but the exact spectrum contains microstate-dependent modulations.  Equivalently, a dual CFT state has discrete energy levels, so bulk emission lines become subtly spaced or entangled.  These fine-grained features ensure that the radiation encodes the hidden information.  

In summary, the **departures from a perfect blackbody spectrum** are no accident but a requirement of holographic duality.  They encode the correlations and discreteness that unitarity demands.  As [46] shows, once back-reaction is included the emission probability deviates from $\exp(-\omega/T_H)$, so the black hole “does not emit like a perfect black body” ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4434/3/2/103#:~:text=,Dirac)).  This is exactly what one expects if the black hole’s microstate (in the dual quantum theory) is being gradually “read out” by the radiation.  Thus Hawking radiation’s non-thermal spectrum is the smoking gun of holographic constraints: it signals that the process is ultimately unitary and dualizable to a quantum system without loss of information ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4434/3/2/103#:~:text=semiclassical%20approximation%20in%20the%20tunnelling,loss%20paradox%20of%20BH%20evaporation)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2218-1997/7/8/297?type=check_update&version=2#:~:text=conformal%20field%20theory,allow%20to%20save%20the%20unitarity)).
