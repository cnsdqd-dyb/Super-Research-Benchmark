An effective way to handle **both the intense aerodynamic heating and the structural loads** in a re-entry heat shield is to use an *integrated, load‐bearing ablative panel*.  In practice this means embedding the ablative material into a structural sandwich (for example, packing carbon–phenolic or syntactic ablative foam into a metal or composite honeycomb panel).  The honeycomb (or equivalent core) carries the mechanical loads (compression, shear, impact) while the ablative foam provides the heat shielding.  Such designs combine the two functions: the ablative char absorbs heat, and the honeycomb provides compressive stiffness and thermal anchoring.  For example, the Apollo/Agena Avcoat heat shield was an ablative resin cast into a titanium honeycomb ‘sandwich’ – the honeycomb backbone “adds compressive strength and helps maintain structural integrity” of the ablative foam ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/materials-science/ablative-material#:~:text=inset%20in%20Fig,the%20syntactic%2Fcomposite%20foam%20during%20service)).  Modern analyses likewise show that a honeycomb-backed ablative panel “supports the heat shield from compression buckling loads” under re-entry pressure and impact, improving the coupled thermal‐mechanical response ([www.scribd.com](https://www.scribd.com/document/940565054/1-s2-0-S1359836821006752-main#:~:text=insulator,and%20Ogasa%C2%AD%20induced%20by)). 

In the same spirit, current TPS research uses multi-directional fiber reinforcement to achieve this integration.  For example, NASA’s 3D‐woven ablative composite (“3DMAT”) for the Orion capsule is a resin-impregnated, three‐dimensionally woven carbon/quartz preform.  This architecture provides high interlaminar strength and structural rigidity *and* high heat resistance: the outer face can withstand the shock heating while the inner surface remains relatively cool ([technology.nasa.gov](https://technology.nasa.gov/patent/TOP2-244#:~:text=woven%20fiber%20preform%20fully%20densified,The)).  In short, a **structural sandwich or 3D‐reinforced panel filled with an ablative matrix** is considered the best design to handle the coupled aerothermal and thermal-stress fields in re-entry.  By uniting the heat-shield and load-bearing roles in one component, these integrated ablative TPS configurations manage both heating and stress more safely and efficiently than a simple non-load-bearing ablative panel.
