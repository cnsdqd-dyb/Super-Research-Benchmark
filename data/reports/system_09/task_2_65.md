# Indications for Laser Photocoagulation in Diabetic Retinopathy

- **Proliferative DR (PDR) with high-risk features** – Panretinal laser (scatter PRP) is indicated once neovascularization reaches “high-risk” criteria (e.g. ≥1/4 disc area of new vessels on the disc, or new vessels with associated vitreous hemorrhage) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=Laser%20photocoagulation%20is%20indicated%20in,blood%20sugars%2C%20poor%20patient%20follow)).  PRP in this setting greatly reduces the risk of severe vision loss (as shown by the DRS/ETDRS trials) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=Laser%20photocoagulation%20is%20indicated%20in,blood%20sugars%2C%20poor%20patient%20follow)).  
- **Clinically Significant Macular Edema (CSME)** – Focal/grid laser to leaking microaneurysms near the fovea is indicated in CSME to prevent moderate vision loss ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=Laser%20photocoagulation%20is%20indicated%20in,blood%20sugars%2C%20poor%20patient%20follow)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=demonstrated%20that%20treatment%20prevented%20moderate,months%20after%20the%20initial%20treatment)). (The ETDRS found that laser halved the rate of ≥3-line vision loss in treated eyes versus controls ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=demonstrated%20that%20treatment%20prevented%20moderate,months%20after%20the%20initial%20treatment)).)  
- **Severe NPDR (pre-proliferative DR) with risk factors** – Patients with extensive NPDR (e.g. the “4–2–1” rule) are usually observed, but PRP may be offered if additional high-risk conditions are present.  For example, the DRS suggested laser in eyes with severe NPDR in *both* eyes, or with severe retinal ischemia, pregnancy, renal failure or other factors that accelerate retinopathy ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850856/#:~:text=photocoagulation%20they%20did%20not%20recommend,might%20accelerate%20the%20course%20of)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=Laser%20photocoagulation%20is%20indicated%20in,blood%20sugars%2C%20poor%20patient%20follow)).  In these cases the likelihood of progression to PDR justifies treating early.  
- **Mild/Moderate NPDR** – Laser is generally *deferred* when only mild-to-moderate NPDR is present, because the short-term risk of vision loss is low.  The ETDRS showed that elective scatter laser in moderate NPDR (before PDR develops) did not improve 5-year visual outcomes (2.6% vs 3.7% severe loss for immediate vs deferred PRP) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850856/#:~:text=fifth,4)).  In practice we spare PRP in these eyes to avoid unnecessary collateral damage.  
- **Balancing benefit versus field loss** – Panretinal laser predictably narrows the peripheral visual field (Fong et al. found objective field defects in ~50% of PRP-treated eyes) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850856/#:~:text=The%20most%20common%20complications%20of,46%5D%7D%20A)).  It can also worsen or precipitate macular edema.  Thus laser is used only when the threat of vision loss from disease progression is high enough to outweigh these risks ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850856/#:~:text=The%20most%20common%20complications%20of,46%5D%7D%20A)).  In summary, we reserve laser for high-risk PDR (and CSME) cases where preventing blindness clearly outweighs the risk of peripheral field loss ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=Laser%20photocoagulation%20is%20indicated%20in,blood%20sugars%2C%20poor%20patient%20follow)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850856/#:~:text=The%20most%20common%20complications%20of,46%5D%7D%20A)).  

**Sources:** Large trials and guidelines (DRS/ETDRS) underpin these indications.  Their recommendations emphasize treating PDR and CSME aggressively, but avoiding early scatter laser in moderate NPDR so as not to incur avoidable visual‐field loss ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850856/#:~:text=photocoagulation%20they%20did%20not%20recommend,might%20accelerate%20the%20course%20of)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=Laser%20photocoagulation%20is%20indicated%20in,blood%20sugars%2C%20poor%20patient%20follow)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2636123/#:~:text=demonstrated%20that%20treatment%20prevented%20moderate,months%20after%20the%20initial%20treatment)).
