The “human‐in‐the‐loop” point in automated review grading is not magic – it’s an explicit policy parameter set by the platform’s moderation system.  In practice each review is scored by algorithms (for spam, abuse, authenticity, etc.), and the product team chooses a cutoff or confidence band where one-on-one review is needed.  In other words, the system defines “safe” and “unsafe” score thresholds: very high confidence (e.g. clearly legit or clearly abusive) may be auto-approved or auto-removed, while anything in the gray zone is routed to a human moderator ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bugfree-ai_machinelearning-contentmoderation-mlops-activity-7411108104379146241-Qg0H#:~:text=In%20content%20moderation%20systems%2C%20remember%3A,is%20key%20for%20building%20reliable)) ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=violence,legal%20risk%20and%20user%20trust)). 

These thresholds are tuned to balance trade‐offs: for example, raising the threshold (becoming more strict) cuts down on false negatives (e.g. missed bad reviews) but risks more false positives (legitimate reviews getting caught), and vice versa.  Teams usually set the cutoffs based on policy goals, legal/regulatory requirements and historical data.  In fact, modern moderation frameworks treat thresholds as “policy in action” – they adjust them until the system hits target precision/recall and cost metrics ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=violence,legal%20risk%20and%20user%20trust)) ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=violence,legal%20risk%20and%20user%20trust)).  (Some even A/B test different thresholds on real traffic to find the optimal balance.) 

In concrete terms, an automated pipeline might specify a score band: for instance, an NSFW classifier example is having “>0.85 → block, 0.6–0.85 → blur/flag” ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=,keep%20engineers%2C%20auditors%20and%20regulators)).  Similarly, a product review filter would use its own calibrated score bands.  Reviews that trigger “red flags” – e.g. too many identical phrases, rapid bursts of reviews from a single reviewer, lots of down-votes/flags, etc. – effectively push their score past the human‐review threshold.  As one expert notes, spotting such red‐flag patterns “increases the likelihood that [a review] will be…deprioritized for human review” over just letting it through ([tracefuse.ai](https://tracefuse.ai/blog/does-amazon-filter-out-bad-product-reviews/#:~:text=These%20red%20flags%20don%E2%80%99t%20guarantee,blatant%20and%20subtle%20manipulation%20tactics)). 

In short, the human‐intervention point is set by the platform’s trust & safety policy.  It’s typically a confidence cutoff in the review‐grading classifier (or a flag‐count trigger), chosen to hit the desired error‐rate trade‐off.  That choice is continually refined – e.g. by logging outcomes and tuning – so that human reviewers only handle borderline cases while the bulk of obvious reviews are auto-processed ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bugfree-ai_machinelearning-contentmoderation-mlops-activity-7411108104379146241-Qg0H#:~:text=In%20content%20moderation%20systems%2C%20remember%3A,is%20key%20for%20building%20reliable)) ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=violence,legal%20risk%20and%20user%20trust)).

**Sources:** Industry practices in automated moderation (which apply equally to review grading) explain that human review is consulted when a classifier’s score falls into an uncertain range.  By design, content pipelines “set thresholds…deciding when to block, blur, escalate or let content through,” balancing false positives/negatives, legal risk and user trust ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=violence,legal%20risk%20and%20user%20trust)).  In effect, the threshold policy – often tuned via analytics or A/B tests ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bugfree-ai_machinelearning-contentmoderation-mlops-activity-7411108104379146241-Qg0H#:~:text=In%20content%20moderation%20systems%2C%20remember%3A,is%20key%20for%20building%20reliable)) – determines exactly when a review is sent for manual inspection.
