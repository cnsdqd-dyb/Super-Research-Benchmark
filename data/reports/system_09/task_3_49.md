# Influence of ocean heat-transport parameterization on ENSO forecast skill

- **Improved mixing → higher ENSO skill.**  When ocean heat redistribution (turbulent mixing, wave‐driven mixing, etc.) is parameterized more realistically, seasonal ENSO forecasts become much more accurate.  In one study, introducing refined mixing schemes (including surface-wave and internal-wave mixing) raised the Niño 3.4 anomaly correlation and cut the forecast RMSE by ∼20% at 4–5 month lead time ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/14/3363#:~:text=results%20illustrate%20that%20the%20anomaly,year%20lead%20time%20and%20RMSE)).  In effect, better heat transport increases the predictability of ENSO amplitude.  
- **More realistic ENSO amplitudes.**  Enhanced heat-redistribution schemes also yield ENSO amplitudes closer to observed values.  For example, the above modified model predicted Niño 3.4 event magnitudes that matched reality much better; the original (coarser) scheme **overestimated** ENSO amplitude ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/14/3363#:~:text=Over%20longer%20leads%2C%20and%20in,year%20lead%20time%20and%20RMSE)).  In general, mis-specified mixing can lead to biased ENSO strengths (either too strong or too weak) and thus poorer forecast skill.  
- **Reduced bias via calibration.**  Tuning the mixing parameters (e.g. calibrating vertical diffusivity through data assimilation) improves the equatorial thermocline and background SST.  This produces better initial conditions for forecasts.  For instance, estimating the ocean’s background diffusivity reduced thermocline errors and enhanced global vertical mixing; as a result the Niño 3.4 forecasts showed higher correlation with observations at longer leads ([gmd.copernicus.org](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/1651/2024/index.html#:~:text=reveal%20a%20notable%20reduction%20in,our%20forecasts%20incorporating%20parameter%20estimation)).  In summary, improving the ocean heat-transport parameterization – or correctly calibrating it – consistently raises seasonal ENSO prediction skill and produces more accurate event amplitudes ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/14/3363#:~:text=results%20illustrate%20that%20the%20anomaly,year%20lead%20time%20and%20RMSE)) ([gmd.copernicus.org](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/1651/2024/index.html#:~:text=reveal%20a%20notable%20reduction%20in,our%20forecasts%20incorporating%20parameter%20estimation)).  

**References:**  Studies of enhanced mixing parameterizations (Yang et al. 2022) and calibrated diffusivity (Shen et al. 2024) demonstrate that more realistic ocean heat redistribution significantly increases Niño 3.4 forecast skill (higher ACC, lower RMSE) and yields ENSO amplitudes much closer to observed values ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/14/3363#:~:text=results%20illustrate%20that%20the%20anomaly,year%20lead%20time%20and%20RMSE)) ([gmd.copernicus.org](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/1651/2024/index.html#:~:text=reveal%20a%20notable%20reduction%20in,our%20forecasts%20incorporating%20parameter%20estimation)).
