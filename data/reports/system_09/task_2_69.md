# rTMS in Depression – Indications and Dosing Balancing Efficacy vs. Seizure Risk

rTMS is primarily indicated for patients with moderate-to-severe major depression (unipolar or bipolar) who have not adequately responded to conventional therapies. In practice this means *treatment-resistant depression* (e.g. failure of ≥1–2 antidepressant trials) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=Depression%20Acute%2FUnipolar%20%20,Yes)).  FDA‐cleared protocols (e.g. NeuroStar, Magstim, BrainsWay) generally target the **left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex** with high-frequency pulses in such patients ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=significantly%20higher%20for%20the%20use,75)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=Depression%20Acute%2FUnipolar%20%20,Yes)).  The goal is to surpass the neural “therapeutic threshold” for clinical effect while staying within established safety margins for seizure prevention. 

- **Patient selection:**  rTMS has strong evidence in acute unipolar depression and bipolar depression (especially treatment-resistant cases) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=significantly%20higher%20for%20the%20use,75)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=Depression%20Acute%2FUnipolar%20%20,Yes)). Standard guidelines list high-frequency (10–20 Hz) left-DLPFC stimulation as a first-line protocol for these patients ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=significantly%20higher%20for%20the%20use,75)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=Depression%20Acute%2FUnipolar%20%20,Yes)).  Deep-coil (H1) or bilateral protocols are alternative options, but all require similar safety considerations.  Contraindications (e.g. implanted metal, epilepsy history) are screened out before treatment to keep seizure risk low ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5846193/#:~:text=match%20at%20L967%20cortex%2C%20which,clonic%20movement%20pattern)).

- **Stimulation threshold (dose):**  Each patient’s **resting motor threshold (RMT)** is measured – the minimum intensity eliciting a visible motor response in hand muscles ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=MT%20is%20defined%20as%20the,29)).  Therapy is then delivered at or above this RMT, ensuring the magnetic field is strong enough to activate cortical neurons (often 100–120% of RMT) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5846193/#:~:text=Magnetic%20field%20intensity%20relative%20to,2%20seconds)).  In pivotal trials, for example, rTMS was applied at 120% RMT to the left DLPFC (10–18 Hz, 4–8 second trains, 20–30 second inter-train intervals, ~3000 pulses/session) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5846193/#:~:text=Magnetic%20field%20intensity%20relative%20to,2%20seconds)).  Using ≥100% RMT “knocks” the cortex past the excitability threshold needed for antidepressant effect.  These settings represent the **therapeutic response threshold** – strong enough to produce clinical benefit (e.g. higher response/remission odds ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=significantly%20higher%20for%20the%20use,75))) but not excessively high.

- **Safety limits (seizure boundaries):** International safety guidelines (Wassermann 1998, Rossi 2009, updated 2021) define **maximum safe combinations** of frequency, intensity and train duration to prevent seizures.  For example, at 10 Hz and 110% RMT, a single train should not exceed ~50 pulses (5 seconds) with at least a 5–10 second pause ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=must%20be%20considered,in%20case%20the%20combination%20of)).  In standard 120% RMT protocols, 10 Hz trains are typically limited to ~4 seconds (40 pulses) with 20–30 second inter-train intervals ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=must%20be%20considered,in%20case%20the%20combination%20of)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5846193/#:~:text=Magnetic%20field%20intensity%20relative%20to,2%20seconds)).  Staying within these bounds is crucial: adherence to the Rossi safety tables virtually eliminates seizures.  One survey of >300,000 clinical rTMS sessions found only *4 seizures* occurred when stimulation parameters were within published safety limits (≈0.002% risk) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=reported%20an%20estimated%20318%2C560%20TMS,frequency%20rTMS%20delivered)).  In contrast, exceeding frequency/duration limits or treating patients with known risk factors (e.g. active epilepsy, proconvulsant drugs, recent sleep deprivation) markedly raises seizure risk ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5846193/#:~:text=match%20at%20L967%20cortex%2C%20which,clonic%20movement%20pattern)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=reported%20an%20estimated%20318%2C560%20TMS,frequency%20rTMS%20delivered)).  

**Summary:** In depression, rTMS is used in carefully selected patients (often treatment-resistant) with stimulation **just above the motor threshold** – enough to induce a robust neuroplastic effect but constrained by safety tables.  Conventional protocols (e.g. 10 Hz, 4 sec @120% RMT) intentionally “balance” efficacy and safety: they exceed the minimum activation threshold for antidepressant response yet remain well under the empirically derived seizure‐risk limits ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5846193/#:~:text=Magnetic%20field%20intensity%20relative%20to,2%20seconds)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=must%20be%20considered,in%20case%20the%20combination%20of)).  When these guidelines are followed and high-risk patients are screened out, clinical studies report an extremely low seizure incidence (on the order of one in 100,000 sessions ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=reported%20an%20estimated%20318%2C560%20TMS,frequency%20rTMS%20delivered))).  In short, rTMS protocols for depression aim to reach a **therapeutic threshold** for mood improvement while strictly observing “safety boundary” rules on intensity and train parameters to keep seizures exceedingly rare ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=must%20be%20considered,in%20case%20the%20combination%20of)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=reported%20an%20estimated%20318%2C560%20TMS,frequency%20rTMS%20delivered)).

**Sources:** Established rTMS guidelines and consensus statements for MDD, which recommend 10–20 Hz left-DLPFC at ~100–120% RMT ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=significantly%20higher%20for%20the%20use,75)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10096206/#:~:text=Depression%20Acute%2FUnipolar%20%20,Yes)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5846193/#:~:text=Magnetic%20field%20intensity%20relative%20to,2%20seconds)); and safety reviews reporting that following these dosing tables yields very low seizure risk ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=must%20be%20considered,in%20case%20the%20combination%20of)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879417/#:~:text=reported%20an%20estimated%20318%2C560%20TMS,frequency%20rTMS%20delivered)).
