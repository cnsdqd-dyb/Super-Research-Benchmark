# Network structure and biodiversity‐loss thresholds

Biodiversity‐loss **thresholds** – the critical fraction of species or habitat removal at which an ecosystem collapses – are determined by a network’s connectivity.  In network models of ecosystems (e.g. habitat patch networks or species interaction webs), **clustering coefficient** (a measure of local “triangles” or closed loops) turns out to be a *key indicator* of robustness.  In contrast, **modularity** (degree of community compartmentalization) is usually *not* directly used in threshold formulas.  In fact, empirical studies show that higher clustering generally makes a network **more** robust (pushing the collapse threshold higher), whereas higher modularity often does not.  For example:  

- **Clustering boosts robustness.**  Heer et al. (2020, 2021) and others have shown that networks with higher clustering coefficients tend to withstand more loss before breaking apart ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7575089/#:~:text=The%20resilience%20of%20networks%20against,of%20the%20relative%20density%20of)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X20307470#:~:text=networks%2C%20a%20network%E2%80%99s%20average%20clique,reliable%20indicator%20of%20this%20robustness)).  In habitat‐network models, adding links to maximize clustering vastly increased network robustness.  One study notes that “a network’s clustering coefficient…is a particularly reliable indicator” of metapopulation robustness against habitat loss ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X20307470#:~:text=networks%2C%20a%20network%E2%80%99s%20average%20clique,reliable%20indicator%20of%20this%20robustness)).  In other words, highly clustered networks have many alternate paths, so the **predicted loss‐threshold is higher** (it takes more species or habitat removal to collapse the system) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7575089/#:~:text=The%20resilience%20of%20networks%20against,of%20the%20relative%20density%20of)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X20307470#:~:text=networks%2C%20a%20network%E2%80%99s%20average%20clique,reliable%20indicator%20of%20this%20robustness)).  

- **Modularity has little direct role (and can lower robustness).**  By contrast, network **modularity** (the extent of distinct communities or compartments) is not usually an explicit predictor in threshold models.  In fact, some modeling studies found that **more modular** ecological networks were **less** robust – i.e. more vulnerable to perturbation ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5720727/#:~:text=Specifically%2C%20adaptive%20networks%20with%20modularity,it%20is%20to%20external%20perturbations)).  (Nuwagaba et al. 2017 observed that “the more modular a network is, the less robust it is to external perturbations” in their food‐web simulations ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5720727/#:~:text=Specifically%2C%20adaptive%20networks%20with%20modularity,it%20is%20to%20external%20perturbations)).)  This means that high compartmentalization does *not* automatically raise the collapse threshold and can even correlate with a lower threshold.  

In summary, **clustering coefficient strongly influences** biodiversity‐loss threshold predictions (networks with higher clustering survive longer) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7575089/#:~:text=The%20resilience%20of%20networks%20against,of%20the%20relative%20density%20of)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X20307470#:~:text=networks%2C%20a%20network%E2%80%99s%20average%20clique,reliable%20indicator%20of%20this%20robustness)).  On the other hand, **network modularity as such is not a key factor in threshold formulas** – it is connectance, degree distribution and clustering that matter most.  Indeed, recent analyses replace complex simulations with “indicator‐based” predictions, finding clustering coefficient to be one of the best single metrics of ecosystem collapse point ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7575089/#:~:text=The%20resilience%20of%20networks%20against,of%20the%20relative%20density%20of)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X20307470#:~:text=networks%2C%20a%20network%E2%80%99s%20average%20clique,reliable%20indicator%20of%20this%20robustness)), whereas modularity is largely omitted or even negatively correlated with robustness ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5720727/#:~:text=Specifically%2C%20adaptive%20networks%20with%20modularity,it%20is%20to%20external%20perturbations)).  Thus, clustering (not modularity) is the network property that most influences predicted biodiversity‐loss thresholds.  

**Sources:** Modern studies of ecological network robustness find clustering coefficient to be a reliable proxy for resilience ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7575089/#:~:text=The%20resilience%20of%20networks%20against,of%20the%20relative%20density%20of)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X20307470#:~:text=networks%2C%20a%20network%E2%80%99s%20average%20clique,reliable%20indicator%20of%20this%20robustness)), whereas increased modularity tends to correlate with reduced robustness ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5720727/#:~:text=Specifically%2C%20adaptive%20networks%20with%20modularity,it%20is%20to%20external%20perturbations)). These imply that clustering – by creating many local loops – raises the collapse threshold, but pure community modularity does not.
