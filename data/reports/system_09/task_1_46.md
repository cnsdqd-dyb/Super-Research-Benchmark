In an MDP formulation the AI’s responsibility‐assignment matrix is treated as the system’s “state,” and a decision (action) corresponds to reassigning responsibilities.  The AI then uses standard dynamic‐programming or reinforcement‐learning methods (e.g. value iteration or policy iteration) to update this assignment.  Concretely, one defines a reward (or performance) function over responsibility allocations and iteratively solves the Bellman optimality equations.  Over many trials the MDP solver converges to an **optimal stationary policy** – i.e. a fixed mapping from each assignment‐state to an action – that maximizes long‐run reward ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/mathematics/markov-decision-process#:~:text=The%20policy%20iteration%20method%20will,be%20performed%20simply%20and%20quickly)).  

Crucially, under this stationary policy the induced Markov chain over assignment‐states becomes ergodic.  In an ergodic MDP the chain has a **unique steady‐state (stationary) distribution** of states that does not depend on the initial state ([ai.stackexchange.com](https://ai.stackexchange.com/questions/27196/what-is-ergodicity-in-a-markov-decision-process-mdp#:~:text=,0)).  In other words, after enough transitions the probability of each assignment configuration stabilizes.  These limiting probabilities (the “limiting‐state probabilities” of the Markov chain) are constants ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/mathematics/markov-decision-process#:~:text=where%20,substituting%20for%20%2C%20we%20obtain)), meaning each element of the responsibility matrix has settled into a fixed long‐run frequency.  This stationary distribution of states is precisely the **dynamic equilibrium** of the assignment matrix. 

In summary: by modeling responsibility assignments as an MDP and performing policy (or value) iteration, the system iteratively adjusts task allocations until it finds a fixed (stationary) policy ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/mathematics/markov-decision-process#:~:text=The%20policy%20iteration%20method%20will,be%20performed%20simply%20and%20quickly)).  Under that policy the Markov chain of assignments converges to a steady‐state distribution, yielding a stable (“equilibrium”) responsibility matrix ([ai.stackexchange.com](https://ai.stackexchange.com/questions/27196/what-is-ergodicity-in-a-markov-decision-process-mdp#:~:text=,0)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/mathematics/markov-decision-process#:~:text=where%20,substituting%20for%20%2C%20we%20obtain)). 

**Sources:** Standard MDP theory shows that infinite‐horizon policy iteration converges to an optimal _stationary_ policy ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/mathematics/markov-decision-process#:~:text=The%20policy%20iteration%20method%20will,be%20performed%20simply%20and%20quickly)), and in an ergodic MDP the state distribution under any fixed policy converges to a unique steady‐state (limiting) distribution ([ai.stackexchange.com](https://ai.stackexchange.com/questions/27196/what-is-ergodicity-in-a-markov-decision-process-mdp#:~:text=,0)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/mathematics/markov-decision-process#:~:text=where%20,substituting%20for%20%2C%20we%20obtain)), which embodies the dynamic equilibrium of the system.
