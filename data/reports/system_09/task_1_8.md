Byzantine‐fault‐tolerant (BFT) consensus is fundamentally constrained by the same impossibility and lower‐bound results that govern distributed agreement.  In practice this means you **cannot arbitrarily increase throughput and fault‐tolerance without paying in latency or communication overhead**.  In fact, classic results show any worst-case BFT protocol requires Ω(n²) message exchanges (Dolev–Reischuk bound) to agree ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09123#:~:text=Byzantine%20Agreement%20is%20a%20key,In%20this%20paper%20we)), and at least a constant number of network round-trips (on the order of f, the max faults) before committing.  In other words, consensus throughput is upper‐bounded by the network latency and per‐node processing rate: adding more nodes only increases the O(n²) “chatter” without raising the slowest node’s capacity ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/1/19/xml#:~:text=Regarding%20throughput%20limitations%2C%20each%20node,to%20processing%20and%20bandwidth%20constraints)).  

In short, the **“limit”** is the Pareto‐frontier of these trade-offs: every gain in raw TPS or fault‐security requires extra communication rounds or risk (longer finality), and vice versa.  This is essentially the well‐known blockchain “trilemma” or CAP trade‐off: you can’t maximize throughput, low latency, and high security (decentralization) all at once ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/1/19/xml#:~:text=The%20Blockchain%20Trilemma%20posits%20that,the%20expense%20of%20the%20third)) ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09123#:~:text=Byzantine%20Agreement%20is%20a%20key,In%20this%20paper%20we)).  No BFT scheme can beat these bounds without weakening safety.  Thus the theoretical cap is set by the consensus lower bounds and network constraints – empirically one finds that peak TPS ≲ (block size)/(≈2–3×network–latency) and grows no faster than linear in n when communication is optimized.  Pushing past that frontier inevitably sacrifices security or requires extra trust assumptions.  

**Answer:**  In effect, the ultimate limit is the inherent **consensus trade‐off frontier** – as formalized by FLP/CAP impossibilities and Ω(n²) message‐complexity bounds ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09123#:~:text=Byzantine%20Agreement%20is%20a%20key,In%20this%20paper%20we)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/1/19/xml#:~:text=The%20Blockchain%20Trilemma%20posits%20that,the%20expense%20of%20the%20third)). It means you cannot arbitrarily increase throughput/security without incurring extra latency or message overhead.
