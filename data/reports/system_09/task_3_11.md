The trade‐off between dose-dependent anti-inflammatory efficacy and HPA‐axis (adrenal) suppression is essentially captured by the **therapeutic index/safety margin** of the steroid.  In other words, researchers aim to maximize the anti-inflammatory “benefit” at doses well below those that significantly suppress ACTH/cortisol.  This has led to the search for **“dissociated” glucocorticoids** (also called selective GR modulators, SEGRA) which preferentially engage the anti-inflammatory (transrepressive) pathways while minimizing endocrine (transactivating) effects ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11448149/#:~:text=pharmacology%20is%20the%20development%20of,the%20glucocorticoid%20receptor%20that%20may)) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15202726/#:~:text=made%2C%20with%20the%20discovery%20that,are%20currently%20pursuing%20this%20goal)).  In practice, this means designing GR ligands that retain strong NF-κB/AP‐1 inhibition (anti-inflammatory) but have little activation of classic GRE-driven genes.  Such agents can achieve potent inflammation control (“on-target” transrepression) at doses that only minimally trigger feedback suppression of the HPA axis ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11448149/#:~:text=pharmacology%20is%20the%20development%20of,the%20glucocorticoid%20receptor%20that%20may)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC314167/#:~:text=216348%20should%20have%20a%20lower,useful%20tool%20compounds%20for%20further)).  In short, what “balances” the two is the compound’s **therapeutic window (therapeutic ratio)** – a measure of how much drug is needed for efficacy versus how much causes adrenal suppression.  By increasing this ratio (for example via dissociated or selective GR agonists), one maximizes anti-inflammatory dose‐dependence while keeping adrenal suppression risk low ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11448149/#:~:text=pharmacology%20is%20the%20development%20of,the%20glucocorticoid%20receptor%20that%20may)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC314167/#:~:text=216348%20should%20have%20a%20lower,useful%20tool%20compounds%20for%20further)). 

**Key point:**  The selective GR‐modulator approach has shown that you can separate many side effects from anti-inflammation.  Studies of compounds like ZK216348 demonstrate anti-inflammatory effects equivalent to prednisone but with fewer metabolic side-effects ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC314167/#:~:text=216348%20should%20have%20a%20lower,useful%20tool%20compounds%20for%20further)).  (Notably, however, ACTH/cortisol suppression itself still tracks with transrepression, so even these drugs can suppress the HPA axis at higher doses ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC314167/#:~:text=Regarding%20ACTH%20suppression%2C%20an%20advantage,there%20is%20a%20significant%20difference)).)  

**Answer:**  In practice, this balance is managed by optimizing the **therapeutic index** via selective GR agonists (dissociated steroids) that uncouple anti-inflammatory transrepression from unwanted transactivation (e.g. HPA-axis feedback) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11448149/#:~:text=pharmacology%20is%20the%20development%20of,the%20glucocorticoid%20receptor%20that%20may)) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15202726/#:~:text=made%2C%20with%20the%20discovery%20that,are%20currently%20pursuing%20this%20goal)).  

**Sources:** The concept of improving the steroid *therapeutic ratio* – achieving “marked antiinflammatory effects” with “minor … endocrine responses” – is well discussed in the literature ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11448149/#:~:text=pharmacology%20is%20the%20development%20of,the%20glucocorticoid%20receptor%20that%20may)).  Likewise, it is noted that selective GR agonists (SEGRA) can yield classical anti-inflammatory efficacy with fewer systemic side effects (“improved therapeutic index” over standard GCs) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15202726/#:~:text=made%2C%20with%20the%20discovery%20that,are%20currently%20pursuing%20this%20goal)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC314167/#:~:text=216348%20should%20have%20a%20lower,useful%20tool%20compounds%20for%20further)). These and related studies explicitly describe how dissociated GR ligands are used to balance inflammation control against HPA-axis suppression.
