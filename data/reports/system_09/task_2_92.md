The “intensity” or strength of authentication used (e.g. single‐factor vs. multi‐factor, biometric, etc.) is driven by the assessed risk and sensitivity of the account/action.  In practice systems use *risk‐based* or *adaptive authentication*: they examine contextual signals (geolocation, device trust, time of access, unusual behavior, number of failed attempts, etc.) and elevate the required proof when the situation is riskier.  For example, high‐sensitivity operations or access from a new/untrusted device often trigger extra steps (like sending an OTP or requiring a hardware key), whereas routine logins from familiar devices may only need a simple password.  Standards and best practices (e.g. NIST 800-63) explicitly call for extra controls on anomalous logins (e.g. “an unexpected geolocation…might prompt additional risk‐based controls” ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/#:~:text=At%20all%20AALs%2C%20indicators%20of,impact%20or%20change%20the%20AAL))).  In short, the hierarchy of authentication strength is determined by the account’s *risk profile* and *policy requirements*: low‐risk scenarios use lower‐intensity auth, and higher‐risk or high‐privilege scenarios require stronger, multi‐factor authentication ([concertium.com](https://concertium.com/multi-factor-authentication/#:~:text=By%20evaluating%20factors%20such%20as,robust%20defense%20against%20unauthorized%20access)) ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/#:~:text=At%20all%20AALs%2C%20indicators%20of,impact%20or%20change%20the%20AAL)). 

**Key determinants include:** the sensitivity or value of the protected resource (administrative or financial actions demand more rigorous auth), the user’s role/privileges, and real‐time risk signals such as unusual location, device, or behavior.  Modern systems employ real‐time risk scoring (using factors like login time, IP geolocation, device fingerprint, etc.) to *tailor* the authentication requirement – e.g. accessing sensitive data from a new device might automatically require an extra code from an authenticator app ([concertium.com](https://concertium.com/multi-factor-authentication/#:~:text=By%20evaluating%20factors%20such%20as,robust%20defense%20against%20unauthorized%20access)).  Thus the overall “hierarchy” of authentication intensity is set by these risk and context factors, ensuring stronger proof when and where it’s needed. 

**Sources:** Official guidelines and security best‐practices describe this risk‐based / adaptive approach to MFA ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/#:~:text=At%20all%20AALs%2C%20indicators%20of,impact%20or%20change%20the%20AAL)) ([concertium.com](https://concertium.com/multi-factor-authentication/#:~:text=By%20evaluating%20factors%20such%20as,robust%20defense%20against%20unauthorized%20access)).
