FinFET scaling forces a trade‐off between making the channel *small enough* to suppress leakage and *large enough* to carry current.  In practice this means carefully co‐optimizing fin geometry, gate stack, doping and threshold biasing.  For example, **fin cross section (width and height):** narrower, thinner fins give excellent electrostatic control (lower DIBL and SS) but have smaller channel area and suffer strong quantum confinement (raising the effective Vt and lowering I<sub>on</sub>). Taller fins or more fins in parallel boost drive current (larger total channel area), but if made too large or tall may begin to partially deplete, weakening gate control and re‐introducing SCE (and at some point lithography/resist limits).  In fact, FinFET designers note that fin *width* and gate *length* come in discrete multiples of the process fin pitch, so one typically “gangs” fins to hit a desired W, and cannot freely dial W/L as in planar MOSFETs ([thundertech.tech.blog](https://thundertech.tech.blog/2019/08/04/all-about-finfet/#:~:text=The%20finite%20granularity%20of%20the,Thus%20FinFETs%20have%20a%20significant)).  Thus the W vs. H (and number of fins) must be chosen to balance on‐current vs. off‐leakage.

- **Gate insulator (EOT/high-κ):** A thinner equivalent-oxide-thickness (EOT) strengthens gate control (reducing SCE) but also increases gate tunneling leakage.  In practice high-κ dielectrics are used so that the physical oxide can be thicker (lower tunneling) at the same EOT.  However there is a hard limit: if EOT is made too small, direct tunneling (through the gate or even source–drain barrier) shoots up.  Thus one must balance aggressive EOT scaling against allowable leakage.  (This is why FinFETs use high-κ/metal‐gate stacks to push EOT as low as possible without intolerable gate leakage.)

- **Channel doping vs. work‐function:**  Unlike planar devices, FinFET channels are typically undoped or very lightly doped to maximize mobility and minimize random dopant fluctuations ([thundertech.tech.blog](https://thundertech.tech.blog/2019/08/04/all-about-finfet/#:~:text=FinFET%20has%20a%20lower%20DIBL,less%20effective%2C%20making%20alternatives%20necessary)) ([www.techdesignforums.com](https://www.techdesignforums.com/blog/2012/06/12/doping-gives-finfets-threshold-control/#:~:text=At%20first%20sight%2C%20the%20finFET,increases%20process%20complexity%E2%80%9D%20said%20Lin)).  This means the device is effectively fully depleted, which is good for leakage but *prevents* using body-bias tricks to raise Vt – the threshold is essentially set by fin geometry and gate work-function.  In practice multi-metal gates or specially engineered gate work-functions are used to set different n/p Vt’s.  Introducing any channel doping can indeed help suppress off‐state leakage (by raising Vt and making the channel less abrupt), but it hurts on‐current.  In experiments, adding just enough boron to shift Vt by ~150 mV cut the drive current by ~10% ([www.techdesignforums.com](https://www.techdesignforums.com/blog/2012/06/12/doping-gives-finfets-threshold-control/#:~:text=Using%20boron%20doping%20to%20provide,Work%20on%20long)) (and heavy implantation produced even worse Ion/Ioff degradation ([www.techdesignforums.com](https://www.techdesignforums.com/blog/2012/06/12/doping-gives-finfets-threshold-control/#:~:text=Process%20engineers%20will%20have%20to,Vt%20rather%20than%20cut%20it))).  In short, doping and fin-thickness are the knobs for leakage control – using them pushes up scattering and variability, which degrades drive.

- **Quantum effects:**  As gate lengths reach the few‐nm regime, quantum mechanics intrudes.  Thin fins confine carriers in one dimension, raising subband energies and effectively increasing Vt – an unintended “self-increase” of threshold that reduces drive.  Also, carriers begin to transit quasi‐ballistically (especially in 7–10 nm FinFETs), so velocity saturation limits further gains in I<sub>on</sub> from length reduction ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354560393_Review_of_FinFET_Devices_and_Perspective_on_Circuit_Design_Challenges#:~:text=...%20The%20quasi,mobility%20enhancement%20techniques%20such%20as)).  Perhaps more critically, very short gates and thin oxides lead to noticeable quantum tunneling (direct source–drain tunneling or gate leakage).  To counter this, designers may need to keep a minimum fin width and oxide thickness (albeit with high-κ) even as gate length shrinks.  In effect, there’s a lower limit to how much the channel can be pinched off: too thin and leakage via tunneling dominates, too undoped and Vt might float.

- **Source/Drain engineering:**  For ultra-short L, the source/drain doping profile and extension length become critical.  Deep implants lower series resistance (boosting I<sub>on</sub>) but also create intense fields that can increase punch‐through or band‐to‐band tunneling leakage.  Some designs use asymmetrical drain extensions (wide‐drain structures) to relieve peak fields and reduce leakage while preserving on-current density.

In summary, every attempt to boost drive current (for speed) tends to weaken electrostatic control (raising leakage), and vice-versa.  The solution is a finely tuned compromise: choose fin dimensions and fin count to get desired I<sub>on</sub> without over-thinning the fin, use high-κ/metal‐gate stacks to shrink EOT just enough, and accept light channel doping or work-function engineering to tweak threshold.  Each of these knobs is constrained by scattering, variability or quantum limits.  Thus FinFET gate-length scaling is a multi-dimensional balancing act – tightening short-channel control (via geometry, dielectrics or doping) while avoiding excessive reduction of the channel’s ability to carry current ([thundertech.tech.blog](https://thundertech.tech.blog/2019/08/04/all-about-finfet/#:~:text=The%20finite%20granularity%20of%20the,Thus%20FinFETs%20have%20a%20significant)) ([www.techdesignforums.com](https://www.techdesignforums.com/blog/2012/06/12/doping-gives-finfets-threshold-control/#:~:text=At%20first%20sight%2C%20the%20finFET,increases%20process%20complexity%E2%80%9D%20said%20Lin)).
