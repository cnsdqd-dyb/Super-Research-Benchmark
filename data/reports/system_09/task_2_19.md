The consensus in recent studies is that **no single “always‐offload” or “always‐local” rule is optimal** – instead the device should use an *adaptive, multi‐objective* policy that explicitly trades off delay vs. energy.  In practice this means computing exactly as much as needed locally so as to meet the real‐time deadline, and offloading the rest if that saves battery.  Equivalently, one can minimize a combined cost function (for example, \(C= \alpha\cdot\text{(latency)} + \beta\cdot\text{(energy)}\)) to pick the optimal split of computation.  For instance, Ko et al. (2019) show that an IoT node can decide how many CNN layers to run locally vs. offload by minimizing a weighted latency+energy cost ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/19/4/835#:~:text=%5Cbegin,array)).  In other words, the device processes up to the point where doing more would cost too much energy relative to the delay saved, and then transmits the intermediate result to an edge server.  This **partial‐offloading** strategy – dynamically choosing the offload boundary – achieves far better overall performance than the extremes of “all local” or “all remote” ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/19/4/835#:~:text=find%20the%20optimal%20n%20parameter,and%20the%20IoT%20edge%20server)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/19/4/835#:~:text=%5Cbegin,array)).  

Taking this further, modern schemes use learning or optimization to adapt to network and battery conditions.  For example, recent work employs a deep Q‐network (DQN) that continuously learns whether the next task should be processed locally or pushed to the fog/cloud so as to jointly minimize response time and energy drain ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/15/3090#:~:text=energy%20consumption,This%20integration%20facilitates%20more%20efficient)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/15/3090#:~:text=we%20demonstrate%20the%20superior%20effectiveness,regard%20to%20environmental%20stability%20and)).  In simulations this kind of RL‐based policy cut both latency and battery usage by roughly 30–50% compared to static heuristics ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/15/3090#:~:text=energy%20consumption,This%20integration%20facilitates%20more%20efficient)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/15/3090#:~:text=we%20demonstrate%20the%20superior%20effectiveness,regard%20to%20environmental%20stability%20and)).  In short, the **best** strategy is a *context‐aware offloading policy* – typically implemented as a cost‐optimization or learning algorithm – that dynamically balances local compute time against transmit energy to meet real‐time deadlines while preserving battery life ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/19/4/835#:~:text=%5Cbegin,array)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/15/3090#:~:text=we%20demonstrate%20the%20superior%20effectiveness,regard%20to%20environmental%20stability%20and)).

**Answer:** An adaptive, multi‐criteria offloading policy (often expressed as minimizing a weighted sum of latency and energy) – e.g. partially compute on‐device and offload the rest based on a learned or optimized cost model – yields the best trade‐off between real‐time responsiveness and battery life ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/19/4/835#:~:text=%5Cbegin,array)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/15/3090#:~:text=we%20demonstrate%20the%20superior%20effectiveness,regard%20to%20environmental%20stability%20and)).
