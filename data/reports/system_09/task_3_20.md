The key point is that normalizing migration flows into a random‐walk (Markov) matrix $P$ fundamentally changes the graph’s spectrum (eigenvalues/eigenvectors) and hence how “tight” the communities appear under spectral clustering.  In a migration network, $P$ has entries $P_{ij}\propto f_{ij}/\sum_k f_{ik}$ (flow from node $i$ to $j$), so spectral clustering on $P$ effectively groups nodes into **“macrostates” (clusters)** where random‐walk transitions are fast within a cluster and slow between clusters ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=By%20using%20spectral%20clustering%20methods%2C,grained)).  In the ideal case of a **nearly block‐diagonal** $P$ (i.e. each cultural region mostly exchanges migrants internally), the dominant eigenvectors of $P$ are *constant on each block* ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=eigenvectors%20%20of%20such%20matrices%2C,This%20simplex%20structure%20gets)).  Equivalently, the $\ell$ largest eigenvalues (one per cluster) are all ≈1, and there is a large eigengap after the $\ell$-th eigenvalue ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=eigenvectors%20%20of%20such%20matrices%2C,This%20simplex%20structure%20gets)) ([snap-stanford.github.io](https://snap-stanford.github.io/cs224w-notes/network-methods/spectral-clustering#:~:text=,ordered%20by%20descending%20magnitude)).  In this regime spectral clustering is **very stable**: each traditional cultural community appears as a clean cluster (the eigenvector “rows” form the corners of a simplex) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=possible%20because%20X%24X%24%20contains%20the,can%20be%20assigned%20to%20each)). 

By contrast, if migration flows **strongly link** different cultural groups, the random-walk matrix $P$ loses its near-block structure.  The eigenvectors then mix across groups and the eigenvalues “fan out” away from 1 – in other words, the spectral gap closes.  Spectral clustering no longer finds sharp communities, and cluster assignments become unstable or fuzzy.  In practice one sees that adding cross‐cluster migration edges perturbs the dominant eigenspace, so small changes in the data or algorithm can flip nodes between groups.  Equivalently, the usual eigengap criterion (choosing $k$ to maximize the gap) fails when flows shrink that gap ([snap-stanford.github.io](https://snap-stanford.github.io/cs224w-notes/network-methods/spectral-clustering#:~:text=,ordered%20by%20descending%20magnitude)). 

In summary, the migration-based random-walk matrix impacts stability as follows: if flows mostly **reinforce existing cultural blocks**, $P$ is nearly decomposable and spectral clusters are robust and well-separated ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=eigenvectors%20%20of%20such%20matrices%2C,This%20simplex%20structure%20gets)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=By%20using%20spectral%20clustering%20methods%2C,grained)).  But if flows **bridge different cultures**, the Markov chain mixes faster across communities, reducing the eigengap and making the spectral clustering of the traditional cultural network much less stable ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=possible%20because%20X%24X%24%20contains%20the,can%20be%20assigned%20to%20each)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=By%20using%20spectral%20clustering%20methods%2C,grained)).

**Key points:** Spectral clustering finds coarse “macrostates” with slow inter-cluster transition rates ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=By%20using%20spectral%20clustering%20methods%2C,grained)).  A nearly block‐diagonal flow matrix yields constant-on-block eigenvectors and a large eigengap (stable clustering) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=eigenvectors%20%20of%20such%20matrices%2C,This%20simplex%20structure%20gets)) ([snap-stanford.github.io](https://snap-stanford.github.io/cs224w-notes/network-methods/spectral-clustering#:~:text=,ordered%20by%20descending%20magnitude)), whereas heavy cross‐cluster migration breaks this structure, collapsing the eigengap and destabilizing the clustering ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=possible%20because%20X%24X%24%20contains%20the,can%20be%20assigned%20to%20each)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377042724000402#:~:text=By%20using%20spectral%20clustering%20methods%2C,grained)).
