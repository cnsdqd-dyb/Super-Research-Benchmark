# Dragonfly and Other Low-Diameter Topologies

For HPC clusters the best solution is a **low‐diameter, high‐radix** network (e.g. the *Dragonfly* family of topologies, and its optimized variant *Slim Fly*).  In a Dragonfly network, routers are grouped with dense interconnections and a few high-speed “global” links between groups.  This keeps the network diameter very small – it literally “bypasses higher levels of the tree” and cuts out extra hops ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-431X/12/4/86/xml#:~:text=topology%20,30%5D.%20An%20infinite)) – so inter-node latency stays low even as the cluster scales.  In fact, Dragonfly networks are designed to give “high bandwidth and low latency” communication while supporting huge node counts ([mindovix.com](https://mindovix.com/articles/understanding-dragonfly-network-topology/#:~:text=Dragonfly%20network%20topology%20offers%20several,noteworthy%20advantages)). 

- **Very low latency:**  Because each pair of nodes needs only a few hops, Dragonfly has far lower average latency than a deep fat-tree or torus at scale.  Studies note that by clustering routers into fully-linked groups the architecture “eliminates some latency points” in the path ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-431X/12/4/86/xml#:~:text=topology%20,30%5D.%20An%20infinite)).  In practice this means message latency grows very little as nodes are added.  For example, Bhatia et al. emphasize that Dragonfly “offers high bandwidth and low latency” even for large, performance-sensitive jobs ([mindovix.com](https://mindovix.com/articles/understanding-dragonfly-network-topology/#:~:text=Dragonfly%20network%20topology%20offers%20several,noteworthy%20advantages)). 

- **Massive scalability:**  Dragonfly uses high-port-count (high-radix) routers and a shallow 2–3 level hierarchy.  This lets it interconnect thousands of nodes without the explosion of switches seen in fat-trees.  In fact, evaluations show a large Dragonfly network can be built with **roughly half the cost and number of switches** of a comparable fat-tree while matching its throughput ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-431X/12/4/86/xml#:~:text=While%20a%20Fat,in%20terms%20of%20possible%20configurations)).  In short, you can scale up cluster size (adding entire groups of nodes) with minimal impact on latency or cost. 

- **High throughput (data efficiency):**  The many parallel links between groups give Dragonfly an enormous bisection bandwidth.  With load-balanced, equal-cost routes everywhere, the network “accommodates large volumes of data traffic effectively” ([mindovix.com](https://mindovix.com/articles/understanding-dragonfly-network-topology/#:~:text=leads%20to%20reduced%20latency.%20,demands%20of%20modern%20computational%20tasks)).  In other words, its capacity grows with the cluster so that data-intensive applications can use the full bandwidth of the machine.  The effect is very efficient data movement: throughput remains high and queues low even under heavy communication. 

- **Built-in fault tolerance:**  Dragonfly’s symmetric, richly-connected structure provides multiple disjoint paths between any two endpoints.  Rigorous analysis shows that a properly arranged Dragonfly is *2-edge-Hamilton-connected* – essentially containing multiple redundant Hamiltonian cycles ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0743731525000620#:~:text=In%20this%20paper%2C%20we%20prove,disjoint%20path%20coverable%20with)).  Practically speaking, this means many alternate routes exist if links or routers fail.  Combined with adaptive routing, the result is **strong resilience**: losing a few links or nodes barely degrades performance.  Indeed, the original Dragonfly design boasted “superior resilience and fault tolerance” alongside its bandwidth and latency advantages ([mindovix.com](https://mindovix.com/articles/understanding-dragonfly-network-topology/#:~:text=Dragonfly%20network%20topology%20offers%20several,noteworthy%20advantages)).

- **Next-generation variants:**  New design proposals extend these ideas.  For example, ETH Zurich’s *Slim Fly* topology mathematically minimizes diameter.  Slim Fly achieves near-optimal (near Moore-bound) diameter, further cutting latency.  Studies report that Slim Fly networks outperform traditional fat-tree and even Dragonfly in **latency, bandwidth, resiliency, and cost**, making them extremely attractive for exascale-class systems ([www.microsoft.com](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/video/network-topologies-for-large-scale-datacenters-its-the-diameter-stupid/#:~:text=introduce%20a%20high,art%20networks)).  Like Dragonfly, Slim Fly provides many paths (high reliability) and huge aggregate bandwidth, but with even tighter hop counts.

In summary, a **Dragonfly‐like low-diameter topology** best reconciles the latency-vs-scalability trade-off in HPC clusters.  It achieves minimal inter-node hops (hence low latency) and very high bisection bandwidth, while its rich connectivity naturally provides data efficiency and fault tolerance ([mindovix.com](https://mindovix.com/articles/understanding-dragonfly-network-topology/#:~:text=Dragonfly%20network%20topology%20offers%20several,noteworthy%20advantages)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-431X/12/4/86/xml#:~:text=topology%20,30%5D.%20An%20infinite)).  This design is widely regarded as the optimal way to scale to thousands of nodes without sacrificing performance or reliability.  

**Answer:**  Use a *Dragonfly* (or similar low‐diameter, high‐radix) interconnect topology – e.g. Slim Fly – which minimizes hops for low latency, maximizes bisection bandwidth for data efficiency, and offers many redundant paths for fault tolerance ([mindovix.com](https://mindovix.com/articles/understanding-dragonfly-network-topology/#:~:text=Dragonfly%20network%20topology%20offers%20several,noteworthy%20advantages)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0743731525000620#:~:text=In%20this%20paper%2C%20we%20prove,disjoint%20path%20coverable%20with)).
