In IIT, an experience is modeled as a high‐dimensional “qualia shape” in probability‐space – each axis represents a possible system state and the pattern of cause–effect repertoires defines the contours of the experience ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713405/#:~:text=relationships%20generated%20by%20such%20systems,consciousness%20associated%20with%20the%20experience)).  The **information content** of that shape (its “height” or integrated information Φ) is computed using relative‐entropy (KL‐divergence) measures.  For example, the **effective information** generated by a mechanism in state *x* is defined as the Kullback–Leibler divergence between its *actual* cause–effect repertoire and the *maximum‐entropy* (uniform) repertoire ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713405/#:~:text=is%20the%20maximum%20entropy%20,0%7D%28mech%29%2C%20while)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713405/#:~:text=previous%20states%20,also%20known%20as%20Kullback)).  Likewise, the **integrated information** Φ of a system in state *x* is defined as the KL‐divergence between the joint repertoire of the whole system and the product of the repertoires of its partitioned parts ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713405/#:~:text=comparison%20is%20made%20with%20the,k%7D%20%207)).  In other words, Φ measures how much the system’s state “reduces uncertainty” beyond what its parts do individually – a form of irreducible information that one might loosely call the “entropy” of the subjective experience.

Viewed through information geometry, each repertoire is a point on a statistical manifold of distributions, and KL‐divergence acts like a squared distance between points ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5187746/#:~:text=the%20perspective%20of%20information%20geometry%2C,is%20quantified%20by%20KL%20divergence)).  In this picture, “geometric” integrated information (Φ_G) is obtained by projecting the full joint distribution onto the closest *factorized* (disconnected) manifold and taking the minimal KL‐distance ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5187746/#:~:text=information%2C%20called%20geometric%20integrated%20information,G)).  Equivalently, IIT characterizes experience by how far its probability‐distribution point is from maximal entropy or from separable distributions.  Thus, although IIT does not explicitly label it “subjective experience entropy,” the theory quantifies the informational complexity of experience via these entropy‐like KL measures in its geometric framework ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713405/#:~:text=is%20the%20maximum%20entropy%20,0%7D%28mech%29%2C%20while)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713405/#:~:text=comparison%20is%20made%20with%20the,k%7D%20%207)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5187746/#:~:text=the%20perspective%20of%20information%20geometry%2C,is%20quantified%20by%20KL%20divergence)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5187746/#:~:text=information%2C%20called%20geometric%20integrated%20information,G)).

**Answer:** In IIT, the “entropy” of a conscious experience is captured by relative‐entropy measures on the space of possible system states.  Specifically, intrinsic information (effective information) and integrated information (Φ) are defined as Kullback–Leibler divergences between the actual cause/effect repertoires and maximum‐entropy or factorized distributions ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713405/#:~:text=is%20the%20maximum%20entropy%20,0%7D%28mech%29%2C%20while)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713405/#:~:text=comparison%20is%20made%20with%20the,k%7D%20%207)).  Information geometry then interprets these KL‐divergences as distances on a probability manifold: e.g. Φ can be seen as the minimal KL‐distance from the system’s full distribution to a disconnected (independent) model ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5187746/#:~:text=the%20perspective%20of%20information%20geometry%2C,is%20quantified%20by%20KL%20divergence)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5187746/#:~:text=information%2C%20called%20geometric%20integrated%20information,G)).  In this sense the “entropy” of subjective experience is quantified by how much the system’s cause–effect structure reduces uncertainty (information gain) beyond randomness, as measured by these geometrical KL‐divergences.
