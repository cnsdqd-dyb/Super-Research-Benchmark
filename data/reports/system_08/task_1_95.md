# Continuous Time: A Classical Assumption  
In classical physics, time is treated as a smooth continuum. We assume it can be divided indefinitely and that events can occur at arbitrarily small time intervals. This view is built into Newtonian mechanics and Einstein’s relativity, where time is represented by the real numbers – a continuous line. Standard quantum mechanics and general relativity both use continuous time as a foundational backdrop ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronon#:~:text=While%20time%20is%20a%20continuous,citation%20needed)). Our everyday intuition (“time flows like a river”) reflects this innate assumption of continuity.

## Quantum Gravity and Discrete Spacetime  
Many approaches to quantum gravity challenge the idea of a fundamentally continuous spacetime. The prevailing expectation is that the **smooth geometric fabric of spacetime breaks down at the Planck scale**, about 10^(-35) meters in length and 10^(-43) seconds in time ([philosophyofphysics.lse.ac.uk](https://philosophyofphysics.lse.ac.uk/articles/10.31389/pop.17#:~:text=Many%20workers%20tackling%20the%20problem,it%20is%20discrete%20at%20the)) ([universe-review.ca](https://universe-review.ca/R15-23-noncomm.htm#:~:text=and%20becomes%20a%20froth%20of,BTW%2C%20the%20quantum%20foam%20in)). At such scales, wild quantum fluctuations – the so-called *quantum foam* – make the notion of a well-defined point in space or an instant in time meaningless ([universe-review.ca](https://universe-review.ca/F15-particle12.htm#:~:text=By%20combining%20the%20laws%20of,to%20the%20latest%20idea%20in)) ([universe-review.ca](https://universe-review.ca/R15-23-noncomm.htm#:~:text=and%20becomes%20a%20froth%20of,BTW%2C%20the%20quantum%20foam%20in)). In other words, **spacetime itself may be granular or “pixelated” at extreme magnifications**, rather than a continuous manifold. This idea appears in theories like **loop quantum gravity**, where space is composed of discrete “chunks” (quantized volumes/areas), and in **causal set theory**, which models spacetime as a finite set of events with a partial ordering instead of a continuum ([philosophyofphysics.lse.ac.uk](https://philosophyofphysics.lse.ac.uk/articles/10.31389/pop.17#:~:text=Many%20workers%20tackling%20the%20problem,it%20is%20discrete%20at%20the)). If spacetime is fundamentally discrete, then time as we know it would not flow smoothly, but increment in tiny steps.

## Planck Time: A Fundamental Tick  
A direct consequence of spacetime discreteness is the concept of a **minimum time interval**. Often this minimal “quantum of time” is assumed to be on the order of the *Planck time*, ~5.4×10^(-44) seconds. You cannot meaningfully divide time into slices smaller than this scale. **This hypothetical indivisible unit of time is sometimes called a “chronon,”** which represents the notion that time might be made of discrete ticks rather than a continuum ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronon#:~:text=A%20chronon%20is%20a%20proposed,decomposable%20unit%20of%20time)). In simple terms, if the chronon exists, it is the smallest conceivable duration – an instant that cannot be further split. Between one tick and the next, there is no “in-between” time. While **standard physics treats time as continuous**, some physicists have suggested that combining quantum mechanics and gravity may require time to be *quantized* in this way ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronon#:~:text=While%20time%20is%20a%20continuous,citation%20needed)).

## Implications of Discrete Time  
Adopting a discrete spacetime (and hence discrete time) forces us to rethink several fundamental assumptions:  

- **No Infinite Divisibility:** We normally presume that no matter how short a duration is, we can always find a shorter one. Discrete time implies a limit – once you reach the fundamental tick (e.g. Planck time), you cannot subdivide further. Time would not be a dense line of instants but a sequence of “frames.” This **challenges the continuum-based mathematics of physics**, since calculus relies on taking intervals *dt* to zero. At a fundamental level we’d replace differential equations with difference equations – using tiny finite steps in time ([physics.stackexchange.com](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/805978/does-the-existence-of-planck-units-suggest-discontinuity-in-time-and-space#:~:text=2,And%20sums%20instead%20of%20integrals)) ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=95851#:~:text=In%20this%20paper%20we%20will,laws%20of%20mechanics%E2%80%9D%20or%20%E2%80%9Cfundamental)). Physical evolution would occur in jumps from one state to the next each tick, rather than in an uninterrupted flow ([www.scirp.org](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=95851#:~:text=In%20this%20paper%20we%20will,laws%20of%20mechanics%E2%80%9D%20or%20%E2%80%9Cfundamental)).

- **‘Ticking’ Evolution vs. Flowing Time:** Instead of time flowing smoothly, it would progress in incremental jumps. **Nothing changes until the next discrete tick occurs**, at which point the system updates. One vivid description from loop quantum gravity is that *“time flows not like a river but like the ticking of a clock,”* with each tick on the order of the Planck time ([universe-review.ca](https://universe-review.ca/F15-particle12.htm#:~:text=Just%20as%20space%20is%20defined,and%20the%20nodes%20become%20lines)). In this view, **every location in the quantum spacetime has its own tiny clock**, ticking when a fundamental event or “quantum move” happens there ([universe-review.ca](https://universe-review.ca/F15-particle12.htm#:~:text=Just%20as%20space%20is%20defined,and%20the%20nodes%20become%20lines)). The passage of time is thus a succession of indivisible moments, much like frames in a movie reel, rather than a continuous camera pan.

- **Emergent Continuity:** At human scales, these microscopic ticks are so incredibly small that time **appears continuous**. Just as a fluid seems smooth even though it’s made of molecules, continuous time would be an **emergent approximation** of many tiny discrete intervals ([universe-review.ca](https://universe-review.ca/F15-particle12.htm#:~:text=to%20the%20energy%20levels%20of,discrete%20atoms%20at%20small%20scale)). In other words, the familiar continuity of time could be recovered as an aggregate effect when zillions of Planck-time ticks blur together. Our equations of motion would effectively smooth out, and calculus would become valid again for large timescales because the discrete steps are imperceptibly tiny.

- **Reinterpreting Motion and Change:** With discrete time, motion would be reconceived as a series of instantaneous hops. A particle doesn’t slide continuously along a path but occupies a position at one tick and the next position at the following tick. This could bypass classical paradoxes of continuity – for example, **Zeno’s paradox** (which questions how motion is possible if one can divide time and distance infinitely) might not even arise if time (and space) cannot be divided below a minimum unit. More profoundly, the idea of velocity and acceleration at the Planck scale might need redefinition if ∆t cannot be smaller than one tick. Concepts like “during an infinitesimal moment” would give way to “during the last tick.”

- **Time as an Emergent Property:** In some quantum gravity approaches, time isn’t fundamental at all but **emerges from the order of discrete events**. For instance, in a spin-network (loop quantum gravity) picture, what we call time is basically the **sequence of changes in the network’s state** ([universe-review.ca](https://universe-review.ca/F15-particle12.htm#:~:text=Just%20as%20space%20is%20defined,and%20the%20nodes%20become%20lines)). The network’s evolution – addition or removal of links/nodes in discrete steps – defines a before and after, creating the sense of time. This reshapes the notion of time from an independent flowing backdrop to something more like a bookkeeping of changes. Reality becomes a “clockwork” of quantum events ticking everywhere, and time is just our way of counting those ticks.

- **Challenges with Relativity:** A fundamental **time quantum might seem to conflict with the smooth Lorentz symmetry** of relativity. In Einstein’s theory, there is no universal tick; time is relative and can stretch or shrink continuously depending on the frame. Introducing a fixed tiny interval (like a universal lattice of time points) risks picking out a preferred frame or maximum frequency, which would break relativity’s principles. Quantum gravity theories that invoke discrete time must therefore recover *Lorentz invariance* in the long run. Some models achieve this by randomizing the discrete structure (as in causal set theory) so there’s no observable regular grid to break symmetry. Indeed, experiments have tested for signs of a grainy time: for example, observations of distant gamma-ray bursts found **no energy-dependent differences in light travel time that a naive time lattice would predict**, strongly limiting such effects ([physics.stackexchange.com](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/805978/does-the-existence-of-planck-units-suggest-discontinuity-in-time-and-space#:~:text=scale%20is%20a%20scientific%20hypothesis,depend%20on%20the%20photons%27%20energy)). This suggests that if time is discrete, the theory has to be subtle – preserving the illusion of continuity and smooth symmetry for all practical purposes ([physics.stackexchange.com](https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/805978/does-the-existence-of-planck-units-suggest-discontinuity-in-time-and-space#:~:text=predict%20that%20the%20time%20needed,depend%20on%20the%20photons%27%20energy)).

## Reshaping Our Perspective  
In summary, the idea of discrete spacetime in quantum gravity forces a radical shift in how we view time. We move from believing in an ever-divisible, flowing timeline to imagining time as a staccato sequence of “moments.” The **innate assumption of continuity gives way to a picture of tiny temporal quanta**. At the Planck scale, talk of continuity may simply not apply – time and space might come in fundamental chunks, and what we experience as smooth time is like the grainy picture of a digital screen viewed from far away. This doesn’t invalidate continuous time in everyday physics – much as thinking of matter as atoms doesn’t invalidate continuum fluid mechanics at large scales. Instead, it adds a deeper layer: **continuous time would be an emergent approximation of an underlying discrete reality** ([universe-review.ca](https://universe-review.ca/F15-particle12.htm#:~:text=to%20the%20energy%20levels%20of,discrete%20atoms%20at%20small%20scale)). Embracing this idea means updating our theories and intuitions: calculus and differential equations become tools valid only beyond the smallest scales, and concepts of motion, causality, and even what it means for time to “pass” are recast in terms of fundamental intervals and events. 

Ultimately, discrete spacetime in quantum gravity suggests that time’s familiar flow is not a fundamental given, but a derived phenomenon – a gentle illusion born out of countless quantum ticks. This reshapes our innate assumptions by revealing that continuity, however natural it seems, may not be built into the bedrock of reality. Instead, **time at the smallest scale could be digital** (in ticks) even though it appears analog to us, prompting physicists to rethink the foundation of temporal physics from the ground up.
