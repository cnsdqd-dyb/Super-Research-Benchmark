# Social Network Rewiring and Identity Cluster Structure

In urbanization models where individuals or groups are characterized by multi-dimensional **collective identity vectors**, the pattern of social ties (and how often they are “rewired” to random contacts) strongly affects how those identities cluster. The **rewiring probability** (e.g. in a small-world network model) controls the balance between localized clustering and long-range mixing of the social network. This, in turn, determines whether distinct identity groups persist or merge into a larger collective identity when we perform spectral clustering on the identity vectors.

## Low Rewiring: Fragmented Identity Clusters 
With a low rewiring probability (network connections remain mostly local), the social network is highly clustered and segregated. Individuals primarily interact within their immediate neighborhood or community, so each local cluster tends to reach its *own* consensus identity that differs from other clusters ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254244818_Small_Worlds_and_Cultural_Polarization#:~:text=each%20other%20ove%20r%20time,consensus%20can%20not%20be%20reached)). In other words, multiple distinct cultural/identity domains persist in the population – the spectral clustering of identity vectors would reveal **many well-separated clusters** corresponding to these local communities. In the extreme case of completely disconnected or only-neighbor networks, diversity remains high (each community retains a unique identity) as there are no bridging ties to homogenize them ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254244818_Small_Worlds_and_Cultural_Polarization#:~:text=earlier%20generation%20of%20studies%20predicted,the%20inevitable%20collapse%20of)). The result is a fragmented collective identity structure with **sharply defined clusters** in identity space.

## High Rewiring: Integrated (Homogeneous) Identity Structure 
At high rewiring probabilities (many random long-range ties), the network becomes a **small-world** or more random graph. Distant communities get linked by “short-cut” connections, allowing information and cultural influence to spread globally ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254244818_Small_Worlds_and_Cultural_Polarization#:~:text=Building%20on%20Granovetter%27s%20theory%20of,found%20that%20when%20these%20negative)). This enhanced connectivity promotes **cultural diffusion and integration**, meaning previously separate identity groups begin to influence each other and converge. As a result, the identity vectors of individuals become more homogeneous across the network, collapsing formerly distinct clusters into fewer, larger clusters – often even a single dominant cluster if consensus is reached ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254244818_Small_Worlds_and_Cultural_Polarization#:~:text=Building%20on%20Granovetter%27s%20theory%20of,found%20that%20when%20these%20negative)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=There%20were%20applications%20of%20the,velocity%20to%20reach%20consensus%20is)). In spectral clustering terms, increasing the rewiring probability tends to **reduce the number of identity clusters** and blur the divisions between them. Essentially, long-range social links enable one collective identity (or a few broad identities) to emerge, smoothing out local differences. Empirical studies of Axelrod-style cultural models confirm that introducing random network ties greatly enlarges the parameter range where a single unified culture (an “ordered” absorbing state) will form ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4336/16/2/18#:~:text=There%20were%20applications%20of%20the,velocity%20to%20reach%20consensus%20is)). Thus, a high rewiring (highly networked urban society) produces a more **cohesive collective identity** with spectral clusters that are larger and less isolated, reflecting an integrated identity structure across the population.
