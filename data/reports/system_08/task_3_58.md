# Effective Population Size and Genetic Load  
**Effective population size (N<sub>e</sub>)** is a key factor in how genetic drift and natural selection balance in a population. In large populations (high N<sub>e</sub>), natural selection can more effectively purge deleterious mutations, keeping the **genetic load** (the burden of harmful mutations reducing fitness) relatively low. In contrast, small populations (low N<sub>e</sub>) experience stronger genetic drift, which can allow mildly harmful alleles to increase in frequency or even fix, since purifying selection becomes less efficient ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=Finally%2C%20we%20discuss%20genomic%20load%2C,a%20first%20approximation%2C%20that%20the)). As a result, **smaller N<sub>e</sub> tends to increase genetic load**, because deleterious variants are less likely to be weeded out. Highly deleterious mutations might still be purged or lost, but many slightly deleterious ones can accumulate when drift outweighs weak selection ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=effective%20determinant%20of%20allelic%20fate,Charlesworth)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=alleles,small%E2%80%90effect%20recessive%20deleterious%20alleles%20rise)). This accumulation of mutations lowers average fitness and can even accelerate extinction risk if severe ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=of%20purifying%20selection%2C%20so%20that,27)). In summary, **larger effective populations maintain lower genetic load through efficient selection**, whereas **shrinking populations face rising genetic load due to drift and inbreeding effects**.  

- **Large N<sub>e</sub>**: Strong selection removes many harmful alleles, keeping genetic load low.  
- **Small N<sub>e</sub>**: Genetic drift dominates, allowing deleterious alleles to persist and increase, raising the genetic load ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=Finally%2C%20we%20discuss%20genomic%20load%2C,a%20first%20approximation%2C%20that%20the)).  
- **Inbreeding in small populations**: Increases **expression of recessive deleterious alleles**, causing inbreeding depression and contributing to the realized genetic load ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=proportion%20of%20strongly%20deleterious%20variants,while%20simultaneously%20considering%20deeper%20demographic)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=small%20populations%2C%20highly%20deleterious%20mutations,Overall)).  

# Range Contraction, Climate Change, and Genetic Load  
**Climate-change-induced range contraction** typically forces species into smaller, fragmented habitats, effectively reducing population sizes. As habitat shrinks, local populations become **isolated and smaller**, leading to a drop in N<sub>e</sub> and often a loss of genetic diversity ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=its%20ability%20to%20adapt%20to,Loeschcke%2C%202012%3B%20Leroy%20et%20al)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8210801/#:~:text=populations%20exhibit%20a%20more%20rapid,2018)). This reduction in effective population size has direct consequences for genetic load. **Enhanced genetic drift in these climate-stressed, contracting populations allows deleterious mutations to accumulate** more readily ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9314787/#:~:text=accumulation%20of%20mutational%20load%20in,Roff)) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9314787/#:~:text=thousands%20of%20generations%20,best%20to%20rear%20edges%20of)). Studies and theory predict that populations at **range edges or in climate refugia** – which are usually the ones surviving after contraction – often have **lower N<sub>e</sub> and higher genetic load** compared to central, stable populations ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=roughly%20assess%20future%20trends,mutations%20to%20accumulate%20on%20the)) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=change%3A%20the%20fate%20of%20small,flow%2C%20dispersal%20and%20load%20that)). In other words, as climate change shrinks a species’ range, the remaining populations carry a heavier mutational burden.

Climate-driven range contraction can therefore create a **vicious cycle**: smaller populations accumulate more harmful mutations (increasing genetic load), which reduces individual fitness and adaptive potential. This lowered fitness can further depress population growth or survival, compounding the threats from climate change. For example, edge populations of plants and animals have been observed with **elevated genomic load and reduced fitness**, likely due to past bottlenecks and persistent drift at low N<sub>e</sub> ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=genetic%20bottlenecks,113)) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=range,102)). Over time, **rising genetic load in contracting populations** can lead to **inbreeding depression, loss of resilience, and higher extinction risk** if the trend isn’t reversed ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=of%20purifying%20selection%2C%20so%20that,27)) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=population%20size%20and%20history%20can,change%20is%20inversely%20related%20to)).  

In summary, **effective population size strongly mediates genetic load during climate-induced range contractions**. When climate change squeezes a species into a smaller range, the accompanying drop in N<sub>e</sub> means: 

- **More genetic drift and inbreeding:** deleterious alleles are less efficiently removed.  
- **Higher genetic load:** a greater accumulation of harmful mutations reduces average fitness ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9314787/#:~:text=accumulation%20of%20mutational%20load%20in,Roff)) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=change%3A%20the%20fate%20of%20small,flow%2C%20dispersal%20and%20load%20that)).  
- **Lower adaptive potential:** the population is less able to adapt to further environmental change or stress, making it more vulnerable ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=Drift%20is%20higher%20in%20small,27)) ([www.nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01526-9#:~:text=efficacy%20of%20selection%20can%20accelerate,27)).  

Ultimately, maintaining larger effective population sizes (through habitat connectivity, corridors, or assisted gene flow) is crucial to minimize genetic load as species’ ranges shift or contract under climate change. Reducing the loss of N<sub>e</sub> can help preserve genetic health and improve the long-term survival odds of species facing rapidly changing climates.
