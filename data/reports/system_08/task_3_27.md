## Jordan Form and Social Stratification Matrices  
**Social Stratification Matrix:** In sociology, social stratification (class structure) can be modeled with a transition matrix that encodes mobility probabilities between strata or groups. Each entry $P_{ij}$ might represent the probability of moving from class $i$ to class $j$ (over a generation, for instance). This matrix’s **Jordan normal form** in linear algebra reveals its eigenvalues and eigenvectors (and any Jordan blocks), which encapsulate the system’s long-term behavior. In particular, the eigenvalue $\lambda = 1$ plays a crucial role: an eigenvector for $\lambda=1$ corresponds to a **stationary distribution** (a long-run equilibrium of class proportions) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_distribution#:~:text=Crudely%20stated%2C%20all%20of%20the,the%20%2010%20is%20unity)). The Jordan form helps us identify how many independent $\lambda=1$ eigenvectors exist and whether $\lambda=1$ is a **simple** eigenvalue or has higher multiplicity. This is key to understanding **ergodicity** in social mobility dynamics.

**Ergodicity and Eigenvalues:** *Ergodicity* here means that regardless of the initial class distribution, the system eventually “forgets” its start and converges to a unique stable distribution of society across strata. In Markov chain terms, the mobility process is ergodic if there is **one** stationary distribution that attracts all initial states. This happens when the transition matrix is **irreducible** (all strata communicate) and **aperiodic** (no deterministic cycles): under these conditions, the stationary distribution is unique and the chain has a single dominant eigenvalue $\lambda_1=1$ (with all other eigenvalues $|\lambda_i|<1$) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_distribution#:~:text=,chain%20is%20irreducible%20and%20aperiodic)) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_distribution#:~:text=Crudely%20stated%2C%20all%20of%20the,the%20%2010%20is%20unity)). The Jordan form of an ergodic mobility matrix will thus have a single $\lambda=1$ eigenvalue (one Jordan block for 1) and all other Jordan blocks associated with eigenvalues of magnitude less than 1. As a result, repeated application of the transition matrix (taking powers $P^k$ as $k\to\infty$) causes every initial state to converge to the same equilibrium proportions ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chain#:~:text=stationary%20distribution%20,This%20is%20stated%20by%20the)). In other words, with one $\lambda=1$ eigenvector, $P^k$ tends toward a rank-one matrix whose every row equals the stationary distribution vector, ensuring a unified long-run outcome.

**Ergodicity Boundary in Jordan Form:** The **ergodicity boundary** is essentially the tipping point at which the mobility process loses this single long-run equilibrium. In the language of eigenvalues, it’s crossed when *$\lambda=1$ ceases to be unique or dominant*. The Jordan form makes this boundary precise. If the social stratification matrix develops **additional eigenvalues on the unit circle (|λ| = 1)** – for example, another eigenvalue $1$ (multiplicity $>1$) or a pair of eigenvalues like $±1$ – the chain is no longer fully mixing. Multiple $\lambda=1$ eigenvalues mean there are multiple independent stationary states (each eigenvector giving a different invariant distribution) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_distribution#:~:text=Crudely%20stated%2C%20all%20of%20the,the%20%2010%20is%20unity)). This occurs if the society splits into isolated strata or subsystems that don’t communicate. Likewise, an eigenvalue of $-1$ (or other complex unit-modulus eigenvalues) indicates a **periodic oscillation**, preventing convergence to a single steady state ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chain#:~:text=exist%20while%20the%20stationary%20distribution,pmatrix%7D%200%26%201%5C%5C%201%26%200)). In either case, the Jordan canonical form would show *either* several 1-eigenvalue blocks (for independent stagnant sub-distributions) or a Jordan block of size >1 (indicating a cycle or long transient). Thus, the appearance of more than one $\lambda=1$ eigenvector (or any eigenvalue on the unit circle aside from 1) **delimits the boundary of ergodicity**: it signals that the system has transitioned from one overall equilibrium to either **multiple equilibria or persistent cycles**. Simply put, as long as the Jordan form has a single $\lambda=1$ with no other $|\lambda|=1$ peers, the mobility function is ergodic – beyond that, it isn’t.

## Impact of Counter-Globalization Flow Fields  
**Global vs. Counter-Globalization Flows:** “Flow fields” refer to patterns of movement – here, flows of people, capital, or status changes that contribute to social mobility. In a **globalized flow field**, high connectivity allows individuals to move across regions and classes freely, making the social mobility matrix *well-mixed* (irreducible and aperiodic). The Jordan form in this case has one dominant eigenvalue 1 (Perron–Frobenius theorem ensures uniqueness given full connectivity) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_distribution#:~:text=,chain%20is%20irreducible%20and%20aperiodic)), so the system is ergodic. By contrast, in **counter-globalization flow fields**, global flows recede as borders harden or social barriers strengthen. Mobility becomes confined within local or regional segments – think of societies or sub-communities that no longer interchange members freely. Mathematically, the transition matrix approaches a **block-diagonal or nearly decomposable form** (with blocks connected by only weak or zero links) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearly_completely_decomposable_Markov_chain#:~:text=Ando%20%20and%20%204,and%20small%20nonzeros%20everywhere%20else)). Each block (isolated region/class group) can develop its own stationary distribution, essentially functioning as a closed subsystem.

**Jordan Form Delimits Ergodicity Boundary:** As counter-globalization intensifies, the mobility matrix transitions from a single integrated block to **multiple blocks**. In the **extreme case** (complete segmentation), the matrix becomes block-diagonal; its Jordan form will exhibit *multiple $\lambda=1$ eigenvalues*, one per block, since each submatrix has its own Perron eigenvector. This clearly crosses the ergodicity boundary – instead of one global equilibrium, there are now several distinct equilibria (one for each isolated strata cluster). Even in less extreme “nearly decomposed” cases with small inter-group flows, the Jordan form signals approach to this boundary: the secondary eigenvalues creep **closer to 1**, slowing convergence. Essentially, the **spectral gap** (difference between the leading eigenvalue 1 and the second-largest eigenvalue) shrinks as globalization’s connecting flows weaken. When that gap closes (additional eigenvalue reaches 1 in magnitude), the chain is no longer ergodic. The Jordan form thus cleanly delineates this transition by showing when new eigenvalue-1 blocks appear or when a 1-eigenvalue block enlarges.  

**Summary:** The Jordan normal form serves as a mathematical lens to identify when a social mobility system switches from an **integrated, ergodic regime** to a **segmented, non-ergodic regime**. In a globally connected scenario, the Jordan form has a single dominant eigenvalue $1$, guaranteeing a unique long-run social composition. In a counter-globalization scenario, as flows that once linked disparate social strata or regions diminish, the stratification matrix’s Jordan form splits into independent components or acquires additional $\lambda=1$ eigenvectors. This split **“delimits the ergodicity boundary”** – marking the point where the mobility function no longer yields a single equilibrium for the whole system. In practical terms, the society breaks into separate strata or locales that each retain their own structure (no universal mobility outcome), exactly as the emergence of multiple 1-eigenvalue blocks in the Jordan form indicates. Thus, the Jordan form provides a clear criterion: **ergodic mobility (global mixing) prevails while there’s one unified $\lambda=1$ eigenstructure, ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_distribution#:~:text=,chain%20is%20irreducible%20and%20aperiodic)) and it fails once the eigen-spectrum gains additional unit-modulus members, betraying fragmentation ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_distribution#:~:text=Crudely%20stated%2C%20all%20of%20the,the%20%2010%20is%20unity)).** In sum, by examining the Jordan form of the social stratification matrix, one can pinpoint the threshold where counter-globalization-driven segmentation undermines the ergodicity of social mobility.
