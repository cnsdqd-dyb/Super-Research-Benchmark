## Balancing Centralized and Distributed Control in SDN Traffic Engineering

SDN’s control plane achieves this balance by using a *logically centralized, but physically distributed* controller architecture. In practice, a cluster of SDN controllers (or a hierarchical set of global and local controllers) maintains a **global network view** for optimal, centralized traffic engineering decisions, while **deploying controllers near the data-plane devices** to reduce latency. The controllers coordinate and synchronize network state among themselves via east–west interfaces or distributed data stores, ensuring each controller has the global view needed for traffic optimization ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4001/4/3/477#:~:text=In%20HyperFlow%2C%20every%20controller%20in,298)) ([senjlearning.space](https://senjlearning.space/1-Theory/6-Network/Top-to-down/50-Network-layer-Control-plane#:~:text=Modern%20controllers%20such%20as%20OpenDaylight,control%20applications%20alike)). This way, the network benefits from centralized decision-making (global optimization of routes and resource use) but still achieves low-latency, **distributed enforcement** of those decisions. In short, **multiple cooperating SDN controllers** (often in a hierarchical or flat distributed design) provide a unified global brain for the network while acting locally for quick response ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-5903/12/3/49#:~:text=Hyperflow%2C%20Onix%20and%20DISCO%20adopt,latency%20or%20even%20connection%20failure)) ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-5903/12/3/49#:~:text=ElastiCon%20and%20ONOS%20adopt%20the,latency%20and%20avoiding%20controller%20failure)). This mechanism balances the centralized intelligence of SDN with the responsiveness of distributed implementation, which is crucial for effective traffic engineering.
