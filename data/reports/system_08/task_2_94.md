# Human Intervention Threshold in Review Grading

## Automated Grading vs. Human Oversight
Many online platforms use algorithms to **grade** or evaluate product reviews for issues like spam, policy violations, or quality. However, these systems aren’t fully autonomous – they include a **human intervention threshold**. This threshold is a preset decision point at which the system stops acting automatically and hands the review off to a human moderator for judgment. In practice, the algorithm might assign each review a confidence score (e.g. how likely it is spam or harmful), and if that score crosses a certain threshold, a human will review the case before the content is published or finalized. The goal is to let the AI handle routine cases while ensuring ambiguous or extreme cases get human scrutiny.

## How the Threshold Is Determined
The human intervention threshold is **set by policy and calibrated through testing**. Platforms tune this threshold as a strategic balance between catching bad content and avoiding unnecessary human reviews ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=Moderation%20models%20don%E2%80%99t%20deliver%20yes,legal%20risk%20and%20user%20trust)). In other words, it’s chosen to **minimize errors**: a higher sensitivity catches more problematic reviews but risks flagging some legitimate ones (false positives), whereas a lower sensitivity might miss some bad reviews (false negatives). Finding the sweet spot requires considering *false positives vs. false negatives*, legal risks, and user trust ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=violence,legal%20risk%20and%20user%20trust)). Each platform sets the bar according to its **risk appetite** and user expectations – for example, a site that values openness might tolerate more questionable content, whereas one focused on safety will use a stricter threshold ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=No%20threshold%20is%20perfect%20across,thresholds%20based%20on%20their%20domain)) ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=This%20means%20that%20even%20when,user%20base%20and%20risk%20appetite)).

## Examples of Threshold Criteria
Threshold rules can be **simple or complex**. Some systems use straightforward rules like star ratings: for instance, one review platform automatically flags all **1-star and 2-star reviews** for manual moderation before they go live ([help.rr.skeepers.com](https://help.rr.skeepers.com/hc/en-us/articles/10318931830940-The-moderation-threshold#:~:text=By%20default%2C%20reviews%20with%20a,the%20setting%20via%20the%20platform)). This ensures very negative feedback is double-checked by a person (often to handle customer issues or filter out malicious reviews). More sophisticated moderation uses **confidence scores** from AI models. For example, a review classified as clearly safe (below the risk threshold) might be auto-approved, an obviously harmful review (above a high threshold) could be auto-rejected, and any **middle-ground cases get “soft flagged” for human review** ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=%2A%20Auto,content%20sent%20to%20human%20moderators)). These graduated responses prevent overwhelming human moderators while still catching uncertain cases. The exact threshold values are usually refined over time – sometimes even adjusted by context (product category, region, or current trends) – to maintain the right balance as conditions change ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=4.1%20Precision%E2%80%93Recall%20Trade)) ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=This%20means%20that%20even%20when,user%20base%20and%20risk%20appetite)).

## Summary
In summary, the **human intervention threshold** in product review grading is set by the platform’s policies and risk management strategy. It’s essentially a cutoff (whether based on star ratings, AI confidence scores, or specific keywords) that decides when an **automated system defers to human judgment**. This threshold is carefully chosen and tuned: it acts as a **safety net**, making sure that when the algorithm isn’t **highly confident** or a review triggers certain red flags, a human will step in to make the final call ([www.linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/content-moderation-scale-balancing-speed-ethics-api4ai-sxvaf#:~:text=%2A%20Auto,content%20sent%20to%20human%20moderators)). By adjusting this threshold, companies ensure that the review process is efficient and scalable **without compromising quality or fairness**, catching problematic content when needed and letting routine reviews pass through.
