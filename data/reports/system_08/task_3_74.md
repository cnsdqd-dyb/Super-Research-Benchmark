**Classification Boundaries for Problem Severity in After-Sales Service**  
In after-sales support, issues are categorized by severity based on how seriously they impact the customer and the product’s functionality ([www.atlassian.com](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/kpis/severity-levels#:~:text=Incident%20severity%20levels%20are%20a,a%20system%20is%20not%20functioning)) ([www.sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796655/000115752311005435/a6864082_ex102.htm#:~:text=APAR%20Severity%20Levels%20means%20designations,has%20on%20the%20customer%E2%80%99s%20operation)). The boundary between severity levels is defined by specific criteria that distinguish a critical problem from a minor one. Key factors include:  

- **Impact on Business Operations** – The foremost factor is how much the problem impairs the customer’s ability to use the product or continue normal operations ([www.sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796655/000115752311005435/a6864082_ex102.htm#:~:text=APAR%20Severity%20Levels%20means%20designations,has%20on%20the%20customer%E2%80%99s%20operation)). If an issue **severely disrupts business** – for example, the customer cannot use the product at all – it crosses the threshold into the highest severity category ([www.sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796655/000115752311005435/a6864082_ex102.htm#:~:text=a.%20%20,a%20severe%20or%20critical%20impact)).  

- **Scope of Functional Loss** – This considers whether the product or service is completely **down** or partially functional. A problem causing a **total outage** (system inoperative for all users) is classified as most severe, whereas a **partial failure** (only certain features broken or a subset of users affected) is one step lower ([www.atlassian.com](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/kpis/severity-levels#:~:text=incident%20with%20very%20high%20impact,is%20causing%20customers%20slight%20inconvenience)). For instance, Atlassian treats an incident as **Severity 1** if a client-facing service is down for all customers, but as **Severity 2** if only a subset of customers or a critical feature is impacted ([www.atlassian.com](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/kpis/severity-levels#:~:text=For%20example%3A%20At%20Atlassian%2C%20we,is%20causing%20customers%20slight%20inconvenience)).  

- **Availability of Workarounds** – **No viable workaround** for the customer elevates the issue’s severity, since the problem can’t be mitigated until a fix is provided ([www.lawinsider.com](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/severity-levels-and-response-times#:~:text=loss%20of%20platform%20functionality%20or,The%20issue%20consists%20of)). Conversely, if there’s an **effective workaround** or alternative that keeps the product usable, the issue might be classified in a lower severity level ([www.lawinsider.com](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/severity-levels-and-response-times#:~:text=inconvenient%20workaround%20or%20no%20workaround,Low%3A%20Minor%20loss%20of%20application)). In other words, an issue that completely blocks usage with no way around it will be marked as more severe than one with a temporary fix or bypass.  

- **Number of Users or Extent Affected** – The **breadth of impact** helps define severity boundaries ([www.lawinsider.com](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/severity-levels-and-response-times#:~:text=services%20down%20or%20major%20malfunction,a%20convenient%20workaround%20exists%2Fminor%20performance)). A widespread problem affecting many customers or critical system components is classified as high severity, whereas an issue impacting only a single user or a small subset is usually considered less severe. For example, a malfunction that **prevents all users from performing normal functions** would be top priority, while one that affects a few users or a non-critical module would fall below the highest severity tier ([www.lawinsider.com](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/severity-levels-and-response-times#:~:text=services%20down%20or%20major%20malfunction,a%20convenient%20workaround%20exists%2Fminor%20performance)).  

- **Risk and Safety Considerations** – If the issue poses **safety hazards, security breaches, or data loss risks**, it immediately meets the criteria for highest severity. These scenarios demand urgent attention similar to a major product failure ([www.atlassian.com](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/kpis/severity-levels#:~:text=For%20example%3A%20At%20Atlassian%2C%20we,a%20system%20is%20not%20functioning)). For instance, a critical severity might include cases like customer data loss or a serious security vulnerability, even if the product remains partially operational ([www.atlassian.com](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/kpis/severity-levels#:~:text=For%20example%3A%20At%20Atlassian%2C%20we,significant%20impact%2C%E2%80%9D%20including%20when%20a)). Such high-risk problems are classified as severe because of their potential harm, defining a clear boundary from routine issues.  

**Example of Severity Thresholds:** To illustrate, many support agreements define **Severity 1** (critical) as a **catastrophic problem** that halts the customer’s use of the product (e.g. the product is completely down with no workaround) ([www.sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796655/000115752311005435/a6864082_ex102.htm#:~:text=a.%20%20,a%20severe%20or%20critical%20impact)). **Severity 2** might be a major issue where the product is still operable but an important function is unavailable or heavily impaired ([www.sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796655/000115752311005435/a6864082_ex102.htm#:~:text=a.%20%20,a%20severe%20or%20critical%20impact)). By contrast, **Severity 3** and **Severity 4** are reserved for problems that have only minor impact on operations – the product can still be used with some restrictions or the issue is cosmetic and easily circumvented ([www.sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796655/000115752311005435/a6864082_ex102.htm#:~:text=important%20function%20is%20not%20available,able%20to%20circumvent%20the%20problem)). In summary, the classification boundary for problem severity is defined by the point at which an issue’s **impact, scope, and urgency** escalate from a tolerable inconvenience to a serious disruption. When a problem crosses that line – by significantly impeding functionality, lacking any workaround, or threatening safety/business continuity – it is classified in a higher severity category, prompting a faster and more robust after-sales service response ([www.sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796655/000115752311005435/a6864082_ex102.htm#:~:text=customer%E2%80%99s%20operation%3A)) ([www.lawinsider.com](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/severity-levels-and-response-times#:~:text=manner,%E2%80%A2%20High%3A%20Critical)).
