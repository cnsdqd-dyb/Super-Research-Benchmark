# Determinants of Authentication Intensity in Account Security

In account security, the “authentication intensity hierarchy” refers to how robust the verification process is (from simple single-factor logins up to strong multi-factor methods). Several key factors determine the level of authentication strength required for protecting an account:

- **Risk and account sensitivity** – The greater the potential impact of an account being compromised, the stronger the authentication needed. Higher-risk or more sensitive accounts (e.g. admin accounts, financial data) demand higher assurance (stricter authentication). In practice, risk assessment results are often the primary factor in selecting the appropriate authentication level ([openid-foundation-japan.github.io](https://openid-foundation-japan.github.io/800-63-3-final/sp800-63-3.html#:~:text=The%20risk%20assessment%20results%20are,the%20most%20advantageous%20xAL%20selection)).

- **Single vs. multi-factor authentication** – Accounts secured with only one factor (like just a password) sit at the lowest security tier. By contrast, adding additional factors (multi-factor authentication, or MFA) raises the protection level significantly. For example, a password-only login is considered the weakest (often the lowest assurance level), whereas enabling a second factor (such as an OTP code or app prompt) greatly increases authentication strength ([www.securityvision.ru](https://www.securityvision.ru/en/blog/loginy-paroli-i-drugie-sposoby-autentifikatsii-opisanie-osobennosti-ugrozy/#:~:text=,time%20code%20for%20authentication)) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authenticator#:~:text=Security%20code)).

- **Type and quality of factors used** – The kind of authentication factors also influences the intensity. Authentication methods fall into three categories – *something you know, something you have, and something you are* ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authentication#:~:text=The%20ways%20in%20which%20someone,or%20verify%20a%20person%27s%20identity)). Combining different factor types yields stronger security than using a single type. For instance, supplementing a password with a hard-to-steal factor (like a physical security key or biometric ID) provides very high assurance, since top-tier authentication methods require multi-factor proofs that include phishing-resistant tokens or biometrics for maximum security ([www.securityvision.ru](https://www.securityvision.ru/en/blog/loginy-paroli-i-drugie-sposoby-autentifikatsii-opisanie-osobennosti-ugrozy/#:~:text=,hardware%20devices%20with%20phishing%20protection)).

- **Context and policy requirements** – The required authentication intensity can adjust based on context and organizational policies. Many systems use risk-based authentication that evaluates login context (device, location, time, etc.) and will **increase** the challenge if something seems suspicious. A higher risk profile triggers more complex authentication steps (e.g. asking for an extra code or verification) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk-based_authentication#:~:text=system%20which%20takes%20into%20account,Higher%20risk%20profiles)) ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk-based_authentication#:~:text=lower,account%20all%20the%20background%20information)). Additionally, companies or regulations may enforce certain levels of authentication — for example, mandating MFA for all privileged or sensitive accounts. Such policies effectively raise the baseline authentication intensity for those accounts by requiring stronger login measures.

In summary, an account’s security **grade** or authentication intensity level is determined by the number of authentication factors in use and their strength, guided by the risk associated with that account. More critical accounts and scenarios warrant more robust, multi-factor authentication, whereas lower-risk situations might suffice with simpler (but less secure) methods. Each added layer or stronger factor moves the account up the hierarchy of authentication intensity, providing greater protection against unauthorized access.
