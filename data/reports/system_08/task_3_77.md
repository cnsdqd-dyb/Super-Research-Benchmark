# Determining Customer Grade Boundaries in RFM Stratification

In RFM analysis, *customer grades* (segments) are defined by splitting Recency, Frequency, and Monetary values into a few ranked categories. The boundaries between these categories are typically determined by examining the distribution of each RFM metric and **binning** the values into meaningful ranges. Here’s how these partition boundaries are usually set:

- **Quantile (Rank) Binning:** The most common approach is to sort customers by each RFM metric and divide them into equal-sized groups. For example, if using five RFM grades (scores 1–5), each group would contain roughly 20% of the customers ([communities.sas.com](https://communities.sas.com/t5/SAS-Communities-Library/Which-RFM-binning-method-is-best-for-me/ta-p/341327#:~:text=Independent%20binning%20is%20a%20relatively,5%20and%20a%20customer%20who)). The cutoff points at 20%, 40%, 60%, and 80% of the sorted values become the **partition boundaries** between grades. This means the top 20% (best values) get the highest score (e.g. 5) and the bottom 20% get the lowest score (1). These percentile-based thresholds ensure each grade holds about the same number of customers ([www.ibm.com](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/spss-statistics/cd?topic=analysis-rfm-binning#:~:text=A%20,two%20alternatives%20for%20handling%20ties)). In practice, this method adapts to your data – the exact boundary values come from your dataset’s quartiles or quintiles, making the grading fair and relative to performance ([mintea.blog](https://mintea.blog/?p=1214#:~:text=Take%20your%20customer%20data%2C%20give,adjusts%20according%20to%20your%20data)). For instance, the recency value that 80% of customers haven’t exceeded might define the cutoff between “recent” grade 5 and grade 4 customers.

- **Custom or Algorithmic Thresholds:** In some cases, businesses set **fixed criteria** or use data-driven algorithms to determine grade boundaries. A company might use domain knowledge to pick specific cutoffs (e.g. “grade A” customers are those who purchased within 30 days, or spent over \$1,000). Alternatively, clustering techniques or *natural breaks* algorithms (like Jenks) can find optimal breakpoints in the RFM distributions ([medium.com](https://medium.com/data-science/rfm-segmentation-using-quartiles-and-jenks-natural-breaks-924f4d8baee1#:~:text=This%20make%20a%20little%20more,of%20the%20partitions%20using%20Jenks)). These methods seek gaps or clusters in the data – for example, Jenks will partition the values where there are natural jumps, rather than forcing equal-sized groups. The resulting breakpoints still serve as the grade boundaries, but they are chosen to reflect inherent groupings in customer behavior instead of strict percentiles. This can yield segments that are more internally consistent (at the cost of uneven group sizes) if the data is very skewed or clustered.

In summary, **RFM grade boundaries** are typically determined by dividing the sorted R, F, and M values into a small number of ranked bins. Most implementations use quantile-based binning (such as quartiles or quintiles) to set the thresholds so that each grade contains roughly equal customers ([communities.sas.com](https://communities.sas.com/t5/SAS-Communities-Library/Which-RFM-binning-method-is-best-for-me/ta-p/341327#:~:text=Independent%20binning%20is%20a%20relatively,5%20and%20a%20customer%20who)) ([www.ibm.com](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/spss-statistics/cd?topic=analysis-rfm-binning#:~:text=A%20,two%20alternatives%20for%20handling%20ties)). The specific boundary values are drawn from the data (e.g. the 80th percentile of frequency might separate “high” vs. “medium” frequency grades). This approach yields clear, data-driven cutoffs for each customer grade. In more advanced or tailored analyses, businesses may adjust these boundaries using domain-specific rules or clustering algorithms to better capture meaningful differences in their customer base. But regardless of method, the key is that the **partition boundaries come from analyzing the distribution of RFM values** and choosing breakpoints that segment customers into distinct value tiers. 

These well-defined boundaries let you label customers by grade (for example, VIPs vs. lapsed) based on where their RFM metrics fall in relation to the chosen thresholds, enabling targeted marketing strategies for each segment.
