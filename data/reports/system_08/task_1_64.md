# Stability Boundaries of Signal Coordination and Congestion Propagation

## Stability Boundaries in Traffic Signal Coordination  
 ([eng.libretexts.org](https://eng.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Civil_Engineering/Fundamentals_of_Transportation/06%3A_Traffice_Control/6.02%3A_Traffic_Signals#:~:text=Overflow%20delay%20generally%20only%20applies,into%20account%20for%20simple%20problems))In traffic signal coordination, *stability boundaries* refer to the conditions under which the coordinated signals can handle traffic demand without persistent queue growth. When traffic volumes and timing plans stay within these boundaries (i.e. each intersection’s capacity isn’t exceeded), queues remain manageable. Coordinated signals (like well-timed green waves) clear arriving vehicles each cycle, so any congestion stays *local and short-lived*. In practice, queue spillover into upstream intersections is rare under proper coordination – it usually only occurs with **poor signal timing or unexpected surges in demand** ([eng.libretexts.org](https://eng.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Civil_Engineering/Fundamentals_of_Transportation/06%3A_Traffice_Control/6.02%3A_Traffic_Signals#:~:text=Overflow%20delay%20generally%20only%20applies,into%20account%20for%20simple%20problems)). In other words, within the stable regime, the network can absorb disturbances: each red-light queue dissipates during the following green, preventing jams from proliferating beyond the immediate intersection.

## Effects on System-Level Congestion Propagation  
 ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4309220/#:~:text=accommodation%20space%20for%20the%20vehicle,will%20be%20difficult%20to%20restore))If traffic demand pushes beyond these stability boundaries, the coordination breaks down and **local jams can spread system-wide**. In saturated conditions (where intersections operate at or above capacity), a queue at one signal often **spills back** into upstream links, triggering a domino effect of congestion ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4309220/#:~:text=accommodation%20space%20for%20the%20vehicle,will%20be%20difficult%20to%20restore)). Essentially, an oversaturated intersection fails to clear its queue, so the leftover vehicles block or delay upstream traffic – much like a “virtual red light” imposed on the approaching flow ([bohrium.dp.tech](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/2401.15433#:~:text=Discrete%20Dynamics%20in%20Nature%20and,are%20the%20same%20as%20that)). Research shows that large-scale traffic gridlock often *begins with a single local jam* that propagates when signals can no longer contain it ([bohrium.dp.tech](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/2401.15433#:~:text=Discrete%20Dynamics%20in%20Nature%20and,are%20the%20same%20as%20that)). Once one intersection overflows, adjacent intersections get jammed in turn, and the **congestion wave ripples through the network**. This system-level propagation makes recovery difficult, since every intersection’s throughput is throttled by downstream queues ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4309220/#:~:text=accommodation%20space%20for%20the%20vehicle,will%20be%20difficult%20to%20restore)). In summary, operating *within* the stability boundaries of signal coordination localizes and dampens congestion, whereas exceeding those boundaries causes small bottlenecks to **amplify into regional congestion**. The stability threshold thus dictates whether traffic disturbances remain isolated or escalate into widespread network clogs.
