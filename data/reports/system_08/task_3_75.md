## Recency in Reputation Systems  
Seller reputation systems often **discount older ratings** to keep scores relevant over time. Feedback can become “stale” as sellers change behavior or market conditions evolve ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=disproportionately%20valued%20over%20time%2C%20but,becomes%20obsolete%2C%20cultural%20mores%20shift)). To address this, reputation algorithms include a temporal factor that **reduces the influence of older reviews**. In essence, recent customer ratings count more toward a seller’s score, while very old feedback gradually counts for less.

## Designing the Temporal Decay Function  
The **temporal correction function** (time-based weighting) is chosen based on how quickly ratings lose relevance in that context. System designers decide on a decay rate or window that fits the domain. A common approach is to apply an **exponential or scheduled decay** – subtracting value from older feedback at a rate deemed appropriate ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=users%2C%20remember%20to%20account%20for,value%20in%20the%20same%20interval)). For example, a fast-changing product category might use a steep decay (e.g. cutting a rating’s weight in half each year), whereas a slower-changing service might only lose about 10% of a rating’s influence per year ([buildingreputation.com](https://buildingreputation.com/writings/2009/09/time_decay_in_reputation_syste.html#:~:text=users%2C%20remember%20to%20account%20for,value%20in%20the%20same%20interval)). The exact function is usually determined through experience or data analysis: the goal is to balance fairness (not penalizing a seller forever for old issues) with accuracy (reflecting current performance). In practice, this could mean defining a “half-life” for reputation or using a fixed time frame after which feedback impact drops off.

## Implementation in Seller Platforms  
Many e-commerce platforms implement time-based weighting in simple, transparent ways. One common method is using a **rolling time window** for feedback. For instance, eBay focuses on a seller’s ratings from the **last 12 months** as a measure of current reputation ([pages.ebay.com](https://pages.ebay.com/ebaymotors/help/feedback/contextual/recent-feedback-ratings.html#:~:text=Recent%20Feedback%20RatingsThe%20recent%20Feedback,review%20other%20information%20in%20your)). Older feedback is still visible, but only the recent 1-year window fully factors into the positive percentage that buyers see. After a year passes, any negative or positive feedback beyond 12 months **no longer affects the “current” score**, effectively resetting its impact. Amazon’s seller reputation works similarly: if a seller has enough feedback in the past year, the site prominently shows a **“XX% positive over the past 12 months”** statistic (along with the total number of ratings) on their profile ([sellercentral.amazon.co.uk](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/8eb8d7ac-3205-4eb4-9690-efaf580cf84f#:~:text=If%20you%20have%20more%20than,on%20the%20Offer%20Listings%20page)) ([sellercentral.amazon.co.uk](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/8eb8d7ac-3205-4eb4-9690-efaf580cf84f#:~:text=If%20you%20have%20more%20than,on%20the%20Offer%20Listings%20page)). If the seller has very few recent reviews, Amazon falls back to a lifetime percentage, but the default is to emphasize the last year of performance ([sellercentral.amazon.co.uk](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/8eb8d7ac-3205-4eb4-9690-efaf580cf84f#:~:text=If%20you%20have%20more%20than,on%20the%20Offer%20Listings%20page)). 

Other systems use a continuous decay curve instead of a hard cutoff. In those cases, each rating’s weight *f*(Δt) gradually diminishes as its age Δt increases (for example, using an exponential decay factor). Regardless of the exact formula, the key is that **recent ratings carry more weight** than older ones ([patents.google.com](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20020138402A1/en#:~:text=,are%20discounted%20over%20time%20by)). The platform’s team typically tunes the temporal function by considering how quickly seller behavior can change and by testing different decay rates. In summary, the temporal correction function is a deliberately chosen rule (like a 12-month window or a decay rate) that the reputation system uses to adjust ratings over time – ensuring a seller’s reputation score always favors their **most up-to-date performance**.
