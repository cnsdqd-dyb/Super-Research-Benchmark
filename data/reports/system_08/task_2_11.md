## Design Approach: Graded Doping and Interface Trap Optimization

A proven strategy is to **engineer the GaN device’s doping profile in tandem with interface trap mitigation**. In practice, this means using a **graded or step-wise doping concentration profile** (rather than abrupt doping changes) and robust interface passivation to smooth out electric fields and minimize trap activation ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-666X/12/10/1244?type=check_update&version=1#:~:text=Apart%20from%20the%20voltage%20handling,%E2%88%923%7D%2Fstep)). By gradually varying the doping concentration (e.g. a step-doped or graded channel/drift region), the peak electric field is reduced, which lessens hot-carrier injection into interface traps and **suppresses dynamic on-resistance increases** ([www.mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-666X/12/10/1244?type=check_update&version=1#:~:text=Apart%20from%20the%20voltage%20handling,%E2%88%923%7D%2Fstep)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1369800117321716#:~:text=the%20physical%20origin%20of%20dynamic,the%20resistance%20of%20the%202DEG)). Simultaneously, **improving interface quality** (through passivation or trap compensation) lowers the interface trap state density. This co-optimized design balances performance and reliability – **maintaining low on-resistance (thanks to adequate doping) while preventing trap-induced degradation** like current collapse or threshold shifts ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1369800117321716#:~:text=the%20physical%20origin%20of%20dynamic,the%20resistance%20of%20the%202DEG)). In essence, a **carefully graded doping profile combined with interface trap reduction** provides the needed trade-off between conduction efficiency and long-term device stability in GaN power devices.
