## Balancing Latency and Energy in Edge Scheduling

Edge computing nodes can employ a **dynamic, energy-aware scheduling strategy** that balances performance and power usage to meet conflicting demands ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11976914/#:~:text=Energy%20consumption%20is%20a%20critical,of%20edge%20workloads%20requires%20adaptive)). This approach prioritizes latency-sensitive tasks with immediate resource allocation (or higher CPU frequency) to meet real-time deadlines, while scheduling less urgent tasks in energy-saving modes or offloading them when possible ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11976914/#:~:text=As%20shown%20in%20Fig,slightly%20longer%20task%20completion%20times)). By dynamically adjusting resource use based on each task’s urgency, the scheduler ensures critical workloads are processed on time without unnecessary energy drain. 

Such a multi-objective scheduling strategy effectively resolves the latency–energy trade-off. In practice, fewer tasks are treated as high priority when deadlines are relaxed, allowing more energy-efficient decisions at the cost of slightly longer processing times ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11976914/#:~:text=As%20shown%20in%20Fig,slightly%20longer%20task%20completion%20times)). Conversely, stringent real-time tasks trigger high-priority (faster) execution to minimize delay ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11976914/#:~:text=The%20optimal%20choice%20of%20the,to%20achieve%20better%20energy%20savings)). Techniques like dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS) and intelligent task offloading are often used in tandem to implement this strategy, guaranteeing **both** immediate responsiveness and sustained long-term operation of edge nodes ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11976914/#:~:text=Energy%20consumption%20is%20a%20critical,of%20edge%20workloads%20requires%20adaptive)).
