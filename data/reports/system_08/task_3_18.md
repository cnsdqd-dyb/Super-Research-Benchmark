## Impact on Entropy Gradient for Cultural Diversity

**Low-rank embedding compression reduces informational diversity:** Low-rank approximation of local node embeddings means we project each node’s high-dimensional local feature vector onto a smaller subspace, keeping only the dominant patterns ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372172255_Effective_Rank_and_Dimensionality_Reduction_From_Complex_Disaggregation_Back_to_a_Simple_World#:~:text=that%20these%20near,C67%2C%20D46%2C%20D57%2C%20E11%2C%20E32)). This compression inevitably **discards fine-grained variations** in the nodes’ local structures. In effect, the network’s representation becomes dominated by just a few latent factors, and many unique details are “averaged out.” Information-theoretically, representing data with only a few strong components corresponds to lower Shannon entropy (less uncertainty/variety) in that representation ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372172255_Effective_Rank_and_Dimensionality_Reduction_From_Complex_Disaggregation_Back_to_a_Simple_World#:~:text=,and%20so%20is%20the%20influence)). Intuitively, if one or two features capture most of the variance, the **diversity of information** present is reduced – analogous to a scenario where one cultural trait dominates across the graph. This **loss of heterogeneity** means local nodes look more similar in the compressed space, flattening differences that would otherwise contribute to cultural diversity.

**Flatter entropy gradient and dampened sensitivity:** Cultural diversity in knowledge transmission graphs is often quantified by an entropy-based function (e.g. Shannon entropy) to measure how varied or balanced the knowledge/cultural traits are ([www.researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372172255_Effective_Rank_and_Dimensionality_Reduction_From_Complex_Disaggregation_Back_to_a_Simple_World#:~:text=encompass%20the%20same%20disorder%20,The)). When the node embeddings are compressed via a low-rank approximation, the entropy measure becomes **less sensitive to small local changes**. Essentially, many subtle variations in cultural information no longer register because they’ve been merged into broad factors. The **gradient of the entropy function** – which indicates how the diversity measure changes as the network’s state shifts – is therefore **diminished or “flatter.”** In practical terms, this means that adjusting a node’s attributes in a minor way (especially along dimensions that were wiped out by the low-rank projection) barely affects the entropy-based diversity score. The gradient is effectively confined to the few retained directions of variation, so it captures only large-scale, coarse changes in cultural makeup. Overall, the low-rank approximation **smooths the entropy landscape**, making cultural diversity appear more stable (less spiked or extreme) but at the cost of potentially underestimating true diversity. In summary, simplifying local embeddings to a low-rank form **dampens the entropy function’s gradient**, reducing its magnitude and nuance, because the graph’s rich local differences have been collapsed into a limited set of factors. This yields a more homogeneous view of the network’s culture, muting the responsiveness of diversity metrics to anything except the most dominant patterns.
