# Laplacian Eigenvalues, Mobility, and Structural Solidification in Stratification Networks

## Social Stratification Networks and Mobility 
Social stratification networks model how individuals or groups are linked across social strata (e.g. classes or status levels). **Mobility functions** in this context describe the likelihood or rate of movement from one stratum to another – essentially capturing social mobility within the network. We can represent the strata and mobility as a graph: nodes represent social positions (or individuals in those positions), and weighted edges represent mobility links (the strength or probability of moving between positions). A highly **mobile** society corresponds to a network with strong inter-stratum connections, while a **solidified** (rigidly stratified) society has weak or no links between strata.

## Laplacian Eigenvalues and Network Connectivity  
In network science, the **graph Laplacian** $L$ is a matrix that reflects the connectivity of the network. Its eigenvalues provide powerful insight into the network’s structure. The smallest Laplacian eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ is always 0 for any connected graph (with an all-ones eigenvector) ([studylib.net](https://studylib.net/doc/14193813/social-network-graphs#:~:text=Graph%20Laplacian%20,the%20algebraic%20connectivity%20of%20%F0%9D%92%A2)). The *second-smallest* eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ (often called the *algebraic connectivity* or **Fiedler value**) is particularly important: it acts as a continuous measure of how well the graph is connected ([www.johndcook.com](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2016/01/07/connectivity-graph-laplacian/#:~:text=second%20smallest%20eigenvalue%20%CE%BB_,well%20a%20graph%20is%20connected)). A larger $\lambda_2$ means the network is tightly knit, whereas a very small $\lambda_2$ indicates the network is nearly disconnected into two large components ([www.johndcook.com](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2016/01/07/connectivity-graph-laplacian/#:~:text=second%20smallest%20eigenvalue%20%CE%BB_,well%20a%20graph%20is%20connected)). In fact, if $\lambda_2 = 0$, the graph actually splits into at least two disconnected sub-networks ([www.johndcook.com](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2016/01/07/connectivity-graph-laplacian/#:~:text=second%20smallest%20eigenvalue%20%CE%BB_,well%20a%20graph%20is%20connected)). More generally, the number of zero eigenvalues of $L$ equals the number of connected components in the graph ([studylib.net](https://studylib.net/doc/14193813/social-network-graphs#:~:text=Graph%20Laplacian%20,the%20algebraic%20connectivity%20of%20%F0%9D%92%A2)). 

- **High connectivity (fluid structure)**: When mobility links are strong, $\lambda_2$ is relatively large, implying one cohesive network. Social strata are well-integrated by mobility ties.  
- **Weak connectivity (stratifying structure)**: As mobility links weaken, $\lambda_2$ shrinks toward 0. A small $\lambda_2$ signals that the network can be almost partitioned into separate groups – in social terms, strata are becoming isolated with little inter-mobility ([www.johndcook.com](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2016/01/07/connectivity-graph-laplacian/#:~:text=second%20smallest%20eigenvalue%20%CE%BB_,well%20a%20graph%20is%20connected)).  
- **Disconnected components (solidified structure)**: If mobility between certain strata disappears entirely, the network breaks into disconnected components. At this point $\lambda_2 = 0$ (and additional Laplacian eigenvalues may also be zero if multiple groups split off), indicating fully separate sub-networks ([studylib.net](https://studylib.net/doc/14193813/social-network-graphs#:~:text=Graph%20Laplacian%20,the%20algebraic%20connectivity%20of%20%F0%9D%92%A2)). This corresponds to complete **structural solidification**, where each social stratum is isolated.

## Eigenvalue Thresholds for Structural Solidification  
The **threshold of structural solidification** in a social network can be understood as the point at which social mobility is so low that the stratification becomes rigid – strata no longer exchange members. Laplacian eigenvalues *delimit* these thresholds by signaling when the network’s connectivity undergoes a qualitative change:

- As we gradually reduce mobility (weaken the inter-stratum edges), the second-smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ will decrease. There is a critical point where $\lambda_2$ approaches zero, which marks an **incipient break** in network connectivity. This is the threshold at which the network is about to fragment into distinct components (social classes solidifying). Essentially, **when $\lambda_2$ becomes near-zero, the network has practically split into weakly connected clusters** – a warning that the structure is solidifying.  

- **At the exact threshold of solidification, $\lambda_2$ reaches zero**, meaning the network has split into at least two separate components ([www.johndcook.com](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2016/01/07/connectivity-graph-laplacian/#:~:text=second%20smallest%20eigenvalue%20%CE%BB_,well%20a%20graph%20is%20connected)). In terms of social structure, this is the point where two (or more) strata become completely disconnected (no mobility between them). Beyond this threshold, additional strata could solidify (each disconnection adding another zero eigenvalue). The Laplacian eigenvalues thus *mark* these transitions: for instance, the first time $\lambda_2 = 0$ indicates the first major solidification (one group splitting into two), and if further solidification occurs, $\lambda_3$ or $\lambda_4$ dropping to (near) zero would indicate additional groups isolating. 

In summary, **Laplacian eigenvalues of the mobility network act as spectral markers for stratification thresholds**. A high algebraic connectivity (high $\lambda_2$) means the social structure is fluid and integrated, whereas a vanishingly small $\lambda_2$ signals approaching structural “freezing.” When $\lambda_2$ finally hits zero, it precisely delineates the threshold where the network’s structure solidifies into disconnected strata, formalizing the point of *no mobility* between those social groups ([www.johndcook.com](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2016/01/07/connectivity-graph-laplacian/#:~:text=second%20smallest%20eigenvalue%20%CE%BB_,well%20a%20graph%20is%20connected)) ([studylib.net](https://studylib.net/doc/14193813/social-network-graphs#:~:text=Graph%20Laplacian%20,the%20algebraic%20connectivity%20of%20%F0%9D%92%A2)). Thus, by analyzing Laplacian eigenvalues one can identify and delimit the critical thresholds at which a social stratification network transitions from a fluid, mobile state to a solidified, segregated state.
