## Effects of UV Irradiation on Backsheet Materials  
Photovoltaic backsheets are typically polymer laminates that degrade under prolonged UV exposure. Early-stage UV aging often manifests as surface discoloration, loss of gloss, or *chalking* – a powdery, white residue indicating polymer breakdown ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927024824004306#:~:text=Discoloration%2C%20chalking%2C%20and%20gloss,world%20exposure)). UV photons break chemical bonds in the backsheet’s outer layers, leading to chain scission and oxidation that embrittle the material. In one study, UV exposure alone caused the backsheet surface to develop pits and “fish-scale” micro-craters; once the material was thus embrittled, even modest mechanical strain induced visible cracking across the surface ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=Single%20UV%20exposure%20caused%20pits,scale%20craters%20on%20the%20surface)). Over time, UV-weakened backsheets may also delaminate or lose adhesion in multi-layer constructions as their outer UV-protective coatings degrade. This photochemical aging ultimately reduces the backsheet’s mechanical integrity and its ability to protect the module.  

## Effects of Humidity Cycling on Backsheet Materials  
Moisture is another major stressor that accelerates backsheet aging, especially when cycles of high and low humidity repeat. Many backsheet polymers (notably PET, a common core layer) are susceptible to **hydrolysis** – chemical breakdown by water. In humid, high-temperature conditions, water molecules infiltrate the polymer and cleave its chains, causing a drop in molecular weight and tensile strength ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092702481300514X#:~:text=Terephthalate%20%28PET%29,the%20dominant%20stress%20factors%20for)). Essentially, the material becomes brittle as its long polymer chains are cut into shorter fragments. Repeated humidity **cycling** (wetting and drying) further stresses the backsheet: as it absorbs moisture it swells, then contracts upon drying. These expansion/contraction cycles can fatigue the polymer and any adhesive layers. Over time, such cycling can initiate micro-cracks or gaps at layer interfaces and even full delamination of multilayer backsheets (for example, between a fluoropolymer outer layer and the PET core) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092702481300514X#:~:text=PV%20module%2C%20cracking%20of%20PET,module%20and%20shorten%20its%20lifetime)). Thus, chronic exposure to humidity – especially with temperature fluctuations – erodes the backsheet’s structural cohesion and protective qualities.  

## Synergistic Aging from Combined UV and Humidity  
In real-world conditions, UV radiation and moisture often act together, compounding the degradation of PV backsheets. UV-induced damage can make the polymer more hydrophilic or chemically reactive, so that it more readily absorbs water. Conversely, the presence of humidity can promote photo-oxidation reactions during UV exposure (since water and oxygen facilitate polymer oxidation). Field studies have shown that prolonged exposure to intense sunlight **plus** environmental humidity and thermal cycling leads to rapid embrittlement of backsheets ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927024824004306#:~:text=chalking%20were%20monitored,blocker%20layer%20and)). In a desert-climate test, for instance, researchers found that a combination of high UV, high operating temperatures, and periodic humidity drove cracking of the backsheet’s outer UV-resistant layer, followed by chemical and physical degradation of the underlying PET core ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927024824004306#:~:text=chalking%20were%20monitored,blocker%20layer%20and)) ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927024824004306#:~:text=relative%20humidity%20resulting%20in%20cracking,spot%20observed%20on%20the%20PA)). In effect, moisture ingress through tiny UV-induced defects accelerates polymer breakdown, and once the outer layer fails, UV can penetrate deeper into the backsheet laminate. Standard accelerated aging tests often separate UV and damp-heat stress, but simultaneous UV+humidity in the field can produce synergistic failure modes more severe than either factor alone. Ultimately, the dual attack of UV light and moisture cycling greatly hastens backsheet aging – manifesting as chalking, deep cracks, and layer delamination much earlier than expected.  

## Impact on Module Sealing Integrity  
As the backsheet ages and develops cracks or pinholes, the **sealing integrity** of the PV module is compromised. A backsheet’s primary role is to serve as a moisture and oxygen barrier, but cracking **significantly increases** its water vapor and oxygen permeation rates ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0038092X24003578#:~:text=Cracking%20increases%20the%20oxygen%2Fwater%20vapor,permeation%20rate%20significantly)). In other words, once fissures form, humidity from the environment can diffuse into the module almost unimpeded. This intrusion of moisture has serious consequences: it can corrode metallic contacts and busbars, induce hydrolytic degradation of the encapsulant (e.g. EVA), and trigger delamination of layers inside the module ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092702481300514X#:~:text=decrease%20over%20time%20as%20water,and%20provides%20the%20necessary%20environmental)). Accordingly, backsheet cracking is considered one of the *most catastrophic* PV module failure modes, since it permits direct ingress of water (vapor and even liquid) that greatly accelerates internal damage and performance loss ([www.sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092702481300514X#:~:text=tensile%20strength%2C%20result%20in%20the,on%20the%20performance%20and%20reliability)). The loss of a tight seal also undermines the module’s electrical insulation – dampness reaching active circuitry raises leakage currents. Field data confirm that modules with heavily degraded or cracked backsheets exhibit declining insulation resistance (especially under humid conditions) and are prone to ground-fault events ([www.susenergy.co.uk](https://www.susenergy.co.uk/wet-leakage-resistance-development-of-modules-with-various-backsheet-types.html#:~:text=manufacturers,site%20near)). In practice, severe backsheet aging can lead to module power loss, safety hazards, and early failure because the compromised backsheet can no longer effectively shield the solar cells from environmental stress.
